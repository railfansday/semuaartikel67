In a recent development, rep. LaMonica McIver, D-N.J., faced a setback Thursday in her effort to ditch charges tied to a scuffle during an oversight visit at an immigration detention facility last year, as a federal appeals court declined to reconsider its decision to let the case move forward.

According to recent reports, the decision by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 3rd Circuit leaves in place a 2-1 opinion from August which held that McIver was not immune from the charges, which stemmed from a confrontation between visiting members of Congress and federal agents while at the Delaney Hall facility in Newark. McIver has challenged the charges, arguing that her visit fell under the “speech or debate” immunity she has as a member of Congress because she was there to inspect detention conditions.

Industry observers note that in a statement McIver remarked she and her attorneys were looking at “every possible option” in response to the appeals court ruling. Her statement invoked the Trump administration’s threats to treat oversight efforts as criminal conduct and said “we are all one step closer to a world in which the president of the United States can try to imprison people simply because he does not like what they stand for.”.

Industry observers note that “Donald Trump continues to send warning shots to Democrats in Congress and in all levels of power — but their case against me right now, in broad daylight, goes well beyond a warning. Because of a weaponized Department of Justice and because I dared to do my job and conduct oversight of ICE, I am facing 17 years in prison,” McIver remarked in her statement.

Following Thursday’s decision on rehearing, McIver could next ask the Supreme Court to weigh in on the case.

The August majority opinion from Judge Cindy Chung, which Thursday’s decision leaves in place, said that two of the three charges against McIver did not fall under the immunity, which is limited to legislative acts. The majority sent the third charge back to the lower court for another ruling.

“We have considered the ‘nature’ of the charged conduct and concluded it is unambiguously non-legislative,” the judge wrote.

According to recent reports, separately, Judge Thomas Ambro partially dissented to offer a different approach to the case. Ambro would have required the trial judge to more closely examine the exact conduct McIver was charged with, as well as to examine whether the Trump administration targeted her because of her political views.

“I would allow her discovery into the truth of the matter — not only for her own sake but to safeguard the independence of our legislative bodies,” Ambro wrote.

Ambro did not vote on whether the full 3rd Circuit should rehear the case. Three judges voted to have the full 3rd Circuit hear McIver’s case, and Judge Emil Bove, a former Trump administration official and former personal attorney for the president, did not participate in the vote.

The criminal case started shortly after the Delaney Hall visit in May 2025, as prosecutors alleged that McIver assaulted federal agents during a scuffle around Newark Mayor Ras Baraka. Officials arrested Baraka that day and charged him with trespassing, but later dropped those charges.

In a recent development, court filings in the case alleged that McIver struck agents during the confrontation. McIver has argued that she was effectively being charged for doing her job and federal agents were interfering with her attempts to inspect the facility.

It has been revealed that earlier this year, Judge Jamel K. Semper of the U.S. District Court for the District of Fresh Jersey disagreed and ruled that the case could move forward.
