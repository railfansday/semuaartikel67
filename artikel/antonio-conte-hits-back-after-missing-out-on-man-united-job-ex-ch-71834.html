Antonio Conte has claimed clubs are 'frightened of winning' if they have concerns over his methods after the Italian was overlooked for the Manchester United job.

Conte had been heavily linked with the role at Old Trafford, joining Julian Nagelsmann, Eddie Howe and Unai Emery among the list bookmakers' favourites to replace Michael Carrick.

Man United ultimately went with another former Chelsea and Tottenham boss in Mauricio Pochettino, paying around £10million in compensation to lure him away from being head coach of the United States' national team.

Conte, who remains out of work after leaving Napoli in the summer, has now hit back at reports suggesting Man United may have been put off by his intensity and demands.

'First, let me address the question of my method,' Conte told the Cronache di Spogliatoio podcast.

'If anyone is frightened by my method, then they are frightened of winning, because that methodology leads to victory. That is my answer.

Conte hit back at suggestions Man United's hierachy might have been relucant to appoint him because of his methods.

'Manchester United has such a significant history that I find it strange to imagine anyone could be afraid of an approach aimed at restoring the club to its true stature.

It has been revealed that '(United) last won the league in 2013, under Ferguson. I have immense respect for Sir Alex Ferguson.

It has been revealed that 'I've read his book, and when I was in London, I was fortunate enough to visit his home and spend a day with him. He has an incredible passion for talking about football, though, at times, his Scottish accent can be quite hard to follow.

'Through his own methodology and approach, he made Manchester United great.

'That is why I find certain remarks almost offensive toward United; it's as if someone were placing a veto on the club's return to its own history.

Recent findings suggest that 'A method is what's needed to achieve major results. And if you're suggesting that this method requires hard work, well, yes, it does. Everyone has to work hard, not just the players.'.

Conte has long been known for his combustible personality - as well as an impressive winning record as a coach.

At Chelsea, he fell out with star striker Diego Costa, centre back David Luiz and the club's board. At Napoli, he left a year after winning the Serie A title and said there was 'too much poison, too much malice' at the club.

Conte has developed a reputation for his high intensity and demands, while the Italian famously left Tottenham after an explosive press conference attacking the club's culture.

Conte has won five Serie A titles across three clubs and won the Premier League with Chelsea.

Conte famously left Tottenham after an explosive press conference in 2023, where he labelled his players as 'selfish' and took aim at the club's culture.

The Italian, however, has overseen five Serie A title wins during his spells at Juventus, Inter Milan and Napoli, along with winning the Premier League and FA Cup with Chelsea.

It is not the first time Conte has spoken out in recent weeks following links to Old Trafford, with the 57-year-old having denied he had set 'specific conditions' about taking over at United.

La Repubblica claimed Conte wanted 'carte blanche on the transfer market' and to act as a manager, rather than a head coach, with a technical staff behind the scenes chosen by him involving 'trusted collaborators'.

'My name has been linked with numerous clubs, but there are currently no negotiations at an advanced stage,' Conte responded on social media last month.

According to recent reports, 'Following a wonderful and successful spell in charge of Napoli, I will take the time I need to consider how and when to return to management. It will be a carefully considered decision, one I will make when I believe the conditions are right.

'I have read reports of financial demands and requests for full control that I have supposedly already made to the clubs mentioned, as well as reservations about what has been described as my "strong personality". These reports are unfounded, as there is presently no concrete basis for such assessments.

'Should a concrete opportunity arise, that will be the time to hold discussions with the club concerned and jointly consider the terms of a potential agreement, with due regard for both parties' needs and the qualities and approach that define my work as a manager.'.

Pochettino, who landed the job at Old Trafford, has faced a whirlwind start having flown into Manchester following his final match in charge of the United States on Tuesday.

The Argentine took training for the first time on Thursday and will lead the Red Devils out for the first time on Sunday in a blockbuster clash with Liverpool at Anfield.

Pochettino takes over with Man United lying in the Premier League's relegation zone having won just one of their opening ten matches.
