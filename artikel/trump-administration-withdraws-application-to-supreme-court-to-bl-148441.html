Trump administration withdraws application to Supreme Court to block lower court ruling on protected status for Venezuelan nationals

Industry observers note that originally published on Nov. 18; updated with latest developments on Nov. 19 at 4:35 p.m.

The battle over the Temporary Protected Status program – which allows foreign nationals to remain in the United States and work here when they cannot safely return home – returned to the Supreme Court on Wednesday afternoon. Specifically, the Trump administration asked the justices to block a September 2025 ruling by a federal judge in San Francisco that kept the program in place for Venezuelan nationals. U.S. Solicitor General D. John Sauer told the court that if they do not act, the order by U.S. District Judge Edward Chen “will spring into effect, inflicting severe and immediate harm.”.

In a recent development, then-DHS Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas designated Venezuela for the TPS program in 2021 and again in 2023. In 2025, he declared that the program would be extended through October 2026. Mayorkas cited a “severe humanitarian emergency” resulting from the “severe political and economic crisis” in Venezuela as the reason for the designation.

After Mayorkas’ successor, Kristi Noem, declared her decisions to end Venezuela’s designation and its subsequent extension, a group representing TPS holders went to federal court in San Francisco to challenge those decisions. Chen set them aside, and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 9th Circuit upheld that ruling. In 2025, while that litigation was moving forward, the Supreme Court paused Chen’s order with regard to Venezuela, effectively clearing the way for the Trump administration to deport Venezuelan TPS beneficiaries while the case continued.

Sources indicate that in June 2026, in Mullin v. Doe, the Supreme Court ruled – in a pair of cases involving Haitian and Syrian TPS beneficiaries – that courts generally cannot review the Secretary of Homeland Security’s decision to designate or terminate a country under the TPS program. Relying on that decision, the Trump administration asked the court to send the Venezuelan TPS case back to the 9th Circuit for another look, which the court did in October of this year.

It has been revealed that when the case returned to the 9th Circuit, Sauer told the justices, and the Supreme Court’s initial stay of Chen’s ruling expired, the court of appeals entered an administrative stay, which is due to expire on Nov. 20. “Absent judicial intervention between now and” then, Sauer wrote, “the Department of Homeland Security will have to resurrect TPS status for hundreds of thousands of Venezuelans after a nineteen-month hiatus.” Indeed, he noted, his office “has been informed that the government could be forced to immediately release into the country some 1800 or so Venezuelan aliens from detention.” And all of this would result, he continued, even though the Supreme Court has already “conclusively held in Doe” that the TPS statute bars courts from reviewing the DHS secretary’s decision to designate or end the designation of TPS.

It has been revealed that on Thursday, the Trump administration withdrew its application. In a letter to Scott Harris, the clerk of the Supreme Court, Sauer explained that “a few hours after we filed our stay application, the court of appeals issued an order” extending its current administrative stay (and therefore keeping Chen’s ruling on hold) until seven days after it concerns a decision. “Should the Ninth Circuit enter an adverse judgment,” Sauer added, “we intend to immediately return to this Court for additional relief.”.
