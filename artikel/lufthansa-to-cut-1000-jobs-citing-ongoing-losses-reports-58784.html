📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Germany's flagship airline Lufthansa plans to cut 1,000 jobs at its Frankfurt and Munich hubs, according to a firm letter seen by German news agency DPA and news outlets.

The letter was reportedly signed by Lufthansa CEO Jens Ritter and four other board members.

This is reportedly down to ongoing financial losses. In the first nine months of the year, the airline recorded an operating loss of €180 million ($197 million).

In a recent development, the executive board remarked on average the loss amounts to €20 million every month, according to media reports. The board stated in the letter that "this is the third year in a row of not being financially successful.".

"Lufthansa is presently not competitive in terms of its cost structure, efficiency and productivity," the letter remarked.

At the end of 2025, the Lufthansa group employed some 67,100 people, up 1% from the previous year.

The company also intends to cut employee travel benefits, restructure its hub operations to reduce crew overnight stays abroad, and reduce short- and medium-haul capacity by replacing some A320 aircraft with A220 jets.

In a recent development, furthermore, it plans to equip ten aircraft per month with Starlink, a satellite internet service developed by tech billionaire Elon Musk. The airline hopes to generate "additional revenue” by monetizing onboard internet access, according to the letter.

Sources indicate that the time frame of the cuts is unclear. It's also unknown whether the cuts were a part of a previously declared downsizing.

In September 2025, Lufthansa mentioned it would slash 4,000 administrative positions due to the implementation of AI and digitalization.

In regards to this year, Lufthansa has faced rising fuel costs spurred by the US-Israeli war with Iran, further complicating its financial outlook.
