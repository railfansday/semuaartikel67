📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

After a recent slew of tech outages, service disruptions, and flight chaos this week, it’s now Apple customers who are facing technical issues.

According to recent reports, the iPhone maker’s System Status page indicates that there are challenges with iCloud’s Account & Sign In services affecting “all people,” which could make the service slow or unavailable. The App Store, AppleCare on Device, and Apple Music are also experiencing issues, the page indicates.

Sources indicate that like all major tech companies, Apple faces outages and other technical challenges affecting its cloud services at times. However, today’s issues are likely more noticeable to end owners because problems with iCloud can lead to account sign-in problems. This could cause your iPhone to ask for your Apple ID and password, and you could see other attempts to sign in to services with your Apple account information fail.

On Reddit, some folks also noted that they were unable to activate a new iPhone, which is likely related to these ongoing challenges.

According to recent reports, given the recent increase in AI-driven cyberattacks, users across social media have already begun to speculate about the cause. In particular, users are expressing concerns about the Apple outage, as it arrives only days after technical failures at Microsoft led to its cloud systems that power flagship services like Outlook and Teams failing due to cascading software complications.

Similarly, a brief outage of Chase’s banking app this week garnered attention, as did an ongoing partial outage (at the time of publication) of the National Security Agency’s internal classified network, both of which were blamed on software glitches.

So far, none of the outages in the past week have been attributed to cyberattacks, but rather to technical problems.

Apple responded to TechCrunch’s request for comment (which it initially gave to Bloomberg), noting that the outages were caused by an internal patch and not a cyberattack. “Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal update caused some outages this morning. We’re working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible,” the statement read.

After the initial outages were reported, Apple’s status page was updated to include other outages impacting Apple School Manager, iCloud Mail, iCloud Web Apps (iCloud.com), Schoolwork, and the Volume Purchase Program.

By 6 PM ET on Tuesday, the status page revealed all the outages were resolved.
