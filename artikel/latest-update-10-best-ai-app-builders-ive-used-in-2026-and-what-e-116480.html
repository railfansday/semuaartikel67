It has been revealed that AI App Builders are exploding in popularity, and for good reason. It’s never been easier to build real, functional apps and internal tools using app builders. To test them all, I built the same app on 10 of the most popular AI app builders and tested each across usability, cost, correctness, and ease of use. Most of them can turn a paragraph of description into something that looks like software within a single session. The differences show up around the tenth prompt, when you learn what each edit costs and who has to fix the code once something breaks. I took every price from the vendor's own pricing page on October 29, 2026.

It has been revealed that TLDR: Of the best AI app builders I've used, Softr is the one I'd hand to a team that needs auth, data, user groups or integrations. The other nine I tested include code generators such as Lovable and Bolt and visual platforms such as Bubble and Glide, and each one carries a cost or upkeep trade-off worth seeing before you commit.

I looked at the same seven things in every tool, from the first prompt onward:.

In a recent development, softr gets used a lot by operations and business teams as their AI app builder for secure client portals, internal tools and other business apps, none of which require code. Write a short brief in your own words, and the AI Co-Builder turns it into a database plus the core app that sits on it: tables, pages and layouts, the data connections behind them, plus user groups and permissions, so real owners can sign in from day one. Workflows come as the next step, either by prompting the Co-Builder again or by adding them in the visual editor.

Sources indicate that the Free initiative is $0 for unlimited apps, with room for 5 app consumers, 3 builders, 5,000 records and 5 AI credits each month. Basic runs $19 per month, Pro $99 and Business $329, all on annual billing. Enterprise pricing is custom and includes SSO and SCIM for organizations managing access at scale. The full plan table sits on Softr's pricing page.

Recent findings suggest that lovable builds full-stack web apps through conversation, writing React code while it takes care of hosting, SSL and the backend. It's designed to move a founder or product team from a prompt to a shareable prototype, and the preview updates as the agent works.

Base44 calls itself a no-code AI platform: you explain the app, and it generates the structure, design and logic while running the backend itself. Authentication, a database and analytics come on every plan, Free included.

According to recent reports, base44 is free for 25 message credits a month. Starter costs $16 per month and Builder $40 per month, both on annual billing, with Pro at $80 and Elite at $160.

Bolt is a coding agent that writes full-stack projects in the browser and runs them on Bolt Cloud, which supplies hosting, databases and integrations. It suits people who want to watch the code take shape and edit it themselves.

According to recent reports, bolt Free is $0 with 1M tokens a month and Bolt branding. Pro costs $25 per month with 10M tokens, and Teams runs $30 a month for each member on monthly billing.

Replit pairs its Agent with a cloud workspace, so a description turns into a running app whose sign-in, database, hosting and monitoring come wired up. The homepage pitches it to people who don't code, though the result underneath is a code project.

In a recent development, core costs $20 per month ($18 with annual billing) and Pro costs $100 per month ($90 with annual billing). Enterprise is custom and adds SSO, SCIM and audit logs.

Glide starts internal business apps from whatever you hand it, whether a spreadsheet, a written prompt or a PDF, and its newer platform, GlideOS, reads a sheet's structure and relationships to draft screens and workflows. Its apps grew up on phones, and that heritage still shapes the product.

Glide Free is $0 with limited credits. Basic costs $25 per month, Plus $50 per month and Pro $125 per month; annual billing isn't offered yet, according to Glide.

Bubble is a visual builder that targets the browser as well as native iOS and Android, with an AI agent that drafts pages and logic before you refine them by hand. One editor covers the design, the database, the logic and the privacy rules behind each page.

In a recent development, bubble Free gives you 50K workload units and 500 AI credits each month. Web looks run $32 per month for Starter, $119 for Growth and $349 for Team, all on annual billing.

Retool is an internal tools engine aimed at engineering teams. You describe or assemble an app that connects straight to production databases, APIs and LLMs, with auth and access controls in place at deployment.

Sources indicate that v0 generates full-stack Next.js web apps from a prompt and deploys them to Vercel, with Design Mode for visual tweaks and GitHub sync for the code. It's designed around a developer's workflow.

v0's Free plan gives new customers $5 of one-time credits and caps messages at 7 per day. Plus runs $30 a month per user, and Business $100 a month per user.

Sources indicate that figma Make turns prompts and Figma design context into code-backed prototypes and web apps you can publish at a URL. It can also scaffold a Payload backend with a database, APIs, auth and an admin panel, or connect to Supabase for sign-in and stored data.

In a recent development, figma's Starter plan is free with up to 500 AI credits a month. Figma lists a Professional Full seat at $16 per month with 3,000 AI credits, and Organization Full seats cost $55 per month on annual billing.

In a recent development, collins Dictionary named "vibe coding" its Word of the Year for 2025, and the phrase now covers any workflow where you describe software in plain language and an AI produces it. AI app builders package that workflow with hosting, data and sign-in, so a non-developer can get from idea to something clickable without installing anything.

The questions buyers ask have turned plainer since then: what each edit costs, who keeps the app running, and whether a client can sign in and see their own records alone. A generated front end makes a good demo, and a team using it every day needs permissions and a predictable bill for the next hundred edits.

Softr is now my default when recommending AI app builders. It handles teams, auth, and real company data better than any other product. The AI Co-Builder drafts tables, pages and user groups, workflows follow as the next step, and the app keeps running on maintained platform components with no codebase to patch. App-user pricing and an editor that works without AI credits keep later changes from turning into a running meter.

It has been revealed that three situations call for a different tool. Glide makes a better mobile-first choice when the app spends its days on phones in the field. Bubble gives more front-end design freedom when a project calls for bespoke layouts, interactive animation or heavy brand styling. If your engineers want version control and audit logs and can handle a technical setup, Retool covers that need.

Agents are moving inside the apps these tools produce. Gartner expects task-specific AI agents inside 40% of enterprise applications by the close of 2026, against under 5% in 2025, so a 2027 app builder will need to host agents that respect the same permissions as human customers.

According to recent reports, building is also leaving the builder's own window. The Model Context Protocol now sits under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation and reports more than 97 million SDK downloads a month, with client support in ChatGPT and Claude, among others. Softr's changelog dates its official Claude and ChatGPT connector to October 6, 2026, and I expect more teams to start an app from the chat assistant they already use.

The caution comes from Gartner too: it predicts organizations will scrap upward of 40% of agentic AI projects before 2028, citing escalating costs, unclear business value and inadequate risk controls. Going into 2027, I'd judge any builder on whether it keeps agent costs and access rules visible to the team running the app.
