How to put your iPad in recovery mode (with and without a home button)

A failed update or a forgotten passcode doesn't have to mean the end of your iPad.

It has been revealed that many iPad problems can be fixed with a simple restart, but sometimes that's not enough. Severe issues with the tablet's software can make it completely inoperable, and in these cases, putting the iPad in recovery mode might be your only option.

In a recent development, recovery mode also lets you connect your iPad to your computer to update it, or in the worst-case scenario, restore it to its factory default settings. It's best to save recovery mode for when nothing else works, but when it's needed, it can mean the difference between a bricked iPad and one that works just like new.

There are a few situations when you might want to use recovery mode. Major software updates can sometimes fail, leaving you with a partially installed update that keeps your iPad from turning on. Your iPad might reboot itself continuously, or you might enter the wrong passcode too many times and get locked out. Your computer might also stop recognizing your iPad, or your iPad might show a screen prompting you to connect it to a computer.

To put your iPad in recovery mode, first connect it to your computer with a USB cable, and open Finder on Mac or the Apple Devices app on PC. From here, the process differs based on whether your iPad has a home button or not. If it does, hold the home and the power button down at the same time. Eventually, your iPad screen will go black and then show the Apple logo. Finally, it will show the recovery mode icon: an image of a USB cable connecting to a computer. Now release the buttons.

Recent findings suggest that newer iPads, like the iPad Pro, don't have a home button, so you need to use the power and volume buttons instead. First press and release the volume up button and then the volume down button. Next, press and hold the power button, and keep holding it through the Apple logo until the recovery mode icon appears. Then let go.

At this point, you can use the Finder or Apple Devices on your computer to find your iPad. If the process worked, you should see a pop-up explaining that the iPad needs to be restored or updated. It's best to start with the update option, which will allow your computer to reinstall iPadOS without affecting anything else stored on your device.

Sources indicate that if this option is successful, you'll be able to use your iPad again without losing any data. If it doesn't work, though, you'll have to put it back in recovery mode and choose the restore option. This will restore the iPad to its factory settings, completely erasing your data in the process.

You can restore everything from your most recent backup (if you have enough iCloud storage), but it's a far more disruptive process that should only be used as a last resort. If your iPad still isn't working after trying both options or you can't get it to enter recovery mode, it may be time to get it professionally repaired or shop for a new iPad.
