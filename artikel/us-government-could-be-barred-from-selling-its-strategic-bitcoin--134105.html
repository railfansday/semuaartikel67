The US Treasury could be required to publicly verify its Bitcoin holdings under legislation advancing through Congress.

Sources indicate that the House Financial Services Committee formally reported the amended American Reserve Modernization Act of 2026 on Nov. 24, placing it on the House Union Calendar for the full chamber to consider.

Recent findings suggest that the legislative development follows the committee's Sept. 16 vote, when lawmakers approved the measure 28-21.

Industry observers note that introduced in May by Republican Rep. Nick Begich, the bill would give statutory backing to the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve that President Donald Trump established through a March 2025 executive order.

Under Section 6 of the legislation, Treasury would establish a proof-of-reserves system using public cryptographic attestations and publish annual reports detailing its Bitcoin holdings, transactions, and control of the associated private keys.

Treasury would also appoint an independent auditor specializing in cryptographic attestations to verify the accuracy of its disclosures. The US Comptroller General would regularly review the reserve's management and audits.

The proposed requirements would give investors a clearer picture of how much Bitcoin the federal government has accumulated as a strategic asset.

Trump's executive order required federal agencies to account for their digital asset holdings within 30 days, but directed those disclosures to Treasury and the President's Working Group on Digital Asset Markets rather than the public.

H.R. 8957 would require a broad internal agency inventory and a public report on Bitcoin deposited in the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve.

The annual public report would detail reserve holdings, transactions and control of private keys.

The distinction is significant because government-linked blockchain wallets can contain seized Bitcoin that remains subject to legal proceedings or restitution claims. Cryptographic verification, combined with independent accounting, could help establish how much Bitcoin actually belongs to the reserve.

Beyond transparency, the legislation would formalize the federal government's long-term Bitcoin accumulation strategy and limit its ability to liquidate reserve assets.

Federal agencies would have 60 days after enactment to submit comprehensive inventories of their cryptocurrency holdings to Treasury.

The department would then have six months from enactment to establish the statutory Bitcoin reserve and a separate stockpile for other digital assets. Agencies would have another 30 days to transfer eligible holdings.

Federally owned Bitcoin deposited into the reserve would face a minimum 20-year holding period beginning at enactment, during which Treasury could neither sell the assets nor pledge them as collateral.

According to recent reports, two years before that period expires, Treasury would recommend whether to continue holding Bitcoin or begin gradually reducing its position. Potential sales could reach 10% of reserve assets over any two-year period following the lockup.

Meanwhile, the legislation directs the Treasury and Commerce departments to evaluate ways of expanding the reserve without increasing the national debt or imposing additional costs on taxpayers.

The six-month study would examine potential exchanges of other government-held cryptocurrencies for Bitcoin, additional asset forfeitures and partnerships with states, private entities and international counterparts.

However, the legislation faces a tightening congressional timetable. With the 119th Congress approaching its January conclusion, lawmakers have limited time to secure House and Senate approval.

Failure to pass the measure before the current Congress ends would require its reintroduction in the next session, leaving America's Bitcoin reserve governed by executive action without the proposed public auditing requirements.
