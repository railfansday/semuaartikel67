Industry News: PENN’s Interactive revenue rise rests on partner taxes as gaming revenue slips 2.1%

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that PENN Entertainment's online segment grew revenue in the third quarter, but the gain came from passed-through partner gaming taxes, not from players. The segment now needs a positive fourth quarter to reach a full-year target that has already been lowered.

It has been revealed that other revenues carried the whole of the Interactive segment’s third-quarter gain at PENN Entertainment. Segment revenue rose to $348.4 million from $297.7 million, while the third-party partner gaming-tax amounts booked there climbed to $191.4 million from $139.5 million a year earlier. Online gaming revenue itself fell 2.1% to $126.6 million from $129.3 million, as iCasino growth was offset by lower sports betting handle.

This pattern places PENN within the slowing-iGaming-growth story running through this week’s US operator reports.

According to recent reports, the nine-month figures show the same reliance on the gross-up. Interactive revenue reached $1,056.0 million, up from $903.9 million. The tax gross-up within it rose to $562.7 million from $405.6 million.

According to recent reports, the group as a whole had a stronger quarter. Total revenue was $1,840.0 million. Net income came in at $32.6 million, against a $865.1 million net loss a year earlier. PENN also raised its full-year retail guidance to $5.89 billion in revenue and $1.973 billion in adjusted EBITDAR, increases of $16 million and $10 million.

Profitability is improving faster than revenue. The Interactive adjusted EBITDA loss for the first nine months shrank to $38.7 million from $227.6 million. PENN attributes that mainly to lower marketing, general and administrative, and labor costs.

Industry observers note that expansion has weighed on the quarter. PENN launched theScore Bet, theScore Casino and standalone Hollywood iCasino apps in Alberta on July 13, 2026. Excluding that third-quarter launch investment, Interactive adjusted EBITDA improved quarter over quarter throughout 2026, management stated on the earnings call.

Sources indicate that the standalone Hollywood iCasino brand is central to the blueprint. It posted record quarterly revenue in the second quarter. PENN is now leaning on its US growth, and on steady trends through October, to support the full-year Interactive target. That makes the brand a test for whether a mid-tier US online casino can turn profitable.

According to recent reports, the target itself has moved. In February, PENN guided the segment to breakeven adjusted EBITDA for 2026, which it described as a $268 million improvement from 2025. The cost of the Alberta launch is now built into a weaker outlook.

PENN Entertainment CEO Jay Snowden discussed the potential of more Canadian provinces launching iGaming on an earnings call today.

Land-based trends offer some support. CEO Jay Snowden noted PENN’s own Pennsylvania gaming revenue in October posted stronger year-over-year growth than in any other month of 2026. Most of that growth came in the second half of the month, after skill games were shut down. In his view, the midterm results could help PENN further by weakening support for skill-game machines.

The online segment still has to deliver. Reaching the revised target depends on Interactive turning its third-quarter loss into positive adjusted EBITDA in the fourth quarter.
