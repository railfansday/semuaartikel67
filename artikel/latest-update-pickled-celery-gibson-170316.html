Recent findings suggest that traditionally, a Gibson is a savory twist on a martini, substituting a cocktail onion for the olive. My version, which incorporates the very easy homemade Pickled Celery, has the same clean, briny qualities as the classic but with a touch of complexity from the pickling spices and a bright, leafy flavor. I like to stir my Gibson with a handful of celery leaves to amp up that fresh flavor, but if your celery is leafless, or if you’re making this for a substantial group, feel free to exclude that ingredient.

In a recent development, from Victorian celery vases to midcentury relish trays, celery has been part of hosting parties in the United States for many generations. At gatherings hosted by my grandma Dorothy in western New York in the ’90s, I remember significant stalks of celery resting in pitchers of salt water in the middle of every table, acting as both a palate-cleansing snack and a centerpiece. Similarly, this recipe for pickled celery can function as a garnish (consider skewering it onto a cocktail pick with a cornichon and a cherry tomato for a Bloody Mary), part of a canapé, or just as a snack on its own.

According to recent reports, in a small saucepan over low heat, combine ½ cup [120 ml] distilled vinegar, 1 tsp. kosher salt, 1 tsp. sugar, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp. black peppercorns, 1 clove, and ¼ tsp. coriander seeds with ½ cup [120 ml] water. Cook for about 5 minutes, until the sugar and salt are dissolved. Place 4 celery stalks (about 200 g), cut into 1½" [4 cm] lengths, in a heatproof bowl, and pour the hot brine over it. Allow the mixture to cool to room temperature, then transfer it to an airtight container. Refrigerate at least overnight before using, and store, refrigerated, for up to 1 week.

Place a coupe, Nick and Nora, or martini glass in the freezer to chill.
