Jensen Huang’s Houses: Exploring the Former Nvidia CEO’s $38 Million San Francisco Mansion and Other Properties

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It’s been a massive week for Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang. The billionaire revealed he is stepping down as CEO of the AI hardware giant following reports that he had at least two extramarital affairs: one with a suspected Chinese spy, and another with one of his tech giant’s senior executives (other reports allege a third affair with another Nvidia employee). Huang will reportedly testify before Congress later this month to discuss his relationship with the alleged spy. We can only assume that the Taipei City native is weathering the storm at home, but where are Jensen Huang’s houses? Even Huang himself may be wondering about the answer to that question, if a recent Upcoming York Times interview is any indication (more on that later).

According to recent reports, unsurprisingly, the tech titan hasn’t strayed too far from Silicon Valley, where Nvidia has been based since it was founded in the mid-’80s, with his real estate purchases. After graduating from Oregon State University, where he met his wife, Lori Huang, Huang began buying properties in California’s Bay Area, and he and Lori still own two of them. So far, the superbillionaire’s only exception to that rule has been an estate on Maui in Hawaii. Time will tell if the scandal spurs any real estate changes for him.

One thing that will presumably change about Huang’s home life in the immediate future: footing the bill for his security team. Nvidia has, until now, covered the cost of its former CEO’s private security. The manufacturer spent about $4 million in 2026 on “home security, protection during personal travel, consulting, and drivers” for Huang, per the Fresh York Post. Though that arrangement is likely coming to an end, that shouldn’t be too substantial a problem for Jensen; the scandal has put Nvidia’s stock—and therefore Jensen’s net worth—in a slight flux, but he is still worth an estimated $144.7 billion at the time of publication.

Read on for a rundown of Jensen Huang’s houses dating back to his arrival in Silicon Valley.

According to recent reports, according to The Fresh York Times, Huang married Lori shortly after his 1984 graduation from Oregon State University, where they met as lab partners in an electrical engineering class. The newlywed graduates hightailed it to Silicon Valley to start their careers as microchip designers, and Jensen bought his first house: a 1,500-square-foot three-bedroom home in San Jose, California. It’s unclear exactly when or for how much he bought the property, but Jensen sold it in 1988 for $185,000.

That same year, Huang and Lori moved into a larger San Jose home that they bought for $338,000. With 2,300 square feet of interior living space, it had four bedrooms. Huang lived here when he co-founded Nvidia in 1993; he sold the house in 2002 for $500,000, the Wall Street Journal reports.

From there, the Huangs moved into an even more upgraded residence: a 7,000-square-foot home in Los Altos Hills, a tony area about 15 miles from the Nvidia headquarters. They paid $6.9 million for the newly built six-bedroom dwelling in 2003. According to Realtor.com, the two-story house hosts a resort-worthy swimming pool. The Huangs still own this house.

Recent findings suggest that A year after buying his house in Los Altos Hills, Huang paid $7.5 million for a Hawaiian estate. The oceanfront property is located within Wailea Resort, an exclusive gated community on the south side of Maui, per the Wall Street Journal. The contemporary-style mansion reportedly has seven bedrooms across about 8,000 square feet. The Huangs still maintain this residence too.

In 2017, Huang made his most recent real estate purchase: a newly completed $38 million mansion in the high-end Pacific Heights neighborhood on San Francisco’s Gold Coast. The residence is modern in style, with a curved roofline and a limestone exterior. It boasts 11,400 square feet of interior living space, with seven bedrooms, seven full bathrooms, three half-baths, a wine cellar, an outdoor kitchen, an elevator, a home theater, a gym with a sauna, a library, numerous fireplaces, and a fifth-floor observation deck with views of the Golden Gate Bridge. The house took four years to construct, its developer told the WSJ.

San Francisco’s famous Golden Gate Bridge.

In a recent development, this seems to be Huang’s primary residence—although, according to a September 2026 New York Times interview, he could not tell you where it is. “There are a lot of skills that don’t matter,” Huang told the outlet in response to a question about AI weakening students’ basic math skills. “My first confession, I actually don’t know my address.… One day I had to pump gas—it was a few years ago. They needed my ZIP code, and I panicked. I didn’t know my ZIP code. I don’t know my telephone number. I forget these things. I can live with it.”.
