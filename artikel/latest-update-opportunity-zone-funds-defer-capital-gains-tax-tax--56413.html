Latest Update: Opportunity Zone Funds Defer Capital Gains Tax – Tax-Free Growth Takes 10 Years

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Put a capital gain into an opportunity zone fund only if you’d reinvest that money anyway and can leave it there for 10 years. For money invested from 2027, the tax on the gain is put off for five years and trimmed, not erased. The fund’s own growth goes tax-free only after a decade.

Sources indicate that the 2025 tax law that made opportunity zones permanent set those terms, and they start on Jan. 1, 2027, when the Treasury’s upcoming zones take effect.

Sources indicate that after you sell stock, a business, or property at a gain, you have 180 days to put that gain into a qualified opportunity fund, which invests in designated low-income areas called opportunity zones. The tax on the gain is deferred for five years. Then 10% of the gain goes untaxed, or 30% if the fund keeps at least 90% of its assets in rural zones. Hold the fund at least 10 years, and its growth is tax-free when you sell, though growth after its 30th year is taxed.

Industry observers note that say a fund at least holds its value, and you put a $200,000 gain from a 2027 stock sale into it. Your 2032 return reports $180,000 of that gain, and that tax comes due while the fund still holds your money. Sell after 10 years, in 2037 at the earliest, and the fund’s growth is tax-free.

Industry observers note that the switch to the fresh rules makes timing matter. Gains already deferred under the old rules become taxable on Dec. 31, 2026, and IRS guidance bars rolling them into a new fund. A 2026 gain whose 180 days run into January 2027 can wait and go in under the new rules.

Sources indicate that only the gain qualifies, so any extra money you add counts as a separate investment with no tax break. Getting the full break also means leaving your money in projects in low-income areas for a decade, and the manager’s fees cut your return. The state securities regulators’ association warns that you could escape the tax and still lose your whole investment.

Sources indicate that so judge the fund as an investment first. Read what it will build and where, look up its manager and anyone selling it to you on FINRA’s BrokerCheck, and get every fee in writing.

Before you sign, ask whether you’d buy the fund with no tax break, and walk away if the answer is no.
