TRAINING WITHOUT TRACKING is like shooting a basketball without knowing where the hoop is, especially if you have an ultimate goal like running a marathon or increasing your VO2 max. Many people reach a point in their fitness journey where they hit a plateau, and more often than not, that flatline results from not having the right information. So if you're hitting the gym on a regular basis and putting in the work, it can be key to make sure that you are getting all the relevant health insights to make sure you're using your time efficiently.

According to recent reports, A few of the most crucial insights are heart rate variability (HRV), which describes the difference in time between the beats of your heart, and resting heart rate (RHR), which is the number of times your heart pumps while you are awake, relaxed and not moving. Heart rate monitors (HRMs) are the best way to get this data.

Industry observers note that understanding your HRV can help you make better workout choices and understand when it's time to recover. Generally speaking, your resting heart rate will become lower as your fitness level increases, indicating more efficient heart function and improved cardiovascular fitness. “Over time (and months of tracking your resting heart rate), you'll notice patterns. Maybe your RHR spikes every weekend, in part because you're out late Saturday nights. Or it's lower on Mondays, because you go to bed earlier on Sundays," indicates MH Fitness Director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. "All of this data can help you make smart lifestyle tweaks that aid your overall wellness — and set you up to move, look, and feel your best and most alert, whether you're in the gym or at the office.”.

Industry observers note that with the holidays coming up fast, it’s a good time to add a heart rate monitor to your wish list. It can help you start your New Year’s training goals on a data-driven note, and allow you to track your progress over time.

Recent findings suggest that ready to take your training to the next level? Read on to find the best heart rate monitors for you and what to consider when looking for a HRM.

Sources indicate that we've been raving about Whoop for years, and it's still one of our favorite fitness trackers simply because it collects so much useful data. This stylish and understated device uses PPG sensors to continuously check your heart rate, and can use the data it captures to figure out when your deepest sleep is happening.

According to recent reports, every day it delivers a recovery percentage and suggests the level of activity you should aim for, whether that a five-mile run or a rest day. In the past, the brand stuck with a simple black band, but these days there are more color and style options for anyone wanting to elevate the look of their HRM.

I personally am a fan of the ability to check my insights when I want, without a distractingly bright screen that pulls my attention.

In a recent development, while products like the Whoop band and the Oura Ring are relatively newly introduced on the scene, Garmin has been making wearable fitness trackers for a long time now. This heart rate monitor transmits real-time heart rate data over ANT+ connectivity and Bluetooth, giving you the immediate feedback you need to take your performance to the next level.

Industry observers note that this Garmin comes with a rechargeable battery with a seriously long life (two months). It charges easily using the provided 4-pin charging cable, similar to the ones that come with their watches.

According to recent reports, we were also impressed with how comfortable this strap is to wear, made out of a lightweight nylon weave. For placement, you want to put the sensor in the middle of your ribcage against your skin, not over your clothes, so that it can get the best readings. Once you are done with your run, you will be able to connect it to your smartphone or Garmin watch via Bluetooth, and then pull up your stats from the menu option.

Because this Garmin has Bluetooth, it is also able to connect pretty simply to cardio machines at your gym like treadmills if you want to track your workouts there. If you are a Garmin fan, and want even more functionality and access to the Garmin Connect app, you may want to spring for the Garmin HRM Pro model (about $60 more).

The biggest determination you need to make before purchasing is what your primary goal is for the heart rate monitor. Competitive triathletes may want to splurge on a top-shelf device that wraps around your chest for the most accurate data. Whereas beginners or more casual consumers might opt for a more modest option they can wear throughout the day (like a general fitness tracker) for daily health insights. Perhaps you spend a lot of time on the golf course and want to know how your heart handles hours of walking combined with the stress of trying to make par. Of course, you may be one of those high-performing individuals who uses both.

Beyond finding a device that fits your performance needs, you also need one that fits well on your body. This depends on whether you choose a chest strap, a wristband, or a smart ring. In most cases, the sensors need to be secured tightly against your body to get accurate readings. Take accurate body measurements before ordering offline, and be prepared to return for a different size if you need to (especially with something like a chest strap). If you are looking into smart rings, many of the options, like the Oura Ring we list, will ship out sizing kits for a small fee to get the correct fit.

According to recent reports, great data is useless if you don’t have an easy means of getting it. For devices without screens, make sure you check which smartphones and apps they are compatible with. They will usually connect with your personal devices through Bluetooth. For devices with screens, check images of the user interface to make sure you’ll enjoy it. Of course, the devices we are suggesting here with screens will display all the crucial information you’ll want. But some may have better compatibility with iOS or Android, which you’ll want to consider.

Recent findings suggest that with the exception of the Apple Watches (Ultra 3 and Series 11) and the Samsung Galaxy Watch 8 (two heart rate monitors that are equal parts smartwatch), the heart rate monitors we tested are all designed to run for days (even weeks) on a single charge. We love a heart rate monitor that needs minimal charging, and we also love one that lasts a long time in non-connected mode. Two heart rate monitors that surprised us when testing battery life include the Garmin HRM-Dual (3.5 years if used for one hour a day) and the Oura Ring (7 days while on).
