World of Warcraft: Forever has officially been out in the wild for a good few weeks now, and that's given fans plenty of time to uncover get-rich-quick exploits – including one that's made a good few players especially wealthy… much to the community's dismay.

According to recent reports, dungeons have been an especially hot topic in the community since the MMO left Beta earlier this month, with fan feedback on them leading to a 50% XP boost and further changes as recently as yesterday. The whole experience controversy isn't the only reason dungeons – specifically the Blackmaw Hold – have been in the limelight, though. An exploit that grants folks hundreds of thousands of gold is another.

Recent findings suggest that blizzard did nerf the exploit this week, which let players burst through enemies for wildly expensive drops in the dungeon over and over again; however, its effects on the economy of WoW: Forever are, unsurprisingly, lasting. So much so, in fact, that those innocent of the exploit's shady dealings are calling for Blizzard to enact a heavier hand. Over on its official forums, for example, one fan writes, "Remove the gold and ban them." They continue: "Remove the gold or I and many others will just quit. This is worse than the bots at this point." Comments see fellow players agreeing with the poster, with the top response reading, "100% the gold needs to be removed and bans need to be handed out." It's not an isolated conversation, either. Threads on Reddit see fans sharing in on the sentiment, with one in particular drawing attention to Blizzard axing the "100+ gold per hour farm.".

It has been revealed that replies align well with those on the World of Warcraft forums. "Remove all gold and hand out at minimum a month ban," one exclaims. "Make them miss raid introduces or straight up permaban them." They're not the only person to call for a permanent ban here: "If they don't revert gold earned, the economy is already shot with an exploit mafia controlling the market. Remove all gold and ban one month or permaban.".

It might seem like a drastic response, but this isn't the first time WoW: Forever fans have had to grapple with gold-related cheating. Back during the beta, Blizzard actually nabbed a good $200 worth of gold off a streamer who was, uh, pretty public with it (spoiler alert: they likely got it via real-money trading, which is strictly forbidden). Prior to that, the studio had stated that its fresh MMO's economy is "something we're taking very seriously" as well.

According to recent reports, so, will we see Blizzard crack down more strongly and dish out some bans to players abusing patched exploits like the Blackmaw Hold one in question here? Only time can tell, I suppose, but I'd argue it'll already have a negative effect on the game's economy by then, sadly.

Blizzard knows World of Warcraft: Forever has a gold problem – but that doesn't mean a feature everyone used to grind it in the OG MMO is coming back.
