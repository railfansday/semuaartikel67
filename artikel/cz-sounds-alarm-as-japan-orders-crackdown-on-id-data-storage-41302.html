📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Binance founder Changpeng Zhao, widely known as CZ, has expressed his concerns about the storage of sensitive personal data after Japanese authorities called on rental car services to delete customers' identity documents following a massive data breach.

In his latest X post, CZ pointed to a Nov. 18 notice issued by Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC). The notice urges rental car services to be cautious about unnecessarily storing images of driver’s licenses and other government-issued identification documents.

The recent call by Japanese authorities comes after a massive cybersecurity attack on a popular Japanese car-sharing service called Times Car, operated by Park24 Group.

In a recent development, the incident began when the company noticed suspicious activity on its system on Sept. 25. As a result, it launched an investigation into whether someone had accessed its systems without permission, potentially exposing customer data.

Industry observers note that as per the disclosure by Park24 on Oct. 23, about 6.63 million customer accounts were impacted by the breach.

Worse, the investigation uncovered evidence that hackers had transmitted 1,464,800 encrypted images of identification documents, including driver’s licenses, to external locations.

In a recent development, besides that, another 891,602 encrypted images of identification documents were accessed suspiciously. However, investigators found no evidence that these additional ID images had been transmitted outside the organization’s systems.

Japan’s Personal Information Protection Commission issued its notice on Nov. 18, focusing on the risks of retaining sensitive personal data even after its original purpose has been fulfilled.

The regulatory body stressed that storing unnecessary identification images could significantly worsen the consequences of cyberattacks.

Although Japan’s latest warning specifically targets the rental car industry, CZ’s decision to share his concerns about this issue also raises questions about personal data protection in the crypto industry.

Generally, centralized crypto exchanges require users to go through a Know Your Customer (KYC) process to access their trading platforms.

Depending on the platform and jurisdiction, these processes may require owners to provide photos of their passports, driver’s licenses, national ID cards, and other sensitive information.

This kind of sensitive data can be a goldmine for cybercriminals, who can exploit stolen personal information for various activities such as identity theft, phishing attacks, fraudulent registrations, and more.
