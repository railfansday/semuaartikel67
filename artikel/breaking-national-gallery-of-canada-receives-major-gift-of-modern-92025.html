The National Gallery of Canada (NGC) in Ottawa is embarking on a upcoming chapter in its collecting history with a promised gift of 56 modern and contemporary glass works from Canadian American businessman and philanthropist Charles Bronfman and his late wife, Andrea Bronfman. Accompanied by an endowment to establish the institution’s first curatorial position dedicated to decorative arts and design, the donation will help broaden the museum’s collection and increase its scholarly contributions.

“This is the gallery’s first gift of modern and 20th-century glass,” Jean-François Bélisle, director and chief executive of the NGC, tells The Art Newspaper. “It significantly expands our understanding of art history, bringing together artistic expression and material and technical innovation.”.

In a recent development, the gifted Bronfman Collection spans more than a century of glass experimentation, with works by 29 artists from Canada, Europe, the United States and Asia. The collection traces the medium’s evolution from the sinuous forms of Art Nouveau and Arts and Crafts to the streamlined aesthetics of Art Deco and Modernism. Early 20th-century examples by Tiffany Studios, Émile Gallé and the renowned studio Daum demonstrate how artists pushed glass beyond traditional decorative applications.

The works also chart the emergence of studio glass as an independent discipline in the post-war period. Additional artists with works in the donation include Dale Chihuly, whose gravity-defying installations are in museums worldwide, as well as the studio glass pioneer Toots Zynsky and the well-known Canadian American artist Laura Donefer.

Recent findings suggest that A selection of pieces from the collection is at the moment on view in the NGC’s European galleries (until 30 April 2027). Highlights of the installation include Edward Hald’s The Fireworks Bowl (around 1921), alongside architectural works by Tiffany and Frank Lloyd Wright.

“Harvey Littleton’s Red Crossed Form (1990) is another notable work that reflects the development of studio glass as an independent artistic medium,” states Anabelle Kienle Ponka, the NGC’s senior curator of European, American and Asian Art. “The presentation brings together very different approaches to glass, from the decorative traditions of the early 20th century to the formal and technical experimentation of contemporary artists.”.

In a recent development, the international scope of the donation highlights the exchange of technical traditions, particularly the influence of Venetian glassmaking on successive generations of artists, Kienle Ponka notes. Together, the works demonstrate the medium’s versatility across sculpture, architecture and ornamentation.

Sources indicate that equally significant for the NGC is the accompanying endowment, which will fund the museum’s first curatorial position dedicated to decorative arts and design. The NGC has not shared the sum of the endowment, but notes that the post will support research, exhibitions and future acquisitions, establishing a foundation for sustained institutional engagement with the field.

“[The donation’s] significance extends well beyond this gift,” Bélisle claims. “The position will allow us to pursue further acquisitions, develop exhibitions and scholarship, and build relationships with artists and institutions.”.

Sources indicate that the donation builds on a long history of the Bronfman family’s philanthropy in Canada's cultural sector. “My late wife Andy and I were admirers of the art of glass making and wanted to share this love with other Canadians,” Charles Bronfman noted in a statement. “With the planned celebrations marking the upcoming 100th birthday of my sister, Phyllis Lambert, founder of the Canadian Centre for Architecture, and the 50th anniversary of the Canada Council for the Arts’ Saidye Bronfman Award, named after my mother, I am truly delighted to be contributing in this meaningful way to the national collections.”.

In a recent development, the NGC has received other transformative gifts in recent years, including a 2025 donation of 61 contemporary works valued at C$22.8m ($16.8m) from collector Bob Rennie and his family. “The Bronfman gift is significant in a different way,” Bélisle says. “Its importance lies not only in the quality and range of the works, but also in the accompanying endowment, which will provide the expertise and resources to develop decorative arts and design as a sustained area of collecting and scholarship.”.
