Google has added Wallet integration to Personal Intelligence in AI Mode, letting you learn about your spending habits, plan a budget, maximize credit card rewards, and more with a simple Search.

Starting today, Google confirmed, Personal Intelligence for Search is expanding to more apps, including Wallet and Drive. Google can’t move or manage any money, but it can give you tailored, personalized search results about your finances, like showing where you spend the most, identifying recurring subscriptions you might not be aware of, and suggesting which credit card might be best to use for travel. All you have to do is ask in a search.

Also: I tried Personal Intelligence in Android Auto – and it knew exactly where I wanted to go.

This feature comes just over a week after Google introduced Personal Intelligence to Wallet.

According to recent reports, once your Wallet is connected, it takes a few minutes to fully sync. Once it’s finished, you can ask questions through AI mode in Search.

I decided to give it a try for myself, starting off with a simple query: “Based on my spending, what’s a quick way I can save money?” I could have told you one of the primary culprits, but Google happily responded with what I already knew: far too much of my money goes to fast food and coffee.

“You frequently grab quick-service meals,” the response mentioned, pointing out several fast food places that I do indeed frequent, “and specialty coffees throughout the week.” If I just cut that down to two meals and two coffees a week, Gemini explained, I’d save about $30 a week, or $1,500 a year.

Also: This powerful Gemini setting made my AI results way more personal and accurate.

It has been revealed that when I asked it to find recurring subscriptions, Gemini noted that I had several monthly charges. What I didn’t ask for, but it did, was to break down those subscriptions into an easy-to-see chart, complete with the name, price, category, the card it’s being charged to, and the timeframe it usually comes out. Gemini even offered a few free alternatives to services I was paying for.

Sources indicate that since Gemini also knows what cards are linked to your Wallet, it’s able to offer advice there, too. I’m planning a trip to Upcoming York City, so I asked which cards might be best to use for the hotels and airfare. Gemini was able to analyze my card benefits, considering things like rewards and built-in travel insurance, and make a recommendation.

Recent findings suggest that while Gemini Personal Intelligence can access your Gmail to learn more about your spending, it can’t access your bank account. The only information it knows about your finances and spending comes from receipts in your inbox or from your Wallet transaction history. I do use Wallet often (mostly when ordering through online apps), but it’s not quite my go-to option when I’m in a store. Given how useful this feature can be, though, I might just start using Wallet more to give it more information to work with.
