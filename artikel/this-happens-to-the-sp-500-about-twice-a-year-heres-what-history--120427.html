According to recent reports, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -2.80%) and Nasdaq-100 are going through their deepest corrections since April 2025. Right now, a lot of investors are wondering whether the worst is over or still ahead.

But the crucial thing worth remembering is that 5% to 10% pullbacks in stocks are entirely normal and common. Since 2009, declines of 5% or more have occurred roughly twice a year, according to research from Creative Planning.

The current correction is deeper than that, but history offers some encouragement for investors regarding what typically happens afterward.

It has been revealed that according to the Creative Planning study, the S&P 500 has experienced 34 declines of more than 5% between March 2009 and early November 2026, including the current one. Again, that's approximately one every six months.

Another study by Reuters found that 5% pullbacks are usually just a speed bump on the way to positive future returns.
