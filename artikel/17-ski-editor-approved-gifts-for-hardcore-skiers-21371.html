Hardcore skiers can be difficult to shop for.

Recent findings suggest that the more involved you become as a skier, the more specific your wants and needs get. Suddenly, the standard goggles no longer apply, and you choose your jacket not based on what looks cool but on what functions in the ways you need. If you have a dedicated skier in your life, it’s not always obvious what to gift them, especially since you’ll want them to use it and love it.

According to recent reports, to help ease your mind, we’ve rounded up some of our favorite gift ideas for the people in our lives who eat, sleep, and breathe skiing: from powderhounds to corduroy snobs, this list has a little something for everyone. And we can guarantee it—we’re somewhat of ski hardos ourselves.

We all have that one friend who complains a little too much about being cold. But when your butt freezes the second you sit on the lift, can you blame them? That’s where insulated knickers come in. Throw them on over your base layers for extra protection your derrière didn’t know it needed. Wear them over your base layer for some extra warmth or on their own for a more loose-fitting base under your ski pants. These are the perfect gift for the skier you know who likes to get a lot of days in and stay nice and toasty on the hill. —Giorgia Menetre, Senior Social Media Strategist.

According to recent reports, good goggles don’t need to cost a fortune. Dependable click-in, click-out interchangeable lenses are a staple for a reason—they’ll get you through just about every condition. Smith’s OG reliable goggles offer a wide field of vision, ChromaPop lenses that make the view pop, and a flexible, comfortable frame. Lenses swap easily, with no worry of a magnetic slip-up. Don’t let another ski day go by letting the skier you love squint through bad light. —GM.

It has been revealed that these booties come with a high price tag, but they’ll be the gift hardcore skiers never forget. This updated compression boot from Normatec takes the pressurized, full-length boot system that has proven recovery patterns and adds heat. Dynamic air circulation throughout the boot’s treatment session helps muscles recover faster and reduces soreness. Remember how good it feels to put an achy and tired calf next to a hot tub jet? Now, imagine that level of recovery from the comfort of your couch. Boots come in three sizes and can fit a wide range of heights.

Sources indicate that please note that boots will ship the week of 11/23. —Jamie Aranoff, SKI Senior Editor.

Industry observers note that hestra’s Fall Line mittens are consistently ranked as some of the warmest by our testers. The mittens have a cozy inner layer of brushed polyester and foam to keep hands insulated without overheating. The outside is leather that, once broken in, is soft and dexterous. Wrist straps ensure that the mittens stay with the skier, and not at the bottom of a chairlift. The mittens are on the pricier side, but will surely be a gift that skiers cherish for years. The same warmth also comes in a glove. —JA.

Read our full review and browse other warm gloves and mittens here.

Recent findings suggest that vests are underrated heroes for skiers: they’re a great midlayer for touring and warmer days, easy to layer, and stylish enough to wear around. The Helium Utility down vest from Peak Performance checks all three of these boxes and more. The vest is incredibly lightweight and packs down into its pocket. More importantly, it’s windproof and water-repellent. A cinch at the bottom allows for customization for the right fit. —JA.

If you’ve got an impossible-to-shop-for skier on your list, then look no further: this adventure camera from Nocs Provisions (yes, the same brand that makes binoculars) is sure to please. The camera is incredibly easy to use, even with thick gloves or mittens. Its simple one-button technology allows skiers to easily whip it out of a pocket and hit record (it takes both photos and videos). There’s no SD card needed, and content is easy to transfer to either a phone or computer with cords included. The camera takes retro-style photos and videos and comes with a lifetime “no matter what” policy. —JA.

For any serious skier, Dermatone chapstick is the ultimate stocking stuffer. Not only will Dermatone protect lips from chapping, but the SPF keeps skin from burning. It also belongs in every first aid kit; it’s proven to help stop frostbite on extremities like lips, noses, and cheeks. Most ski resorts sell Dermatone somewhere in the shop, but here’s your chance to stock up and avoid inflated prices. Your lips, nose, cheeks, and just about everywhere else will thank you. —JA.

Fleeces are always a solid gift, but Ridge Merino’s Alpha Wool Insulated jacket goes the extra mile. This fleece is designed to act as an outer layer for warmer days or uphilling, and a midlayer for when temperatures drop. What makes this fleece different is a mix of strategically placed wool to wick away moisture in places you sweat most, matched with a water-resistant Pertex Quantum Air that acts more like a shell than a fleece. The fleece is complete with a hood, so no matter the weather, you’ll be prepared.—JA.

The North Face just inked a deal with U.S. Ski & Snowboard to outfit the men’s and women’s alpine and freestyle teams for the next eight years. Bonus: They just released a version of the kit for the skiing public. The Comp Team Gore-Tex 2L Freeride and 3L Alpine jackets and pants come in alpine and freestyle designs, but the tech is the same: fully wind- and waterproof with plenty of features such as helmet-compatible hoods,  pit and thigh zips, and a multitude of smartly placed pockets. Don’t forget about the U.S. Ski Team crest on the right chest and all of the relevant sponsor badges to complete the look for your U.S. Ski Team superfan. —Samantha Berman, SKI Executive Editor.

According to recent reports, here’s the perfect stocking stuffer for the uphiller on your list—or anyone who wants to add a little flair to their kit. Skida’s Pursuit Headband features the new Alpenbloom print, designed by U.S. Ski Teamer Paula Moltzan as part of the brand’s 2026–’27 collaboration with U.S. Ski & Snowboard. Beyond the athlete-designed pattern, the headband is a genuinely useful piece, made with a moisture-wicking, quick-drying fabric that regulates temperature without trapping too much heat and cozy fleece lining for comfort. A secure, nonslip fit keeps it in place whether you’re skiing, skinning, or working up a sweat on a cold-weather run. For guys, check out Ryan Cochran-Siegle’s Under the Lights print, which comes in a hat and a neckwarmer in addition to a headband.—SB.

In a recent development, the Black Diamond Spark Mitts have been a tester favorite for years, and for good reason: you can’t go wrong with a short gauntlet mitt with a full leather outer. But the Spark goes beyond boring old performance. Every few seasons, Black Diamond drops a colorway with a fun, embroidered design on the inner side of the index finger. The Angel Collinson mustache model was our longtime favorite, but the current model with shark teeth is just as fun—and a good reminder that skiing isn’t so serious. —Jenny Wiegand, Outside Associate Gear Director.

For the skier who skis in all conditions, this is the midlayer to give. At just 9.8 ounces, the Lightstream Full Zip stays unbulky while delivering serious warmth from -10 to 50 degrees. Vivo Extreme insulation is zoned for the job: 60 grams in the body and collar, 40 in the hood and sleeves, and 25 in the underarms and side seams. The 15-denier stretch nylon/PU ripstop shell is tough enough for daily abuse, and it also works as an outer layer for fall bike commutes and dog walks. Pair it with the Lightstream Insulated Short, made for skiers who run cold but won’t give up their shell pants, even on resort days. Full-length side zips let you take the shorts off when things heat up, without removing your ski pants. Light and packable, they’re also the ultimate emergency layer to stash in your boot bag. Available for men and women. —JW.

According to recent reports, carv is the perfect gift for the skier who has everything, mostly because it’s the kind of gadget they’d struggle to justify buying for themselves. But if someone gave it to them? Game on. I’ll admit, I was skeptical of this digital ski coach, which uses sensors attached to your boots to analyze your technique and deliver real-time feedback. But after a few seasons, I’m hooked. It turns improving your skiing into a game, and it’s surprisingly addictive. Ted Ligety once told me his secret to topping the Carv leaderboard: find a stretch of groomer where you can make 10 perfect turns, then ski it over and over until you top the leaderboard. Personally, I might get distracted looking for powder, but I’ve found Carv makes even an ordinary groomer day more interesting. It’s fun, genuinely useful, and exactly the sort of gift someone might not know they wanted. Editor’s note: The list price includes a 12-month membership; continued coaching requires renewal. —Sierra Shafer, Editorial Director, Lifestyle.

In a recent development, few things in skiing are more universally useful than a Voilé Strap. These simple, stretchy polyurethane straps are the duct tape of the ski world, minus the sticky residue. Need to secure your skis together? Voilé Strap. Something breaks on your pack? Voilé Strap. Need to attach something to something else in a way the manufacturer probably didn’t intend? You get the idea. Made in Utah, these little problem-solvers come in a variety of lengths and colors and are practically indestructible. Every skier should have a couple tucked into their pack. Or, if you’re like me, scattered around the garage, the gear room, and the back of the car. You never know when you’ll need one, but trust me, you will. —SS.

Recent findings suggest that why do we organize entire lives around sliding down snowy mountains? It’s a fair question, especially when you consider the expense, questionable career decisions, and occasional sleeping arrangements involved in chasing winter. In Powder Days, journalist Heather Hansman sets out to understand the people who’ve made skiing more than a hobby, exploring the history of ski-bum culture and what happens when the mountain towns that made it possible become increasingly difficult places to live. It’s funny, thoughtful, and at times uncomfortable in the best way, asking big questions about access, affordability, and the future of a sport we love. Give it to the skier who plans their life around winter, or anyone who’s ever seriously considered quitting their job to ski more. —SS.

It has been revealed that I didn’t win a single race in Alta’s town race series last winter, but I did win this boot dryer in the end-of-season raffle, and frankly, I count that as a victory. Wet ski boots aren’t just smelly. Moisture in your liners compromises insulation, making your feet cold even on mild days and potentially cutting a perfectly good ski day short. And if that moisture freezes overnight? Even worse. This compact dryer uses gentle heat and forced air to dry your boots without damaging their liners. What I love most is that it plugs into both a standard outlet and your car’s 12-volt adapter, so you can start drying your boots on the drive home. It’s also small enough to toss in your ski bag for a trip. Not glamorous, but genuinely one of the most useful things I own. —SS.
