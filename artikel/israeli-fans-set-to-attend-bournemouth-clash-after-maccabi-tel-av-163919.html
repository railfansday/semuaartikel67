Supporters of Israeli side Hapoel Beer-Sheva are expected to be permitted to travel for their Europa League fixture against Bournemouth in January.

A previous decision to ban fans of fellow Israeli team Maccabi Tel Aviv from a Europa League game at Aston Villa last November caused controversy, with the move openly denounced by the then Prime Minister, Sir Keir Starmer.

That call prompted an investigation by the Independent Office for Police Conduct in August into former West Midlands Police chief constable Craig Guildford over allegations of gross misconduct.

Currently, Dorset Police are planning on the basis that Hapoel travelling fans will attend the 28 January match, though security assessments and intelligence reviews remain ongoing with two months remaining before kick-off.

Dorset Police’s Gold Commander for the game, Assistance Chief Constable Lindsay Dudfield, stated: "We are continuing to work closely with partners to ensure we can facilitate a safe and enjoyable match for all concerned.

In a recent development, "As with previous fixtures, we regularly review the latest information and intelligence to inform our policing operation and the resources required. All intelligence received will be carefully verified before informing any decisions we make around our plans.

"We are also in regular contact with interested parties in our communities to ensure we communicate clearly with them around what to expect and our policing plans for the fixture, as well as listening to any concerns they may have.".

The match between Villa and Maccabi last year required the deployment of over 700 police officers.

On Thursday, Hapoel’s encounter with Besiktas was interrupted following altercations involving police and supporters of the Turkish side. The fixture had been relocated to Georgia following recommendations from Turkish authorities, having originally been scheduled as a home game for Besiktas.

Industry observers note that reports indicated that Besiktas supporters displayed Palestinian flags alongside pro-Palestine banners before unrest broke out, forcing play to be stopped. Georgia’s interior ministry stated that 16 individuals were arrested and three officers suffered injuries.

The Turkish football federation described the actions of Georgian police towards Besiktas supporters as "unacceptable".

Denouncing the unrest and violence during the fixture, European football's governing body Uefa released a statement saying: "Violence, aggression, hatred and any form of provocative, discriminatory or extremist behaviour have no place in football.

Industry observers note that "Uefa competitions are founded on the principles of respect, fair play, integrity and safety. The events witnessed at the above-mentioned matches run counter to these fundamental values and to Uefa’s objective, enshrined in its Statutes, of ensuring that football matches are played in a spirit of peace, mutual respect and sporting fairness.".

Uefa confirmed that an ethics and disciplinary inspector has been designated to investigate the incident.
