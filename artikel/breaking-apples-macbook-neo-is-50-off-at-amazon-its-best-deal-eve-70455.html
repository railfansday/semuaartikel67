SAVE $50: As of Nov. 19, the Apple MacBook Neo is finally $50 off at Amazon and Best Buy — its first real discount for non-students. Prices start at $649 (normally $699) for a model with 256GB of storage and no Touch ID.

It's an early holiday miracle: The MacBook Neo just got its first real discount. We know fifty bucks may not seem like a huge deal, but we've been waiting for this laptop to go on sale since March, and we were starting to think it might never happen, especially after Apple increased the price in June. (Previously, Amazon offered a measly $9.01 discount.).

According to recent reports, we were on hand when Apple launched the MacBook Neo in Upcoming York City, and we predicted it was going to become a best-seller. The budget laptop put the entire Windows laptop world on notice, bringing the premium features of the MacBook line into the budget category for the first time. And, bonus, it comes in fun colors.

Both variants of Apple's stellar budget laptop are currently $50 off at Amazon and Best Buy in the retailers' Black Friday sales. The base model with 256GB of storage is now $649, while the upgraded Neo with 512GB of storage and Touch ID has fallen to $749. Target Circle members, take note — the Neo is also on sale at Target. Though the Bullseye brand is charging a whopping 99 cents more, FYI.

This price drop couldn't come at a better time. Mashable recently named the Neo a Gift of the Year winner. If a teen or younger student you know needs a fresh laptop, we think they'll be delighted to unwrap this one. It's just as well-made as other MacBooks, has an all-day battery life (it lasted exactly 14 hours and 50 minutes in our battery rundown test), comes in fun colors, and runs basic tasks faster than most premium Windows laptops we've tried.

As we said when it originally launched, the "MacBook Neo is a shot across the bow of the Windows laptop market." To quote our tech editor, "This colorful budget laptop brings the MacBook's premium design elements and user experience to a whole fresh customer base.".

In terms of performance, the A18 Pro chip held up surprisingly well in our stress testing. "I was able to edit a simple, cheesy video in Final Cut Pro using the MacBook Neo without struggling against the spinning wheel of death. Even when I layered video clips on top of each other and added a series of totally unnecessary transitions, the Neo handled the tasks just fine," our tech editor wrote.
