According to recent reports, it’s been a significant year for shoes. In addition to the supreme reign of the It shoe, designers have fully entered a new era of experimentalism when it comes to footwear. And truly, nothing feels off-limits. Earlier this year, Schiaparelli took the cake with faux-taxidermy kitten heels, replete with shockingly lifelike snarling cat heads, eyes, whiskers, and tongues included. As did Prada, with Victorian-style boots covered in a collage of feathers and decadent, chandelier-studded low heels straight out of a Marie Antoinette fever dream. Fashion insiders and the masses got swept up in the hunt for Chanel’s ponyhair ballet flats, in a moment that felt like a new level of footwear obsession. Now, we have the runway-approved gardening shoe. Dior, TWP, and Public School have all shown their own takes on the look, each reinterpreting a classic garden clog.

Consider it the fashion equivalent of touching grass in the digital age. Gardening shoes have a clear connection to nature that is quite literally (and aesthetically) grounding. With their neutral beige tones and down-to-earth materials, they read as the opposite of the feathers, crystals, and animal-themed ponyhair of It shoes of the last few seasons. The garden-ready shoe combines the unapologetic high-fashion ugly-chic notion with the idea of practicality.

Sources indicate that at Miu Miu fall/winter 2026, the runway was literally a mossy, grassy display ripe for stomping around in lug soles, and that’s what the models did, in bubble-soled slip-ons and chunky lace-up boots that almost looked like something you’d go hiking in, paired with technical nylon jackets and funky fedoras. Miu Miu called it “Clothes that are a method of self-preservation, a means of valuing that inside,” in the show notes. “They emblematize the individual, and in turn the identity of Miu Miu itself.”.

There’s also undoubtedly a little bit of irony to this style. Most of the gardening shoes on the runway serve as a high-contrast answer to flashy, anything-but-toned-down runway garments. Take Loewe’s slip-on shoes with a rubberized toe that also looked ready for the task of planting biennials and perennials. They were styled with lush shearlings and near-neon orange latex dresses, the shoes put the classic jolie laide terminology to work. Shoes, after all, have for decades served as storytelling anchors within fashion collections. Elsewhere at Dior’s spring/summer 2027 show in Paris, brown suede shoes were capped off with rubberized toes and practical soles, worn with silk petal jackets and textural, couture-like miniskirts.

In a recent development, simone Rocha just released its final collaboration with Crocs, including a Quick Trail Racer style that looks like something a real gardener would wear, only with Simone Rocha’s saccharine crystals and ruffles. Retailers have seen the look come and go: “The garden-like lug sole trend ebbs and flows in the background of footwear,” Moda Operandi’s Ryan Kleman, VP of buying, accessories, tells ELLE. “Ultimately, the appeal mostly stems from the idea of putting outfits together ‘with the wrong shoe.’ The garden shoe is the wrong shoe, in many ways. Otherwise, the shoe’s practicality and function underscore how easily customers can convert to them.”.

The It shoe is officially grounded.
