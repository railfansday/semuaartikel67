Anthropic raises $93.5 billion total as underwriters scoop up ‘greenshoe’ overallotment

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anthropic underwriters have officially exercised their overallotment of shares in the historic initial public offering, bringing the total raised to $93.5 billion, according to a release Friday.

The artificial intelligence organization raised $81.3 billion on Wednesday and netted an additional $12.2 billion through the overallotment, surpassing SpaceX, which held a record IPO in June.

Recent findings suggest that anthropic said it sold an aggregate of 1,127,000,000 shares of its Class A common stock at $83 per share in the offering. It sold 980 million shares on Wednesday and its underwriters had the option to purchase up to an additional 147 million shares by Friday as part of the overallotment, which is commonly referred to as the "greenshoe.".

Sources indicate that shares of the Claude maker opened trading at $111.50 on the Nasdaq on Thursday, but slid throughout the day and closed at $97.17. The stock sank 12% in premarket trading on Friday during a broad tech sell-off.

According to recent reports, in addition to the sale of its Class A common stock, Anthropic closed a concurrent sale of its Class N common stock after a group of tech companies, including Advanced Micro Devices, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and others, agreed to participate in a $26 billion private placement at the IPO price. Anthropic remarked Friday that aggregate gross proceeds from its IPO, including the private placement, totaled $119.5 billion, before deducting offering expenses.

Anthropic dodged an unforgiving series of obstacles on its road to the Nasdaq on Thursday, as investors brooded over Jensen Huang's shocking ouster at Nvidia, voters' stinging rebuke of the tech during the midterms and whether rising interest rates would stymie industry plans for a multitrillion-dollar AI infrastructure buildout.

CEO Dario Amodei co-founded the tech giant in 2021 after he and a handful of other researchers, including his sister Daniela, quit their jobs at OpenAI, now Anthropic's chief rival. Anthropic is best known for developing a family of AI models called Claude, which have captivated the corporate world and fueled the company's historic rise.

The siblings celebrated the IPO as a "milestone" in an interview with CNBC on Thursday, but mentioned there's more work ahead for Anthropic.

"For us, it's like one stop on the journey," Daniela Amodei noted. "We think about this as, 'Wow, this is an incredible moment for Anthropic for our mission, but we're going to wake up tomorrow and we're going to be the same firm.'".
