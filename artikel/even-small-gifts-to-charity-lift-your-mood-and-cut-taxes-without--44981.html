Even Small Gifts to Charity Lift Your Mood – and Cut Taxes Without Itemizing

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Giving to charity pays you back. Research finds spending on others makes people happier than spending on themselves, and starting in 2026, you can deduct some cash gifts even if you take the standard deduction. Give a set amount to a charity whose work you can see, and keep every receipt.

A tight budget is no reason to put off giving, because even a small gift can lift your mood.

Recent findings suggest that in a 2008 study published in Science, Elizabeth Dunn, Lara Aknin, and Michael Norton gave people $5 or $20 to spend by 5 p.m. Those told to buy someone else a gift or give the money to charity were happier that evening than those told to spend it on themselves. The authors concluded that as little as $5 may be enough for a real boost in happiness that day.

It helps to see what your money does. In a 2013 follow-up study by Aknin, Dunn, Norton, and two colleagues, giving more made people happier only when the charity explained how the money would help someone, such as $10 buying a child a bed net.

The tax payoff is new. Starting in 2026, you can deduct up to $1,000 of cash gifts to eligible charities, or $2,000 on a joint return, without itemizing (listing your deductions one by one). Say a married couple who file jointly, don’t itemize, and are in the 12% tax bracket give $2,000 to their local food bank. They can deduct the whole gift, which cuts their federal income tax by $240.

Industry observers note that if you do itemize, you can still deduct gifts, but only the amount above 0.50% of your adjusted gross income, your total income minus certain adjustments. Either way, only gifts to qualified charities count, and the IRS’s Tax Exempt Organization Search shows whether a charity can receive tax-deductible gifts. Money given straight to a friend for medical bills can lift your mood too, but it isn’t deductible.

For every gift, keep a bank record or a written receipt from the charity showing its name, the date, and the amount. A single gift of $250 or more also needs a written acknowledgment from the charity that notes whether you got anything in return.

Pick a charity whose work you can see, set up a monthly gift from your checking account, and keep the bank statements that show each payment.
