Hydrangeas really have their moment during the summer months, and then spend the winter looking dead, brown, and crispy while we politely look away. Shea McGee, however, has made a point of doing the opposite, and to beautiful effect!

According to recent reports, that's right, folks; not only did the Netflix Dream Home Makeover star give her hydrangeas some attention in winter, but she also added them to the star of the show during the holidays: her Christmas tree! And, much like Sarah Jessica Parker's floral Christmas tree, it's one of those oh-so-simple but oh-so-effective ideas that we can't believe we didn't think of first.

Recent findings suggest that as you may or may not know, Shea grows Limelight hydrangeas at her Salt Lake City home, telling fans that the blooms “bring me so much joy in the summer”. Is it any wonder, then, that when the interior designer shared a video of her Christmas decor to Instagram, the blooms were front and center?

As you can see above, Shea McGee has decorated her tree with warm brown, tan, and cream ornaments, dried orange slices, clip-on LED candlesticks, and dried hydrangeas from her own garden (although you can buy ready-to-go cone hydrangeas from McGee & Co if you prefer an easier life).

According to recent reports, it's a beyond brilliant idea, as it means the blooms get a second act, which is a better return than most shrubs give you. If you want a similar look, just read on for some tips from the experts.

Sources indicate that cameron of DreamHowYouLive favors Limelight (the same as Shea McGee grows) and Annabelle for a hydrangea arrangement. “Their petals dry stiff and papery, so they keep their shape, where softer mopheads crumble.”.

According to recent reports, flower farmer-florist Joey McFarland of Flowers by Dr. Jo agrees that panicle types like Limelight are best for long stems. He warns that bigleaf hydrangeas set buds in fall, so cutting too deeply means “cutting off your blooms for next year.” Shopping for plants? Browse our list of panicle hydrangea varieties.

Timing is highly notable as well, especially when it comes to hydrangeas. Morgan Brooks of Plant Style Home waits until the heads “turn papery and antique in early fall.” Cut too soon and, as Cameron warns, “they wilt instead of drying.” (Which means you need to show patience, grasshopper).

When it comes to drying hydrangeas the experts all have a different opinion, which tells you how difficult it can actually be. Floral preservation artist Nadi Melam of Florals Memories says, “I always dry hydrangeas in a vase of water,” about an inch, for a “lovely muted antique color.”.

Cameron hangs them upside down in a dark, dry spot for two or three weeks, since vase-dried heads “tend to droop.” Our guide to drying hydrangea flowers covers both.

In a recent development, the best rule to follow if you want the Shea McGee effect? Water-drying holds shape and color well, while air-drying is quicker but leaves blooms more fragile.

There’s one place where most people go wrong when drying hydrangeas for Shea McGee-style decor. As Cameron tells me me he cuts the head town to 3-inch stems, wires each branch and tucks the whole bloom into a gap. His personal rule: “Three small clusters spread around the tree look intentional. One substantial bunch at the top looks like a mistake.”.

Arslan Ali of DecoroDea advises, “Treat them like picks, not ornaments,” and says to place them last, “after the ornaments and lights are already on,” so you fill the gaps that are actually still dark. Brooks wires clusters low near the trunk first, then moves smaller heads toward the tips. In essence, you can take a bit from every expert tip.

For McGee’s look, add dried orange slices, cream and tan ornaments, and warm white lights. Bright white, Cameron notes, makes “the whole thing look cold.”.

Dried petals shed, which is why Melam is so blunt with their advice: “You lose petals every time you touch a flower.” Cameron’s fix is a light hairspray mist that “keeps the shedding down.” And, as for those annoying relatives, who “just want to adjust one thing,” tell them that your Shea McGee-inspired Christmas tree is now a museum exhibit, so hands off".

According to recent reports, if you don't have real hydrangeas in your yard, faux hydrangeas as a no-mess alternative. These cream-white stems give the same soft look without the shedding.

Hydrangeas are also toxic if chewed, so keep clusters out of reach of curious pets.

Those candles on Shea McGee’s Christmas tree? Make them LED.

Melam indicates, “Open flame is a no-no since the dry material will ignite right away.” As for saving them for next year, the experts are once again split.

Want a warm glow like MsGee's but without the fire risk? These warm white, dimmable clip-on LED candles come with a remote, so you can keep real flames far from your dried hydrangeas.

Melam stores heads flat in a box with tissue between layers, and Cameron has reused the same heads two years running. However, McFarland indicates dried blooms “won’t do particularly well in storage” and suggests composting them and cutting fresh ones.

Either way, it’s a good excuse to plant more hydrangeas and see what tip works best for you.
