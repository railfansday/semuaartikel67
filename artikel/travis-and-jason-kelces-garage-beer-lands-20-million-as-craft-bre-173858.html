Travis and Jason Kelce’s Garage Beer lands $20 million as craft brewers pull back

Sources indicate that travis Kelce’s beer brand just secured a huge financial deal to help promote its popular brew. On Tuesday, Travis and his brother Jason Kelce’s Garage Beer brand secured a $20 million revolving credit from Tiger Finance, according to a release. The funding comes at a time when the independent beer industry has been facing many challenges. The rise in operation costs, slowing business openings, and declines in sales have forced many breweries to close down or refinance. But this celebrity-backed brand is only seeing success. Andy Cerussi, the senior managing director of Tiger Finance, remarked in the release that the manufacturer is thrilled to have been chosen as the preferred financial partner, saying that the company is “impressed with [the] impact the Kelce brothers have had on positively influencing brand awareness.” Brian Amicon, president of Garage Beer, told Inc. that as the company continues to grow, this financing allows it greater flexibility. “From strengthening our distribution to investing in the brand and creating more opportunities to connect with consumers, it puts us in a strong position to keep building what we’ve started,” Amicon said. “We’re proud of the progress we’ve made and excited to keep pushing forward.” A $20 million bet on Garage Beer’s brand strength The debt deal allows Garage Beer to expand its product line and enter new markets. At $20 million, the financing sits on the smaller side of the funding spectrum—in the bottom quarter of deals tracked, according to Deal Room. But for a consumer company like a beer line, debt against brand strength gives growth capital without diluting equity. This revolving line will give Garage Beer flexible access to capital tied to its brand value. The Columbus, Ohio-based brand originally released as a local craft light lager by Braxton Brewing Co., but was spun off into an independent company in 2023 by its CEO, Andy Sauer. According to VinePair, the Kelce brothers joined Garage Beer in the summer of 2024 by acquiring a majority stake and becoming co-owners and operators. This move rapidly boosted the brand’s national visibility. In late 2025, Durational Capital Management confirmed its strategic investment partnership with the company. A cofounder of Durational Capital said in the release that the company appreciates Tiger’s creativity and partnership “to structure a credit facility that leverages the strength of Garage Beer’s brand to support the company’s growth objectives.” Garage Beer is expanding while competitors retrench Neil Reid, a professor at the University of Toledo who studies the craft beer industry, told Inc. that the brand’s trajectory stands out. “Garage Beer’s aggressive expansion is highly unusual in today’s beer market,” Reid said. He noted that the company has expanded distribution to all 50 states and added more than 100,000 barrels of volume in 2025, reportedly the largest increase among U.S. craft brewers. “Rather than retrenching, Garage Beer is using the downturn to gain market share from competitors,” Reid told Inc. He added that brands with the capacity for growth through distribution often tend to be a simple product and have broad appeal, sales momentum, enough capital to fund expansion, name recognition, and scalable operations. Brands like small craft breweries that rely on taprooms and direct sales struggle with reaching that same momentum. Even if they have strong local followings, having fewer financial resources and less distributor support can be a challenge. “By offering a simple, familiar product rather than a niche craft beer, the brand can attract a larger audience, generate stronger retail turnover, and expand more easily across geographic markets,” Reid said. But he cautioned against treating Garage Beer as a model for the rest of the industry, noting, “The brand benefits from factors that most breweries lack, including celebrity backing, substantial capital, extensive marketing reach, and a highly scalable business model. Differentiation remains crucial, but differentiation alone is rarely sufficient.” Celebrity attention still has to translate into repeat sales Claudia Kubowicz Malhotra, marketing professor at the University of North Carolina’s Kenan-Flagler Business School, told Inc. that celebrity attention is only the first step. “To continue to build the brand, awareness needs to translate to interest, desire, and action,” Kubowicz Malhotra said. “Consumers need to look and ask for the beer by name. Ultimately, this needs to translate to sales.” As Garage Beer adds new products, she said, it can grow successfully “if new product offerings reinforce the brand positioning—a light beer that tastes like beer.” To judge whether the brand has staying power, Kubowicz Malhotra told Inc. she would look beyond sales: “I would also look for customer retention and repurchase behavior as well as word of mouth.” Tiger Finance and Durational Capital Management did not immediately respond to Inc.’s request for comment. —Lucia Auerbach This article originally appeared on Fast Company’s sister website, Inc.com.
