Remodel for Yourself – Small Projects Recoup the Most When You Sell

🌐 Source: Moneycrashers • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (488 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Most home improvements add less to your sale price than they cost, and the bigger the project, the less of its cost you get back.
  • Value report compares the average cost of 28 common remodeling projects with what each adds to a home’s sale price.
  • Recent findings suggest that small, visible projects lead the list.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Most home improvements add less to your sale price than they cost, and the bigger the project, the less of its cost you get back. Remodel for your own use, and if you’ll sell within a few years, pick small, visible projects like a new garage door or entry door, which recoup the most.

Zonda’s 2025 Cost vs. Value report compares the average cost of 28 common remodeling projects with what each adds to a home’s sale price. By its national averages, only five added more than they cost, and each of those cost less than $30,000.

Recent findings suggest that small, visible projects lead the list. A latest garage door cost an average $4,672 and added an estimated $12,507, and a steel entry door averaged $2,435 and added $5,270. Manufactured stone veneer on the lower front of the house and fiber-cement siding also paid for themselves. So did a midrange minor kitchen remodel, which keeps the cabinet boxes but replaces the doors, counters, and appliances.

Industry observers note that massive projects sit near the bottom. A midrange primary suite addition averaged $170,517 and recouped 32% of its cost, an upscale major kitchen remodel 36%, and an upscale suite addition 18%. A buyer pays for what most buyers want, and a big remodel is built to one owner’s taste. So remodel for the years you’ll live in the house, and choose projects for resale only when a sale is near.

Planning to sell within three years? By those averages, a new garage door and steel entry door would cost about $7,100 together and add about $17,800, so you’d come out about $10,700 ahead. The midrange suite addition would add about $55,100 for its $170,517 and leave you about $115,400 behind.

Sources indicate that before any upgrade, keep the roof, furnace, plumbing, and wiring sound and up to code. A buyer’s inspection will turn up a failing one, and you’ll pay to fix it or take a lower offer. Keep a kitchen or bath in line with the houses around you, too. A midrange bath remodel recouped 80% of its $26,138 average cost, and an upscale one 42% of $81,612.

Favor energy upgrades that pay for themselves in lower bills, since solar panels recouped just 30% of their $55,937 average cost at sale.

Buyers judge a remodel by its condition, not its cost, so a kitchen redone 10 years before you list is priced as a 10-year-old kitchen.
