Brit List 2026: Crime Series & Coming-Of-Age Movie Lead List Of Best Unproduced Screenplays

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The 2026 Brit List, the selection of promising and so far unproduced British film and TV screenplays, is led jointly by a crime series and a coming-of-age movie. Scroll down for list in full.

Compiled from recommendations by the British industry, 13 writers made it onto the list this year with a minimum of ten recommendations required.

Leading the pack with 25 recommendations each were feature Dig For Victory by Gemma Addy (repped by The Artists Partnership), and series Petroc by Archie Thomson (repped by Curtis Brown). According to recent reports, the former, which doesn’t right now have a producer aboard, is set in rural Wiltshire, and follows “an isolated girl in the closing months of World War II. When a violent incident interrupts a new friendship with an older woman, she uncovers a series of secrets that will shape a deadly future.” Watch on Deadline.

Crime series Petroc has About Premium Content and Bosena aboard as producers. The synopsis reads: “When a charismatic and volatile Cornish outlaw returns home after twelve years in prison, he sets about reclaiming what he believes has been taken from his family, transforming the Penrose’s from small-time crooks into a rising criminal power.” The series comes at a time when UK-created crime sagas like MobLand and This City are scoring well for broadcasters.

Sources indicate that also scoring well on this year’s list was Hinterland Pictures and Fable Pictures thriller Do No Harm by Polly Findlay (Casarotto Ramsay). The film follows “Anna, a young doctor, who accepts a voluntary night shift away from her family on Christmas Eve. When her mentor, Dr. Petersen, is called away, Anna is left in charge of an overstretched maternity ward. With the pressure mounting, she has to make a series of urgent critical decisions way beyond her experience. Just as things seem to be under control, Anna realises that she’s made a tiny and very human error that may end up costing a patient her life.”.

Previous Brit List alumni include Moira Buffini, Aschlin Ditta, Emerald Fennell, James Graham, George Kay, Kelly Marcel, Abi Morgan, Ava Pickett, Jack Thorne and Peter Straughan.

According to recent reports, projects are compiled from recommendations by British production companies, talent agencies, sales companies, financiers, distributors and broadcasters. Previous projects include The King’s Speech, Paddington, Lion, The Favourite and Emmy winner Responsible Child.

The Brit List also offers selected writers a place on its six-month development programme of weekly seminars with leading industry professionals – agents, producers, writers and show runners – alongside specialists in personal finance, mental health, pitching technique and personal branding.

Recent findings suggest that founder and director of The Brit List, Alexandra Arlango, said: “In The Brit List’s 19th year, we are delighted to announce Disney+ as our headline sponsor. In 2026, The Brit List and Disney+ will go further than ever before to support the great British writing talent of the future. We look forward to introducing this year’s writers to the team at Disney+ and to our returning partners, Audible and the Film Distributors Association.”.

Industry observers note that angela Jain, Head of Content, Disney+ EMEA, added: “We’re excited to see the names on this year’s list, as we’re committed to investing in British screenwriters and empowering the future storytellers in the industry. It’s vital to drive the local creative economy, which is why initiatives such as The Brit List are notable; we’re proud to be a part of it.”.

Dig For Victory by Gemma Addy (The Artists Partnership).

Form: Feature Film Genre: Coming-of-Age Drama.

Recent findings suggest that summary: In rural Wiltshire, an isolated girl comes of age in the closing months of World War II. But when a violent incident interrupts a newly introduced friendship with an older woman, she uncovers a series of secrets that will shape a deadly future.

Petroc by Archie Thomson (Curtis Brown).

Producers: About Premium Content / Bosena.

Industry observers note that summary: When a charismatic and volatile Cornish outlaw returns home after twelve years in prison, he sets about reclaiming what he believes has been taken from his family, transforming the Penrose’s from small-time crooks into a rising criminal power. Set against the wild Cornish coastline, PETROC is a propulsive family crime saga about loyalty, belonging and identity, and the devastating consequences that follow when the past refuses to stay buried.

Do No Harm by Polly Findlay (Casarotto Ramsay).

Producers: Hinterland Pictures / Fable Pictures.

Form: Feature Film Genre: Thriller.

Summary: Anna, a young doctor, accepts a voluntary night shift away from her family on Christmas Eve. When her mentor, Dr. Petersen, is called away, Anna is left in charge of an overstretched maternity ward. With the pressure mounting, she has to make a series of urgent critical decisions way beyond her experience. Just as things seem to be under control, Anna realises that she’s made a tiny and very human error that may end up costing a patient her life.

Mrs. X by Rían Smith (Independent Talent Group).

Form: TV Genre: Psychological Thriller.

In a recent development, summary: When details of her secret affair show up in a student’s homework, an English teacher must do battle with a mysterious blackmailer in this cat-and-mouse game that soon escalates to murder. MRS. X is a twisty-turny thriller, where our lead characters – a tense-but-warm sister pairing – are always racing against the clock, trying to get one step ahead (while always being ten steps behind). The show interrogates whether we have truly thrown off the shackles of conservative thinking, or if the societal need to control others – especially women – still bubbles under the surface ….

The Trolley Problem by Polly Cavendish (Independent Talent Group).

Form: Feature Film Genre: Psychological Thriller.

Summary: Young married couple Nick and Georgie are involved in a horrific accident in which a stranger dies. Years later, the decisions they made during those fateful moments come back to haunt them.

15 Summers by Courttia Newland (Dench Arnold).

Form: Feature Film Genre: Drama.

Summary: Dimes is a successful rapper whose pursuit of fame estranges him from his teenage son, Tristian. When Tristian joins Dimes on tour, father and son are forced into close proximity, exposing resentment, disappointment and misunderstanding. As Tristian witnesses the contradictions of his father’s world – glamour, violence and chaos – Dimes sees the young man his son is. But when a night out turns violent and Tristian is harmed, Dimes finally confronts the life he has chosen. Over one transformative summer, both must decide whether it’s too late for them – and whether Dimes can put his son before everything.

The Royal No One by Levi Scott (Curtis Brown).

Sources indicate that summary: A star finance graduate with severe social anxiety discovers that secretly selling cocaine at the nightclub where he works as a busboy boosts his confidence far more than his medication. Unbeknownst to him, the club is run by gangsters who soon coerce him into becoming their in-house dealer.

The Lambeth Worm by Bec Boey (The Agency).

Form: Feature Film Genre: Horror.

Sources indicate that summary: Inspired by a traditional folklore tale from the writer’s native Northeast England, The Lambeth Worm tells the story of two young BESEA women who find a small creature in their tower block apartment. After washing it down the sink, the creature takes up residence in the building’s pipes. As fear spreads though the apartment block the other inhabitants become increasingly hostile and toxic, and a culture of terror and blame begins to take hold.

Baboons by Alfie Flewitt (Independent Talent Group).

Recent findings suggest that summary: The funeral of his younger brother Ernie brings Ned back to his East Midlands family home. As he and his parents grapple with their grief, they go from being a broken family to a healed one, finding love, pain, and a stray baboon along the way ….

Hello Stranger by Rory Platt (Curtis Brown).

Form: TV Genre: Comedy Drama/Thriller.

Summary: When an outlaw on the run gets stranded in a sleepy Welsh village, the locals fall prey to her charms, lies … and crimes. A female-led, Western-flavoured crime thriller: Far From the Madding Crowd meets The Good, The Bad & The Ugly.

Grey Matter by Theo Toksvig-Stewart (Knight Hall).

Form: Feature Film Genre: Psychological Near-Fi Thriller.

Summary: In near-future London, humanoid care robot Blanche, built to nurse and programmed to forget, is taught to feel by her patient’s son. But the romance that awakens her soon exposes her to the dark side of devotion.

Pegged by Rachel Tookey (The Artists Partnership).

Summary: A brocaster hiding his submissive streak. A feminist craving control over others while losing it in her own life. After clashing in a bar, they unwittingly match on Tinder under anonymous kink profiles. Their explosive chemistry – and conflicting ideologies – drag them into the weirdest, most revealing situationship of their lives.

Overturn by Joan Iyiola (Independent Talent Group).

Producers: Joi Productions / Miramax.

Summary: Leading prosecutor Kemi Odutola is on the brink of becoming the youngest ever judge when a high-profile murder case threatens to unravel everything she has built.
