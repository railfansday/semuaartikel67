Ripple's RLUSD stablecoin led the daily ranking of stablecoin inflows into decentralized finance (DeFi), according to Token Terminal data that RWA Foundation shared on X. The DeFi total value locked (TVL) of Ripple's stablecoin grew by $22.3 million over 24 hours, and Tether added $21.6 million. RWA Foundation titled its chart "Ripple led stablecoin DeFi growth.".

In a recent development, paxos, Open Standard and Societe Generale followed with $7.8 million, $4 million and $1.2 million, respectively. World Liberty, United Stables, MetaMask, Tori and Liquity each added less than $700,000 over the same period.

Recent findings suggest that about $2.8 billion worth of RLUSD is in circulation, compared with about $180 billion worth of Tether's USDT. Tether's stablecoin is still more than 60 times bigger than RLUSD.

According to recent reports, ripple launched RLUSD in December 2024. In mid-September 2026, RLUSD supply passed $2.44 billion, and about $1.38 billion of it was on Ethereum. About $1.05 billion was on the XRP Ledger, Ripple's home network. On Ethereum, the stablecoin is used in DeFi pools on Aave V3, Curve and Morpho.

Industry observers note that RLUSD is issued by Standard Custody & Trust under the supervision of the New York State Department of Financial Services (NYDFS), and its reserves are held at custodian bank BNY. In the fall, RLUSD received a Moody's rating and was listed on Coinbase and Robinhood, and Ripple received final approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) to launch Ripple National Trust Bank.

Ripple has also integrated RLUSD with payment providers Arf, Convera and Cross River Bank, and the stablecoin is now used in dollar settlements between companies.

Industry observers note that in a CNBC interview on Nov. 19, Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse called blockchain-based transactions "a wave of emerging technology." "I think 10 years from now you'll see blockchain implemented across our financial infrastructure," he noted.
