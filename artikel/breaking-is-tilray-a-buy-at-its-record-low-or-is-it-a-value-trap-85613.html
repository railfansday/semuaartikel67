The cannabis industry has been a massive disappointment in recent years, and even most industry leaders haven't escaped the bloodbath. Take, for instance, Tilray Brands (TLRY -8.70%), one of the largest players in the Canadian cannabis market. Tilray has significantly underperformed the broader market over the past half-decade, losing more than 90% of its value. The company is now near record lows. Is Tilray stock a buy at current levels, or would investors be catching a falling knife?

Tilray Brands faces a difficult regulatory landscape in the cannabis market. Recreational use of the substance remains illegal at the federal level in the U.S. It is legal in Canada, where the manufacturer is headquartered, but that market has its own challenges.

It has been revealed that obtaining the proper licenses in Canada is a complex and time-consuming process. The market north of the border is also characterized by stiff competition and persistent supply gluts. Although Tilray is an industry leader, these problems have harmed its financial results. The company has diversified its business in response.

In a recent development, it now operates four segments: cannabis, beverage, wellness (which offers a variety of products, including hemp-based foods), and distribution (which focuses on selling pharmaceutical products in certain international markets). But even a more diversified pool of operations has done little to improve Tilray's financial results.

In the first quarter of its fiscal year 2027, which ended on Aug. 31, Tilray's revenue increased by 23% year over year to a record $257 million. That looks impressive at first glance, but Tilray's top line did not grow that much organically. Earlier this year, it acquired certain assets associated with BrewDog, a craft brewery based in the United Kingdom.

This acquisition added $55.9 million in revenue to the organization's total for the period. If we subtract that from Tilray's total revenue, its top line comes in at $201.2 million for the period, down about 4% year over year.

This includes cannabis revenue of $56.1 million, which dropped 13% year over year. Wellness revenue stayed roughly flat at $15.3 million, while distribution revenue was $84.3 million, 14% higher than the year-ago period. Beverage revenue minus the BrewDog acquisition declined 18% year over year to $45.6 million.

So of Tilray's four business segments, only one posted strong year-over-year organic sales growth. Meanwhile, the organization's operating loss was $24.1 million, compared with $2.1 million in operating income in the year-ago period. It also turned in a net loss per share of $0.32, after breaking even in the comparable period of the previous fiscal year.

It has been revealed that tilray's poor financial results aren't the only problem. The company also faces a highly uncertain future, even given recent regulatory progress in the U.S. The federal government reclassified certain medical cannabis products and has discussed broader reclassification, which could encompass recreational cannabis.

But the substance remains illegal at the federal level. That means it still cannot be transported across state lines, among other restrictions. So, Tilray's opportunities in the U.S. remain severely limited. Will that change anytime soon? It's hard to say. Nobody knows if or when the U.S. will legalize recreational use of cannabis at the federal level.

Recent findings suggest that even if it does, Tilray might face problems similar to those it faced in Canada. What about other countries? Could Tilray bounce back by tapping into international markets? Unfortunately, that also seems unlikely. For instance, Germany legalized recreational cannabis in 2024, but this did very little to boost Tilray's results, despite the firm's presence in the country.

Germany allowed people to grow cannabis at home (within certain limits) or join non-commercial cannabis clubs. The law left little room for commercial companies like Tilray. So, the pot grower faces a stringent regulatory environment practically wherever it goes, and that might not change anytime soon. Tilray is unlikely to bounce back.

Investing in the stock, even at its record low, isn't worth the trouble.
