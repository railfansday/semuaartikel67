US women’s head coach won Olympics in 2024.

Hayes enjoyed trophy-laden tenure at Chelsea.

The US women’s national team head coach, Emma Hayes, remarked she was “confident in the group that is being put together” to select her next counterpart for the men’s team – a group that includes her.

Speaking after announcing her team’s squad for the ­Concacaf championship and World Cup qualifier, Hayes shed light for the first time on her role in searching for Mauricio Pochettino’s replacement after the Argentinian decamped to Manchester United. US Soccer had previously said Hayes – alongside the federation CEO, JT Batson, the chief operating officer, Dan Helfrich, and Oguchi Onyewu, the vice-president of sporting – would assist with the search.

“Listen, I’m very embedded into the federation, and I work really closely with JT, in particular, and even more so with Dan as we’re growing to develop the American soccer movement,” Hayes said. “And you know, JT asked if I would be involved as a technical voice to be involved with the process. And I feel confident in the group of people that have been put together to create a thorough process to select the next men’s national team coach – my role is merely to support that through a technical lens, knowing I do this job … albeit with the women’s team, and that I’ve coached for a number of years, and can offer some of that insight into the application of what to look for and why.”.

Hayes résumé spans 25 years and is distinguished, having coached at the very highest level for club and country alike. The Englishwoman, who took over the US team in 2024, moved from being the assistant coach at Arsenal to taking charge of the Chicago Red Stars in Women’s Professional Soccer in 2008, and later moved to Chelsea, winning the Women’s Super League seven times. Since accepting the US job, she has guided the women to an Olympic gold medal and taken on an expansive role at the federation as it looks to overhaul and grow the women’s game.

“I think it will be a very thorough process,” Hayes mentioned. “I think that’s the most notable part of it. I feel very embedded in what we’re doing with the federation, so it doesn’t feel unusual to me [to help with the search]. So, like I’ve said many times, let’s build off the good work that Mauricio has done. I think that’s the important part, and let’s get it right.”.

Hayes offered a heartfelt goodbye to Pochettino on the TNT broadcast of the his final match with the USMNT on Tuesday night, a 2-0 victory over Haiti in their Concacaf Nations League quarter-final. “I just want to say thank you on behalf of all of us at US Soccer, it’s been a privilege to work with you here, see the amazing work you’ve done with the team,” she said. “I think you’ve left an amazing foundation for someone to come in and build on that great work and the good feeling and certainly the good habits you’ve left with the team. You’ve left a lasting impression on the American people.

According to recent reports, pochettino replied on the broadcast, saying: “I admire a lot Emma. She is a wonderful coach, and a wonderful person. It was an honor to share [this experience] with her.”.
