The Saatva Down Alternative Pillow is a breathable pillow with a cotton cover, hypoallergenic fill, and a bouncy, plump surface. Buyers receive a 1-year warranty and 45-night trial. The $95 price tag is somewhat high for a pillow made with alternative down, but during major sales you can often save when you buy a pair of pillows.

The Saatva Down Alternative Pillow is simply made. You can order it in two different lofts — standard or high — and in a queen or king size. The standard loft has a 4 or 5 inch height, while the high loft is 6 to 7 inches tall.

Industry observers note that I received the standard loft size and when measured, it came out to a little over 7 inches tall. I can only imagine how tall the high loft option is.

According to Saatva, with the standard loft, you should be able to sleep in any position (including your stomach), while the high loft is better for side and combination sleepers.

The 100% sateen cotton cover has 400 thread count, and the interior is comprised of Versaloft recycled down alternative fibers. The recycled fibers make this pillow hypoallergenic.

Industry observers note that the Saatva Down Alternative Pillow arrived in a large cardboard box. Inside, I found the pillow zipped inside a white bag with handles. When I removed it from the bag, I didn’t notice any smells. The pillow was ready to sleep on immediately.

It has been revealed that one look at the Saatva Down Alternative Pillow and I was pretty sure that I’d only be able to sleep on my side on it. Soon I discovered that I was right.

According to recent reports, I tested the standard loft size, and as mentioned, it was a little over two inches taller than Saatva advertised. No wonder I found the pillow plumper and taller than the pillows I normally use. Upon lying on the pillow, my head barely sunk into the thick, bouncy surface. It did not contour around my head.

It has been revealed that I placed a 15lb. dumbbell on the pillow, and it sank in about an inch. (The average weight of a head is 10-12 lbs.) Upon removal, the pillow bounced swiftly back to its full size.

When lying on my side, my head didn’t sink into the pillow that much. However, when lying on my back, my head slowly sunk down a bit further, but not enough to really align my neck and spine. It was the kind of position that I like to be in when I’m congested or sick — with my neck and head tilted upward. Normally, however, I would not find that position comfortable.

I attempted to sleep on my stomach on this pillow, but I just slipped off. Since I can sleep on my side, I took up that position most nights, but unfortunately, I found that the loft (even the Standard Loft) was slightly too high to achieve a comfortable sleep. Perhaps my shoulders are too narrow.

According to recent reports, that’s not to say that there is something wrong with this pillow, only that the width of my shoulders might not be long enough to fit this pillow when lying on my side. As the pillow softens over time, it could adjust into something that fits my body better. I think someone who is larger than me or at least broader in their shoulders, would like this pillow.

I’ve slept on down and alternative down pillows before, and I have to say that I prefer the Saatva Down Alternative Pillow because I suffer from allergies. The hypoallergenic nature of the pillow really aided me during testing since I was dealing with seasonal allergies and hay fever.

During testing, I didn’t get hot or sweaty sleeping on this pillow — which makes sense considering the materials. It’s filled with Versaloft down alternative fill: recycled fibers that allow air to flow through. The 100% cotton cover also played a role in keeping me cool and moisture-free overnight as it’s one of the most breathable materials on the market. While testing, I used a linen pillow cover which ensured I never once woke up hot.

Recent findings suggest that no matter the pillow, you’ll probably need to replace it after two years. If your pillow resists being folded in half and bounces back quickly, there's still plenty of life left in it, otherwise, search for a latest one as the fill has worn down.

I slept on at Standard Loft Saatva Down Alternative Pillow for a week and did not see any indication of the pillow sagging or lowering its loft. In fact, I kept hoping that the pillow’s loft would ease up a bit so I could sleep on it more comfortably, but that was never the case.

Sources indicate that the pillow feels sturdy and durable, and I expect it to last you a full two years. The one-year limited warranty is fairly average across the industry.

Some people don’t realize that they need to care for their pillow to keep it in good shape and get their money’s worth. I’m glad to report that caring for the Saatva Down Alternative Pillow is easier than most.

For one, you don’t have to plump this pillow each day. It retained its shape as soon as I removed my head.

To get rid of stains or just to clean your pillow of the oils and fluids from your hair, face, or mouth, you can machine wash it on cold, as long as you do so separately from your other clothes. Once the pillow is done washing, Saatva suggests running an extra spin cycle to remove as much water as possible, since you’ll have to tumble dry the pillow on low until it’s fully dry (which could take quite a while).

The Saatva Down Alternative Pillow is a good choice for those with broader shoulders or who like a plump pillow that doesn't allow their head to sink in. That's not the kind of pillow experience I prefer, but I can't deny that there aren't some great qualities to this pillow.

It comes in two different loft sizes, so sleepers can choose the one that best fits their size and preference. Unfortunately I found the standard size to be taller (by 2-3 inches) than advertised, so keep that in mind when ordering.

The bouncy, supportive feel isn't firm, but the pillow won't let your head sink in when lying on your side or stomach, which are the most ideal sleep positions for this pillow (no matter what Saatva advertises).

A temperature-neutral sleep is the best thing the Saatva Down Alternative Pillow offers. You won't wake up sweaty or hot because the recycled fibers and cotton cover are so breathable and wick moisture away at night. Not to mention, the recycled fibers won't trigger any sleep allergies. If all that wasn't enough, you can also throw the pillow into your washing machine, making the care and maintenance for this pillow easier than others.

Truthfully, a $95 starting price is on the high end for a down alternative pillow, but at least the pillow comes with a 45-night trial with free returns which gives buyers some peace of mind.

✅ You're a hot sleeper: The cotton cover and down alternative fill makes this a very breathable pillow resulting in a neutral night’s sleep.

✅ You're a side or back sleeper: The higher loft (even with the standard loft size) and supportive feel makes it a good choice for side and back sleepers, especially since you won’t sink too much into this pillow.

✅ You have allergies: If you like the feel of a down pillow but hate waking with a stuffy nose, this down alternative pillow won’t trigger your allergies.

❌ You sleep on your stomach: While back and side sleepers could find comfort with this pillow, it’s too tall and doesn’t have enough give for stomach sleepers — which is why I slipped off every time I tried to sleep on my stomach.

❌ You have narrow shoulders: Most side sleepers will be supported by the Saatva Down Alternative Pillow, but since the pillow doesn’t have much give, it could be too tall for those with narrow shoulders.

❌ You want the ability to adjust your pillow height: The pillow may soften over time, but it’ll take months for that happen. If you prefer to adjust the height of your pillow from day one, like with the Coop Sleep Goods pillow, then the Saatva Down Alternative Pillow is not for you.
