Propel Holdings revealed an enhanced AI cash-flow underwriting model and a partnership with Plaid for consumer-permissioned financial data on November 18, 2026, estimating that the technology could expand credit access by up to 15%. The model is designed to assess consumers whose financial circumstances may not be fully captured by traditional credit-bureau data.

The in-house system entered operation in late Q3 2026. Using bank transactions recorded over six months, it produces an assessment in seconds and supplements conventional bureau information. Propel remarked early results showed it could increase approvals while keeping its risk thresholds unchanged.

Sources indicate that under the partnership, Plaid supplies insights from consumer-permissioned financial data, covering income, cash movement and borrowers’ financial obligations. Propel remarked these inputs are intended to help it and its bank partners identify creditworthy applicants whom conventional underwriting may overlook.

Dr. Jonathan Goler, Propel’s co-founder and chief risk officer, described the reasoning behind that approach: “By combining open banking data, supported by Plaid, with sophisticated time series deep learning models, we can better understand a consumer’s current financial circumstances and identify patterns that are genuinely predictive of repayment.”.

According to recent reports, separately, Plaid’s underwriting documentation reports connectivity to more than 12,000 financial institutions. It describes risk signals drawn from cash-flow patterns, categorized income and account-connection activity across its network, with integration available through an API or a no-code dashboard.

Propel reports that it and its bank partners have underwritten more than two million loans and lines of credit, building a proprietary dataset in a segment of the credit market that can be particularly complex to assess.

According to recent reports, the organization’s platform page reports that its AI can consider up to 5,000 data points per applicant, with automatic credit adjudication taking approximately six seconds and more than 100,000 unique applications processed daily. The application figure covers the trailing 12 months as of June 30, 2026 and excludes QuidMarket. The platform also works alongside Propel’s risk team to incorporate broader macroeconomic trends.

Recent findings suggest that the deployment follows a period of rapid lending growth. In third-quarter results published November 3, 2026, Propel reported revenue of $201.5 million, up 32% year over year, while originations funded by Propel and its bank partners rose 27% to $260.7 million.

In a recent development, credit costs provide a counterweight to that growth. The provision for loan losses and other liabilities was 53% of revenue, compared with 52% a year earlier; Propel attributed the increase mainly to a higher proportion of upcoming-customer originations. Net charge-offs stayed at approximately 12% of the average combined balance of loans and advances, consistent with the prior year. These organization-wide third-quarter measures do not isolate the new model’s effect.

In a recent development, propel expects to bring the underwriting technology to the United States in early 2027 and said the capabilities could eventually support more tailored lending offers and additional financial products. The central question for that expansion is whether higher approvals can be sustained without weakening the credit performance Propel reported for the third quarter.
