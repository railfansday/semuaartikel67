Bitcoin’s turbulent week has created a $16 billion opportunity for a short squeeze

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that bitcoin is trying to recover after a volatile week. If buying carries it higher, leveraged short sellers forced to close their positions could add to the rebound.

Bitcoin traded at $61,436 at 13:09 UTC on Nov. 27, down 0.2% over the previous seven days, according to CryptoSlate’s Bitcoin price tracker.

Bitcoin climbed above $64,000 on Monday following Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO, which also drove the chipmaker's shares 31% higher and helped major US equity indices register their strongest sessions in more than a year.

However, the cryptocurrency surrendered its gains within hours and subsequently fell below $58,000 as concerns about the sustainability of the AI investment boom resurfaced.

According to recent reports, glassnode’s market analysis described Bitcoin’s reversal as an early sign that investors were selling into strength. The Nasdaq came under pressure a day later. However, global markets showed renewed signs of recovery on Friday. S&P 500 futures rose nearly 2%, Nasdaq-100 futures advanced 2.8%, and Dow Jones futures gained 655 points ahead of a shortened US trading session following Thanksgiving.

Equities across Asia and Europe also advanced, providing a more favorable backdrop for Bitcoin as bearish bets against the digital asset accumulate.

CryptoQuant analyst Darkfost noted that Bitcoin's price struggles this week have pushed its short-term holder market value-to-realized value (MVRV) ratio to 0.85, approaching the 0.81 reading recorded during the market's July 1 downturn.

The indicator measures the relationship between the market value of Bitcoin held by short-term investors and their aggregate acquisition cost, providing insight into the financial pressure facing recent buyers.

The ratio peaked at 1.18 on Sept. 22, when short-term holders were sitting on average unrealized profits of approximately 18%.

The subsequent decline has erased those gains, leaving the cohort with average unrealized losses of around 15% and an estimated cost basis of $71,600.

Darkfost remarked similar periods of financial stress have coincided with past Bitcoin bottoms, when prices traded below recent buyers’ acquisition costs.

Alphractal CEO Joao Wedson identified similar patterns across previous Bitcoin halving cycles, noting that the broader MVRV ratio's historical trajectories have remained remarkably consistent despite differences in the magnitude of price fluctuations.

According to Wedson, the current cycle's valuation trajectory suggests Bitcoin may be approaching the final stages of one of its strongest accumulation opportunities.

“History may not repeat itself perfectly, but the cyclical behavior of Bitcoin remains difficult to ignore.”.

At the UTC afternoon price, Bitcoin would need to rise about 16.5% to reach that estimated cost basis.

Meanwhile, the derivatives market has developed a substantial liquidation imbalance that could accelerate such a recovery if buying demand returns.

It has been revealed that cryptoQuant CEO Ki Young Ju’s Nov. 26 Bitcoin liquidation map estimated $16 billion in potential short liquidations across prices from $60,000 to $100,000, against less than $5 billion in potential long liquidations below that range.

A move through nearby resistance would still leave much of the $60,000 to $100,000 range untested.

“Short squeeze fuel is piling up for the next rally, imo.”.

An upward price move could force leveraged traders betting against Bitcoin to close their positions, adding buying pressure and potentially accelerating the cryptocurrency's recovery.

It has been revealed that cryptoSlate tracked a similar short-squeeze setup on Nov. 20, when a sell-off left a large cluster of shorts vulnerable to a price rebound. In the latest figures supplied by Bitunix, open interest rose while trading volume fell.

Analysts at crypto exchange Bitunix told CryptoSlate that Bitcoin's trading volume had declined nearly 47% over the preceding 24 hours, while liquidations fell approximately 69%.

Open interest, however, rose 1.2% to 1.5% over the same period, indicating that outstanding derivatives exposure grew despite the slowdown in trading activity.

Meanwhile, cumulative volume delta (CVD), which measures the difference between aggressive buying and selling, remained negative across both spot and futures markets.

Bitunix noted Bitcoin's ability to rebound despite negative CVD suggests that buyers are absorbing selling pressure, although aggressive buying demand has yet to materialize.

Recent findings suggest that the analysts identified $62,000 as Bitcoin's immediate resistance level, followed by a more concentrated supply zone between $62,400 and $62,640. A sustained break above these levels could expose nearby bearish positions to liquidation, providing the initial momentum needed to trigger a broader short squeeze.

Conversely, Bitunix warned that renewed dollar strength and a breakdown below $61,050 could send Bitcoin back toward the $60,350 to $60,600 support zone.

Friday's recovery in global equities provides a favorable backdrop for testing those resistance levels. Whether Bitcoin can sustain that momentum will depend on buyers absorbing the overhead supply that has repeatedly frustrated its recovery this week.
