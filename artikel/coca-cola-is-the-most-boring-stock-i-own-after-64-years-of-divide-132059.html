Recent findings suggest that I consider myself adventurous, but I often prefer boring when it comes to the stocks I own. I have my fair share of growth stocks, for sure, but my staple "boring" stock is Coca-Cola (KO -0.03%).

Industry observers note that although Coca-Cola is up over 40% this year (as of Nov. 24), its main appeal has long been its dividend. It has increased its annual dividend for 64 consecutive years, making it one of the market's premier Dividend Kings ( companies with at least 50 consecutive years of dividend hikes). Considering that and its sustained success, it's a stock I'll never sell.

It has been revealed that you can count on one hand the number of brands more well-known globally than Coca-Cola. Its products (especially its namesake soda) are available in virtually every part of the world, and its asset-light business model enables it to operate efficiently.

Industry observers note that usually, if a organization wants distribution somewhere, it needs plants, warehouses, and delivery fleets in that market. In Coca-Cola's case, it relies on bottling and distribution partners to do so. Instead of owning factories and delivery fleets around the world, Coca-Cola sells concentrates and syrups to its partners and lets them do the heavy lifting.

Keeping its business asset-light lets Coca-Cola maintain high margins and an ultra-safe dividend. You don't get to 64 years of dividend increases without consistent, high-margin cash flows.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Not many companies fit the "recession-proof" narrative, but Coca-Cola is one of them. Even when the economy slows and money is tight, people still buy their favorite Coca-Cola brands. That's the beauty of its brand moat.

And despite being the undisputed leader in its space, Coca-Cola hasn't gotten complacent with its drink portfolio. It constantly refines its lineup to adjust to changing consumer preferences. Just six years ago, it had over 400 brands in its portfolio. Today, it has around half of that.

When you have an option for virtually every occasion, you keep customers in your ecosystem. It's how the company has sustained success for so long.

In a recent development, coca-Cola is a premium business that's priced like one. At the time of this writing, it's trading at 28.9 times its earnings, which is higher than its average over the past few years and higher than the S&P 500's (^GSPC -0.25%)23.7 P/E ratio. The stock is still a buy, but after a 40% run-up this year, the upside may be more limited. Luckily, that doesn't affect its dividend.

Sources indicate that coca-Cola is always a good long-term investment, but with bear-market fears lingering, a short-term silver lining is that the stock typically outperforms the market during sell-offs. Past results don't guarantee future performance, but Coca-Cola has strong momentum at a time when the tech sector is dragging down the broader market.
