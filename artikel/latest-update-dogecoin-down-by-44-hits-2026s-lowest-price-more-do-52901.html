Dogecoin [DOGE] has been more bark than bite lately.

Last week’s sell-off pushed it down to a new yearly low on the charts and unfortunately, it doesn’t look like it’ll get back up anytime soon.

At the time of writing, Dogecoin was valued at $0.058. This followed a 3.3% dip over the day.

Things really fell apart on 14th November. DOGE fell from $0.065 to $0.057 in a single day. It also briefly hit $0.055, the lowest level of 2026. DOGE’s volume jumped too, implying that too many were selling at once.

Now, while it managed a small bounce to about $0.060 later, it wasn’t able to hold on to that level.

In a recent development, for context, DOGE was above $0.10 in late September. That’s 44% gone… in less than two months.

The RSI was at 28.9, hinting at oversold conditions. It even sank close to 18 during the crash. The MACD was still deep in the red, though the bars were shrinking just a tad.

Simply put, while the sellers seemed to be worn out, buyers are yet to show up.

Finally, leverage has been draining out too, with Open Interest sliding from about $475 million on the 11th to $362.5 at press time.

That’s a quarter of it gone in a week!

It has been revealed that it is a nervous state of things, and one look at the funding rates will tell you why. It was red during the crash of the 14th, flipped a couple more times, and at press time was back to 0.0052%.

It has been revealed that some traders may be betting on a bounce again, but not in substantial numbers. Not yet.

ETFs have been even quieter. According to data from SoSoValue, Spot DOGE ETFs haven’t seen a single day of inflows or outflows since mid-October. A whole month of nothing.

According to recent reports, their total net assets still dropped though. We went from $17 million in late September to about $9.74 million at the time of writing. In simple words, Dogecoin’s falling price has been shrinking what’s already there.
