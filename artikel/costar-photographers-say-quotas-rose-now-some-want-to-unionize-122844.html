CoStar photographers say quotas rose, now some want to unionize

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Earlier this fall, CoStar Group hosted its first Global Photography Conference in Richmond, Virginia. The event, which was held at the firm’s upcoming Foundry Park campus, was largely lauded as a success by CoStar and even members of its photography team.

CoStar CEO Andy Florance, also celebrated what appeared to be a successful event, writing in a post on LinkedIn that the team set the standard “for future collaboration as they shared techniques, tested new ideas and innovated what’s next for visual storytelling in real estate.”.

“I’m excited to see what they continue to build, one image at a time,” Florance, a HousingWire Vanguard, added.

But under the surface, sources that spoke with HousingWire noted that things within the listing portal giant’s photography team are not as hunky-dory as the social media posts paint them out to be. Over the past few weeks HousingWire has spoken with several sources within CoStar about a recent unionization effort among the organization’s architectural photographers, which were the photographers behind the content used for the Homes.com Neighborhood Guides.

The sources, who asked for anonymity due to fear of retribution, claim that the workload for architectural photographers is “unmanageable” and “impossible to complete within the working hours.” In 2025, CoStar raised daily quotas for architectural photographers, which was followed by a move this September to lower the point value of assignments, which means, according to the sources, that photographers have to do more work each day to meet their minimum quotas.

Last year, work quotas were raised from 0.8 per day to 1.0 per day and the point value of different tiers was dropped from 1 point to  0.75 point  for Diamond, from 0.75 point  to 0.50 point for Platinum and from 0.50 point to 0.25 point for Gold. This means that, in the past, photographers could complete one Diamond project and meet their daily quota, but now they have to do one Diamond project and one Gold project to meet quota.

Sources also claimed that there is no work-life balance as they sometimes have to drive over an hour to go to and from appointments and that it takes well over an hour to upload the images captured that day.

However, sources did note that CoStar has outsourced the photo editing work to help offset some of these issues, which the company confirmed.

In a recent development, despite this shift in responsibilities, the sources noted all of these challenges have been compounded by layoffs creating shortages of architectural photographers across the country. Sources said that this sometimes requires them to travel far outside their normal service areas further increasing drive times.

Industry observers note that “Not only did they bump up the daily requirement by 25%, they also scrapped any sort of flexible scheduling. Now every single photographer is expected to be on-call 12 hours a day, Monday through Friday. No exceptions. This change came [out of] nowhere,” a user named richmondrefugee posted in a subreddit called r/RealEstatePhotography. “And if that wasn’t enough, today they let go of two dozen folks — just like that. It’s been a mess, y’all.”.

In addition to this, the sources remarked they are concerned for their job security as the company continues to cut the architectural photography staff while hiring residential photographers. This is a more entry-level position at CoStar and as such these photographers are compensated at a lower rate. Sources within the company said that these other photographers were brought in to do Matterports, but are being sent out to do assignments that architectural photographers have done in the past.

“They had me train my replacement! I was getting paid six figures and one day they asked me to train this “Matterport Photographer,” making $40K. So I spent the week with him training. One day I was at his trunk when he was getting his Matterport and saw his Costar bag with all the same gear as me. I stated why are they giving a Matterport photographer over 10K in camera equipment. A month later I was gone and they have him doing my job,” KeepitMelloOoW posted in the r/RealEstatePhotogrpahy subreddit.

Sources told HousingWire that these concerns about job security, work load and work-life balance are what prompted the architectural photography team to explore the idea of unionizing by joining the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW). This resulted in a link to an IBEW unionization interest form being circulated within CoStar.

Unsurprisingly, the link caught the attention of CoStar management. In response to the link, Lisa Ruggles, CoStar’s senior vice president of global operations sent an email to all of the firm’s photographers that was obtained by HousingWire and verified by CoStar, in which she noted that CoStar was “was surprised to hear of an anonymous email afterward urging Architectural Photographers to join the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers,” in light of the success of the Global Photography Conference.

According to the email, “media shoots” are the “top priority” for the firm’s architectural photography team and that the point values for assignment changed due to the outsourcing of editing and post-production of images.

Recent findings suggest that “Photo retouching now goes to a dedicated resource, while Matterport and floor-strategy uploads are automated —removing tasks photographers once handled. We must use upcoming technology to compete. A union cannot change the work our customers demand or our business requires,” Ruggles wrote.

Industry observers note that ruggles also noted that the change in point values also helped level the playing field and make sure all photographers from across the company were completing the same level of work. She also noted that CoStar is monitoring how long it takes photographers to complete various projects and adjusting their value.

“We are building a dynamic system to better account for longer trips in rural markets,” the email stated. “Outside of the news and media industries, we know of no company that directly employs as many photographers as CoStar. We want to preserve as many of these well-compensated positions as possible because they provide meaningful work, competitive benefits, and financial stability for employees and their families — but the business must remain economically sustainable.”.

Ruggles added that CoStar feels unionization would make it harder for the company to respond to market pressures and shifts in business needs, which would not be in the best interests of employees. Additionally, Ruggles wrote that an IBEW election victory would bind all employees in the bargaining unit to a negotiated contract, and that a customary dues provision could require payments of 2% of pay, with opt-outs unavailable in most states where it operates. The company said employment terms could improve, stay the same or decline through bargaining and urges employees to make an informed decision, noting that unions are usually difficult to remove once elected.

Sources within CoStar, as well as the firm itself, have confirmed that no formal reply regarding Ruggles’ email has been sent. Despite this, sources told HousingWire that the unionization effort is continuing to proceed.

CoStar did not wish to share any additional comments regarding the unionization effort.
