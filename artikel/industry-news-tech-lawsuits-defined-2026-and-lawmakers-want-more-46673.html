Congress is scrambling to provide legal recourse for deepfakes and online grooming.

With just a few weeks remaining, lawmakers are trying to pass new protections for victims of online harm before the end of 2026, targeting deepfake creators and online platforms that block people from filing lawsuits.

In a recent development, on Tuesday, members of a Senate Judiciary subcommittee hosted a hearing calling for the end of forced arbitration for online harms. Their immediate target was Roblox, which they accused of evading legal complaints about children allegedly groomed on the platform by enforcing its own forced arbitration clause against people who agreed to the terms as minors, in some cases even under the age of 10. Separately, a few blocks away, Reps. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) and Laurel Lee (R-FL) brought hotel heiress Paris Hilton to Capitol Hill to advocate for the Defiance Act, which would let victims of nonconsensual intimate deepfakes sue their creators.

“The best thing we can do in terms of enforcement is we can open up the courthouse doors,” Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO), who chairs the Judiciary subcommittee, told reporters after the Roblox hearing. “The more I see these companies evade regulations, the more I think there is nothing as powerful as a jury.” Hawley introduced a upcoming bill with ranking member Dick Durbin (D-IL) to render arbitration agreements unenforceable for disputes over alleged child sexual exploitation or “child cyber injury,” which could include “reputational harm, mental suffering,” or death.

“The best thing we can do in terms of enforcement is we can open up the courthouse doors”.

Industry observers note that roblox has sought to enforce its arbitration clause in lawsuits it’s facing over allegations that users were groomed on the gaming platform as minors, despite the manufacturer’s claims of protections. Roblox is facing about 180 lawsuits in a consolidated case over its alleged role in failing to protect children. Under the arbitration process, the public would not get to see legal discovery like the kind that’s come to light under recent cases against Meta. Hawley called the jury system “corruption-proof” and “an incredibly big hammer.”.

In a recent development, roblox has denied that it’s trying to dodge accountability. “Safety has been central to Roblox since we rolled out. We require age checks for chat, place customers under 16 in age-based accounts with protective defaults, and block images and video in chat,” Roblox’s global head of public policy Nicky Jackson Colaço mentioned in a statement. “A legal position about arbitration is not a measure of a tech giant’s safety record, and a hearing built around it fails to advance safety.”.

In a recent development, congress has only a little more than three more weeks left in session before the end of the year, and a latest Democratic majority will be sworn in come January. Republicans are expected to do their best to push through President Donald Trump’s nominees for judgeships and executive branch posts like FCC commissioner in the lame-duck session, as well as try to pass year-end bills like the National Defense Authorization Act, which often includes a range of other policy items. But some lawmakers are holding out hope that they can open up courthouses to new litigation over deepfake abuse and child predation.

In a recent development, litigation has become one of the more successful ways for survivors of online harm to seek change. Congress has failed to pass many prominent child safety, privacy, and consumer protection bills, though it has begun to tackle the deepfake issue through the Take It Down Act. At the same time, attorneys general and individual plaintiffs have collected significant settlements and legal wins despite the broad scope of tech’s legal shield, Section 230. If bills like the Defiance Act or those seeking to end forced arbitration were to pass, tech companies and perpetrators of online harms could become vulnerable to a upcoming wave of legal challenges.

“You will be amazed by how promptly the company will change its behavior once it is sued in public court”.

Recent findings suggest that in addition to Hawley and Durbin’s bill about forced arbitration, Sens. Richard Blumenthal (D-CT), Katie Britt (R-AL), and Cory Booker (D-NJ) introduced a similar bill with a slightly different approach, including explicitly covering data privacy violations. At the Roblox hearing, Northwestern University law professor Myriam Gilles, an expert on forced arbitration, noted she’s testified before Congress many times on this topic, but “never expected to come before this body to talk about its use against 9 year olds.” Passing either proposal, she said, would have a profound impact. “You will be amazed by how swiftly the company will change its behavior once it is sued in public court.”.

Sources indicate that the Defiance Act would let victims of nonconsensual intimate deepfakes sue their creators for damages, in an attempt to raise the cost of creating these kinds of images. Hilton visited Washington earlier this year to share how an intimate non-deepfake video of her at age 19 shared without her consent impacted her life, and she used her story to advocate for the Defiance Act. At Tuesday’s press conference, she noted she would come back to Congress as many times as necessary for the bill to become law. “Some people might have thought that was the end of my fight,” Hilton said. “But when I make a promise to survivors, I keep it.”.

Hilton stated over 50 million people have been victimized by sexually explicit deepfakes. “For me personally, strangers have created over 100,000 AI-generated explicit images and videos portraying me in the most graphic and disturbing ways imaginable,” Hilton said. “If this can happen to someone with my resources and my platform, imagine how powerless a young girl might feel facing this completely alone.”.

In a recent development, hilton’s comments were followed by Elliston Berry, an advocate who said a classmate made an explicit deepfake image of her friend group “from an innocent social media post” when she was 14, and distributed it throughout her school. “In a matter of seconds, my sense of security disappeared,” she said. “For me, the Defiance Act means recognition that victims deserve to be heard, that their experience matters, and that their right to seek justice should not depend on how quickly technology advances.”.
