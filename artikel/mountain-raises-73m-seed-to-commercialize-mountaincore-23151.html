MountAIn revealed €7.3 million in seed financing on November 17, 2026, to commercialize MountainCore, its platform for running complete computer-vision applications on microcontrollers costing less than $10.

The financing came from 360 Capital, Supernova Invest and Crédit Agricole Alpes Développement, with additional support from Bpifrance. MountAIn plans to expand its engineering team in Grenoble, strengthen its presence in Europe and Silicon Valley, and target semiconductor, smart-camera and industrial-equipment manufacturers.

In a recent development, mountAIn is addressing the gap between a trained model and the limited processors inside cameras, industrial equipment and other connected devices. Memory and power are constrained, connectivity can be unreliable, and hardware-specific integration can turn deployment into a lengthy engineering program. Buying powerful, energy-hungry hardware increases costs, while sending data to the cloud adds expense, latency, connectivity requirements and privacy concerns.

MountainCore adapts the model and its supporting software together and supports multiple ARM-based microcontroller families. The organization’s product site describes the underlying MountAIn Core as a developer workflow, compiler and middleware layer that optimizes the full application path for its target environment. Its Onboard route is intended to bring a model into an existing board, camera, sensor or device stack without rebuilding the product around a upcoming AI platform.

MountAIn says the platform can reduce deployment engineering from months to hours, cut model memory requirements by up to threefold, make better use of on-chip AI accelerators and preserve accuracy without retraining. Applications then run on the device, reducing cloud costs and keeping sensitive data local.

Recent findings suggest that “AI models are designed for GPUs and data centers, yet most equipment in the real world could run on microcontrollers consuming just milliwatts of power,” remarked Vincent Huard, MountAIn’s CEO and co-founder. He said the financing is intended to turn early industrial use into large-scale commercial deployment.

Teledyne e2v demonstrated MountainCore at VISION Stuttgart in 2026, using it in an edge-AI system that combined 2D and 3D vision from its Perciva 5D camera. Roboflow, whose platform MountAIn notes is used by more than two million developers and 80,000 companies, now offers direct model export to IBEX. Alif Semiconductor has also partnered with MountAIn on computer-vision applications for its energy-efficient Ensemble and Balletto processor families.

Recent findings suggest that IBEX demonstrates how the technology can operate in remote, off-grid locations. MountAIn says the autonomous vision sensor consumes less than 100 milliwatts and was named a CES 2026 Innovation Awards Honoree. Solar-powered and satellite-connected, IBEX analyzes images locally for applications such as early wildfire detection and infrastructure monitoring.

MountAIn plans to return to CES in Las Vegas in January 2027 to demonstrate MountainCore’s industrial capabilities and show IBEX applications designed for remote locations.

For MountAIn, the central commercial question is whether those partner integrations and demonstrations can become large-scale deployments while preserving model accuracy and delivering the engineering-time savings the tech giant reports on constrained hardware.
