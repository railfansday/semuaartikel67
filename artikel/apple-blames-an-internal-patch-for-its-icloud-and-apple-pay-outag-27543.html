Apple blames an internal patch for its iCloud and Apple Pay outage

It has been revealed that apple said an internal update caused Tuesday’s outage, which hit iCloud, Apple Pay, the App Store and other services. The problem was not a cyberattack, the tech giant said, Reuters reported.

It has been revealed that “Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal patch caused some outages this morning. We’re working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible,” Apple said in a statement.

By early evening, all systems were working normally again, according to Apple, The New York Times reported.

Industry observers note that consumers reported problems with Apple Pay and iCloud sign-ins on social media and on Downdetector, which counts user reports. Apple Pay drew about 2,300 reports in the US around 12:20pm ET, Reuters reported. iCloud reports peaked at about 1,800.

Apple confirmed full outages at Apple Music, the App Store and AppleCare, the Associated Press reported. Apple’s retail stores worldwide also reportedly had problems that halted sales and in-person support, according to The Register.

Industry observers note that the Apple outage came after a run of failures in the US. Microsoft blamed a dormant July defect for its Microsoft 365 outage last Thursday. On Friday, a router upgrade in Arkansas led to a nationwide ground stop of US flights.

Industry observers note that on Monday, Chase blamed a software bug for problems at several US banks. United Airlines also had a system outage that held up departing flights for about an hour.

On Tuesday, the NSA’s classified internal network, NSANet, went offline too. The agency described it as a “temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance”, The Register reported.

On Wednesday, Secretary of State Marco Rubio mentioned the US was not under systemic attack, The Guardian reported.

“We have zero evidence or any reason to believe that any of these isolated or individual instances have anything to do with nefarious actors, with cyber-hackers, with criminals, with another nation state, with a malign actor of any kind,” Rubio remarked.

Industry observers note that director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told Fox Business that the failures came from security upgrades that took longer than planned or did not come back as expected. Fixes for the recent PHP flaws were one example, The Guardian reported. Senator Mark Warner, the vice-chair of the Senate intelligence committee, backed that view.

It has been revealed that security experts told The Register that outages like these may now be routine. AI tools are finding old flaws faster, and companies are racing to patch them. Mike Wiacek, Stairwell’s chief technology officer, founded Google’s Threat Analysis Group. The Register asked him if this is the latest normal.

According to recent reports, “The brutal truth is yes. Especially in the AI world that we live in,” Wiacek noted.
