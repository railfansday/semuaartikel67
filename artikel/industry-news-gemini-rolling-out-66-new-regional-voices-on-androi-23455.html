📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Following a preview at Google I/O, Google is rolling out 66 latest regional voices to the Gemini app on both Android and iOS today. The expansion introduces tailored voice options across U.S. English, U.K. English, Indian English, Hindi, Brazilian Portuguese, Indonesian, and Korean.

For owners in the UK, the revision brings over 10 distinct English voice options covering a wide range of regional cadences. The lineup includes Geordie, Scouse, Brummie, Scottish, Welsh, Northern Irish, Modern RP, and Multicultural London English (MLE).

Sources indicate that this might help reduce the challenges that sometimes still occur when English accents, in particular, morph into a hybrid of Australian or even American that has been going on for a while with Gemini Live. We have not yet been able to test to confirm this has been resolved with the enhancement. As someone with a regional English accent, this extra representation with Gemini voices is quite welcome.

It has been revealed that according to Google, these voices are tuned for natural pronunciation, pitch, and everyday local rhythm rather than exaggerated stereotypes. Because the focus is on cadence over slang, Gemini will sound like a local without filling its text responses with forced street slang. Once selected, your chosen voice carries across the entire app, applying to both Gemini Live conversations and standard text-to-speech playback when tapping the “Listen” icon on chat responses.

You can preview and select the upcoming options today by tapping the three-dot menu inside Gemini Live, or by heading into the Gemini app settings and selecting “Gemini’s voice.” It’s worth noting that the voices aren’t explicitly labeled by city or region in the app menu, and Gemini isn’t programmed to self-identify which accent it is using if you ask it directly.

Instead, Google notes that locally rooted prompts about food, culture, or sport, such as asking for the best pub in Sheffield for a Sunday roast – my suggestion is not a pub; it’s Turnip & Thyme on Eccleshall Road, FWIW. Or another suggestion from big G is where to find proper ingredients for a traditional Scouse stew; the company claims these will naturally bring out the warmth and rhythm of the fresh dialects.

I’d suggest trying to ask where to get the best pattie and chips in Hull or where to get the best pint in Leeds (it’s Whitelocks).
