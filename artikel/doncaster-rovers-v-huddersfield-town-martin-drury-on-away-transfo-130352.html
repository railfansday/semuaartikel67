One of the most uplifting facets of Martin Drury's full-time tenure has been the transformation so far in 2026-27.

A good barometer of their improvement arrives in tonight's Yorkshire derby at Doncaster Rovers - on an evening when three points would see the Terriers move level on points with leaders Stockport County.

A poor night at the South Yorkshire venue in February saw Town lose 1-0 in a toothless and tepid showing which received jeers from travelling fans at half-time and the final whistle.

It was one a number of bad days on the road. Others were played out at Oakwell, Valley Parade and further afield at Adams Park and Home Park.

There were plenty more too, especially in the second half of the season when the entertainment value was lamentably low.

In the away table in 25-26, Huddersfield were ranked a below-par 13th, with 25 points from 23 games. Refreshingly, the picture is changing, not just in terms of points ratio, but goals and chance creation.

According to recent reports, town have averaged two points a game on the road so far, with 14 points from their seven matches. It's currently the second best record in the division, factoring in goal difference.

Industry observers note that drury observed: "There was some criticism of the away form last season. Obviously, I came in February. Between February and the end of the season, the away form wasn't brilliant.

Sources indicate that "Prior to that, for the seven months before that, there weren't enough points picked up on the road. And if you want to be successful, you need to do that.

"So that was certainly an area that we addressed in the summer. And we did some things around that with the players, both on and off the grass, around certain scenarios and places that you're going to go to and how we might play when we're home and away. I think the players have been brilliant.

"I think we're fourth or third best in the league, maybe, for away form at the current time.

"We're going to lose some games away. That's going to happen. But I have to say, the things that we wanted to try and learn from last season and get better at, the players have done really, really well with that.

"And we've gone to some tough places so far with different types of challenges that we've enjoyed and embraced.".

Town face a Rovers side who don't usually mind derbies. They excelled, certainly in the first half, of the home game with Sheffield Wednesday last month and revealed character to come from behind to secure a hard-earned stoppage-time point at Bradford City on Saturday.

The latter derby marked the first-team return of ex-Terriers defender Matty Pearson after almost two-and-a-half months out with injury.

Pearson came through the weekend draw, while Jamie Sterry, James Husband and Tommi O’Reilly are all in contention for tonight's game.

Eager to make up for lost time, Pearson commented: "It's one of those things, you feel helpless when you're injured. It's been a tough few months but I'm well past that now.

"It's how you handle things and react. I think everyone's career has ups and downs and it's how you bounce back and how you react to them situations.".

Pearson unquestionably had a mixed time during his four seasons at Huddersfield.

The Keighley-born player continued: "It was a big change really. We had an unbelievable first season (2021-22) and got into the play-offs and final (against Nottingham Forest at Wembley).

It has been revealed that "And then it was 'sliding doors'. I don't know the full ins and outs but we struggled for the following two or three seasons after that. We went from playoffs to relegation battles every season.

"It's hard to say exactly what went on, it's one of those where it's been taken over now and they look like they're having a good season so far.".

While Town have incentives this evening, so do Rovers; victory would see them move out of the relegation zone and jump up three places to 18th.

Drury added: "They obviously scored late on Saturday, which gives them a little bit of a boost when they get a goal to draw the game.

"I'm sure they've wanted to pick up more points than they have done this season, but I think they're a team with energy and players that want to get on the ball and play and take the ball under pressure.

"They're a team that have scored a lot of goals from set-pieces and scored goals from transition, so they can attack fast.

"But for all the stats, there's also a lot of things where we feel we can impose ourselves and really hurt them. We're in a good place.".

Recent findings suggest that star Town winger Derensili Sanches Fernandes is available for tonight's game, despite coming off with a slight foot concern/cramp on Saturday's loss to Leicester City. David Kasumu and Samson Tovide are sidelined.
