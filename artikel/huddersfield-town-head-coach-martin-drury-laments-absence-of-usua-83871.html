Huddersfield Town head coach Martin Drury lamented the absence of his side’s “usual spark” after their 1-0 defeat to Leicester City.

The Terriers suffered just their second League One defeat of the season against the Foxes, who struck early in the second half through Louis Page.

Drury did not feel Huddersfield deserved to lose, but also conceded they did not do enough to claim three points.

He remarked: “I didn’t think we did enough to win the game but I don’t think we deserved to lose the game. At the top end of the pitch, we didn’t have the usual spark about us.

“The goal we concede is beyond disappointing. It’s not a goal that’s well worked and has cut us open. It’s a long ball that we need to deal with better.

“I’m not sure you’ll see a softer goal across the country. We could have created far more chances. We didn’t execute well enough.”.

In a recent development, regarding their approach to tackling Leicester, Drury added: “We didn’t over respect them but realised they are the highest in the league for having the ball. We wanted to make sure that didn’t lead to chances.

“There are things to learn and grow from, like our crossing into the box. You have to make things happen and I didn’t feel here we made it happen. We just lacked that little bit of quality in the final third.”.

It was a vital win for Leicester and one that eased the pressure on boss Russell Martin, who basked in the celebrations with fans following the full-time whistle.

In a recent development, he mentioned: “It was a bit of relief for me really, because I’ve not done that this season. The fans were amazing here when we needed them. The last 15 minutes they were brilliant and I’m really grateful for that. Hopefully they can feel what we are trying to build.

“My son has been telling me that he’s been desperate for a fist pump! But it was just a relief. And I’m so proud of the players.
