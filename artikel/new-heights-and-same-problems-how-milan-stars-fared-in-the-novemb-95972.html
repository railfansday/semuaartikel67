New heights and same problems: How Milan stars fared in the November international break

With just a few days until the return of AC Milan action, the international break is now done. Let’s take a look at how the internationals fared.

Whether you love it or you hate it, the international break is now over and players are trickling back into Milanello. Even those who didn’t head away are only just really returning, with Ruben Amorim offering the squad a week off.

So, let’s look at what Amorim has returning, with several things to shout about….

Christian Pulisic probably leaves the break as the star of the week(s). His brace against Haiti helped secure a place in the CONCACAF Nations League semifinals, and he then rounded it off with a bicycle kick in the second leg.

After missing the penultimate international break of the season – to ensure he was fully back to fitness – he’s promptly reminded everyone of how vital he can be for club and country, scoring his seventh goal of the season in only 13 games.

He’s on fire and there are no signs of him slowing down anytime soon.

Industry observers note that you’d then have to speak about Diego Moreira, who has left the international break – somehow – with more praise than he started it with. He starred in Belgium’s 2-1 win against Turkiye and then followed that up with a goal against Italy.

Sources indicate that it gets to a point where you run out of superlatives to describe a player and his teammates are already at that stage. Both Mark Van Bommel and Koni De Winter have called him crazy (in a good way), with the former even putting him in the same group as Kevin De Bruyne and Jeremy Doku.

He’s become a starter for the Red Devils and it’s difficult to see that changing.

It has been revealed that in terms of the Italians, Milan had a few reasons to smile and it starts with Alphadjo Cisse. At first, it seemed he’d been wasted during the break, with Roberto Mancini not calling him up for the game against France.

However, he was given the nod for the second game and got some valuable minutes on the field. As the Rossoneri have become familiar with, he took that chance with both hands, scoring a disallowed goal and winning a penalty in a defeat to Belgium.

He’s shining with confidence currently and long may it continue.

Then, Francesco Camarda is probably at the other end of the scale. He’s clearly not got the most confidence when representing the Diavolo, but returning to the youth level, he shone like he always has.

In a recent development, against Sankhoun Diawara’s France side, he was excellent. In terms of his hold-up play, finishing and just general performance, he was almost perfect, if not perfect. With a goal to round it off too, he leaves the break with a much-needed confidence boost.

Sources indicate that unfortunately, we can’t say the same for Goncalo Ramos. After Cristiano Ronaldo’s retirement, this was a chance for him to take the star role and prove that he’s going to be the man to ensure things keep ticking.

Instead, he left the break leaving Portugal with more questions than answers, which is a familiar feeling.

He played just shy of 150 minutes during the break and mustered just one shot. It’s not like he provided an assist or any key passes either. In other words, it’s another blank for him and it’s clearly having an effect.

Without a goal in 661 minutes, it’s no shock why the Diavolo are so heavily pursuing a striker ahead of January.

In a recent development, for France, it was a relatively quiet break for Mike Maignan and Adrien Rabiot. Neither performed especially well against Italy in a 3-1 loss at San Siro, though Rabiot was named as the captain for that game. Then, in the second game, Maignan was injured. However, it seems he’ll be fine for this weekend.

Not a lot to shout about for those two, but that’s not necessarily a bad thing either.

For Luka Modric, his minutes were focused in the right areas. He played 14 minutes in a nothing game against England, but was then essential in a 2-1 win against the Czech Republic, which may help them qualify for the latter stages of the Nations League.

It was another strange international break for Ardon Jashari, too. He was given no minutes against Slovenia in a 0-0 draw, but then played the full 90 minutes against Scotland, where they won 4-0. He made 76 passes from 90 touches, so he had some involvement. However, he’s still a way away from being a dictator of the team, as it was hoped he’d be.

Davide Bartesaghi, Christian Comotto and Sankhoun Diawara were all given valuable minutes during the break. Finally, Pervis Estupinan played 36 minutes over the break. He returns without any injuries and that’s all there is to really say about his past fortnight.
