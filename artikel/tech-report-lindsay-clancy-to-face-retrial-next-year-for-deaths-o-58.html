Lindsay Clancy, the Massachusetts woman accused of murdering her three children, will face a retrial next year, a judge has stated, after a jury deadlocked last time in a case that gripped the nation.

The judge set a trial date for 29 March 2027, though the district attorney did not explicitly say what charges Clancy would face.

Prosecutors said they wanted a jury trial, and the defence said they took that to mean Clancy, 36, would again face first-degree murder charges.

Five-year-old Cora, Dawson, three, and eight-month-old Callan were strangled at the family home on 24 January 2023. The defence argues she had severe postpartum psychosis.

"I think you can read between the lines that when they say that the case is going to require a jury, that it's an indirect comment from the DA [district attorney] that they intend to proceed on first-degree murder indictments, so we will pick a trial date," defence lawyer Kevin Reddington said at Monday's hearing.

Prosecutors wanted a May trial date, but Judge William Sullivan decided on March instead. The judge is due to retire in June.

Clancy was charged in the first trial with three counts of first-degree-murder, which is the intentional and premeditated killing of another person.

Sources indicate that the district attorney has not yet officially said he will proceed with first-degree-murder charges. The BBC has contacted the district attorney's office for comment.

Clancy with her ex-husband and their children - five-year-old Cora, Dawson, three, and eight-month-old Callan.

The prosecution could choose to bring lesser charges against Clancy, such as second-degree-murder, which applies to unlawful killings committed with malice or extreme recklessness, but without planning.

A week ago, Judge Sullivan rejected a defence attempt to throw out the case on the grounds of double jeopardy.

Double jeopardy is a legal principle that protects someone from being prosecuted more than once for the same crime after an acquittal or conviction.

Recent findings suggest that the judge's decision cleared the way for a second trial. It was Judge Sullivan who declared the mistrial on 4 September because of the hung jury.

Afterwards, some of the 12 jurors mentioned in media interviews that they had been ready to find Clancy not guilty by reason of insanity, but that one juror had held out.

It was not disputed at Clancy's first trial that she strangled the children with exercise bands in the basement of the family home in the Boston suburb of Duxbury.

She had asked her now-ex husband, Patrick Clancy, to leave the house to pick up takeout food and children's medicine shortly before the children were attacked.

The defence team wanted her found not guilty by reason of insanity, known in Massachusetts as lack of criminal responsibility.

The case sparked a debate over how postpartum mental illness is treated in the US.

Clancy is right now in a state-run psychiatric hospital and medical facility in Massachusetts.
