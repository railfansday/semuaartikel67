The U.K.’s harassment and bullying watchdog has seen a spike in whistleblowing reports following Russell Brand’s conviction for rape earlier this month.

It has been revealed that creative Industries Independent Standards Authority, a voluntary body with support across media and the arts, has said it received over 90 reports since launching its whistleblowing service in June, with a spike following Brand’s conviction. The former comedian was sentenced to 10 and a half years in prison after a jury found him guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

The reports cover all the sectors represented by CIISA including film, tv, music, theatre and live performance.

In a recent development, CIISA is an officially designated whistleblowing body and its confidential whistleblowing service is available for those in need of immediate support while enabling it to build up insight. Reports will be used to identify patterns and problems which it can then report back to policymakers and to the industry.

Where necessary, the body will also make “lawful referrals.”.

The news comes as CIISA hosted an event in Parliament Wednesday to engage industry leaders and lawmakers on preventing harmful behaviour in the creative industries. It was hosted by CIISA chair Baroness Helena Kennedy with attendees including numerous MPs, including chair of the digital, culture, media and sport committee Caroline Dinenage as well as media figures such as BBC chair Samir Shah and ex-BBC director general Tim Davie.

Recent findings suggest that the event marked the official launch of CIISA’s register, which displays the organizations financially contributing to CIISA. Orgs supporting the watchdog will be entitled to carry a CIISA registration mark, demonstrating their commitment to the body’s standards, as well as access to events and resources.Contributions are made on a financial sliding scale, with no tech giant expected to pay more than 0.1% of its U.K. turnover.

“CIISA has a pivotal role to play in preventing harm and closing accountability gaps in the creative industry infrastructure, offering a unique opportunity to identify harmful patterns of behaviour across different projects,” noted CIISA CEO Jen Smith.

“We thank the organisations who have already pledged their support for this important work and we will continue to work alongside industry and Government to ensure that CIISA is adequately resourced to offer a safe, confidential route for people to seek independent advice and report concerns, and to embed the consistent cross-sector standards that will drive lasting change.”.
