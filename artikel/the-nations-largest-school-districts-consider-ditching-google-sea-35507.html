Google is trying to force Gemini on schools, and they’re not thrilled.

According to recent reports, new York, Los Angeles, and Chicago schools are considering Google alternatives after the company stated it will disable Search for schools that opt out of its AI features, according to The New York Times. Starting next year, schools will not have the option to disable Gemini-powered features without also disabling Search. Chris Phillips, Google’s vice president of education, told the Times that, “Search and AI are deeply integrated,” and that schools that turn off Google’s AI features “will not have access to Search.”.

Sources indicate that many elected officials, including New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Senator Bernie Sanders, responded harshly, calling Google’s behavior “extortion.” Mamdani had recently confirmed a policy that barred 2-K-8 students from using AI in the classroom. In a statement to the Times, New York City schools chancellor Kamar H. Samuels noted, “If Google doesn’t reconsider their position, we will explore other search options.” Chicago’s public schools have also reportedly told Google that if it can’t provide a version of Search without AI, “the district will look to other search solutions.”.

Sources indicate that in a statement to The Verge, Google spokesperson Laura Breen mentioned, “We hear parents and teachers’ concerns about students using AI to shortcut learning, and we share the same goal: help students learn. To improve Search for the classroom, we’re starting to pilot Guided Learning capabilities in Search this month to walk students step-by-step through a range of complex problems, instead of just giving quick answers. Next year, we’re also updating our approach to controls for schools to address feedback we’ve heard from educators, so Gemini app, Gemini Notebook and Search will be off by default until schools decide which services to enable. We are actively building these experiences in partnership with educators, listening closely to ensure our products support students and teachers.”.

It has been revealed that educators are wary of AI’s effect on students’ critical thinking skills, and some studies have shown that students who use AI generally perform worse on tests. There are also concerns about the mental health effects on children who develop relationships with chatbots at a young age. But Google is deeply embedded in classrooms across the US, which rely on Google Classroom, Chromebooks, and other services, and going fully Google-free might prove difficult.
