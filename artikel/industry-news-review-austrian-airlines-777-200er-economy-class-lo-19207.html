According to recent reports, austrian Airlines is about as old-school as you can get on its 777-200ER, with my 11.5 hour economy class flight featuring plenty of food and efficient service, but an uncomfortable seat, oppressively warm cabin, and no Wi-Fi. Here’s my full review.

My children spend a month in Germany each summer and we kicked off their time with a flight to VIE and a night in Vienna.

This ticket was part of a round-trip economy class itinerary I booked several months earlier. The outbound journey was on SWISS from Zürich to Chicago, while the return brought us from Los Angeles to Zürich via Vienna on Austrian Airlines.

The entire ticket cost $764 round-trip, which was an excellent fare. I booked through United Airlines before it stopped awarding redeemable miles and Premier qualifying points on basic economy tickets, so I still received credit for these flights.

It has been revealed that the tradeoff was that advance seat assignments were not included. I was traveling with two young children, however, and correctly assumed we would be seated together at check-in. We received three adjacent seats without paying anything extra.

I checked in online and we were traveling without larger baggage, so we showed up to LAX about 75 minutes prior to departure, breezed through security, and boarded our flight about 50 minutes before departure.

I did stop briefly at the counter to see if we could be moved to a section of three (instead of the center section of four that we were auto-assigned) and I was obliged, which was fortunate since the flight ended up going out 100% full.

Stepping onboard, we made our way to the rear economy class cabin for our long journey ahead.

Industry observers note that austrian Airlines configures its Boeing 777-200ER with 330 seats: 32 in business class, 40 in premium economy, and 258 in economy class. Economy is arranged in a dense 3-4-3 configuration, with a minimum seat pitch of 30 inches.

Sources indicate that the cabin is dated with the dreary fluorescent light and smaller overhead bins, but was clean and well-maintained. Each Recaro seat included an adjustable headrest, a seatback entertainment screen, a low voltage USB-A port, a tray table, and a literature pocket. Glaringly, there are no power outlets for 110v/220v devices.

Industry observers note that unfortunately, I did not find the seat particularly comfortable. Legroom felt very tight, especially when the passenger in front reclined, and the padding was not generous. Ten seats across on a Boeing 777 also makes for a narrow experience, though that has sadly become common throughout the industry.

Industry observers note that A pillow and blanket were waiting at each seat. Both were perfectly respectable for economy class, but neither could overcome the larger problem: the cabin remained oppressively warm throughout the flight. I already struggle to sleep in economy class, and the combination of limited space and excessive heat eliminated any realistic chance of rest. There were no overhead air vents and it was just too warm onboard.

My children had no such problem. They twisted themselves into positions that would leave me unable to walk for a week and slept for nearly eight hours. Oh, to be young….

Austrian served dinner after takeoff, followed by a cold snack midway through the flight and a hot breakfast before landing in Vienna. Whatever else I may say about the quality, Austrian certainly did not leave passengers hungry.

Service began with a crackers and drinks (all alcohol was free and I had a glass of prosecco).

In a recent development, dinner choices were chicken or pasta. I chose the chicken, which consisted of four large breaded pieces that looked a bit like Wiener schnitzel. It was served with potatoes, green beans, a salad, bread, and cake.

Austrian posts its economy class menus online:.

SEASONAL LETTUCE with balsamic vinaigrette.

FRIED CHICKEN TENDER with steamed potatoes.

CHEESE TORTELLINI with tomato-basil sauce and parmesan.

The portion was enormous for economy class. My children could not finish their meals, but I managed to eat mine and then finished theirs…plenty of protein on this flight.

The chicken was also the popular choice throughout the cabin. Flight attendants ran out a couple of rows behind us, leaving passengers in the rear portion of economy class with pasta as their only option, making me very thankful we were not seated further back.

A cold cheese sandwich was distributed midway through the flight that was not wort eating (highly processed American bread and cheese).

Recent findings suggest that I appreciated that Austrian served a second hot meal before landing, not that it was worth eating either. Breakfast included an omelet with mushrooms, potatoes, and a tomato. Unfortunately, the omelet tasted artificial and powdery, while the mushrooms underneath it were repulsive. I’m not expecting fresh eggs like Lufthansa First Class, but this was a really poor-quality breakfast.

Coffee was another disappointment. Austrian is famous for its elaborate coffee menu in business class, including specialties like the Wiener Melange, but economy class passengers received powdered coffee. Yuck. I do not expect barista-quality coffee in economy class, but surely an Austrian flag carrier can provide freshly brewed filter coffee on a longhaul flight. The powdered substitute was horrible. Alright, I’ll stop griping….

Each economy class seat had a personal touchscreen entertainment system with movies, television programs, music, games, and a moving map. The interface and screen looked dated compared to the latest systems, including on the Austrian 787 Dreamliners, and the overall selection was more limited than what you will find on U.S. and Gulf carriers.

Industry observers note that even so, my children had no trouble finding enough content to keep themselves occupied before sleeping…that is ultimately what mattered most on this flight. Headphones were provided, though passengers who care about sound quality should bring their own (as my children did).

That is increasingly difficult to excuse on an intercontinental aircraft in 2026. I can prepare for a flight without connectivity, and I had done so here, but an 11.5-hour journey represents a substantial portion of the working day and it’s crazy that Austrian hasn’t even installed slow Wi-Fi while its competitors are offering Starlink.

Austrian will eventually replace its aging Boeing 777 fleet with Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners, but until then, passengers on these aircraft should proposal to remain offline throughout the flight and Austrian just unveiled these 777s will remain until the end of 2029 or early 20230.

Service was efficient and professional throughout the flight.

Sources indicate that the crew completed multiple meal and beverage services, distributed water during the journey, and collected trays promptly. While not especially warm or memorable, service competent and orderly.

With a full economy class cabin and several meal services to complete, the flight attendants worked continuously. Our interactions were brief, but everything we needed was provided…no complaints.

My children were offered colored pencils, paper, and a matching game.

As I eluded to above, the greatest problem on this flight was the temperature.

Industry observers note that the cabin remained oppressively warm for most of the flight. I find Lufthansa Group carriers frequently keep their cabins warmer than I prefer, but this flight was particularly uncomfortable. Passengers who feel cold can put on another layer or use the supplied blanket. There is very little someone who feels overheated can do while sitting in a crowded economy cabin….

It has been revealed that even if I had found the seat more comfortable, I doubt I would have slept in that heat. My children somehow slept for about eight hours, but I remained awake for virtually the entire flight.

The Austrian 777 has five lavatories for economy class (also used by premium economy class) including three between the front and rear economy class cabins and two more in the rear.

In a recent development, my first longhaul Austrian Airlines economy class flight was a mixed experience. The airline served a generous amount of food, the crew operated efficiently, and my children remained entertained before sleeping through most of the journey. The fare was also excellent, and the nonstop flight from Los Angeles to Vienna allowed us to enjoy a 23-hour stopover in one of my favorite cities.

But the seat was uncomfortable, legroom was tight, the cabin was far too warm, the entertainment selection was limited, and there was no Wi-Fi. The powdered coffee and unpleasant breakfast did not help.

Recent findings suggest that I am very glad I tried Austrian Airlines economy class, but I would not be eager to repeat an 11.5-hour journey on this aircraft. My children might disagree…they slept better than I ever do in business class. Even so, there are much better economy class products including on United Airlines.
