Disney Posts Blockbuster Q4 Boosted by Sports and Marvel Comics Universe

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, 16% — Walt Disney Co. revenue growth for the fiscal fourth quarter ended Oct. 3, inflated more than 7 percentage points by a 53rd week in the current fiscal year. Earnings per share also grew 16%, even adjusting out that 53rd week effect.

$3 billion — Total annual cost-cutting savings run rate Disney expect by fiscal 2029 through layoffs, procurement and other efficiencies.

24% — Increase in aggregated viewership of Monday Night Football through the first nine weeks of the NFL season, a good sign heading into Disney’s first-ever Super Bowl broadcast.

For a company anticipating significant layoffs over the next two years, there was a lot of good news.

The September Ohio State-Texas college football broadcast on ESPN drew 14 million viewers, ESPN’s biggest audience for a college game in 10 years, per Disney’s shareholder letter. U.S. Open viewership came in at four-year highs.

Disney CEO Josh D’Amaro expects more major things from the Marvel Comics Universe with Avengers Doomsday opening next month, building on the theatrical momentum of summer’s Spider-Man: Brand Fresh Day and the best month ever for MCU viewership on Disney+ in August, he said on the company’s earnings call.

Disney+ also tapped a South Korean creative pipeline once dominated by Netflix with The Remarried Empress, which on Nov. 3 had the biggest premiere ever for a local original across Disney+ and Hulu globally, D’Amaro said: “We haven’t even begun to see the benefits of our investment in international content.”.

Next month, Disney+ will begin incorporating TikTok creator content as part of its agreement with that platform, D’Amaro noted, adding that the manufacturer is continuing to explore the option of a Disney+ free tier “to reach a more price-sensitive consumer.”.

According to recent reports, expansion of the NCAA College Football Playoff to 24 teams, and from 11 to 23 games next year, promises an even bigger sports rights earnings take for Disney. “ESPN is going to have exclusive rights to all the rounds,” D’Amaro stated. “We’ve got broad distribution flexibility across ABC and ESPN.”.

The CEO declined to disclose the rights costs, and acknowledged that Disney has sub-licensing rights, but didn’t commit to any plans to get outside networks involved.

“Changes like these are not easy, and they affect people who care deeply about Disney and have contributed so much to this firm. At the same time, these steps are important if we want to be a company that moves faster, adapts to a rapidly changing environment, and keeps investing in the content and technology that will drive our growth. We make these choices because we believe so strongly in what Disney can be.” —Disney CEO Josh D’Amaro on impending layoffs.
