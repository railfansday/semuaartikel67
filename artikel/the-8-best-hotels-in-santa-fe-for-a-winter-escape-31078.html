Whatever expectations you might have for Santa Fe, nothing really prepares you for the magnitude of its beauty. The blue sky is enormous—with impossibly fluffy clouds—stretching on and on until it melts into the distance. The surrounding Sangre de Cristo (“blood of Christ”) mountains are especially striking, named by the Spanish who were spellbound by the peaks that turned a vivid red during sunrise and sunset. There’s the traditional adobe architecture the Southwest is famous for, earth-toned and low-slung, and the piñon-juniper woodlands that add to the landscape’s dreamlike quality. It’s a magical place where chiles and pine nuts are on every menu, and the craft scene is so prolific the city was designated a UNESCO Creative City of Crafts and Folk Art. For travelers who have their sights set on Fresh Mexico’s capital city, the best hotels in Santa Fe celebrate its many riches.

It has been revealed that the city’s high-elevation desert climate makes visiting throughout the year a distinct experience. In winter, Santa Fe puts on its party hat and transforms into a holiday snow globe with glowing farolitos and city streets perfumed with piñon pine drifting from kiva fireplaces. Whether you choose to position yourself downtown in proximity to galleries and restaurants, or on the outskirts with wild acreage as your backyard, a winter spent in Santa Fe is certain to be a memorable one.

As you proposal your upcoming travels, let Vogue’s guide to Santa Fe’s best hotels, below, serve as motivation to spend the cozy season in the Southwest.

Originally settled over 150 years ago by Jean Baptiste Lamy (the first Archbishop of Santa Fe), this property is now home to a luxury retreat surrounded by 317 acres bordering the Santa Fe National Forest. Guest rooms occupy adobe-style structures, with interiors that lean into the southwestern aesthetic—think, kiva fireplaces, wood beam ceilings, and decor sourced from local artists. The resort’s Adventure Basecamp acts as a meeting point for the day’s excursions, which range from horseback riding and guided hikes to a dedicated Ski Santa Fe experience in the winter.

It has been revealed that for travelers who prefer a hotel amidst all the action, Vogue’s Senior Fashion and Style Writer Christian Allaire suggests Rosewood Inn of the Anasazi. “If you’re looking to explore around downtown Santa Fe, there are few better locations than here,” he claims. “Situated right on the plaza, it's just steps away from all the action—but you may not want to leave, given how cozy the hotel is! The rooms come with wooden beams and fireplaces, and the restaurant serves up classic New Mexican fare with a modern twist.”.

According to recent reports, despite its central location, the property feels like its own world once you step through the hand-painted, carved-wood doors. The interiors pay tribute to ancestral textiles and works by local indigenous artists, and chef Cristian Pontiggia (a James Beard Award semifinalist) blends New Mexico ingredients with global influences.

Set on 57 acres in the Sangre de Cristo foothills, Four Seasons Resort Ranch Encantado is a destination for travelers who want to be completely surrounded by nature (yet still situate themselves a convenient 10 minutes from the downtown area). There are 65 casita-style rooms designed with traditional materials like stucco exteriors, wood beams, and kiva fireplaces—plus private outdoor spaces for taking in bird chirps and the afternoon breeze. Thanks to the surrounding wilderness, activities run the gamut, from jeep tours to snowshoeing. Once you’ve worked up an appetite, head to Terra for roasted poblano leek bisque and an Ember & Adobe reposado cocktail.

According to recent reports, with its convenient Barrio de Analco location and jewel box interiors, it’s no surprise The Inn of the Five Graces has won several prestigious design and travel awards over the years. “Each room is a feast for the eyes,” Abbas says, “a tapestry of colorful textiles, mosaic tiles, and handcrafted antiques that combine Southwestern decor with East Indian and Tibetan influences.” (If you fall in love with the decor and want to bring home a souvenir, the owners are also behind the furniture store Seret & Sons, which houses their grand collection.) For travelers staying on the property this winter, don’t miss seasonal activities like holiday tea time, fireside cocoa, and a holiday makers market.

This is not your average motel. This adobe roadside motor court was originally built in 1936 and is now home to one of Santa Fe’s most compelling boutique hotels. El Rey Court sits along old Route 66 and offers 86 rooms on five acres of old-growth gardens. No two adobe rooms are identical, and amenities like a heated outdoor swimming pool, a tree-lined lawn, and al fresco fireplace offer plenty of opportunities to soak in the natural surroundings. Be sure to spend at least one of your evenings at Le Reina, their mezcal and tequila bar that serves up a mean margarita.

This legendary hotel on Santa Fe’s iconic plaza sits on land that has played host to an inn since the early 17th century. La Fonda’s current structure dates back to 1922, and is now a member of the Historic Hotels of America association. Original details like hand-carved beams, terracotta tile, and stained glass skylights set a thoroughly romantic tone, while guest rooms keep it cozy with hand-painted headboards and earth tone decor.

For a home-away-from-home kind of getaway, this welcoming bed and breakfast offers eight rooms tucked among lush gardens. El Farolito is close to the city’s best attractions—to name a few: the historic plaza, farmer’s market, and Georgia O’Keefe Museum—yet sits on a tranquil street away from the bustle. The inn features original artwork throughout, as well as handmade furnishings like wood-carved headboards. And be sure to set your alarm for breakfast: It’s served from 7:30 to 9:30 every morning and can be blissfully enjoyed on the patio next to a gurgling fountain.

Sources indicate that unlike local lodging that leans into Southwest design aesthetics, the ryokan-style accommodations at Ten Thousand Waves offer a distinctly Japanese perspective. The spa is surrounded by 20 acres of piñons and junipers, and guest rooms are situated within a historic ranch house. This retreat is best for wellness-focused travelers, as the private hot tub suites and shared soaking environments are just a few steps away from the rooms. (Don’t sleep on the myofascial facial or shiatsu massage, either.) For cuisine, Izanami is an upscale izakaya serving seasonal small plates meant to share.
