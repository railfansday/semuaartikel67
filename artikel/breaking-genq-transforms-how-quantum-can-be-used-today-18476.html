LOS ANGELES, Nov. 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- For decades, practical, scalable applications of quantum technology have been purely aspirational. Now, Generation Quantum ("GenQ") is taking a different approach to create quantum capabilities that work today.

It has been revealed that "GenQ completely changed the way I think about quantum computing and its near-term potential," states Valentina Rodriguez of Wonder Ventures, who co-led GenQ's recent funding round. "Since I first saw their tech, I've been amazed by their ability to take incredibly advanced technology and apply it to practical, everyday settings.".

Dr. Haley Weinstein, founder of GenQ, brings a distinct perspective to quantum innovation. Before completing a PhD at the University of Southern California, Weinstein worked on intersatellite laser communications at SpaceX. There, she saw the potential for integrating quantum technology with preexisting classical infrastructure.

According to recent reports, "GenQ has proven that quantum is more than computing," says Yuri Namikawa of Offline Ventures, who co-led GenQ's recent funding round. "Their technology has already outperformed classical monitoring, turning the challenges of quantum physics into real benefits for everyday infrastructure. We see applications across government, commercial networks, and the future quantum internet.".

Weinstein founded GenQ on the principle that practical quantum technology is not only scientifically feasible, but commercially viable.

"GenQ has transitioned the implementation of quantum measurement into compact, robust, room temperature products," indicates Dr. Jonathan Habif, who leads the Quantum Limited Information Lab (QLILab) at USC. "The result is a revolutionary capability for commercial and government applications operating at the fundamental quantum limit of sensitivity.".

To bring this vision to light, Weinstein built a team at GenQ that merges theoretical quantum physics with a pragmatic engineering mindset, resulting in quantum solutions that can be readily deployed across private and public sectors. The GenQ team draws on experience from the NSA, Boeing, and USC's Quantum Information Science program to build quantum-augmented capabilities leveraging technology already in widespread use, such as quantum measurement deployed for optical network monitoring.

In a recent development, "What immediately excited us about GenQ was the rare combination of truly bleeding-edge quantum technology and the ability to commercialize it today," adds Jonathan Lacoste, General Partner at Ambition Capital and pre-seed investor for GenQ. "So much of quantum is defined by the promise of what might be possible a decade from now, often requiring years of speculative R&D. GenQ is challenging that narrative, demonstrating that quantum can deliver meaningful, real-world impact today.".
