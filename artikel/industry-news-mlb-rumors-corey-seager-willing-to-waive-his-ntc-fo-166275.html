Industry News: MLB rumors: Corey Seager willing to waive his NTC for Phillies, plus news on Mets, Nationals, Yankees, more

The uncertain 2026-27 Major League Baseball offseason is fully underway. The ongoing CBA negotiations and the near certainty of an owner-implemented lockout on Dec. 2 loom over all, and it may indeed mean a flurry of trades and free agent signings leading up to that date. That was certainly the case leading up to the last lockout of 2021-22, and this time around we've already seen some leading examples. Just recently, for instance, the Upcoming York Mets landed the biggest fish in the pond by signing ace Tarik Skubal to a record contract. Not long after, the Chicago Cubs fortified their rotation with Trevor Rogers and their outfield with Teruaki Sato, and Freddy Peralta is headed to the Baltimore Orioles. More's surely to come before lockout day arrives.

Thanks to that prospect of a hastened pace, offseason rumors are flowing. Speaking of which, we'll be here each day to round up what's out there. You can find Tuesday's supply just below.

Free agent catcher Tyler Stephenson has agreed to a three-year contract to return to the Reds, reports ESPN. The deal is worth $30 million and includes an opt-out after the second year. Cincinnati selected Stephenson, 30, with the No. 11 pick in the 2015 draft, and he's played his entire career with the Reds. We ranked him as the 46th-best free agent available.

The Reds will again pair Stephenson with glove-first backup Jose Trevino behind the plate. Cincinnati now figures to turn their attention to the pitching staff, both the rotation and bullpen. Hunter Greene's UCL surgery means Andrew Abbott and Chase Burns are the only locks for the rotation, though Nick Lodolo will have a spot as long as he's healthy.

Recent findings suggest that the Yankees are among the many teams with interest in top free agent reliever Adrian Morejon, reports the New York Post. Some teams view him as a potential starter, though there were reports earlier this month that he prefers to stay in the bullpen. The Phillies, meanwhile, are not considered a serious candidate to sign Morejon, according to The Athletic. We ranked him as the second-best free agent available this offseason, in part due to his potential to start.

Adding to the bullpen has been the Yankees' primary focus so far this offseason. Closer David Bednar accepted the qualifying offer, they re-signed lefty matchup guy Tim Hill, and signed Jonathan Loáisiga and Tim Hill as free agents. At some point they will need to add to their lineup, particularly at third base, but there's always room for a reliever like Morejon in the bullpen.

The Philadelphia Phillies are open to moving Trea Turner off of shortstop given that he's now a pronounced defensive liability. Speaking of openness, veteran shortstop Corey Seager of the Texas Rangers is willing to waive his no-trade clause to join the Phillies and upgrade the position for them, NBC Sports Philadelphia reports.

It has been revealed that now 32, Seager will always have health and durability concerns, but he remains a plus defensive shortstop who produces well at the plate by positional standards. Signed to a 10-year, $325 million deal prior to the 2022 season (just before the last lockout, in fact), Seager is owed another $155 million through the 2031 season. The Rangers are reportedly looking to shake things up. The Phillies, meantime, are trying to keep their contention window open. It's a fit both ways, at least in theory. Such a move would likely mean a move to the outfield for Turner, who himself has very little trade value thanks to his ongoing decline and substantial contract.

Even in a down 2026 year, Jazz Chisholm Jr. put up 19 homers and stole 41 bases while manning an up-the-middle position. Still just 28 years of age, he figures to have a healthy market this offseason. Speaking of which, the Washington Nationals are one of multiple teams presently in contact with Chisholm, FanSided reports.

Sources indicate that earlier in the offseason, CBS Sports ranked Chisholm as the No. 6 free agent in the current class. Here's part of that write-up:.

Industry observers note that "In what was his worst season as a hitter since his rookie year in 2021, Chisholm was still a league-average bat in 2026, and his top-shelf defense and baserunning still landed him at 3.5 WAR. Chisholm is a style-over-substance player who is not for everyone, but his second base defense and near-MLB-best baserunning skills give him a very high floor. He's a reasonable bet for 20-ish home runs with the potential to go 30-30, and Chisholm has improved his plate discipline enough to run walk rates north of 10% the last two years. The downside is his propensity to swing and miss. Chisholm has posted below-average in-zone contact rates throughout his career and contact rates that low always come with risk. That risk is mitigated somewhat by the secondary skills (defense, etc.) and the fact that Chisholm will play the entire 2027 season at age 29. You probably don't want him as your best or second-best player, but he's also an overqualified No. 6-7 hitter who brings so much else to the table.".

Sources indicate that the Cleveland Guardians have agreed to a free-agent contract with left-hander Kris Bubic, Sportsnet reports. The contract is for $17.5 million over one year, according to ESPN. Bubic, 29, made nine starts for the Kansas City Royals last season, and after the trade to the Los Angeles Dodgers, he was used out of the bullpen. Bubic has an outlier fastball thanks to its rise and his unusual release point, and he's an interesting "ceiling" candidate in the rotation. However, his substantial history of injuries may mean he's best used out of the bullpen.

Sources indicate that the Detroit Tigers have added infield depth by agreeing to terms with Ha-Seong Kim on a one-year, $5 million free agent contract, ESPN reports. Kim, 31, is coming off a rough partial season with the Atlanta Braves, but he has a more palatable OPS+ of 91 for his career. He's been a primary shortstop in recent years and also has significant time spent at second base and third base.

In a recent development, even after achieving a reunion with veteran starting pitcher Sonny Gray, the Minnesota Twins are still looking to fortify their roster for a return to contention in 2027. The club still hopes to make one more significant addition this offseason and add another player who may not grab as many headlines, the Athletic reports. Atop the wishlist are a first baseman or a second baseman and another lefty reliever.

Even after the splash addition of Skubal, the Mets may not be done adding starting pitchers in the service of a rebound season in 2027. On that front, the Mets may be interested in adding a starting pitcher on a short-term deal for next season, the Athletic reports. That would likely limit them to the back half of the market, especially with so many bigger names reaching agreements.
