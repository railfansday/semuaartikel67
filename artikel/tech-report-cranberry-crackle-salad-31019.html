In a recent development, cranberry crackle salad combines crunchy and creamy textures with sweet and tangy flavors. It's a retro-style dessert with a hearty dose of seasonal cheer.

Industry observers note that I live in cranberry country (aka northern Wisconsin), and when fresh cranberries hit store shelves, there’s a good chance you’ll find me in the kitchen baking with them. One sweet treat that often finds its way onto our holiday brunch menus is a loaf (or three) of cranberry-orange bread. There’s just something so cozy about the combination of tart cranberries and sweet, bright orange juice—the inspiration for this cranberry crackle salad recipe.

According to recent reports, crackle salad is a variation on retro fluff salad that combines cream cheese, whipped topping and fruit mix-ins with a crunchy, candied “crackle” topping (made with crushed pretzels, chopped pecans, brown sugar and melted butter). In this holiday-inspired crackle salad, the cream mixture is flavored with whole-berry cranberry sauce and orange zest. Fresh orange juice in the crackle adds a subtle citrus flavor, too. This recipe makes enough for a small crowd, and is perfect for sharing at a Thanksgiving potluck or as a fun throwback side dish at holiday gatherings.

According to recent reports, preheat the oven to 400°F. Line a 15x10x1-inch rimmed baking sheet with parchment paper or a silicone baking mat.

It has been revealed that in a large bowl, stir together the broken pretzels, chopped pecans and brown sugar. Stir in the melted butter and orange juice to coat.

It has been revealed that spread the pretzel mixture in an even layer on the prepared baking sheet. Bake it for five to eight minutes or until it’s bubbly. Let it cool completely on the baking sheet before breaking it into pieces.

Editor’s Tip: Keep your eye on the crackle as it bakes; it can easily burn if baked too long.

Sources indicate that in a large bowl, beat together the softened cream cheese and vanilla. Stir in the cranberry sauce until it’s combined. Gently fold in the whipped topping until it’s incorporated.

Industry observers note that fold in the orange zest. Cover the bowl and refrigerate the mixture for at least one hour.

Sources indicate that just before serving, stir in half of the pretzel mixture. Top the salad with the remaining pretzel mixture to serve. If you like, add sugared cranberries as an additional garnish.

Editor’s Tip: Avoid adding the crunch to the salad too early, as it will soften up over time.

Leftover cranberry crackle salad should be stored in an airtight container in the fridge. It will last for up to three days in the fridge. The crackle will soften over time once added to the salad.

Sources indicate that any extra crackle topping should be stored in an airtight container at room temperature to keep it crunchy. It should keep for up to one week.

You can make the cream mixture and crackle up to two day in advance. Store the salad and crackle separately, and don’t combine them until just before serving the salad.

If you want to dial up the crunch factor even more, allow the baked crackle to cool thoroughly before using it. This will ensure it has the crunchiest texture.

Sources indicate that fresh cranberries are a great option when they are in season. To make this cranberry crackle salad with fresh cranberries, you’ll need a 12-ounce package of whole cranberries. (You could also use frozen whole cranberries, but they need to thaw first.).

Sources indicate that in a food processor or blender, pulse the berries until they’re finely chopped. Pour the berries into a large bowl and stir in 1/2 cup of granulated sugar. Cover and refrigerate them for at least one hour or overnight. Use the fresh berry mixture in place of the canned cranberry sauce in this recipe.

This cranberry crackle salad will fit right in with any holiday potluck spread. It’s also a fun retro dessert salad to share at a holiday meal alongside classic savory offerings such as roasted turkey, mashed potatoes and green bean casserole.
