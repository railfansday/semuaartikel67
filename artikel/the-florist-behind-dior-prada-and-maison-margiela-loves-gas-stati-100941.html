In a recent development, despite creating elaborate floral arrangements for the crème de la crème of luxury fashion, Mark Colle prefers a humbler bouquet to gift. “If I give flowers to someone, I buy them at the gas station,” he says plainly. “They have a trashy charm to them that I appreciate.”.

It has been revealed that that irreverent sensibility sits at the heart of the Belgian florist’s métier: Flowers should shock and delight—and sometimes make you squirm. “I want people to be surprised most of all, which, with social media and Instagram, is becoming more difficult,” he claims. Take his collaboration with Glenn Martens for Maison Margiela’s spring/summer 2027 runway show. It was the punkier answer to Colle’s arrangements for Raf Simons’s Dior couture debut in 2012, when he transformed the rooms of a Paris hôtel particulier with a million blooms.

For Margiela, he lined seven rooms with flowers at different stages of decay, with the blooms left to show their age. “I start with color, [that] is always the most notable to me,” he notes. “Dior, for example. You have red, pink, and gray. But it’s a very specific red and a very specific pink. It’s maybe not so much about flower, but more about the color the flower has.”.

Sources indicate that it takes a sure hand to make flowers feel at home in a space—whether that’s a runway, a grand gala, or your dining room table—something Colle knows well. For arrangements, he looks to flowers with personality, like goldenrod, a bright, textural accent, or dahlias, whose many different forms can set very different moods. Colle’s anything-goes floral work has caught the attention of a who’s who of fashion heavyweights, from Saint Laurent and Chloé to Louis Vuitton and Prada, whose co-creative director Raf Simons first crossed paths with him while shopping for plants at his Antwerp shop, Baltimore Bloemen.

Here, Colle talks about choosing flowers, creating a mood, and finding inspiration in the unexpected.

Sources indicate that dahlias. They come in so many different shapes and colors. You can use them for something that resembles a medieval painting, but you could also use them for something that needs to look very futuristic, because there are certain typesthat have super-colorful spikes. But for me, it can be any flower, really. There is nothing that I consider ugly or unusable. It depends on the context or how you use it.

Nobody ever gives me flowers. But if I give flowers to somebody, I honestly buy them at the gas station, because they have a trashy charm to them that I really appreciate. And also, it’s not what people expect to receive from me flower-wise.

Goldenrod, which over here is associated with something you would find in a gas station bouquet, so to speak, because it has no presence at all. But for me, that makes it interesting that you can use this common flower that people even almost look down upon into something very chic. That’s sometimes a nice challenge for me.
