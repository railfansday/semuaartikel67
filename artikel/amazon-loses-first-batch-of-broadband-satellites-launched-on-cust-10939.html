Amazon loses first batch of broadband satellites launched on custom rocket

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

United Launch Alliance’s Vulcan LEO didn’t reach orbit.

Amazon’s ambition to operate a constellation of broadband satellites took a hit on Sunday, when 40 of its craft failed to make it into orbit.

According to recent reports, the failure is significant because it was the first Amazon launch to use a rocket designed especially for the job of carrying its broadband birds to low Earth orbit. The Lockheed/Boeing joint venture United Launch Alliance (ULA) created that rocket, a version of its Vulcan heavy lifter using a six-booster configuration and a Centaur V upper stage optimized to carry payloads into low Earth orbit.

Last week, Amazon previewed the launch with a post that noted this mission, code-named LV-01, is the first of 38 planned missions with ULA, and that LV-02 would likely follow within weeks.

In a recent development, the two companies planned to test newly introduced hardware on the first “several” flights using the custom rocket. Amazon put 40 satellites aboard the Sunday mission and noted it is “already planning larger configurations for subsequent missions.”.

Those plans, and the LV-02 launch, are probably on hold after the rocket suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight.

The flight began well, with the Vulcan reaching an altitude of 95 miles (150 km) and completing the first phase of its flight.

Six minutes into the mission, the main booster and payload fairing both separated as planned, and ULA lit up the Centaur V’s engines for the first planned firings needed to reach low Earth orbit.

Then came the anomaly, which UAL’s live patch stream states meant the mission “failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit.”.

“Every flight generates data, and every flight teaches us something,” UAL wrote. “Since the anomaly just occurred, we don’t have any specific data yet to understand what occurred today.”.

Amazon already has over 200 satellites in orbit, had hoped to launch its satellite broadband service this year, and plans 20 or more debuts in 2027.

It has been revealed that it is unclear if this mission failure will change those ambitions. ®.
