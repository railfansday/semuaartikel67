Jurors found National Indemnity complied with its obligations under the reinsurance certificate and proved its breach of contract claim against TIG. They awarded National Indemnity $39.2 million in indemnity, $3.2 million in prejudgment interest, $21.1 million in postjudgment interest and $16.1 million in expenses.

They declined to award National Indemnity any future defense costs.

Attorneys for both sides declined to comment after the verdict was read.

National Indemnity Insurance Company turned to TIG Insurance Company and other reinsurers after agreeing in 2022, with Montana, to resolve all coverage claims over asbestos exposure from the mine since the 1960s for $157 million.

National Indemnity insured the state from July 1, 1973, to July 1, 1975; TIG provided the firm’s reinsurance at that time.

TIG claimed National Indemnity did not offer adequate evidence and billed amounts not covered by the reinsurance agreement, essentially finagling the numbers to make TIG cover part of the massive bill.

In 2004, the state of Montana was found liable for asbestos risk to miners and residents near Libby, a small city in the state’s northwest.

Recent findings suggest that from 1963 to 1990, W.R. Grace & Co. extracted vermiculite at the mine, about 7 miles outside of Libby. Vermiculite, once commonly used for fireproof roofing, insulation and wallpaper, is not dangerous. But the asbestos the mining stirred up was.

Decades of widespread asbestos contamination from the mine caused the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency to declare its first-ever public health emergency during a 2009 Superfund cleanup.

Sources indicate that thousands of W.R. Grace workers, their families and residents since the 1960s have been injured or died from lung disease related to working at or living near the mine.

Recent findings suggest that the fight over who should be held liable started about 2000, shortly after reports of workers falling ill sparked an emergency cleanup. Contamination has since been blamed for hundreds of deaths.

It has been revealed that according to National Indemnity, the insurance contract required National Indemnity to pay as much as $3 million per loss, with unlimited occurrences. At first, the state said 21 occurrences involved state inspectors not warning miners about the asbestos risk.

But National Indemnity feared that, with potentially thousands of miners and locals affected by asbestos exposure, the number of occurrences could multiply as the matter moved again through Montana courts, with the state Supreme Court telling the parties to litigate the number of occurrences.

Ultimately, National Indemnity and the state settled at 27 occurrences. At $3 million apiece, the bill came to $81 million. The roughly $76 million remaining largely came from past and present defense costs.
