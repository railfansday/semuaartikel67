4 takeaways from the Chargers' 23-20 win over the Ravens

Four lessons from the Chargers' first win of 2026 in Baltimore, from a late forced fumble to a run defense that held the Ravens to 16 yards.

A team that keeps losing has to stop losing before anything else about it can be judged fairly. For the Chargers, the losing outlasted an entire offseason. Their last win before Monday came against the Cowboys in Week 16 of 2025, according to NBC Sports. The drought spanned two seasons, not just a bad start to one. Ending it was a genuine release for a team that had gone most of a calendar year without a victory. The relief also makes it tempting to treat everything that worked on that one night as the start of a turnaround, and that temptation is worth resisting.

Sources indicate that the team across the field was struggling, too. The game was Baltimore's fifth straight loss, according to NBC Sports, and ESPN reports that it dropped the Ravens to 4-6. Monday night matched two slumping teams rather than handing the Chargers an upset of a contender. Baltimore was also without its star quarterback. Lamar Jackson was out with a right thumb injury, according to ESPN, and Jacoby Brissett played his first game for the Ravens after Baltimore acquired him in a trade. The Chargers' defense faced a quarterback who was still new to his offense. The win counts in the standings, but it came in a softer test than a typical Monday night in the NFL. The useful question for each lesson from the game is whether it would hold up against a healthier, sharper opponent.

Industry observers note that the Los Angeles Chargers set out on the team's website to draw the key lessons from the Monday night win over Baltimore, from a defense that shut down the Ravens' running game to a late forced fumble and a ground attack that the offense relied on. Each of those lessons holds true for that one night. They differ in how much of each result the Chargers earned on their own and how much came from the condition of the team they played. Some rest on choices the coaching staff can make again next week, while others depended on what Baltimore could and couldn't do. The Chargers get their first chance to find out promptly, when they host the Jets at SoFi Stadium in Week 11.

The Chargers led the Ravens from start to finish on Monday night, according to NBC Sports, and still had to hang on for their first win of the season. A road win in Baltimore in prime time would usually point to a team that finally took control of a game. The Chargers instead won 23-20, according to chargers.com, and finished only one score ahead of an opponent that never once held the lead. They pulled ahead by double digits twice in the second half, and Baltimore kept closing the gap after each of those leads. The close finish did not come from a stronger team rallying late. It came from the Chargers failing to put away an opponent they were already beating.

Chargers head coach Jim Harbaugh credited his offense, defense and special teams alike for the win, but Baltimore moved the ball better than his team did. The Ravens gained 347 yards to the Chargers' 287, according to NBC Sports, and the Chargers' offense did not carry the night. The defense helped make up for that gap by forcing two turnovers. Takeaways tend to rise and fall from week to week, while a yardage edge is a steadier sign of which team controlled play. The Chargers won without that steadier sign on their side. After eight straight losses, the result mattered more than the way the Chargers reached it. Whether a win built this way can hold up is the question the rest of the night's lessons have to answer.

The Chargers' first win of the season came down to one Baltimore possession, and their defense ended it by taking the ball away. Baltimore had the ball on its final drive with the Chargers up three, according to chargers.com. ESPN reports that Zay Flowers lost the ball with 2:03 left, after cornerback Tarheeb Still kept working for it following the catch and pulled it free. "That was huge. Tarheeb was able — the ball come away from the body, and he was right there to claw it out," Chargers coach Jim Harbaugh told ESPN. With the ball back, the Chargers' offense no longer needed to score. It only needed to keep possession, and Justin Herbert's pass to Charlie Kolar on the next play gave the Chargers the first down they needed to run out the clock.

The defense had to make that play because the offense had stopped scoring touchdowns after halftime. The Chargers managed only a field goal in each of the third and fourth quarters, according to ESPN, so the offense added nothing in the second half that could put the game out of reach. Baltimore's passing game was carrying its offense, with Jacoby Brissett finishing the night at 340 yards, and Flowers had just caught one of his throws when Still stripped him. Still took the ball away from the part of Baltimore's offense that had been working, at the last moment when Baltimore could still tie or win the game.

A single forced fumble, however well timed, does not yet make a pattern. Chargers safety Tony Jefferson indicates the defense discusses going after the ball every day and treats it as a habit to keep repeating. The coming weeks will show whether the defense takes the ball away from offenses that are moving it well. Until then, Still's strip saved one win but has not shown that this defense can produce more of them.

Industry observers note that A team trying to finish off its first win of the season needs a way to move the ball without giving it back, and the Chargers chose to run it. They ran for 160 yards at Baltimore, a total they had not reached in any earlier game this season, according to chargers.com. The offense also played its most careful game of the year and finished without a turnover. USA TODAY reports that it was Justin Herbert's first game of 2026 without an interception. By running the ball, the Chargers kept moving it without putting it at risk, which is what a winless team holding a lead needed most.

The big rushing night came from a decision about who would carry the ball. Mike McDaniel, who calls the Chargers' plays, stopped splitting carries among his running backs for this game, USA TODAY reports, and Omarion Hampton answered with the team's first 100-yard rushing game of 2026. Hampton finished with 105 yards on the full workload of a featured back. His longest run went only 19 yards, so he built his total from steady gains, not from a single breakaway. "The downhill runs were extremely good," Chargers head coach Jim Harbaugh remarked. Runs like those depend on how the staff uses Hampton, not on luck, and the coaches can call them again as long as they keep giving him the ball.

Hampton's touchdown followed an interception of Jacoby Brissett that the Chargers turned into points, according to USA TODAY. The defense helped set up that score as much as the runners did, so the running game's clearest value on Monday was control and safety.

This lesson depends least on Baltimore's condition, because it rests on a choice the Chargers make for themselves.

Baltimore had no running game to fall back on at any point in the night. The Ravens finished with 16 rushing yards and gained none before halftime, the Chargers' best run defense of the season, according to chargers.com. That left Baltimore with an offense that could only throw. Stopping the run was the Chargers' plan from the opening snaps, and it worked before Baltimore had any chance to wear the defense down. "There was a heck of a run wall, big emphasis on that," Chargers head coach Jim Harbaugh mentioned. The Chargers also stopped the back Baltimore trusted most. Derrick Henry gained 10 yards on 10 carries, according to ESPN, so Baltimore could not keep handing him the ball until he found room.

It has been revealed that the defenders who stopped the run also reached the quarterback when Baltimore threw. Jacoby Brissett faced 23 pressures from the Chargers, according to ESPN, on a night when his team could give him almost no help on the ground. Together, those results point to a Chargers front that won at the line of scrimmage on running plays and passing plays alike.

According to recent reports, one strong night does not yet show a lasting change. Holding Baltimore to 16 yards stands out against every earlier game this season, and a single game that stands out that much is hard to treat as the new standard. Harbaugh's emphasis on the run suggests the Chargers can bring the same effort again. They have not yet shown they can get the same result against other offenses.
