Rangers host Aberdeen in one of two Scottish Premiership games on Tuesday.

One between two sides looking to recover from embarrassing home defeats, another a match-up between two teams looking to build on decent away victories.

All four looking to make their game in hand count in Tuesday's Scottish Premiership double-header.

So what's to look out for as second-top Rangers host Aberdeen and second-bottom Hibernian entertain Dundee?

Just over a fortnight ago, a Rangers win over Aberdeen at Ibrox on Tuesday would have seemed a surefire bet.

The Dons went into the international break on the back of a 4-0 thumping at home to a Hibernian side whose victory lifted them above their hosts and left Stephen Robinson's side sitting in second-bottom place.

With his team only two points clear of bottom-placed Kilmarnock, and with the fans having failed to take the Northern Irishman to their heart, the Dons acted immediately to end his eight-month spell in charge.

They acted similarly swiftly to appoint former Luzern head coach Mario Frick as the fresh man in charge and his promise of "heavy metal football" immediately got a tune out of his players as they blew away Kilmarnock at Rugby Park to end a dismal run of four straight defeats.

Whether or not it was merely the traditional "upcoming manager bounce", only time will tell, but they will certainly be heading to Glasgow with more of a spring in their step after a win that moved them up to eighth.

Given Rangers' two victories over Aberdeen this season, once in the league at Pittodrie and most recently in the League Cup semi-final at Hampden, have been by a single goal, will that renewed confidence and belief from that 4-2 win in Ayrshire be enough to tip the balance of power?

Certainly, everything else points to a home win.

In recent times, Rangers have had a tendency to pass up chances to put pressure on Celtic - and this time a win would take them to within a point of the leaders and reigning champions.

However, Saturday's late winner away to Motherwell suggests the team that Derek McInnes has built is of more sturdy stuff.

That 2-1 win at Fir Park leaves them unbeaten in 13 matches and they are also eight unbeaten at home, having won seven in a row at Ibrox by an aggregate score of 18-2.

McInnes recognises Rangers have yet to dominate games the way he would like, but at least they now appear to have found a way to create the chances that were lacking early in his tenure.

Indeed, Rangers have now scored in every one of their last 27 games in the Premiership and at Fir Park conjured up 29 goal attempts - their highest total in a single league match this season.

To put a spanner in that increasingly well-oiled machine, the Dons will have to end a run of five straight defeats, by an aggregate of 16-2, at Ibrox since their last win there in September 2023.

Overall, they have lost their latest six meetings with the Ibrox side and, to make matters worse, McInnes has won all six of his Premiership home games against Aberdeen since leaving their manager's office, four with Kilmarnock and twice with Heart of Midlothian last season.

Hibs won at Dens Park in August.

Hibs were unable to back-up that aforementioned 4-0 win at Pittodrie as they suffered an identical home hammering against Dundee United on Saturday.

Recently appointed head coach Marink Reedijk admitted he was taken by surprise by what he stated was his side's inability to do the "brilliant basics" as he calls them.

A failure to win at home will not have come as a shock to Hibs fans considering they have watched them seven times in a row now at Easter Road without being able to celebrate a victory.

However, this third straight home defeat came on the back of a three-game unbeaten run away from Leith, including that League Cup semi-final derby win over Hearts.

Reedijk refused to concede that Hibs were suffering a home jinx, but he might reconsider if his side falter again on Tuesday considering visitors Dundee are reeling from an even more painful home battering.

Hearts' 8-0 win was the Dark Blues' heaviest-ever top-flight defeat at home and has left head coach Steven Pressley admitting that "at times, I've wanted to crawl under a rock and hide".

While the former Scotland centre-half preferred to shoulder the blame for letting a bit of complacency develop, he echoed his Dutch counterpart by stressing that his side paid for not doing the basics.

In his case, that meant an admission that his squad have a fragile mentality and are not good enough to compete without working their wee cotton socks off.

Pressley is massive on psychology, but how will his squad respond to such a public confession at a time when they are visiting a ground where they have not won in 20 visits since October 2001 under Ivano Bonetti?

Indeed, while Dundee are four places and six points above Tuesday's hosts, they have currently lost five of their last seven games on the road and, with Hibs having won 2-1 in the reverse fixture at Dens Park in August, are without a win in five meetings with the Edinburgh side.

To change that sorry trend and continue Hibs' home jinx, they will have to improve on Saturday's statistic of failing to even conjure up an attempt on target.
