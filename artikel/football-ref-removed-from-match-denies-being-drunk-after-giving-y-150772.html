A football referee has denied accusations of being drunk after being removed from a match at halftime following a bizarre incident in which he showed a yellow card to a goalkeeper nearly half the pitch away from a foul.

Syrian referee Hanna Hattab was officiating an AFC Champions League Two match between the United Arab Emirates’ Al Jazira and Iran’s Gol Gohar when his decision-making raised eyebrows.

In the 32nd minute, Hattab awarded a free kick following a foul by Al Jazira’s Felicio Milson and approached the winger with a yellow card in hand.

However, as reported by the Daily Mail, the referee appeared to receive a message through his earpiece before suddenly changing direction and walking toward Al Jazira goalkeeper Ersin Destanoglu.

Despite being nowhere near the foul, the stunned goalkeeper was shown a yellow card.

Hattab eventually overturned the decision after consulting his fourth official, while Milson avoided receiving what would have been his second yellow card of the match.

Gol Gohar manager Mehdi Rahmati was reportedly seen shouting “he’s drunk” at Hattab, while the club’s sporting director, Heydar Afsari, revealed the team had threatened to boycott the second half.

“The referee against Al Jazira was not in a normal state, and even Al Jazira’s players told us the same thing,” Afsari remarked.

Hattab was ultimately replaced by the fourth official at halftime, with Al Jazira winning 1-0 thanks to a goal from Anderson Talisca.

However, the referee has since denied the allegations and threatened legal action against those responsible for spreading them.

According to BBC Sport, Omani sports journalist Issa Said claimed there was a medical explanation for Hattab’s unusual behavior.

“He underwent a medical examination after the match and was not intoxicated, contrary to what has been circulating.”.

The Syrian Football Association has also backed Hattab, describing the allegations as “entirely unfounded” following an assessment of his health.

The association has requested medical and match officials’ reports from the AFC as part of its investigation, while Hattab has vowed to release a statement detailing exactly what happened.
