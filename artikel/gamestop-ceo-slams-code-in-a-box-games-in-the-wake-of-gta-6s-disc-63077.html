GameStop CEO slams 'code-in-a-box' games in the wake of GTA 6's discless release

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, ryan Cohen, CEO of GameStop - the self-proclaimed "world's largest retail gaming and trade-in destination" - is not happy with the emerging trend of games releasing without discs. Per the CEO, the industry's move away from physical games and towards a digital-only future is harmful to both consumers and businesses.

Recent findings suggest that in an interview with Bloomberg, Cohen explained: "When you buy a game, you should be able to sell it, trade it or lend it to a friend. Unquestionably, the decision to move to all digital has hurt consumers, and it’s also hurt GameStop.".

These comments come in the wake of GTA 6's launch. The game, expected to be the biggest video game launch of all time, is not shipping on discs at all. Even if you purchase a physical copy of the game, what you will actually take home is a 'code in a box' - giving you a code you can redeem on your console of choice that entitles you to a digital version of the game. GTA 6 is not the first game to do this, but it is the most high-profile. Similarly to discs, codes are also region-locked (at least on the PS5), meaning that the proliferation of codes as a format isn't even helping make games more readily available in certain markets.

Cohen notes he's been in conversation with "the obvious [publishers]" but that he's had little sway on their prepares. "Of course they don’t like [trade-ins] because removing a used copy eliminates an alternative to buying new," he told Bloomberg. "They're taking away competition.".

Though Cohen doesn't name GTA 6 specifically in the Bloomberg interview, it's clear that he's talking about Rockstar's megaton launch (he explicitly tweeted "GTA. On a disc. In a case. On your shelf. Like if you still want a physical copy" two days ago). The lack of trade-in potential is clearly harming his business; the Bloomberg article reveals pre-owned and refurbished products make up 22% of the GameStop's sales, down 32 percent from the same time a year ago.

In a recent development, of course, the proliferation of game-in-a-box products doesn't end with GTA 6. Sony plans to stop producing physical discs for fresh PlayStation games starting in January 2028, moving to a digital-only model despite pushback from consumers and certain developers and publishers. The outcry is doing little to influence Sony's decision, however.

Disc or no disc, GTA 6 is expected to cross $2bn in its opening weekend, and at the moment that is looking like a conservative figure, too. It's estimated the game has sold well over 19.05m units at the time of writing. Despite the astronomical sales, though, employees at Rockstar are still worried about layoffs. Additionally, a tribunal between the studio and fired IWBG union members (who were fired for apparent breaches of their NDAs) just concluded, though the verdict may not arrive for many weeks or months.
