Swiss club Luzern did not renew Mario Frick's contract this summer.

Mario Frick arrives at Aberdeen with the Pittodrie side sitting second bottom of the table and a game against relegation rivals Kilmarnock first up.

Lose that match and they fall to the foot of the Scottish Premiership. No pressure.

Apart from the obvious, what is it that the new head coach has to do to restore Aberdeen to previous glory days and avoid becoming yet another managerial failure at Pittodrie?

It has been revealed that okay, maybe this was obvious. But it's Aberdeen's clearest weakness this season.

They have the joint worst attacking record in the league, having only scored 12 goals and routinely looked completely short of confidence in front of goal.

Recent findings suggest that kevin Nisbet, the club captain, has 14 goals for the season. Nine of those came in the run to the Premier Sports Cup semi-final. Behind him, no-one has more than two.

According to recent reports, most concerning of all is that the Dons' have created the lowest xG (15.3) in the league so far. Not only are they not creating very many quality chances, they're struggling to take them.

Recent findings suggest that they have just 3.3 shots on target per match, with a shot conversion rate of just 7.3%. Both metrics are the worst in the league.

Only Falkirk have created fewer big chances (19 to Aberdeen's 22) - the difference being John McGlynn's side score them.

Getting the best out of Connor Ronan could be key for Mario Frick.

One of the most frequent accusations that came predecessor Stephen Robinson's way was that his team lacked an identity and players looked unsure as to the tactical plan they had been told to carry out.

Given the Northern Irishman's clear style of play with St Mirren, that was a surprise.

His team in Paisley were known for their physically-domineering displays that were full of grit and graft. That wasn't the case in the North East.

Frick seemingly prefers a 4-4-2 formation, with the midfield playing in a diamond.

That might be bad news for new signings Tony Yogane and Lewis Smith, wingers who did get a look in under Robinson but struggled to make much of an impact.

Sticking to a consistent midfield will help with that. While Robinson regularly relied on Afeez Aremu in the middle of the park, the head coach often chopped and changed his midfield partner.

It has been revealed that of all of Aberdeen's 15 summer signings, Connor Ronan was the one that probably caused the most optimism. Another former St Mirren man, he impressed in his time in Paisley.

The Irishman won a move to Major League Soccer with Colorado Rapids off the back of that form and was also called up to the Republic of Ireland squad without winning a cap.

Eight goals from midfield in the 2021-22 season was a good return - and a couple of them were screamers.

Former Aberdeen midfielder Jamie McGrath recently reminded his old fanbase that they could do with replacing him after he scored a rocket for Hibs in the 4-0 win that did for Robinson.

Ronan could be the answer, playing in a similar role with the same attacking capabilities.

He has yet to hit the heights at Pittodrie, but if Frick can make him tick, the 28-year-old could fire Aberdeen back up the league table.
