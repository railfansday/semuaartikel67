📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ragged Edge gives US fintech Tilt a brand identity built on trust: a signature logo, tilted script type and chartreuse grit.

Industry observers note that the idea lands in the first frame. A signature-style Tilt logo, handwritten and leaning, sits against stark black and white, with chartreuse doing all the shouting. Heavy grotesque headlines carry the lines, Credit for what you're capable of and Tilt the odds in your favor, and one italic script word inside each line does the leaning: capable, favor, all. Under the headline, six portraits run in a row, each captioned with a first name and the words good for it. The promise belongs to the people in the pictures. Black and white say no nonsense. Chartreuse claims urgency.

According to recent reports, the type system is two voices in one grid. The grotesque runs huge and tight, stacked in short lines that fill the poster edge to edge, while the custom script tilts inside it, one italic word per line doing the emphasis. On the manifesto poster the same pairing slows down: Imagine a world where simply trying tallies up sits above a short paragraph in small, even type, ending on the line Because we know you're good for it. The app screens keep the discipline. The script logo rides at the top of black screens, the grotesque handles the numbers, $400 for a Cash Advance, $50 and $25 for a savings target, and the chartreuse button is the only color allowed to shout.

In a recent development, the system holds at every scale. A desert billboard reads Credit history is in the past in black on white, a subway poster claims Value is already in you, a taxi top carries You're capable. We're credit. The same lines shrink onto a chartreuse notebook, a Tilt Visa card in a tray of keys and coins, and a yellow desk mug that reads Working on it. Pearl Chuaynarong's brushstroke illustrations run through the product screens, a food stall steaming, a wallet spilling cards, loose and painterly against the strict grid, keeping the mess of real effort inside the system instead of cleaning it out.
