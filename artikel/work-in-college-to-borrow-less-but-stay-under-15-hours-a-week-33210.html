Work in College to Borrow Less – but Stay Under 15 Hours a Week

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Keep a college job under 15 hours a week, and it can shrink your student loans and build your resume without the slide in grades and graduation odds that heavier schedules bring. Look first for a Federal Work-Study job, whose hours are set around your classes and whose pay won’t count against future aid.

According to recent reports, paychecks during the school year cover books, rent, and other costs you’d otherwise borrow for and repay with interest. The job also brings a reference, practice managing your time, and, in the right role, contacts in your field.

The hours decide whether a job helps. Georgetown University’s Center on Education and the Workforce found that working under 15 hours a week tends to help students, and that grades start to suffer somewhere between 15 and 20 hours. Nearly half of students working 15 hours or more carried a C average or lower, and they were less likely than students working fewer hours to finish their programs.

Start with Federal Work-Study, a need-based aid program that funds part-time jobs on and off campus, since your employer and financial aid office initiative your hours around your classes. Without it, look on campus next, where you can work between classes with no commute.

Work-study pay never counts against future aid, but other paychecks do once they pass an allowance. If your parents fill out part of your FAFSA, the federal aid application, the 2027-28 aid formula ignores your first $12,220 of earnings and the taxes you paid on them. Above that, half of each dollar counts toward your Student Aid Index, the number that sets your eligibility for need-based federal aid.

Take a sophomore who works 12 hours a week at the campus library for $11 an hour. Over two 15-week semesters she earns $3,960, well under the allowance, so she borrows $3,000 less than she planned. By graduation, three years of that means borrowing $9,000 less and paying no interest on it.

File the FAFSA early each year, since work-study is awarded only through it and early filers usually have a better shot at its limited funds than late ones. Then ask your school’s student employment office for a job you can keep under 15 hours a week.
