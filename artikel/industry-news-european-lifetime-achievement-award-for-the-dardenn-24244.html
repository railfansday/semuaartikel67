📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sibling directors Jean-Pierre and Luc Dardenne will be honored with a lifetime achievement award at the 2027 European Film Awards.

The acclaimed Belgian filmmakers, two-time Palme d’Or winners for Rosetta (1999) and The Child (2005), will be feted at the 39th European Film Awards in Athens on Jan.16.

Sources indicate that over nearly 50 years, with more than a dozen features and a handful of documentaries, the Dardenne brothers have established themselves as the preeminent humanist filmmakers of our time. Their work, finely-honed social dramas about the lives of the marginalised, the impoverished and the vulnerable, most of them shot on location in the industrial town of Seraing, where they grew up, have shaped a generation of filmmakers, in Europe and worldwide. Industry observers note that the brothers began as documentarians, recording the lives and experiences of their local community. Those early films helped shape the directors’ later neo-realistic style as well as their enduring commitment to social issues. Rosetta (1999), the story of a turbulent teenager living in a trailer park with her alcoholic mother, trying to hold down any job she can get, premiered at the Cannes Film Festival and won the Palme d’Or for best film.

Recent findings suggest that every Dardenne film since has premiered in Cannes competition. They won their second Palme d’Or for The Child (2005), the story of two young parents on social benefits who sell their newborn baby on the black market. Lorna’s Silence (2008), about a young Albanian woman caught in a deadly citizenship scam, won Cannes’ Best Screenplay honor, as did their most recent feature, Young Mothers (2025), about a shelter for unwed teenage mums.

It has been revealed that the Kid With a Bike (2011), about an 11-year-old boy struggling with abandonment, took Cannes’ runner-up prize, the Grand Prix. The brothers won the Best Director Award in Cannes for Young Ahmed (2019), about a teenage Muslim boy who is drawn to extremism.

The Dardennes typically work with non-professional actors but made an exception for French star Marion Cotillard, who played a woman struggling to save her job in the workplace drama Two Days, One Night (2014). Cotillard won Best Actress at the European Film Awards for her performance.

Sources indicate that the Dardennes won their first European Film Award for Best Documentary for Gigi, Monica… back in 1997 and took the European Screenwriter Award for The Kid With a Bike in 2011. Surprisingly, given their reputation and the consistent quality of their work, the Dardennes have never been nominated for an Academy Award.
