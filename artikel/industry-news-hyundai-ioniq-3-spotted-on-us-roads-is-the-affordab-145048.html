Hyundai’s newly introduced IONIQ 3 checks all the boxes as an affordable EV: it offers over 300 miles of range, a practical interior, and Hyundai’s new Pleos infotainment system. So, will Hyundai bring it to the US?

The Hyundai IONIQ 3 is now on sale across Europe and the UK as a smaller sibling to the IONIQ 5, positioned between the Inster EV and Hyundai Kona Electric.

Sources indicate that although it’s part of the IONIQ sub-brand, the IONIQ 3 features a new “Aero Hatch” design. The new look “reimagines the compact EV silhouette,” Hyundai remarked, designed to optimize efficiency and interior space.

The response so far has been “extraordinary,” Ashley Andrew, president of Hyundai Motor UK, remarked after launching the IONIQ 3 last month.

According to Andrew, the compact EV received “more customers registering their interest before launch than any fresh Hyundai we’ve introduced in the UK,” and that’s including gas-powered and electric vehicles.

The first reviews of the IONIQ 3 have been overwhelmingly positive, highlighting its efficiency, practical interior, and affordable price tag.

Update 11/25/2026: A Hyundai spokesperson told Electrek that “it’s likely that the IONIQ 3 that was apparently spotted in the US recently was the same unit that was brought in for World Car of the Year’s annual test days” held in Pasadena, California.

It looks like the IONIQ 3 will be another affordable, practical EV the US will miss out on.

A post shared by KindelAuto (@kindelauto).

The update from Hyundai comes after the IONIQ 3 was recently captured driving on public US roads, raising speculation, or perhaps hope, that it will come to the States after all.

The model spotted in the images from KindelAuto is the IONIQ 3 N Line, a sportier, higher-priced trim.

While it’s among the first sightings in the US, we have seen several Hyundai Motor Group electric vehicles testing in the US that are still not available and likely won’t ever be, such as Kia’s EV5 SUV and PV5 van.

The IONIQ 3 is built at Hyundai Motor’s Izmit, Turkey, plant and is specifically designed for European drivers.

Recent findings suggest that in Europe and the UK, Hyundai offers the IONIQ 3 with two battery packs: 42 kWh and 61 kWh. The base IONIQ 3 Advance trim with the 42 kWh battery starts at £18,495 ($25,000) OTR and offers up to 213 miles of WLTP range. That’s including the UK’s £3,750 Electric Car Grant.

The IONIQ 3 Advance with the 61 kWh battery starts at £21,995 ($29,000) and offers a “class-leading” WLTP range of up to 308 miles. That also includes the UK government ECG grant.

Measuring 4,155 mm (163.5″) long, 1,800 mm (70.9″) wide, and 1,505 mm (59.3″) high, with a wheelbase of 2,680 mm (105.5″), Hyundai’s electric hatch is even smaller than the Kia EV3 and Volkswagen ID.3.

The IONIQ 3 is also Hyundai’s first vehicle in Europe equipped with its fresh Pleos Connect infotainment system.

Hyundai’s latest system is more like using a smartphone. It’s based on the Android Automotive OS with apps and customizable screens, similar to Tesla models. It’s available in the IONIQ 3 with a 12.9″ or 14.6″ center infotainment screen, plus an optional driver display.

Unlike the larger IONIQ 5, the IONIQ 3 uses a 400V architecture. However, the 42 kWh battery can still recharge from 10% to 80% in about 29 minutes. The 61 kWh battery takes around 30 minutes.

In Germany and other European markets, Hyundai’s compact IONIQ 3 starts at about €28,950 ($33,000).

While Hyundai at the moment has no plans to bring the IONIQ 3 to the US, it does sell the IONIQ 5, built at the Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America outside Savannah, GA, alongside the three-row IONIQ 9.

Starting at about $35,000, Hyundai’s IONIQ 5 has remained among the top-selling electric vehicles in the US outside of the Tesla Model Y and Model 3.

In fact, Hyundai was just named the “Best Electric Vehicle Brand” by US News & World Report for the first time for 2027.
