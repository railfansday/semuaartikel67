The Graphite™ Business Cash Unlimited Card launched in March 2026. On the surface, the card sounds rather uncompetitive — it has a $295 annual fee (Rates & Fees), and offers “just” 2% cash back on everyday spending, with limited bonus categories.

In a recent development, however, there are some things that could make this card worthwhile, including a potentially lucrative welcome offer, a solid return on spending, and some credits that can help offset the annual fee. If your business spends a lot, you value having an Amex, and you just want to earn simple cash back without a complex rewards structure, this could be a good option.

In a recent development, this post is my in-depth Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited review. Is the card worth it, and if so, under what circumstances? And what are the best alternatives?

With the above out of the way, let’s look at the various aspects of the Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited, from the welcome offer, to the fees, to the rewards structure, to the other perks. This card is fairly straightforward, so there’s not all that much to understand, for better or worse.

The Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited at the moment has a welcome offer where you can earn as high as $2,000 cash back after spending $50,000 within the first six months on eligible purchases. Reward Dollars can be redeemed as statement credits to offset transactions, or as a payment method for Amazon purchases.

Given that the card ordinarily offers at least 2% cash back, that means spending $50,000 could earn you a minimum of $3,000 (if offered the full bonus amount), equivalent to 6% cash back on that amount of spending, which is solid.

It has been revealed that admittedly the spending requirement is massive (it averages out to $8,333 per month over the course of six months), but I do think businesses that spend a lot might find this to be achievable. Furthermore, it’s nice that you have six months to complete the spending requirement, while many other cards give you only three months to reach the spending requirement.

If you’re applying for the Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited, be aware of Amex’s general restrictions on applying for cards, including the “once in a lifetime rule.” That means you’re not eligible for the welcome offer on the card if you’ve already had this card, though you are potentially eligible for the bonus if you’ve had other Amex cards.

Anecdotally, I find Amex business cards to be quite easy to get approved for. I also love how with Amex, you can know if you’ll be approved with no credit score impact — the hard pull only happens after you’re told if you’re approved, and you accept the offer.

Also keep in mind that if you’re concerned about staying under Chase’s 5/24 limit, applying for an Amex business card won’t count toward that, since it doesn’t show up on your personal credit report in that way.

Industry observers note that when it comes to credit score, there’s not a consistent credit score you need to get approved for the card, as it’s one of only many factors that determines eligibility. Personally, I’d hope to have a score in the “good” to “excellent” range, ideally 700+ points. However, people with lower scores have been approved, and people with higher scores have been rejected.

The Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited has an annual fee of $295 (Rates & Fees), which is definitely on the steep side, especially given the relatively narrow appeal of the credits offered on the card, which require a lot of spending.

With the card’s 2% cash back rewards structure, you’d essentially recoup the annual fee (in terms of rewards) after $14,750 in spending.

The Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited has an authorized user fee of $95 for the first 5 cards, and a $95 annual fee for each thereafter (Rates & Fees), though you can also get a no annual fee Employee Business Expense Card with no additional fee. Still, that’s a rather steep authorized user fee for a card that doesn’t actually offer many incremental perks. You can add up to 99 employee cards to your account.

According to recent reports, the Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited has no preset spending limit. The idea is that this gives you flexibility in ways that traditional credit cards don’t. The amount you can spend adapts based on factors such as your purchase, payment, and credit history. Rather than having a set limit, you could grow your purchasing power the more you consistently use your card and regularly make payments.

In fairness, it is also worth mentioning that not having a preset spending limit can also lead to some declines if the amount you can charge decreases, so it’s a little less predictable than a regular credit card. However, I find that more often than not, it works in the favor of cardmembers.

No Preset Spending Limit means your spending limit is flexible. Unlike a traditional card with a set limit, the amount you can spend adapts based on factors such as your purchase, payment, and credit history.

It has been revealed that in terms of its rewards structure, the Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited offers 2% cash back on eligible purchases. On top of that, the card offers 5% cash back on flights and prepaid hotels booked through American Express Travel® Online. Just to do some very basic math:.

According to recent reports, all cash back on the card is earned as Reward Dollars, which can be redeemed as statement credits to offset transactions, or as a payment method for Amazon purchases. Unfortunately there are no other redemption options, so you couldn’t directly deposit the cash back into a business checking account, or anything like that.

2% cash back on credit card spending is the “gold standard,” as I see it, so that’s excellent, and what everyone should be aiming for, at a minimum.

Meanwhile the 5% cash back on Amex Travel might sound appealing, but there are some catches. If you can route travel spending that way, it’s a good option, but the catch is that you may sometimes find lower rates elsewhere, so there could be an opportunity cost to booking that way.

The Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited offers up to $2,400 in American Express One AP® statement credits, after spending $250,000 on eligible purchases on the card in a calendar year. Statement credits will be applied to the monthly bill charged to the card for Amex’s accounts payable solution, One AP, for the following calendar year, and this includes an auto-renewing platform access fee.

Recent findings suggest that american Express One AP® is an accounts payable solution that can help transform the way your business pays suppliers. With One AP, you can streamline your supplier invoice payments on a single platform and pay by virtual card, check, and ACH. Enrollment is required.

According to recent reports, this program charges a $200 monthly fee to pay vendors with non-cards, plus has a fee for checks and ACHs over a certain limit. So the $2,400 annual credit can potentially fully cover your monthly fee. While this is potentially a nice feature, keep in mind that many platforms already offer this as a built in feature at no extra cost, so it’s not really that special.

The Amex Graphite Business Cash Unlimited offers the Pay Over Time feature, whereby cardmembers can carry a balance with interest on eligible charges, up to the Pay Over Time limit. The way this works, eligible charges will automatically be added to a Pay Over Time balance, up to the Pay Over Time limit.

You can choose to pay for these charges over time with interest, or you can pay your full balance each month, whichever is best for your business. You must pay in full any charge or a portion of a charge that is not added to a Pay Over Time balance.

Sources indicate that personally, I recommend paying the balance in full each billing cycle if possible, to avoid interest charges, especially given how high they can be. However, I think it’s worth clarifying that the Pay Over Time option is possible, given that this isn’t a traditional credit card.

It’s also worth emphasizing the difference between the Pay Over Time and the Pay In Full Limit. The latter could be an amount that has to be paid in full, and there’s no option to pay that over time with interest charges.

The Amex Graphite Business Cash Card earns 2% cash back, and I consider that to be the minimum “gold standard” of credit card rewards. So the card’s rewards structure is perfectly fine. The 5% cash back on travel is also pretty limiting, given that you have to book through Amex.

Recent findings suggest that I think the problem is the steep annual fee, given that there are also much more affordable (and even no annual fee) cards with that kind of a rewards structure. In fairness, Amex doesn’t have another business card earning unlimited 2% cash back. It’s just not a new concept more broadly, and there are lots of competing cards that I’d consider to be superior.

So who should consider the card? As I see it, the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card is targeted at a specific demographic. This card could make sense for the following types of customers:.

I do truly think this is a niche product. But the idea is that if you’re a small business owner charging hundreds of thousands or millions of dollars on credit cards per year, that annual fee won’t make much of a difference.

Still, I’d like to compare this to some other popular products, as I think there are other cards that may be a better fit for some other people.

In a recent development, perhaps the best direct comparison to the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card is The American Express Blue Business Cash® Card (learn more). The card has no annual fee (Rates & Fees) and offers 2% cash back on the first $50,000 spent every calendar year (and 1% cash back thereafter).

The upside here is obvious, as you’re earning 2% cash back with no fee. The downside is that the welcome offer is much smaller (though also with a smaller spending requirement), the card has foreign transaction fees, you’re capped at earning 2% cash back on the first $50,000 spent annually, and the card does have a preset spending limit.

For a small business mostly spending money domestically, the Amex Blue Business Cash is a great alternative to the Amex Graphite Business Cash.

Recent findings suggest that another potential alternative to the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card is The Blue Business® Plus Credit Card from American Express (learn more). The card has no annual fee (Rates & Fees) and offers 2x Membership Rewards points on the first $50,000 spent every calendar year (and 1x points thereafter).

Sources indicate that if you’re good at redeeming points, that’s a pretty incredible return, since based on my latest miles & points valuations, Amex points are worth 1.7 cents each, for a return of up to 3.4%. The downsides to the Amex Blue Business Plus are otherwise identical to those on the Amex Blue Business Cash — the welcome offer is much smaller (though also with a smaller spending requirement), the card has foreign transaction fees, you’re capped at earning 2x points on the first $50,000 spent annually, and the card does have a preset spending limit.

So the card is either useful if $50,000 covers your annual business spending, or you’re willing to use another card after you hit that initial $50,000 spending cap.

In a recent development, speaking of premium Amex business cards, it’s also worth comparing the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card to the American Express® Business Gold Card (learn more). The Amex Business Gold has a $375 annual fee (Rates & Fees), and offers an incredible rewards structure (with up to 4x Membership Rewards points in select categories), plus statement credits that can greatly help offset the annual fee.

As I see it, the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card is the better option if you value cash back and a straightforward rewards structure, while the Amex Business Gold is the better option if you can take advantage of the bonus categories, and are willing to put in the effort to maximize points.

While the above comparisons are all to Amex cards, I think it’s also worth comparing the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card to the Capital One Spark Cash Plus (learn more), as it’s perhaps the most direct competitor.

The card has a massive welcome offer of $2,000 cash back after spending $30,000 within the first three months (plus an additional $2,000 cash back bonus for every $500,000 spent during the first year), offers 5% cash back on select Capital One Business Travel purchases and 2% cash back with no caps and no foreign transaction fees on other purchases, and it has a $150 annual fee. Furthermore, the card offers an annual bonus of $150 if you spend at least $150,000 on the card in a calendar year, essentially rebating the annual fee.

I do think the Capital One Spark Cash Plus is more compelling than the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card on balance, unless you specifically have a reason you’d like an Amex, or value the statement credits tied to spending $250,000 per year.

I’d also like to compare the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card to the Ink Business Premier® Credit Card (learn more), as it’s another potentially lucrative cash back card.

In addition to a substantial welcome offer of $1,000 cash back after spending $10,000 within the first three months, the card offers 5% cash back on Chase Travel purchases, 2.5% cash back on purchases of $5,000 or more, and 2% cash back on all other purchases, with no foreign transaction fees, and it has a $195 annual fee. So if you have a business that frequently makes large purchases, the Chase Ink Business Premier could be a great alternative.

It has been revealed that the Amex Graphite Business Cash Card has a $295 annual fee, and offers unlimited 2% cash back, with no preset spending limit, plus a large welcome offer. The card also offers up to $2,400 in American Express One AP® statement credits, after spending $250,000 on eligible purchases on the card in a calendar year.

Sources indicate that ultimately I’d consider this to be a niche card. The card could be worth getting for the excellent welcome offer, and it could be worth it if you have a business that spends a huge amount, and values the lack of a preset spending limit, the card’s Amex branding, and/or the statement credits.

Beyond that, though, I tend to think there are better options. If you’re not a huge spender, the Amex Blue Business Cash and Amex Blue Business Plus are excellent options. If you’re willing to maximize points and bonus categories, consider the Amex Business Gold. And if you’re looking for the best direct alternative that’s arguably more lucrative, consider the Capital One Spark Cash Plus.
