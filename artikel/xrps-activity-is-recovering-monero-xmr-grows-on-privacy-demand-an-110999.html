With the deeper correction in the cryptocurrency market, we are finally witnessing somewhat of a bounce in multiple top-15 digital assets, including XRP, Monero and Dogecoin.

XRP is printing higher network usage compared to October, Monero is gradually outperforming the market in the last 24 hours and Dogecoin is aiming at a local price recovery, even though a modest one.

One important development that we haven't anticipated is Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia (NVDA) CEO on November 23, only 11 days after his resignation. The announcement triggered a substantial market retrace, with NVDA stock gaining over 30% and Nasdaq adding around 6.8%, recovering a large share of last week's losses.

Thankfully, XRP demonstrates us resilience beyond regular market metrics: the average daily payments throughout the month have crossed the 1.1 million threshold, a 107% growth versus the October baseline. The basis behind the reversal is considerable: the rise in market volatility that triggered a substantial breakdown in the asset.

With rapid market swings, it's no surprise that the asset saw more than 1.33 million transactions throughout the first 7 days of the month. New accounts over a rolling 24-hour period reached 4,065 wallets, per XRPScan query on November 23.

Industry observers note that from the technical standpoint, XRP is presently trading near the $0.99 price level, approximately 23% lower compared to the 30-day average, which fits the aforementioned bearish narrative. The decline in spot trading volume is also an vital sign: spot investors dropped around 22.5% of their trading activities throughout the month.

According to recent reports, the drop in futures volume also makes the picture slightly more complicated than it should be. Following the price breakdown, the volatility increase was followed by an increase in open interest, so the drop we're witnessing right now is more of a return to the baseline than an problem on its own.

The current price resistance and support points are quite diverse, but not thoroughly tested, apart from a local support level at $0.9. The immediate breakout test is going to take place at approximately $0.99, with a possibility of a further recovery. The level at $1.00 is more of a psychological barrier, while $1.04 is the threshold that should mark the end of the downtrend.

According to CoinMarketCap's data, Monero has been steadily underperforming XRP and Dogecoin in the last 24 hours, though it's key to separate rising demand and active speculative buying. In the last 24 hours, Monero has gained over 4.7% in price. Futures open interest has crossed the $230 million mark.

As already mentioned, Monero is a weaker performer out of the three, with a 4.7% gain over the last 24 hours. Unexpectedly, a deeper look reveals a set of challenges: spot and futures volume, open interest and liquidation volume suggest a rather stale market with no momentum rather than an explosive recovery that might turn into a proper uptrend. The good news is that most of the liquidated volume is on the short side.

In a recent development, from the network usage perspective, Monero is struggling, with its transaction count dropping below the 30-day average, prompting baseless price resilience. Though Monero's network design certainly limits the possibility of a thorough analysis due to its protected transaction design.

According to recent reports, the most important development around Monero was, of course, the most recent Wrapped Monero incident. Despite the wrapped token itself having little to do with the main network of the asset, the coin itself had to withstand the rippling selling pressure. The token lost more than 99% of its value, causing $13 million in losses.

The main network itself went through a security audit on November 18, which found no high- or medium-severity vulnerabilities and only minor security concerns, which developers have been notified about.

If the technical side of the question is rather notable, the most important immediate price levels are the 508-510 range, which corresponds to the current breakout area, and the immediate support at $500.

Sources indicate that compared to all three cryptocurrencies, Monero is the only one showing some positivity over the last 7 days, despite the wrapped token incident. Privacy assets like XMR or Zcash quite often provide more market resilience during breakdowns, most likely due to the demand for privacy that always exists in the industry.

Dogecoin's technical recovery attempt is hitting somewhat of a wall, with daily, weekly and four-hour trends showing a clear bearish dynamic in addition to the price moving below all 8 tracked moving averages on the chart.

It has been revealed that as the CoinGlass data suggests, the trading activity we are witnessing today is mixed: spot and futures volumes are descending, with a slight increase in open interest. $584 million in futures volume and $86 million in spot volume. Long and short liquidations are balanced.

Unfortunately, the lack of network activity suggests a baseless price recovery on the market right now: only 37 thousand addresses were active in the last 24 hours, a 24% decrease YTD. Additionally, explorer data suggests that the ten largest DOGE addresses hold more than 44% of the token's available supply.

It doesn't suggest that only 10 owners hold a large part of the token's supply, but rather a high concentration of DOGE on exchanges, which quite often prompts additional selling pressure.

With the low volume and open interest, any technical price level, both resistance and support, can get invalidated swiftly; however, highlighting them could be useful. The first resistance rests at $0.0599, with a trend recovery threshold located around $0.06.

Support levels lie all the way down to the key psychological level of $0.05, the nearest being $0.054. The obvious trend reversal threshold is, of course, $0.06. Until the price establishes a foothold above it, discussing a recovery is not the best option out there.
