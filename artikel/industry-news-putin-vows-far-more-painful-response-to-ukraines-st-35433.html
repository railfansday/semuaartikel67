Industry News: Putin vows 'far more painful' response to Ukraine's strikes on Crimean Bridge

Vladimir Putin spoke at a press conference following a historic summit with Xi Jinping and Donald Trump.

Moscow's retaliation for attacks on the Crimean Bridge will be "far more painful" than Kyiv's strikes, Vladimir Putin has vowed.

In the early hours of Thursday, the double-arched, 12-mile bridge connecting Russia to Ukraine's occupied peninsula of Crimea was hit for a third time this week.

A combined missile-and-drone strike on Tuesday destroyed the central road section of the bridge, collapsing part of it into the water and rendering it unusable for road traffic.

But at a press conference after historic talks with his Chinese and US counterparts in Shenzhen, Russian president Putin said Moscow's response would be even more significant.

Putin claimed the bridge was used exclusively for civilian purposes and not to transport military equipment, as Kyiv has alleged, or petroleum products.

Recent findings suggest that since illegally annexing the Crimean Peninsula in 2014, Moscow has used it as a military base, with airbases, troop concentrations, training grounds and artillery stockpiles. From there it has also carried out strikes on the rest of the country and resupplies its troops in southern Ukraine.

Recent findings suggest that commenting on the strikes on the bridge, Kyiv's defence ministry noted that for Ukraine, they amounted to to "time gained and lives saved. The less that reaches the occupiers, the less hits our warriors.".

The collapse of the Crimean Bridge's arch was the worst damage to be inflicted onto the structure in several years.

The bridge had long been in Ukraine's sights because it represents Moscow's pretensions over sovereign Ukrainian land, and because of its logistical importance in supplying Russian troops in the occupied Ukrainian southern regions.

It was built and unveiled to great fanfare in 2018, four years after Russia illegally annexed Ukraine's Crimea.

Thursday's strike was carried out with Neptune cruise missiles, Ukraine said. Russia's transport ministry said nobody had been hurt in the attack, and that specialists were assessing damage to the bridge. Train services on the bridge were suspended but resumed in the afternoon.

Car traffic, however, will be prohibited for the foreseeable future as a section about 200m (660ft) long of the tarmac was dragged below into the sea when missiles hit the arch on Tuesday.

President Volodymyr Zelensky called the initial strikes on the Crimean Bridge an "unprecedented operation" weeks in the making.

In a recent development, the seven-stage operation first required the destruction of air defence systems, radars, electronic warfare assets which protected the bridge. Missiles and drones then dealt the structure the final blow.

Final moments before stretch of Crimean Bridge collapsed after Ukrainian attack earlier this week.

In response to Tuesday's hit, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the bridge "will be rebuilt, and it will be even better than before".

But the operation to repair it could take a long time, and it will likely have to be carried out under frequent Ukrainian fire.

Russia has regularly targeted Ukrainian civilian infrastructure since launching its full-scale invasion in 2022.

This week alone, as the Crimean Bridge was being hit, Moscow targeted key bridge crossings over the Dnipro River in Kyiv.
