The surprisingly awesome arcade podracing game Star Wars: Galactic Racer is getting split-screen multiplayer next year after loud fan requests, developer Fuse Games has unveiled.

Split-screen racing comes as part of an entirely latest mode called Free Play that'll also allow players to mix and match vehicles, characters, tracks and planets in a galaxy far, far away. So, maybe don't expect to bring friends into the game's vast, pre-existing suite of modes just yet.

"We heard you," the studio wrote on social media. "Local split-screen multiplayer racing is coming in early 2027. A dedicated team at Fuse Games has been working hard to bring a brand new mode, Free Play, to Star Wars: Galactic Racer. This new mode will allow you to choose your vehicle, character, planet, and race track to race alone or with up to 3 other players in local split-screen action.".

According to recent reports, we’ve heard you! Local split-screen multiplayer racing is coming in early 2027. A dedicated team at @Fuse_Games has been working hard to bring a brand new mode, Free Play, to STAR WARS: Galactic Racer™. This new mode will allow you to choose your vehicle, character, planet, and race track to race alone or with up to 3 other players in local split-screen action.

Galactic Racer already has a pretty good selection of online multiplayer modes, like the 12-player speeder races and 8-player podracing tours, but there's currently no way to play with your mates in the same room.

Recent findings suggest that chris Donlan loved the thrilling racing game for mixing the best bits of Star Wars and Burnout - Fuse Games does have plenty of old Criterion DNA, mind you. "It's newly introduced games, with Roguelite choices and endless unlockables, and it's also old games, with cruel and ingenious courses where everything can be lost in an instant. It's a Galaxy Far, Far Away, and it's also Paradise City," he wrote in Eurogamer's five-star review.

In a recent development, with Galactic Racer and Zero Company under its belt, Lucasfilm Games stated it's now "more flexible" about which developers it accepts pitches from. Hopefully, that'll lead to even more interesting games set in Star Wars' ultra flexible world.
