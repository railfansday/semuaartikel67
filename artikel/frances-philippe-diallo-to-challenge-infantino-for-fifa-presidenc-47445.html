French Football Federation (FFF) President Philippe Diallo has received UEFA backing to be a candidate in FIFA's presidential election, which will take place in March in Morocco, with criticism against current chief Gianni Infantino growing.

UEFA, European football's governing body, stated it has backed Diallo "unanimously" in a bid to replace Infantino, putting it's "full support" behind the Frenchman.

Veteran Japanese official Kohzo Tashima has also confirmed his candidacy, with the deadline for candidates to stand for election being November 18.

Potential candidates for the post must show letters of support from five of FIFA's 211 member associations before going through eligibility and integrity checks.

Diallo, the 63-year-old head of the FFF, is the son of a Senegalese boxing champion.

Recent findings suggest that he was elected as the federation's president in 2023 with a large majority. He was reelected for another four-year term in 2024.

Back in September, Diallo noted the FFF had withdrawn its support for Infantino ahead of the March 2027 election, saying trust in the FIFA president had "been betrayed.".

"The way in which the governance of world football is envisaged no longer reflects us," the French football head said of the decision.

If elected, the Frenchman would become FIFA's first black president, as well as it's third French leader after Robert Guerin and Jules Rimet, who served as the organization's first and third presidents.

Gianni Infantino has been in charge of world football's governing body since 2016.

He has been facing growing criticism since pitching a plan of sell a stake in the World Cup to private investors. The criticism of the plan was led by UEFA and its President Aleksander Ceferin.

Infantino ended up scrapping the proposal but UEFA went ahead and took FIFA to court in the US, trying to gather evidence ahead of potentially lodging a criminal complaint with Swiss authorities about alleged "mismanagement" by Infantino.

FIFA's headquarters are located in Zürich, Switzerland.

Infantino was also criticized after creating a peace prize and awarding it to US President Donald Trump last year.

The connection between Infantino and Trump came under criticism once again during the World Cup, after United States forward Folarin Balogun was allowed to take part in the round-of-16 game against Belgium despite having been shown a red card in the previous game.

The US president admitted to having personally intervened through a conversation with Infantino.
