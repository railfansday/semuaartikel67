Sources indicate that another year is coming to an end, and (as you and I have gotten used to by now) there’s still no Half-Life 3 in sight. You’d think that 22 years after the release of Half-Life 2, it would hurt any less, but nope.

In a bid to soothe aching hearts and Gordon Freeman enthusiasts all around the world, the wizards over at Crowbar Collective have put out a newly introduced update to Black Mesa, making the 2020 love letter to Half-Life look better than ever.

This enhancement, branded a ‘red letter day’ by the developers, focuses first and foremost on visual improvements. Crowbar Collective has managed to make the game look better, while also significantly improving its performance in the process.

In a showcase by the developers, scenes previously clocking around 130 FPS now reach 170 FPS. It’s not clear how these improvements will scale on different hardware, but expect to see noticeable changes across the board.

On the eyecandy side of things, interiors have now been updated to use parallax-corrected cubemaps (PCC). This technique turns static reflections into dynamic ones by adjusting them based on the player’s perspective. As a result, it adds a lot of depth to interiors while not incurring costs on the performance side. Translucent materials like glass have also been updated to have light behave realistically as it passes through.

It has been revealed that while interiors got plenty of jazz, there have also been major improvements to the game’s environmental lighting as a whole. Crowbar Collective has improved the Source Engine’s light bouncing, making indirect illumination behave more realistically. Outdoor scenes have received image-based lighting, using HDR to bring more accurate outputs.

Sources indicate that as a love letter to Valve’s magnum opus, it’s only natural for Black Mesa to cater to the firm’s handheld console. Following the update’s performance improvements, Black Mesa has received the Steam Deck Verified badge.

According to recent reports, the latest patch to Black Mesa has modernized the user interface to make it behave better with stick controls, benefiting both Steam Deck and PC users who play with controllers. According to Crowbar Collective, keyboard and mouse have also been refined. Nostalgics can still pick the classic Half-Life menu from the Steam Workshop.

The popular Blue Shift mod by HECU Collective, which tells the game’s story through the eyes of Barney Calhoun, has been updated to be compatible with the latest version of Black Mesa.

For now, this massive update to Black Mesa is all we have until the yearly ritual of hoping for a Half-Life 3 reveal at The Game Awards. Earlier this year, Valve composer Mike Morasky alluded to a certain game codenamed ‘HLX’, which some of the more hopeful types have taken to be a working title for the much anticipated sequel.

That is not the first time Morasky has run his mouth about the HLX project, so maybe, just maybe, this time it’s for real. Until more evidence surfaces, you’ll find me playing Black Mesa.

Black Mesa is available via Windows and Linux platforms via Steam.
