Industry observers note that after 13 agonizing years of waiting for Grand Theft Auto VI (GTA 6), gamers worldwide wasted no time tearing up the streets of the game's fictional state of Leonida after it was released yesterday, November 19. The South Florida-inspired game environment is heavily influenced by the culture, environment, and architecture of Miami — from its Art Deco resorts, white-sand beaches, Cuban quarter, and strip clubs, right down to its museums and arts venues.

As fans embed themselves in GTA 6's immersive landscape, many have recognized real-life locations across South Florida making unexpected cameos. One such case is the Vice Museum of Art, which Rockstar Games confirmed is modeled after the Bass Museum of Art in Miami Beach.

“It was totally unexpected to see Grand Theft Auto VI reimagine The Bass as the ‘Vice City Museum of Art,’” mentioned Silvia Karman Cubiñá, executive director of the Bass Museum of Art, in an email to Hyperallergic.

Industry observers note that “It’s a wonderful reminder of how art and architecture can become part of popular culture,” she continued. “Now, I’m just curious: will the Vice City Museum of Art get more visitors than we do?!”.

Recent findings suggest that while not listed as a landmark like the Vice Museum of Art, another building in Downtown Vice City appears to be directly modeled after the Pérez Art Museum Miami (PAMM). Situated near the Vice City Arts Center, which is heavily based on Miami's Adrienne Arsht Center for Performing Arts, the PAMM-inspired building sits right on the water's edge just as the museum does in real life.

“We were as delighted as anyone else to find PAMM in what feels like the biggest video game of all time,” mentioned Alexa Ferra, PAMM's senior director of marketing and communications, in an email to Hyperallergic.

Ferra attached a photo someone sent her from the game showing the PAMM dupe and some gummy bear-inspired sculptures installed in the shallow water.

“It's a testament to our stunning Herzog & de Meuron building, and goes to show if you stick around long enough, you'll pop up in the unlikeliest — but inevitable — of places.” Ferra added.

Other references to Miami's arts and culture scene include Leonida's Stockyard neighborhood, which directly channels the street murals and warehouse-lined roads of the Wynwood Arts District. It was reported earlier this summer that Rockstar Games had hired over 50 real street artists to design the in-game murals across Stockyard. Owners shared they've spotted the works of street artist Ahol Sniffs Glue (David Anasagasti), among others.

Miami-raised contemporary photographer Josh Aronson also took notice of GTA 6's inclusion of his installation at the Catalina Hotel & Beach Club in Miami Beach during Miami Art Week 2024, which Rockstar Games incorporated in Vice City's own Catalina Hotel. From Aronson's Florida Boys (2020–) photo series, the photo “Spring” (2024) was made into an enormous sublimated fabric print that hung from the second-story window of the hotel.

The same printed photo, which features a group of cheerful young men photographed from above while floating in water, is shown at Vice City's Catalina Hotel.

“Florida Boys is my own imagined version of Florida, so I love that it gets to live on in another imagined Florida,” Aronson told Hyperallergic. “The installation at the Catalina was temporary, but in Vice City, someone might stumble upon it years from now. It’s a reminder of the wild ways we encounter art today, and how a work can find upcoming life long after it comes down.”.

GTA6's pre-holiday release comes just before Miami Art Week begins early next month. Those dropping in can find some of Aronson's installations on view beyond Vice City at the Vizcaya Museum and Gardens and the Museum of Contemporary Art, North Miami, and also pay homage to the cultural venues and districts that inspired Leonida.
