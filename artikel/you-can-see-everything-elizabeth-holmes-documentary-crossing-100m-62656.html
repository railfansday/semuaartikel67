A24‘s Lance Oppenheim and Nathan Fielder co-directed groundbreaking documentary, You Can See Everything, continues to have 20/20 vision at the box office and will pass the $100M global mark tonight.

In a recent development, broken out that’s $90.9M at the domestic B.O. with a foreign cume that’s coursing to $8M today. Already, the movie is the second highest grossing non-concert documentary at the domestic B.O. after Michael Moore’s Cannes Palme d’Or winning Fahrenheit 9/11 which counts $119.1M.

Industry observers note that already, You Can See Everything counts $6.4M abroad having already unspooled in Turkey (Oct. 30, $458K), Poland ($1.46M), Czech Republic ($163K) and Australia/Fresh Zealand ($2.5M). Today the movie is clearing $2M offshore inclusive of $1.2M in the UK/Ireland (which also includes Thursday monies). A24 has the movie in the UK. The pic is also hitting Russia and CIS (via VLG) and Philippines, Scandinavia, Middle East, and South Africa (via Sony) today. As Deadline first reported, Sony Pictures acquired rights to the documentary in Germany/Austria, Latin America, Scandinavia, Greece, the Middle East, Israel, India, South Africa, South Korea, and Southeast Asia.

Abroad the Elizabeth Holmes and Billy Evans sit-down is setting market records in some territories as the widest-ever theatrical release for a documentary. In UK/Ireland, You Can See Everything is playing in 600 cinemas; that location count being the widest ever for a non-concert documentary. You Can See Everything also posted the biggest presales in A24 UK history. Screenings there sold out a month in advance of its release with theaters adding extra showtimes due to demand (including midnight indicates and extended operating hours). There’s been a significant turnout by Gen Z and young millennial audiences, which is unusual for documentaries in recent years, outside of concert films. Cineworld, which has 87 sites across the UK reported that a third of advance ticket sales are coming from 18–24 year olds, with about two thirds from the under 35 demo. Picturehouse has presently sold more tickets to the documentary than No Time to Die and Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker at the same point in time before release. Everyman had sold more tickets at eight days out than Deadpool & Wolverine at the same point. At Curzon, only No Time to Die had stronger pre-sales at this stage. Lighthouse Dublin broke its all time presale record with You Can See Everything, ahead of Barbie.

In a recent development, among the Sony market holdovers, Portugal posted the 2nd highest per screen average in the weekend’s top 10, while the pic bowed to No. 1 in the Baltics, No. 2 in Estonia, and No. 2 in Lithuania and Latvia (behind a local title). In Turkey, You Can See Everything has officially sold the most tickets of any international documentary ever in Turkey and reps the third highest opening for A24 behind only Backrooms and The Drama. Last weekend, You Can See Everything opened to $2.5M in Australia/New Zealand for the No. 1 spot, which is the highest start for a non-concert doc in the territory and third highest when including concerts (Michael Jackson’s This Is It and Taylor Swift’s Eras Tour rank ahead). It’s also the third highest opening for an A24 title in the territory.
