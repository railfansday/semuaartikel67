According to recent reports, welcome to The Receipt, a series documenting how Bon Appétit readers eat and what they spend doing it. Each food diary follows one anonymous reader’s week of expenses related to groceries, restaurant meals, coffee runs, and every bite in between. In this time of rising food costs, The Receipt reveals how folks—from different cities, with different incomes, on different schedules—are figuring out their food budgets.

Recent findings suggest that in today's Receipt, we follow a 43-year-old server and drag queen in Toronto, Ontario. Keep reading for his receipts.

In a recent development, what is your occupation? Server, Drag Queen.

What city and state do you live in? Toronto, ON.

What is your annual salary, if you have one? Girl, I have no idea. Waiting tables brings in around $60K CAN, and performing in bars, nightclubs and drag brunches probably makes me another $3K, but all those sweat-and-glitter-covered bills get stuffed under a mattress, so who knows?

Industry observers note that how much is one paycheck, after taxes? Let’s call it an even thousand dollars.

How often are you paid? (e.g., weekly) Weekly or biweekly.

What are your approximate fixed monthly expenses beyond food? (i.e., rent, subscriptions, bills).

Industry observers note that do you follow a certain diet or have dietary restrictions? I avoid salt and gluten as much as possible.

What are the grocery staples you always buy, if any? Eggs, gluten-free pasta, bananas, bone broth, greens, yogurt. I almost always have boiled eggs ready in the fridge, a tub of yogurt, some dark berries, and bananas to keep me fueled up. If I’m going to cook, it’s pasta or meat and veggies.

Industry observers note that how often in a week do you dine out versus cook at home? I work in an upscale Italian restaurant and the food is exceptional. I eat there three or four times a week and have the cholesterol levels to prove it. At 50% off any menu items (minus the $128 porterhouse steak) it’s a bargain to eat delicious, mostly healthy cooked meals. I love going out socially or alone and do so almost weekly.

How often in a week did you dine out while growing up? Once a month? Rural Alberta didn’t have a ton of dining-out choices.

Industry observers note that how often in a week did your parents or guardians cook at home? Daily. My mom’s (and occasionally my dad’s) cooking was excellent. Nothing fancy, but a real meat-and-potatoes type menu that kept us going.

10 a.m. My annual sinus infection came early, and I’ve been sick since Saturday. I’m trying to break my morning caffeine habit, but today I’m too lazy and run down. I hydrate with lemon water while sipping an Americano with brown sugar. Between the coffee and the illness I don’t have much of an appetite. I forage on peanut butter and pumpkin seeds while shuffling around my apartment.

It has been revealed that 4 p.m. A thought occurs to me: Let’s heal this body with some miso and wasabi. I order sushi from Kibo down the street. It’s takeout, so I am forced to don clothing and walk down the street. I pick up 2-for-1 dynamite rolls, chicken gyoza dumplings, miso soup, the obligatory green salad that comes with it all. and a spicy salmon combo. $44.

It has been revealed that as I’m feeling sorry for myself on my walk back home, I stop at the corner store for a bottle of Blake Boulevard Sauvignon Blanc. $21.

10 a.m. I know that I have a shift at the restaurant today, so I’m holding my head up and pretending that I'm not at death's door. A banana-and-blueberry yogurt provide the gas I need to leave the house to get provisions.

We’re out of coffee beans! I have two espresso machines because I am a complete coffee snob. Machine number one, my favorite, is a De’Longhi manual espresso maker, a tall, silver tower with a handle on the side. I grind my own beans for this. Machine number two is a Nespresso Virtuo. It requires aluminum pods stuffed with coffee. It’s sleek and makes a lovely crema, but it’s never hot enough for me. The pods vary between $1.12 and $1.50 each.

Recent findings suggest that on the way home I stop in at St. Clair Avenue Fruit Market for some of my usuals: bok choy, celery, dates, rapini, bananas, and a container of feta, for fun. $31.75.

It has been revealed that 3 p.m. Before work I load up on cold meds even though they make me feel like a bobblehead. Somewhere between 4 p.m. and 7 p.m. I ask my chef to sneak me some arancini to avoid passing out while bringing some nice folks their wine and dinner. Our arancini are filled with mozzarella, rolled in a saffron risotto, and plated with tomato sauce. Retail $24, but for me it’s $12.

According to recent reports, 10 a.m. Americano, brown sugar, lemon water. I have a friend coming in from out of town and she is fully aware how ill I am, and I feel as though she may need a snack after her two-plus-hour drive, so I pop down to another cute hot spot on my street, Le Bleu. I may or may not also have a huge crush on the owner, Iman. I pick up vegan baklava, vegan banana bread, a caprese sandwich for her and prosciutto for me. I try to make small talk with Iman while he packs up my order, but he’s so handsome I end up muttering and mumbling my thank-yous while I stumble out the door. $25.76.

Industry observers note that 6 p.m. Reservations for two at a vegetarian restaurant, Planta Yorkville. I am not a vegetarian. I had mid to low expectations. Guys, this place was incredible.

Delicious regular and zero-proof cocktails. A Cosmic Colada for me: coconut water, lemon, blueberry, and pineapple. For my friend: the Tokyo Blossom made with sake, gin, lychee liquor, and grapefruit tonic.

The unagi eggplant was so good I almost ordered a second one; we also ordered spicy tuna roll (some kind of juiced-up watermelon substitution), miso soup, and bang-bang broccoli. I ordered a chocolate brownie and a sticky toffee pudding for dessert—the latter was so substantial it was almost comical. They also stuffed an entire ice cream cone full of vanilla ice cream upside down onto my pudding. I finished about a quarter of it.

According to recent reports, after she leaves and the day progresses I realize I am in no shape to work. Thankfully someone takes my serving shift so I don’t have to go and cough all over our lovely patrons.

Industry observers note that 6 p.m. My appetite has been suffering, but eventually I whip up one of my favorites: a bean medley, chickpeas, celery, olive oil, and feta salad.

According to recent reports, 8 p.m. As the sun sets and the self pity sinks in deeper, I throw a trench coat over my pajamas and scurry down to the store to get another favorite, a bottle of Te Henga Sauvignon Blanc. $19.45.

10 a.m. Another Americano and lemon water.

In a recent development, 1 p.m. At this point in the week, I am so sick of tea I never want to see it again. I’ve been trying to stave off whatever I have with honey/lemon/ginger tea, and I’m over it. That was lunch, I guess.

According to recent reports, 8 p.m. Guilty pleasure time! I call in an order for Little Caesars Chicago Deep Dish Meat Treat pizza, an order of hot wings, and a ginger ale. Pure class and total nutrition. The good news is that while attempting to remain unseen, I happen to run into someone I’ve been chatting with on a dating app. Oh, did I say good? I meant horrific. $39.77.

Sources indicate that 10 a.m. Americano. Lemon Water. I’m a creature of habit, okay?

In a recent development, 12 p.m. Bananas, blueberries, leftover bean salad, ginger tea.

Sources indicate that 3 p.m. I’m at work early, because we have fresh menu items. At pre-shift, the chef makes one of each and we discuss and sample it. A prime rib–chuck burger with scamorza and tomato bomba, lamb pizza with basil pesto, steak tartare, Atlantic cod and potato risotto, and mushroom-and-pea-stuffed ravioli. Some call this “product knowledge.” I call it a free meal(s).

It has been revealed that 8:45 p.m. Despite all my newfound product knowledge, I still get hungry halfway through my shift. Chef makes me a bulgur, kale, and roasted beet salad with a 7-oz. flatiron steak. $22.

Recent findings suggest that 10:45 p.m. Shift done, head feeling like a block of ice, I sneak off to the market before heading home to grab a bottle of Squealing Pig Sauvignon Blanc. $22.

10 a.m. Pretty much right after I open my eyes, I get a lovely text from a friend; “Wanna get breakfast?” She knows I’ve been sick and we haven’t caught up in a while. She is informed that I will be wearing my now-uniform of pajamas and trench coat.

11 a.m. Three eggs, toast, and bacon from a pub by my house. No culinary awards will be given out here, but it did what it was supposed to do. The bill for both meals with coffee and a couple of spicy Bloody Caesars came to $50.

7 p.m. Another go-to when I’m feeling down or simply have the craving is to simmer some rice noodles in a broth and add miso paste. I stir in fresh chopped bok choy, green onions, and a couple of cut-up hard-boiled eggs. I think I’m gonna make it.
