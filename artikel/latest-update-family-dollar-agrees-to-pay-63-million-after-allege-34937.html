Latest Update: Family Dollar agrees to pay $63 million after allegedly overcharging customers for years

Family Dollar has agreed to a $63 million settlement following allegations that it overcharged customers for years, with federal and state regulators accusing the discount retailer of repeatedly charging more than its advertised prices.

The settlement comes after an investigation by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and 17 states, which alleged that customers were routinely charged higher prices at checkout than those displayed on store shelves, advertisements, and the organization’s website and mobile app.

According to a complaint filed on November 12, the alleged violations affected thousands of Family Dollar locations across the US, with regulators claiming the manufacturer continued its pricing practices despite repeated warnings, customer complaints, and millions of dollars in previous penalties.

The FTC alleged that Family Dollar engaged in deceptive pricing practices that resulted in customers paying more than advertised, sometimes even after bringing discrepancies to employees’ attention.

One example highlighted in the complaint involved a January 2025 promotion offering two packages of diapers for $20. Customers in at least seven states reported that stores refused to honor the advertised deal.

In one instance, a customer claimed they highlighted a store manager the promotion, only for the manager to remove the shelf tags and refuse to sell the diapers at the advertised price.

Regulators also accused the retailer of failing to honor digital discounts, with internal tech giant records reportedly showing six years of complaints about coupons not working, promotional prices failing to appear, and customers being charged more than the prices displayed in the Family Dollar app.

The complaint alleged that the organization received thousands of failed pricing inspections, hundreds of customer complaints, and multiple enforcement actions related to inaccurate prices.

Since 2022, Family Dollar has faced more than $3 million in fines stemming from alleged pricing violations identified by state and local regulators. The company also paid more than $800,000 in settlements with multiple state attorneys general beginning in 2024.

Regulators argued that the alleged overcharging disproportionately affected customers in low-income communities, where shoppers often depend on accurate pricing to manage limited household budgets.

“The consequences of these overcharges fall most heavily on shoppers living on razor-thin margins— families who carefully budget every dollar and rely on accurate, transparent pricing simply to meet their day-to-day needs,” the complaint stated.

Under the proposed settlement, Family Dollar must establish a $40 million consumer redress fund intended to compensate eligible customers affected by the alleged pricing practices.

The retailer will also be required to provide $20 million in consumer discounts and pay another $3 million to the 17 states involved in the case.

Eligible customers will receive notifications explaining their rights and providing instructions for submitting claims to the compensation fund.

Beyond the financial settlement, Family Dollar must implement a pricing accuracy program that will be independently assessed to ensure stores consistently charge customers the correct amounts.

The program will require the tech giant and its employees to verify advertised prices and charge customers the lowest displayed price. The proposed order also prohibits Family Dollar from misrepresenting prices in its stores or promotional materials.

“Today’s order will ensure Family Dollar stops deceiving consumers about its prices and returns money to consumers harmed by these practices,” stated Christopher Mufarrige, director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection.

Family Dollar neither admitted nor denied the allegations in the complaint, except as specified in the proposed order, which still requires approval from a federal district court judge.

The retailer operates approximately 7,000 stores nationwide, according to the FTC complaint.

In a recent development, the settlement also comes as federal regulators pursue a separate case against Dollar General, which was sued by the FTC on October 26 over allegations of deceptive pricing and overcharging customers. That case remains ongoing.
