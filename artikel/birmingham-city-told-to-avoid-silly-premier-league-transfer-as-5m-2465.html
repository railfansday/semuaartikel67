Birmingham City's season has got off to a flyer, and now plenty of discourse throughout the November international break has revolved around how Chris Davies could further strengthen his squad in the January transfer window.

Blues' recent form sees them top the Championship on goal difference ahead of Middlesbrough and Stoke City, and they return to action this weekend to take on the latter at the Bet365 Stadium.

Not many outside the walls of St. Andrew's @ Knighthead Park envisaged such a remarkable start, but supporters in this part of the world are now dreaming of a first Premier League season since 2011.

As well as looking assured in defence, Davies' tactical reshuffles in recent months have made Birmingham a real threat in their attacking play, particularly on the counter-attack through the speed and direct nature of Liam Millar, Patrick Roberts, Jay Stansfield and August Priske.

Millar's predicament, in particular, has been a hot topic of debate in recent weeks given the nature of his loan move from Premier League newcomers, Hull City.

The Canadian international's impact in B9 has slightly decreased in recent weeks compared to his flying start, but Football League World's Birmingham City fan pundit, Jason Moore, has issued the sum of cash Blues should look to front up for his services.

Millar, 27, signed for Hull on a three-year contract from FC Basel in August 2024, making just 53 appearances at the MKM Stadium in the process due to a serious injury.

The 48-time international was a regular in Sergej Jakirovic's play-off winning team last season, but was loaned out to Birmingham in September despite scoring what could be a crucial winner for the Tigers against Coventry City on August 29th.

He has since registered four goals and three assists in 11 games for Blues, and when asked how much money the club should pay for a permanent transfer, Moore told FLW: "His current form, where he's contract-wise and the relationship he seems to have at Hull, I think £5m is fair for all parties involved.

"He started really well for us, but he's not banging in loads and loads of goals. I know that sounds harsh because he has had a very good start and he does look a threat. But I just think £5m is fair," he added.

Sources indicate that "Hull get a decent value, his contract's running out. I know they have the extension on him, but I do think it's a fair price all-round, to be fair. It depends on how much more they want.

According to recent reports, "If they want £7m, I'd be looking at an add-ons deal potentially. But, I think £5-7m is more than fair. Anything else, you start to get a little bit silly.

Industry observers note that "It all depends on where we are as well come January. If we're making a real push to get promoted, then do you want to put that money elsewhere because we've got Millar and then revisit in the summer?

"I think it depends on where we are and what the asking price is. But, right now, I think £5-7m is probably fair.".

In September, Birmingham fended off competition from both Middlesbrough and Stoke for Millar's services, and it is a deal which has certainly paid off thus far.

Davies has two experienced options to select from on the left flank, with Demarai Gray also scoring three times this season after previous injury concerns.

However, it is clear how much attention to detail went into making the deal possible, with the Blues boss revealing after the loan was confirmed: "I've tried to get him in a few transfer windows now - but never been able to.

According to recent reports, "He's a player with a lot of experience of this league. We've had to be patient and finally we've got him. And the fact that he's coming to us indicates a lot, as he had a lot of options.".

With Hull now in the Premier League, the market valuations of many players at the MKM Stadium have increased, and Millar was no exception to that rule, with it being claimed his current value sits at £4.1m.

Sources indicate that that, of course, could increase again if his output at St. Andrew's continues to be strong between now and May in Birmingham's own promotion push.

Hull supporters were recently left perplexed by the Canadian's admissions in a post-match interview, with some even debating that he would never play for the club again.

However, the Tigers still have plenty to say when it comes to his future, as if they extend his contract until 2028, Blues would certainly be forced to cough up a greater sum of cash for his services.
