AO3 is back up, sparking celebrations on and offline

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, archive of Our Own (AO3) is back up, finally ending its lengthy outage after over two weeks. The Hugo Award-winning fanfiction website went down due to a security incident late last month, and was expected to take up to four weeks to return. Fortunately it didn't take quite so long, with users now taking to social media to celebrate AO3's revival.

Non-profit parent organisation the Organization for Transformative Works (OTW) unveiled AO3's triumphant return on Sunday, Nov. 15 at 3:00 a.m. ET / 12:00 a.m. PT, almost exactly 17 days since it first confirmed the outage on Oct. 29 at 3:13 a.m. ET / 12:13 PT. This was also a mere day after the website turned 17, owners having lamented that AO3 spent its birthday "in a coma.".

Now AO3 has woken up, and while some features have been temporarily disabled to get the website back up more quickly, OTW states that no data was lost.

"Your works, bookmarks, kudos, etc., are still safe," OTW said in its update announcing AO3's return.

AO3's return is exciting news for millions of people. The internet went through a multi-fandom meltdown during the AO3 outage, which Forbes previously described as "the gold standard of fanfiction sites." Fanfiction-deprived fans took to sharing saved stories among themselves, while some even flocked to Tumblr for their fandom fix.

As such, the news of AO3's recovery quickly spread, with some fanfiction readers rushing to spread the word on social media like Paul Revere on his midnight ride.

Industry observers note that while many in the U.S. woke up to the news of AO3's recovery, others found out while otherwise going about their day. Attendees of a Yves concert in Melbourne, Australia learned of the development after X user @Alyssa_Aroha held up her phone in the crowd to display the words, "AO3 IS BACK UP." Video of this has since gone viral, racking up over 1.1 million views on X, with attendees reportedly having screamed and cheered at the news.

@Alyssa_Aroha herself found out about AO3's return from X, quickly telling her friend before holding up her now viral sign.

Sources indicate that "I go to a lot of K-pop concerts and people like to hold up signs to make the crowd cheer while waiting," @Alyssa_Aroha told Mashable. "I knew the reception in the venue [170 Russel] was bad so I held it up. No-one saw at first till my friend laughed and took a photo, then someone else yelled in surprise and suddenly EVERYONE was taking videos and photos with flash and cheering...

According to recent reports, "Fan spaces are so important and it's so lovely that a space that is so accessible for people around the world to share art with each other is so widely beloved. [Also] Yves' concert was incredible.".

Not everybody at the K-pop concert understood the significance of @Alyssa_Aroha's message. Concertgoer and X user @gyublickyy noted that "the dude behind me was a self proclaimed 'the only straight person in a yves concert' and he was going what the hell is ao3." But for many others it was a moment of collective joy and community.

It has been revealed that "In the Yves concert, waiting for it to start, and people start screaming so I assume she's come out early," posted @himejoshiorbit. "No. AO3 is back.".

Meanwhile, attendees at Filipino komiks and art market PasKomiket reportedly learned of AO3's return from organisers who revealed it over their PA system. The news prompted cheers from the crowd, many of whom immediately attempted to access the website on their phones.

Recent findings suggest that fortunately, fans have largely been understanding and patient, with many simply happy that AO3 is back. OTW has stated that its volunteers are continuing to work on restoring AO3's functionality, and will provide further updates as they come.

Recent findings suggest that AO3 celebrated 11 million registered owners in June, though it's likely that an even higher number of people missed it while it was down. AO3 only requires registration for publishing fanfiction, not reading it, OTW having reported that AO3 received an average of around 108 million daily page views in 2025.
