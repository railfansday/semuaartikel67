Nvidia Earnings Could Decide the Next Move for AI Stocks: 3 Things to Watch

Recent findings suggest that nvidia earnings could determine whether Wall Street’s AI trade stages a recovery or faces another wave of selling. The chipmaker is preparing to report its fiscal third-quarter 2027 results on November 17, with analysts expecting revenue of nearly $109 billion. However, strong financial results may not be enough to satisfy investors. Concerns surrounding AI spending, rising memory costs and leadership uncertainty have increased the pressure on the company. With Nvidia shares facing renewed volatility, can its latest earnings restore confidence in AI stocks, or is Wall Street preparing for another sell-off?

It has been revealed that nvidia faces exceptionally high expectations heading into its latest earnings report. Wall Street analysts expect the manufacturer to generate approximately $108.85 billion in revenue and $2.47 in adjusted earnings per share for fiscal Q3 2027. That would represent revenue growth of roughly 91% from $57.01 billion reported in the same quarter last year.

The manufacturer had previously guided for third-quarter revenue of $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, putting its official forecast broadly in line with analyst expectations. The previous quarter provides a strong benchmark. Nvidia reported $96.2 billion in Q2 FY2027 revenue, up 106% year over year, while adjusted earnings reached $2.22 per share. Its Data Center business generated $89 billion, accounting for the overwhelming majority of quarterly revenue.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia’s Data Center business remains the biggest driver of its valuation, making this segment the most crucial part of the upcoming earnings report. Analyst estimates put fiscal Q3 Data Center revenue at approximately $101.8 billion. Demand for Blackwell GPUs and expanding AI infrastructure investments from cloud providers are expected to support the increase.

Sources indicate that investors will also examine the company’s Vera Rubin platform, which is expected to become an increasingly key contributor to future revenue. Nvidia has remarked that Vera Rubin is entering production, while its earlier guidance indicated that the platform could account for around 20% of Data Center revenue in Q3.

It has been revealed that the bigger question is whether hyperscalers and AI companies will continue spending aggressively on computing capacity. Microsoft, Amazon, Google and other major technology companies remain crucial customers in the AI infrastructure cycle.

It has been revealed that if Nvidia signals that demand remains strong into fiscal 2028, it could support sentiment across the semiconductor sector. Any indication of slower orders or delayed infrastructure spending, however, could pressure not only NVDA but also AMD, Broadcom and other AI-linked stocks.

Nvidia’s earnings call will face another crucial test: whether rising costs and business uncertainty are beginning to affect its growth outlook. The firm previously guided for a non-GAAP gross margin of approximately 74% in fiscal Q3. However, management also warned that higher memory costs could push margins lower in the following quarter, with a projected range of 71%–72% for Q4 FY2027.

According to recent reports, china exposure is another concern investors will monitor. Nvidia’s previous Q3 revenue outlook did not assume any Data Center compute revenue from China, leaving investors to assess how much additional growth could come from other markets.

Recent findings suggest that leadership uncertainty adds another layer to the earnings event. Recent reports surrounding Jensen Huang’s departure and the company’s leadership transition have increased investor concerns ahead of the results. Interim leadership will need to reassure investors about product execution, customer relationships and the company’s long-term strategy.

Recent findings suggest that the earnings call could therefore matter as much as the financial numbers. Investors will be looking for clear answers on future demand, margins and management’s confidence in the AI growth cycle.

Nvidia stock is approaching a decisive earnings test after losing nearly one-third of its value from its October record high. NVDA stock is trading around $159.92 on November 16, down approximately 32% from the October 6 peak of $243.37. The recent decline has brought the stock close to its 52-week low of $148.80.
