Viral GTA 6 Competitor Gets Promising Anti-Cheat Patch

Rob Run is a free-to-play crime sandbox where you participate in bank robberies, raids, shootouts, and risk losing everything in lobbies of up to 5,000 players. Create your own hideout, upgrade your base, and even join a clan to become the most powerful crime family ever seen. It has a familiar progression system among fans of survival games, thanks to the presence of server wipes, allowing you to write fresh stories every time.

Not only that, but Rob Run has voice chat, so all players can communicate out in the world, set up RP scenarios, and share hilarious encounters.

If you’re not playing GTA 6 or you’re waiting for the fresh era of GTA Online to begin, Rob Run is a solid alternative. However, the biggest criminals right now are the cheaters who are ruining games for everybody else.

At the time of writing, Rob Run’s Steam reviews sit at “Mixed,” with the vast majority of negative reviews pointing out the prevalence of cheaters. In response, Kefir has announced upcoming measures in an attempt to nip the problem in the bud.

The team behind Rob Run is prioritizing strengthening its defenses against cheaters, so much so that an upcoming revision that’s expected to deploy newly introduced in-game mechanics has been temporarily delayed. As well as this, discussions are being had about increased security measures with the Easy Anti-Cheat team, developing their own cheat-detection tools, and expanding the in-house anti-cheat team to deal with the onslaught of hackers.

According to Kefir, they have banned over 200,000 accounts so far, but “we know that in a free-to-play game, a standard ban isn't enough — cheaters can rapidly create a new account and jump right back in. To combat this, we’re exploring hardware bans. Because separate servers with stricter access requirements can be launched much faster, we blueprint to roll those out first.”.

According to recent reports, the first planned solution is to make server access require either a minimum amount of jail time or a certain total of in-game purchases. Unlike the majority of other crime games, jail time is actually something you want to grow in Rob Run. The more wealth you have when the wipe sweeps in, the longer your prison sentence will be.

Players on these restricted servers will be matchmade separately from standard servers, including in Looty Bay. Depending on the minimum amount of jail time required, cheaters could be less incentivized by having to go through quite the grind before reaching regular lobbies.

Of course, there’s a lot more work to be done in order to make Rob Run a zone free of cheaters. This isn’t Kefir’s first rodeo with hackers either, as their mobile game, Last Day on Earth: Survival, has also had its fair share of cheaters.

Hopefully, Kefir’s efforts pay off, but cheating remains one of the biggest problems in multiplayer gaming, especially in free-to-play titles. Counter-Strike 2 and Call of Duty: Warzone are just two such games that have been grappling with cheaters over the years. Cheat providers continue to evolve, despite developers rolling out upcoming anti-cheat updates.

On November 17, Rob Run peaked at 549,661 concurrent players on Steam according to SteamDB. After such a strong start, it would be a real shame if the game is unable to reach its full potential due to cheaters holding it back. So far, the developers have kept communication open with players, which does bode well through these struggles.
