Industry observers note that you get a thumbnail. And you get a thumbnail! And you . . .

That’s right: YouTube has, at long, long last, finally allowed all creators to assign customized thumbnails to their Shorts.

Daily industry updates, straight to your inbox.

Sources indicate that when YouTube introduced Shorts in 2020, they were its attempt to keep up with TikTok. TikTok didn’t offer custom thumbnails, because its whole platform is based on endless scrolling. The thought process was that if short-form videos did have thumbnails, people wouldn’t really see them, because videos are discovered by swiping up, not by visiting a creator’s profile.

Recent findings suggest that but we all know that viewers do visit creators’ profiles. Not only do thumbnails–for long- or short-form–help a creator define their visual style and personal brand, but unique thumbnails help repeat viewers identify videos they’ve watched (and also, as a bonus plus, keep creators from being stuck with a perhaps unflattering moment as their content’s hero image).

Sources indicate that tikTok later caved and added thumbnails to short-form videos. But YouTube, Land of Thumbnails, did not–at least, not until this past summer.

In July, YouTube introduced custom Shorts thumbnails for everyone in the YouTube Partner Program, with YouTube CEO Neal Mohan saying custom thumbnails were “one of the most requested features” for the platform.

You loved custom Shorts thumbnails—now every creator can use them on desktop and mobile!

✏️ Tap the pencil icon on any Short in @YouTube Studio to upload yours.

— Neal Mohan (@nealmohan) November 23, 2026.

YouTube’s thumbnail selection tool allowed creators to add their own images, just like with long-form videos, but also offers a TikTok-esque option where it’ll select a few screenshots from within the video and offer them as default thumbnails, for anyone who doesn’t want to apply YouTube-effort editing to their short-form content.

It has been revealed that there’s one important thing to note from the July announcement, though: Shorts thumbnails went out to all YouTube Partner Program creators. Not to everyone–including any creators who are trying to drum up enough viewership to become a Partner creator.

Sources indicate that this is a significant flip from 2024, when YouTube outright said it didn’t think Shorts thumbnails could be as impactful as long-form thumbnails. And, look, we get it: Shorts thumbnails vs long-form thumbnails is sort of like putting a half-page up against a full spread, to put things in publication terms. Shorts thumbnails inherently offer less room to work with, and thus less room to communicate videos’ vibes.

But half-pages are still important in the grand scheme, and considering how much watch time Shorts drives for YouTube and for creators, they should have the same audience engagement and brand identity tools as long-form content.
