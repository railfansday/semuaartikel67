It has been revealed that if you don’t know what a Rotato is, allow me to introduce you. This rather bare-bones machine will seamlessly peel your potatoes (and other fruits and veggies, too) in just seconds—and the entertainment value is off the charts.

Sources indicate that one day while I was visiting my grandparents, I watched my grandfather use his Rotato to peel enough potatoes for a large batch of latkes, then fry up all the peels. His giant batches allowed him to freeze his latkes and enjoy them for months on end. But why spend time peeling all those potatoes yourself when you could whip out a handy gadget that does it all for you?

The Rotato is a mesmerizing device. Sure, it’s a single-use kitchen product, but I could literally watch this baby peel things for hours. Since I first expressed my awe and received one of my very own (along with my parents, sister, and cousins), I’ve tried the Rotato on all kinds of produce.

In a recent development, mangos? No problem. Apples? Easy as 1-2-3. Any time I’m making a dish that needs something peeled in bulk, the Rotato is my go-to gadget. And my entire family has hopped on board.

In a recent development, using it reminds me of putting on a record. You simply place your potato (or veggie of choice) on the spike at the bottom of the device, lower the top spike to hold it in place, and position the peeling blade against the top of the potato. Just press the red button, and the machine does the rest, automatically stopping when the cutter arm reaches the bottom.

And once you’ve watched it tackle a potato, it’s hard not to wonder what other fruits and vegetables you can try next.

According to the Rotato’s instruction manual, it can also peel cucumbers, beets, turnips, avocados, eggplants, squash, zucchini, tomatoes, apples, pears, peaches, kiwis, lemons, limes, and oranges—way more than I even imagined. I’ll have to start dreaming up fresh recipes.

It has been revealed that since discovering the magic of the Rotato, I’ve used it to make mashed potatoes for Friendsgiving, fruit salad, and much more. Sure, it doesn’t get used consistently year-round, but when you’re cooking for a crew, it’s the handiest thing in the kitchen.
