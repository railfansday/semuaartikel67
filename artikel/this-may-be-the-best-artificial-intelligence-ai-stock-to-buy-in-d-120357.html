Micron Technology and Oracle are poised to release their quarterly reports next month, and both artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure companies will be looking for a turnaround in their fortunes following a major slide in their stock prices lately.

However, weak investor sentiment toward AI hardware stocks may make it difficult for these stocks to rally, even if they deliver strong results and guidance. That's why I think Snowflake (SNOW -5.44%) could be a safer investment compared to Oracle and Micron in December.

In a recent development, the iShares Semiconductor ETF has lost 28% of its value over the past six months. Investors have been booking profits in semiconductor stocks amid concerns about the state of AI infrastructure spending in a higher-interest rate environment. Software stocks have fared substantially better. The iShares Expanded Tech-Software Sector ETF, which tracks North American software companies, has fared better with a 1.5% gain over the past six months.

Recent findings suggest that it is easy to see why that's the case. Companies offering AI products and solutions are witnessing solid growth in revenue and earnings, suggesting that enterprises and governments have been adopting the technology to unlock productivity gains. Snowflake has been a beneficiary of the growing AI adoption, and that reflects on its stock market performance.

NYSE · As of 11:16 AM ET 11/25/26.

Snowflake stock has surged by 57% over the past six months, while the Nasdaq-100 Technology Sector index has lost 17% over the same period. The company will release results for the third quarter of fiscal 2027 on Dec. 2, and I won't be surprised to see its shares get a nice shot in the arm.

It has been revealed that snowflake's fiscal Q2 results, which were released in early September, sent the stock soaring. The company beat Wall Street's expectations and raised its full-year guidance, driven by growing demand for its AI solutions.

Customers can use Snowflake's cloud platform to build AI agents and applications using their proprietary data. The demand for its AI tools is improving at a nice clip, helping Snowflake attract more customers and land bigger contracts with existing customers. The organization's customer base increased by 32% year over year in fiscal Q2 to just over 14,500. Meanwhile, its net revenue retention rate of 126% suggests that existing customers gave Snowflake more business.

It has been revealed that snowflake management remarked on the September earnings call that "growth in both migrations and AI use cases" led to the expansion in its net revenue retention rate. Given that Snowflake saw a sharp jump in new customers last quarter, I won't be surprised to see the manufacturer win more business from them as they expand adoption of its AI solutions.

In a recent development, for instance, management notes that more than 9,100 customer accounts were using its Snowflake CoCo AI coding agent in fiscal Q2, while 5,800 customer accounts were using the CoWork AI platform. So, there are still many customer accounts to which Snowflake can cross-sell its AI solutions. That's why I think the organization can outperform expectations and raise guidance on Dec. 2.

The organization anticipates an increase of 37% to 38% in product revenue for fiscal Q3 to $1.59 billion. That would be a slight improvement over Snowflake's 37% product revenue growth in fiscal Q2. Moreover, Snowflake is launching fresh products at a solid pace this year. It has made more than 330 product capabilities generally available to customers in the first half of fiscal 2027, up 35% from the year-ago period.

The new offerings should increase Snowflake's cross-selling opportunities, eventually boosting its margins and driving stronger earnings growth. Analysts are expecting Snowflake's earnings per share (EPS) to increase by 78% in the current fiscal year to $2.23. The estimates for the next couple of years have been inching higher, as seen in the following chart.

Sources indicate that there is a strong chance Snowflake will deliver stronger-than-expected earnings growth in the future, driven by improving adoption of its AI services. Moreover, Wall Street expects the stock to surge by 62% over the next 12 months, based on its median price target of $440, according to CNN. So, this AI stock can still deliver incredible gains despite rising impressively over the past six months.

As such, buying Snowflake ahead of its upcoming earnings report could be a smart move, as another beat-and-raise quarter could send its shares soaring.
