Auto-reloads YouTube pages when an ad plays — preserving your playback position. Punish the ads.

Sources indicate that ad density on YouTube is channel-dependent and changes over time — some channels front-load several ads, others run none. Traditional ad blockers can get detected by YouTube's anti-adblock system ("Ad blockers are not allowed here"), and the skip button takes a few seconds to appear. You sit there, hostage.

Reload the page. That's it. Two reloads and the ad is gone. Every time.

Recent findings suggest that 🎯 YouTube Ad Punisher automates this: the moment an ad appears, it captures your exact playback timestamp, reloads the page, and YouTube auto-seeks back to where you were. No ads. No waiting. No skip button hunting.

In a recent development, auto escape: after 3 reloads, the extension automatically switches to a helper tab for 4 seconds (George's discovery, now automated), then switches back. No manual tab switching needed.

Ads keep coming after reload? Switch to another tab for a few seconds, then switch back. Chrome throttles background tabs, which lets the ad state settle — George discovered this works every time.

Incognito: see the Incognito section above.

Reload (primary) — reloads the page 1-3 times. YouTube re-evaluates ad serving on each reload; after 1-2 cycles the ad slot is empty and the video plays clean. Timestamp is preserved via &t=Xs URL param + JS seek.

yout-ube.com redirect (fallback) — if reloads don't clear the ad after 3 attempts, redirects to yout-ube.com (an ad-free YouTube mirror). The URL includes &loop=0 to prevent the infinite replay matter that the mirror has by default.

Detect — watches for .ad-showing class, skip buttons, and ad overlays (every 500ms + MutationObserver).

Capture — grabs video.currentTime and saves it to sessionStorage.

Reload — location.replace() with &t=Xs in the URL.

Restore — YouTube auto-seeks to the timestamp; playback resumes.

Cooldown — 3-second guard prevents reload loops.

YouTube's ad system serves ads as part of the page load. When you reload, YouTube re-evaluates whether to serve an ad based on your session state. After 1-2 reloads, the ad slot is typically empty and the video plays directly. This is a client-side, zero-cost workaround — no filter lists, no network blocking, no detection by YouTube's anti-adblock system.

In a recent development, if millions of users did this, each reload = a full page load = server request. YouTube would see a spike in page loads but a drop in ad impressions. It's not a DDoS (no amplification, no botnet), but it's a passive protest: every reload is a vote that says "I will not watch your ad." The ad revenue model breaks when reloads are cheaper than ad views.
