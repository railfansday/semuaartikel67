‘In Starland’: How Ray Panthaki, in His Directing Debut, Cast Clarence Maclin in His First Lead Role and Made a Very Personal Film Built on Meritocracy

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that ray Panthaki (Boiling Point, Colette, Gangs of London) makes us think about modern culture in his feature directorial debut, In Starland. Clarence Maclin (Sing Sing) stars in his, unbelievably, first lead role. The cast also features Bella Maclean (Rivals) and Miss Benny (Club Kid), among others.

Maclin is Ernie, 50, who lives a quiet and unremarkable life – until he becomes involved with online content creators in their 20s. “Finding a place where he finally belongs, Ernie discovers a world of hedonistic pleasure and voyeurism in which every movement he makes begins to blur the boundaries between performance and reality,” reads a synopsis on the website of the 30th edition of the Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival (PÖFF) in Estonia, where the film celebrates its world premiere on Thursday, Nov. 19. “As he searches for an identity lost in a mysterious past, fractures begin to appear between his newfound friends and the town of which he has been a part of for so long, and Ernie begins to wonder about the cost of his newfound notoriety.” Related Stories Movies European Lifetime Achievement Award for the Dardenne Brothers Music Harry Styles, Billie Eilish and Sam Fender Set to Headline Glastonbury 2027.

Panthaki wrote the script with Jason Kavan and also produced the film with Daniel Khalili, Daisy Allsop and Victor Paul Wajnberg. Jaime Ackroyd is the cinematographer, with editing by Iain Kitching. Le Bateau Lavoir, Archface Films and Christine Vachon‘s Killer Films produced In Starland.

“I haven’t slept properly in years,” Panthaki shares. “But I made a film that I wanted to make with 100 percent creative control. I didn’t want to sell myself out to do the very thing I’m talking about in the movie – about numbers dictating life.”.

When he wrote and directed the short film Life Sentence, Panthaki realized that he loved those behind-the-scenes roles. “That film was about something that was really personal to me,” Panthaki tells THR in Tallinn. “And when it came to getting made, I didn’t want anyone else to make it. I’ve lost someone close to me, so this was a story that I had to tell. It was a five-day shoot on a short film with a minuscule budget, and I realized when I stepped on set that this, in some ways, comes more naturally to me than acting. So I realized it was a massive calling.”.

In Starland is also very close to Panthaki’s heart, comparing it to a child. “I realized that the first feature film, which is why it’s taken so many years, had to be something very personal, something that I wanted to say about the world,” he shares.

And he realized early on that his directorial feature debut came with bigger-picture goals. “I wanted to correct some wrongs that have happened to me as an actor in my career,” he tells THR. “I’ve found it frustrating sometimes that this industry is not a meritocracy. I’ve been offered roles where everyone on the film wants you, and then the head of the studio notes, ‘Oh, actually, we want a bigger name. And so this was an opportunity for me to really get the right people for the right roles.”.

In that context, Panthaki also calls the film “non-dependent because I don’t think independent gives the true story. “If a film that has got public funding is called ‘independent,’ then this has to have a different name, right? So I’ve been calling it non-dependent, which has been hard and frustrating at times. But the payoff for that was that I’m allowed to do things that other films can’t. For example, I was allowed to make this a meritocracy, and I’m so proud of that Clarence was the only actor out of the 11 lead actors whose previous work I had seen [before making In Starland]. When I watched Sing Sing, I was like, ‘This guy is captivating.’ There is something in his eyes that you just cannot deny.”.

Industry observers note that maclin lauds Panthaki for his conviction and vision. “Ray sees the world in his own way, and you can feel that in this film,” the star tells THR. “He knows what he wants, but he’s also open to what you bring to it. He gives you room to be yourself, to try things, to find something you maybe didn’t even know was there. And that’s where the magic happens.”.

In Starland premieres at a time when more of its cast members have broken out. “Miss Benny is, of course, in Club Kid,” notes Panthaki. “Then you got Bella, who is everywhere, and Tai’mua, this amazing Hawaiian actor, was in an Oscar-nominated short [Jane Austen’s Period Drama] playing a lead role. Everyone’s having their moment.”.

Recent findings suggest that while In Starland follows a group of young creators obsessed with views, likes and followers, “I didn’t want to know about the cast’s follower count,” the director recalls. “It was only about whether that person would earn the role in the room, and they did.”.

Sources indicate that and he needed actors happy to improvise. “That is how I like to do things,” the writer-director explains. “Once I have a script, I like to find truth and play around and naturalize things as much as possible. So I needed actors who were able to do that. I needed actors that I work very much on instinct, so I could turn up on a day and say, ‘This doesn’t feel right to me today. We’re going to do something completely different.’ That happened a number of times on set.”.

That and the overall non-dependent nature of In Starland made the experience on set different. “There was something special about making this film,” Maclin shares. “We didn’t have all the things a significant Hollywood production might have, but we had people who believed in what we were doing. And Ray, he wasn’t gonna compromise on what he wanted this film to be. You gotta respect that. When somebody believes in something that much, you wanna be part of it.”.

In Starland leaves certain things open, unanswered or answerable in different ways. “I want people to have different opinions of what happens and instigate conversation,” Panthaki explains.

Industry observers note that “What I love about In Starland is it ain’t trying to give you all the answers,” Maclin also emphasizes. “There’s some funny moments, there’s some painful moments, but there’s a whole lot of humanity in there. It makes you look at people a little differently, maybe think about things you hadn’t thought about before. And to me, that’s what storytelling is about.”.

How did Killer Films end up boarding In Starland? “We asked Christine if she’d take a look and read the script and watch what we shot,” Panthaki says. “We had a conversation with her and over many conversations she just believed in the project. For me, that someone of that clout and and someone who has that amazing body of work believed in it is the biggest seal of approval. I’m so grateful to her and to Mason Plotts [, head of film at Killer Films].”.

Audience will recognize 1980s music throughout In Starland. Given his own love for it, Panthaki says that he simply had to make one of his young characters be obsessed with music from that period. One of the key tunes in the film is the Tears for Fears hit “Everybody Wants to Rule the World.”.

In a recent development, the director recalls reached out to the band himself. “I just wrote a letter, and I just told them why I’m doing this, what I’m trying to say about the world, and I don’t know if they ever read the letter or not,” Panthaki explains. “I actually wrote a number of letters during this process, and they came back and said, ‘Yeah, we’re willing to give the song to you for this amount of money.” I was never asking for it for free. I was asking for it for what we could afford within our means, and and they agreed to it.”.

Sources indicate that asked about the film’s title, Panthaki tells THR: “I wanted something that felt poetic and really explained what the film was in a number of ways, and it just came to me. In Starland was a play on words because if you put those words together, it almost sounds like Instaland, so it has the Instagram element, and Ernie was in starland – but I don’t want to talk about a key twist there. I wanted to have a duality in the title, much like the lives of the people in the film.”.

Industry observers note that could we see another feature directed by Panthaki? “Writing, directing and producing this film has been the hardest thing I’ve ever done in my life,” he shares. “Everything that has happened in this movie over the past seven years has been 10 times harder because we were doing it ourselves. So, will I do it again? I think yes. But I don’t know if I can do it again with so little time and money. I think I’ll need more infrastructure in place to be able to do it again.”.

Recent findings suggest that that said, Panthaki’s passion is coming through again. “I have ideas that are burning in me, and it has to be something I want to say about the world,” he tells THR. “It has to be something important that I can say, and something that I can do well. So I’ve got a couple of ideas that I’m working on. But for right now, get me back to acting!”.
