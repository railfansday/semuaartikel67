Movado’s New Tempo-Matic Watch Is Inspired by an Iconic Piece from the ’70s

Movado was founded in 1881 in La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland, which isn’t a particularly odd backstory for a heritage Swiss watch brand. But it has been American owned since 1983 and has a distinct quality tied deeply to its long history in the U.S. market—which puts it slightly outside the predictable purview of many watch collectors.

Right now, a growing band of horological nerds is finding rich pickings in the Movado back catalog and pushing up vintage prices considerably—especially for its mid-century chronographs and its legendary Museum Watch (which celebrates 80 years in 2027; more of that to come) in the process.

In a recent development, it’s not only the collectors who are digging into Movado’s past, however. Last month, the brand released the Heritage Tempo-Matic ($995–$1,095), a re-creation of a watch that merges sporty and retro design in a neat and slinky package. The Tempo-Matic, Movado’s first automatic movement, was released in 1945 and went on to power a broad range of Movado’s wristwatches. But the rounded yet boxy “TV” shape of the latest Heritage Tempo-Matic is unmistakably 1970s, when watch design took a giant leap forward into sexy new territory.

The 2026 version is available in three executions, each powered by the reliable Sellita SW200-1B movement and fitted with integrated steel bracelets that positively scream seventies. Two of them come in brushed steel with vertically brushed burgundy or blue dials. A third—which, we must admit, is our favorite—is gold-tone PVD with a warm gray dial. You’d be hard-pressed to find such an elegant, authentic, and affordable homage to the 1970s—a decade that’s at the moment a very hot motif in watch-collecting circles—anywhere else.
