Roberta Groner had what she calls “a rocky road” for the year leading up to the Latest York City Marathon on November 1.

In her previous three attempts at the marathon, during which she had hoped to run an Olympic Marathon Trials qualifying time (2:37), she had racked up three DNFs in a row.

According to recent reports, she was in great shape for the Valencia Marathon in December 2025, having just run 1:12:21 for the half marathon, and she thought she might be in shape to better her lifetime PR in the marathon, which is 2:29:09, from 2019, when she was 41. Her conservative goal was getting the Trials time.

Sources indicate that but a warm day in Spain—about 75 degrees—and a brain lock about translating kilometers into miles caused her to step off the course late in the race. Her coach, Devon Martin, of the Central Park Track Club, still rues that one. “She had no idea she had seven minutes to spare at mile 18,” Martin mentioned.

According to recent reports, two weeks later, Groner hopped in the Marathon Project in Arizona. Martin had tried to convince her not to. Sure enough, her legs hadn’t recovered from the hard miles in Valencia, and she dropped out there, too.

It has been revealed that then came Grandma’s Marathon in June in Duluth, Minnesota. Groner felt off. She later realized that her body was adjusting to starting hormone replacement therapy for perimenopause symptoms. Another DNF.

By the time she got to New York City, her hometown race, Groner—a former nurse who is now a full-time coach with Central Park Track Club—was uncharacteristically a basket case.

According to recent reports, “I had to fight some mental demons, especially the week before,” she stated. “My coach was probably like, ‘Oh, I can't handle her.’”.

Groner ran with the elite women, always a small group in Upcoming York, which leaves those who can’t keep pace with the strange sensation of running a major-city marathon alone. She was well behind the leaders, running side-by-side with Steph Bruce for the first 17 miles, before Bruce fell back. Groner spent the next 9 miles with her eyes scanning the distance for any women she could pick off on the way to the finish.

She passed a handful of runners in the final stretch and finished 22nd in 2:35:55, her third Olympic Trials qualifier. She’ll be 50 when the race goes off in St. Louis. Dot McMahan of Michigan, who has qualified for her sixth trials, will be 51.

“I was able to race and compete, which was great,” Groner stated. “And yeah, it wasn't my fastest New York, but I was really happy with the performance. Knowing that my faster days could be done, I still want to race really well. To be able to pass a few people at the end. I’ll give it an A.”.

Recent findings suggest that but are her fastest days really behind her? Groner doesn’t necessarily think so. And she’s not backing off her goals just because she’s in her late 40s.

She had a decade away from running when her three sons—now 23, 20, and 19—were growing up, which makes her feel like a younger runner than she is. When she got back to running, most of her career highlights were after she turned 40. In 2019, she represented Team USA at the World Championships marathon in Doha, Qatar, finishing sixth in extreme heat and humidity. The race started at midnight to avoid the worst of the heat.

Since she’s been in the 45–49 age group, Groner has set 10 American records, at distances from the 3,000 meters to the marathon.

Although she says she takes days off more frequently now, she still routinely hits 90 miles per week. She’ll come into Manhattan several times a week from Latest Jersey to coach and train with the top men and women of the Central Park Track Club, many of whom lined the marathon course to cheer for her.

When Martin hired Groner as a coach at CPTC, Martin made her promise she wouldn’t give up on her running. Coaches can become so absorbed in their athletes’ needs, their own training takes a back seat. But for Groner, coaching others has made her even more coachable and tuned into her own abilities. Having a team full of training partners has been helpful, too.

Martin indicates she’s an inspiration to younger runners all over New York.

“We always joke, ‘When is this going to end?’” Martin said of Groner’s fast times. “She just keeps surprising, and she has changed women’s running in New York City. Women in their 30s and their 40s, they look at running differently now.”.

“She’s just a tough racer,” Martin added. “She’s so mentally strong, and just really believes she’s not done.”.
