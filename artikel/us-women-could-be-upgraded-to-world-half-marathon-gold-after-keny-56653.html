U.S. Women Could Be Upgraded to World Half Marathon Gold After Kenyan Runner’s EPO Ban

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Emma Grace Hurley, Weini Kelati, and Jess McClain already had plenty to celebrate after earning the United States its first women's team medal at September's World Road Running Championships in Copenhagen. But nearly two months after finishing with silver, the American trio could soon have gold added to their résumés.

Industry observers note that on Thursday, November 19, the Athletics Integrity Unit (AIU) declared that Kenya's Veronica Loleo had received a three-year doping ban after testing positive for erythropoietin (EPO), a prohibited substance. The decision officially disqualifies her second-place finish at the September 20 event, where her silver medal helped Kenya secure the women's team title. But with Loleo's result now thrown out, Kenya's team score has to be recalculated. Countries are ranked by the combined finishing times of their three fastest athletes, and Loleo’s removal means Kenya must now count Lilian Kasait Rengeruk, who finished 34th in 1:10:36, potentially lifting the U.S. to first place in the team standings.

According to recent reports, the Kenyan women originally led with a combined 3:17:33, ahead of the Americans' 3:19:51. But removing Loleo's 1:06:05 pushes Kenya's total to 3:22:04, leaving the U.S. in first place by two minutes and 13 seconds. Kenya would drop to second and Ethiopia would remain third, although World Athletics has yet to officially confirm the updated results.

Hurley recently acknowledged the uncertainty in a comment on Citius Mag's Instagram, noting that nobody can know exactly how the race would've unfolded without Loleo. Still, she admitted, "it would've been so cool to celebrate gold 🥹.".

Industry observers note that loleo's sample was collected during an out-of-competition test on September 6 ahead of the championships, two weeks prior to the competition. She initially told investigators she couldn't explain the positive result, mentioning an injection she'd received for an injury, but later admitted to the violation on October 30, which earned her a one-year reduction from the standard four-year suspension.

It has been revealed that loleo wasn't the only major Kenyan distance runner to receive a final decision Thursday. Peres Jepchirchir, the reigning world marathon champion and 2021 Olympic gold medalist, was also handed a three-year ban following an EPO finding.

“The Kenyan authorities have made a lot of progress in the fight against doping in the last five years, but these cases highlight that there is still a long way to go,” AIU chief executive Brett Clothier mentioned in a statement.

“The Kenyan government is aware of the current situation and has committed to further strengthen the Anti-Doping Agency of Kenya and take action to address the criminal networks that facilitate doping within the country.”.

In a recent development, jepchirchir, who had been provisionally suspended in October, has accepted the disqualification of her results dating back to July 17, including her August victory at the Sydney Marathon. Fellow Kenyan Irine Cheptai is expected to inherit that title.
