Alarms are one of the most key iPhone features, and iOS 27 offers a key feature that should help your alarm actually wake you: a new volume setting for alarms that’s independent of other volume settings.

Have you ever accidentally slept through an alarm? We’ve all done it. And most often for me, it’s because my alarm volume was unintentionally set to a very low level.

Recent findings suggest that I suspect that’s a pretty common reason for missed alarms. And in iOS 27, Apple finally has the fix—but it requires manually setting up.

Inside Settings ⇾ Sounds & Haptics, you can now set a volume for alarms that’s independent from the systemwide ringtone setting.

Industry observers note that iOS 27 has two new sections in this Settings screen: Alarms and Timers, and Alerts and System Sounds. Both include ‘Match Ringtone Volume’ toggles that are on by default. This means your volume for alarms, timers, alerts, and other system sounds are all determined by the ringtone volume.

But if you disable the Alarms and Timers toggle, you’ll get a fresh volume slider that applies only to those system functions, and won’t be impacted by your ringtone volume.

As seen above, this means you can crank the Alarms and Timers volume up to the maximum level without making every other system sound, including notifications and phone calls, be similarly deafening.

So the next time you sleep through your alarm, it won’t be because the alarm was too quiet.

In case you missed it, iOS 26 introduced a welcome feature that lets you set custom snooze durations for alarms, and also an option to have alarms trigger on your iPhone even if you’re wearing an Apple Watch.
