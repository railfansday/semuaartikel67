Being a member of Capitol society in Panem is an honor not to be taken lightly, at least if you’re Drusilla Sickle, played by screen legend Glenn Close in “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping.”.

In the book, Drusilla is the cruel and vain escort for Haymitch Abernathy (Joseph Zada) and the rest of the District 12 tributes in the 50th Hunger Games, where she uses over-the-top fashion to broadcast her social status. Close worked closely with costume designer Bina Daigeler and hair, makeup and prosthetics designer Natalie MacGowan Spencer to ensure the character translated properly from Suzanne Collins’ bestselling prequel novel to the screen. One challenge Daigeler faced was meeting Close’s request that each outfit felt connected, creating a through line that explained why Drusilla changes from one look to the next. Despite the drastic differences between each outfit, Daigeler established continuity through the character’s accessories, most notably a collection of gold medals and military-inspired epaulets that created a sense of authority.

“She wears all these medals, because somehow she thinks she is the top general of the capital,” Daigeler tells Variety.

And, not dissimilar to red-bottomed Louboutin shoes, Daigeler gave Drusilla dramatically long black nails that were painted red underneath to show her abundant wealth.

The character’s Capitol allegiance also went beyond her wardrobe, with Spencer overseeing her hair and makeup. While creating the looks, Spencer relied heavily on Collins’ source material to remain faithful to the author’s vision. Collins describes Drusilla as having implanted tabs that pull the skin on her face back, which Spencer wanted to honor. But because there wasn’t enough time each morning for an extensive prosthetics process, Spencer turned to Hollywood tape to tightly pull Close’s skin back, creating a distorted appearance. She later added tabs on the sides of Close’s face to complete the illusion, along with fake teeth that heightened Drusilla’s “scary” and “eerie” appearance.

“[Glenn] really wanted to pull it so taut that it changed all of her facial features, which it really did,” Spencer tells Variety.

Daigeler and Spencer’s shared mission: to create visuals that resonate with the franchise’s devoted fans — who “know their stuff inside out,” Spencer quips. “They’re so passionate.” When the book was released and casting announcements began rolling out, Spencer would regularly check social media to see what fans envisioned for the adaptation and what details they hoped to see on screen.

Below, Spencer and Daigeler break down the process of creating each of Drusilla’s costumes, hair and makeup, as well as the inspiration behind each look.

According to recent reports, drusilla makes her grand entrance at the District 12 reaping, wheeled into the frame wearing a bold yellow ensemble. The outfit is a stark contrast to the muted colors worn throughout Panem’s poorest district — the exact irony Spencer and Daigeler hoped to portray by making that the character’s brightest look in the film.

According to recent reports, “It shouldn’t have any practicality. That is why she was driven in,” Daigeler says of the tightly-cinched yellow coat dress. “It was supposed to be very stiff.”.

In a recent development, to complement the dress’ high collar, Spencer styled Close with an orange bob that also paid homage to the film’s ‘70s aesthetic. At Close’s request, the look was completed with a raven headpiece — which, to be clear, was not an intentional nod to Lenore Dove’s name poem.

“For me, that sort of represents the children of the district — something that’s taxidermied and dead on their head,” Spencer indicates about the headpiece. “And [Drusilla’s] so proud of that statement.”.

In a recent development, black medals and military-inspired shoulder plates further accentuated the ensemble, reinforcing Drusilla’s self-appointed authority. “She looked like a yellow general,” Daigeler adds.

In a recent development, when representing District 12 at the Capitol’s chariot parade, Drusilla’s color palette dims to an all-black look that publicly links her to the district’s coal-mining industry. To elevate the outfit and solidify her Capitol status, Daigeler experimented with different textures to create the illusion of leatherlike skin while also keeping her gold medals and fringed military epaulets.

Recent findings suggest that “We wanted to give it strength,” Daigeler claims of the outfit, which, she notes, also signals that things are getting more serious in the film’s storyline. “We wanted it to be a little bit aggressive and very demanding. And very Capitol.”.

In a recent development, close initially wanted to style the look with an extravagant, fluffy hat, but she and Spencer ultimately settled on a simpler black wig with pointed horns so as not to distract from Drusilla’s face. The shorter bangs draw more attention to the exaggerated eye makeup and the face-slimming tabs.

Drusilla settles on a softer green dress for the tributes’ interviews with Caesar Flickerman (Kieran Culkin), which once again challenged Daigeler to maintain the character’s threads between outfit changes.

In a recent development, “Where’s the strength? Where’s the craziness?” she asked herself when accessorizing Drusilla’s third look, a crepey, olive-hued gown with a high neck and massive, billowy sleeves.

This time, Daigeler forwent Drusilla’s medallions and instead made a statement by adding red gloves, tights and boots, paired with spiky necklaces.

It has been revealed that spencer leaned further into the softer ensemble by adding a wave to Drusilla’s hair, washed with a light shade of pink. “I really like fabrics and the colors and how the hair and makeup is going to be one big look altogether,” Spencer notes.

Drusilla’s final appearance comes after Haymitch is declared the victor of the 50th Hunger Games — or, at least in Drusilla’s mind, after she is declared the victor. Complete with a purple sash, the look was designed to make it seem as if Drusilla herself had won the games.

Industry observers note that this was the only outfit not described in Collins’ novel, giving Daigeler the creative freedom to execute her own vision. Knowing Drusilla would appear on the colorful stage, she wanted to put her in a bold red ensemble that would ensure she stood out.

Without a predetermined silhouette in mind, Daigeler drew inspiration from designers Alexander McQueen, Talyano and Balenciaga, as well as Renaissance-era shapes.

In a recent development, “I went to a beautiful fabric store in London, and I just found all these beautiful, different textured red fabrics,” Daigeler claims. “I didn’t have it clear what would be the silhouette of the dress, but I knew that it should be an evening gown and somehow huge and over the top.”.

Spencer completed the transformation by leaning into the showgirl-inspired makeup, settling on a bright red lip and feathered hair.

Recent findings suggest that “[Drusilla’s] like a performer. When you really do think about it, she’s a narcissist. ‘This is me. I’m bold. I’m dominant,’” Spencer states. “We had all the showgirls — all of those substantial colors and the feathers and everything else, so it’s almost like a carnival of entertainers coming together on the stage. The set design, the colors, all the background people in the crowd are so, so important. What you’re portraying is a performance. It’s entertainment, and that’s who they are.”.
