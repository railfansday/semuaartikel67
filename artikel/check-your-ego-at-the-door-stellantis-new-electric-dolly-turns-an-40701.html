Check your ego at the door: Stellantis’ new electric dolly turns ANY car into a tow rig

We all know the type: grizzled stubble, wraparound PitVipers, a MAGA hat quietly retired after last week’s deep-blue shellacking, and a pristine, polished pickup parked in the driveway that they “need for towing.” With Stellantis’ fresh patent for an AI-powered electric tow dolly that turns just about ANY vehicle into a capable tow rig, however, those might need a new to find a new excuse to keep that gas-guzzler.

Originally filed in 2023, Stellantis received US Patent No. 12,779,916 for its electric tow dolly on 03NOV2026. The patent covers a machine that’s designed to shoulder the trailer’s hitch load with its own ball hitch or fifth-wheel plate, using an on-board electric motor and battery to get the trailer going while its regenerative braking system and AI-backed steering and traction control system keeps everything pointed in the right direction.

In other words, the dolly takes on many of the jobs that traditionally require a big tow vehicle: providing the pulling power, managing the trailer’s weight and controlling its braking and traction. The vehicle up front doesn’t necessarily need a massive motor, heavy-duty suspension or oversized brakes. With a system like this, you could argue that the “tow vehicle” isn’t really a tow vehicle at all. It’s more of a “lead vehicle,” since the dolly is the one doing the towing … and it can follow just about anything – including the diminutive (and supremely affordable) Fiat 500e, which is also made by Stellantis.

Recent findings suggest that to be clear, this isn’t just Uncle Jojo making fun of the commuter conveys – Stellantis’ patent describes the dolly as being equipped with an autonomous control system using cameras, sensors, a controller, and an advanced, four wheel steer-by-wire system. It also specifically discusses how the dolly’s independently powered acceleration, braking and steering, and sway controls essentially remove the need to own a truck in the first place.

The dolly system’s truck bed sides and small size allow users to experience the functionality and utility of a truck only when they need it, allowing them to own a smaller, cheaper, and more fuel efficient vehicle that suits daily use needs.

Industry observers note that there are some pretty obvious practical questions still to answer, of course, including how much battery capacity the dolly would need, how much weight it could handle and how it would perform on long grades or in emergency braking situations. And, to be clear, a patent isn’t a product announcement or proof that Stellantis plans to put one of these things on sale – heck, if I were Stellantis’ CEO, I’d patent this thing just to prevent anyone else from building one so I could keep selling ancient, low-tech, high-margin Ram Trucks – but it’s a neat idea.

According to recent reports, okay, I like this idea and appreciate the problem it is trying to solve, but it has the same challenge as any electric-tow situation, which is the charging logistics. Even if you find a pull-through charger (very rare), if you are using an EV, you still need to charge two "vehicles," so you have to charge your car, then pull up and charge the dolly afterwards. Even if you are using a gas car, charging the dolly will be logistically challenging. Of course, that's a problem with an easy solution (more pull-through chargers), but also one that doesn't seem to be on any charging network's radar.

In a recent development, I think it’s neat, anyway. What do you guys think? Are you ready to trade in your substantial-battery Chevy Silverado EV or Volvo VNR 800 on a latest Topolino, or are you going to keep driving inefficient 99% of the time? Let us know, in the comments.
