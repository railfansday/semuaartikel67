Dawn of War 4's campaigns differ based on your choices: 'which character you choose and what decisions you make with them lead down different experiences of an overlapping story'

Sources indicate that the original Dawn of War's Winter Assault expansion added campaigns that let you play two factions at once, with different final missions based on which one you triumphed with. Dawn of War 2's campaign was even more player-led, with distress calls that had different outcomes based on what you prioritized and, in the Chaos Rising expansion, a morality mechanic with Corruption and Redemption points. It even chose a different character to be a traitor based on who gained the most Corruption by the story's end.

Recent findings suggest that I asked John French, Dawn of War 4's writer and a Warhammer novelist, whether Dawn of War 4 would follow this noble tradition by having player decisions alter the plot. "The player's decisions very much change the perspectives that they'll experience the game from," he replied. "So, depending on which heroes from each faction they choose, they'll have a very different experience of the game, in that they'll see different parts of the story and sometimes different views on the same events.".

It has been revealed that based on what we've seen of the Ork and Adeptus Mechanicus campaigns, that means partway through you'll be presented with a choice between two Commanders who have opposed goals and the one you pick will lead to missions with different objectives. Ork Beastboss Guzcutta is trying to unite his clan, the Snakebites, while his rival warboss, Gorgutz, is an off-world interloper trying to rebuild his damaged invasion force.

"With characters like Gorgutz and Guzcutta," French explained, "it can be as simple as one of them wants to do it the traditional, brutal ork way, and one of them wants to do it in a brutal but cunning way, so you have missions that focus on getting lots of shiny kit in one, while the other tries to get as many orks to follow them as possible.

Sources indicate that "In a more complex narrative, like the Adeptus Mechanicus campaign, Potentia-Delta-9's main drive is the quest for knowledge, and she is on a very different path from Skitarii Marshal Sek-Ix-23, who is very much about enforcing the orthodoxy. The story for both overlaps, but which character you choose and what decisions you make with them lead down different experiences of an overlapping story.".

Recent findings suggest that writing an RTS isn't all about plotting out the campaign missions. The Dawn of War games are legendary for their unit barks, like the saber-rattling Lord Commissar who shouts, from the top of an armored Chimera transport, "Drive me closer, I want to hit them with my sword!" Getting across so much personality in a short space is a real skill.

"You can do an awful lot with just a couple of words like 'More dakka!'," French said, "that tells you a huge amount about the orks on its own. Even with factions like the necrons, the way that the lower ranks respond to the higher ones with just 'Obedience,' one word, tells how their faction works.".

Part of the appeal of Warhammer is the berserk variety of its armies, each with its own highly thematic identity. You walk into a shop and see the stoic space marines alongside spiky Chaos daemons and units of Astra Militarum who look like they stepped out of a trench in World War I before being given laser rifles.

Sources indicate that "Dawn of War 4 is deeply Warhammer in that way because it's got all of these different textures of factions and characters next to each other," French said. "You have orks, and they are absolutely fixated on smashing everything they can in the pursuit of trying to have an even bigger fight. Then you've got the Adeptus Mechanicus, who revere machines as holy and worship the machine as god with complete sincerity and total conviction. Those two things literally exist side by side, along with alien races who have made themselves immortal by creating metal bodies with hyper advanced technology. And all of these different types of character come into conflict on one planet cut off in a sea of night—that's very Warhammer.".

Dawn of War 4 is due out on December 3 and will be available on Steam. Owners of the Commander Edition will get advance access and can play from November 30.
