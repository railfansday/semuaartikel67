Workday shares fall as soft subscription forecast overshadows third-quarter beat

UPDATED 17:27 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

Recent findings suggest that shares of human resources and finance software company Workday Inc. fell more than 7% in late trading today after a softer-than-expected subscription revenue forecast for the current quarter overshadowed fiscal third-quarter earnings and revenue that came in ahead of Wall Street’s targets.

For the quarter that ended on Oct. 31, Workday reported adjusted earnings per share of $2.82, up 22% from $2.32 in the same quarter of the previous year, on revenue of $2.705 billion, up 11.2% year-over-year. Analysts were expecting earnings of $2.75 per share on revenue of $2.69 billion.

In a recent development, subscription revenue rose 12.8% from a year earlier, to $2.532 billion. Net income on an unadjusted basis came to $237 million, down from $252 million in the same quarter a year ago. Included in the quarter were $51 million in restructuring expenses, which followed the manufacturer’s disclosure in late September that it would cut about 2.5% of its workforce, mostly in its product and technology team.

Sources indicate that total subscription revenue backlog grew 6.1%, to $27.548 billion, with the 12-month figure up 12.3%, to $9.216 billion. Free cash flow came to $691 million, up from $550 million. During the quarter, Workday spent $750 million buying back about 3.8 million shares.

Sources indicate that the quarter also saw Workday hold its annual Rising customer conference in Las Vegas in October, when the firm made Sana the new home experience for its platform. Built on technology from Sana Labs AB, which Workday acquired for about $1.1 billion last year, Sana is an AI interface that brings tasks, decisions, meetings and agent activity together in one place. Autopilot Agents, also debuted at the event, are designed to handle routine HR, finance and IT processes in the background.

It has been revealed that on the opening day of Rising, Workday declared that it had agreed to acquire agentic identity platform ConductorOne Inc., better known as C1.ai. That deal closed on Monday. A partnership with Anthropic PBC, also unveiled at Rising, is set to bring Claude together with Sana AI agents and Workday’s HR and finance data.

It has been revealed that “The past few quarters have been our strongest on the innovation and invention front, and in Q3, our AI products drove nearly $150 million in new ACV, more than three times what we delivered a year ago,” said co-founder and Chief Executive Aneel Bhusri, who returned to the top job in February after Carl Eschenbach stepped down. The priority now, Bhusri added, is to get more customers running agents in production.

According to Workday, the number of core customers that have bought at least one of its AI products rose nearly 25% from the previous quarter, to more than 2,200.

Pointing to what he called “ongoing adoption across our AI portfolio,” Chief Financial Officer Zane Rowe remarked the company is raising its full-year targets.

Recent findings suggest that for its fiscal fourth quarter, Workday expects subscription revenue of $2.6 billion, up 10%, and an adjusted operating margin of 31.7%. Full-year subscription revenue is now forecast at $9.958 billion, up 13%, compared with the $9.94 billion to $9.95 billion guided in August. The full-year adjusted operating margin forecast was also raised, to 31.5%, ahead of the 31.1% expected by analysts.

In a recent development, though the full-year numbers moved higher, the fourth-quarter subscription forecast came in narrowly below the $2.61 billion analysts had been expecting. Growth of 10% in the period would also be a step down from the 12.8% Workday posted in the third quarter.
