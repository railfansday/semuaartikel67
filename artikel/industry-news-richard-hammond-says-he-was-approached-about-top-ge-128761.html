Former “Top Gear” co-host Richard Hammond has revealed that he was approached about returning to the BBC‘s revived version of the car show, but declined because he’s “too old.”.

In a recent development, hammond appeared on Times Radio on Wednesday to discuss the series, shortly after it was revealed that Gordon Ramsay would be taking the wheel as lead presenter. Hammond co-hosted “Top Gear” alongside Jeremy Clarkson and James May from 2002 to 2015. Recent findings suggest that “I did talk to [the BBC] about ‘Top Gear’ relaunching, and I’m sure they went to the others as well,” Hammond remarked. “They came to me, you know, was I interested? And no, I’m too old. I think it needs a younger cast.”.

Industry observers note that asked why he turned down the idea, Hammond remarked: “No, I’ve done it. I wouldn’t want to revisit it. Well that was our ‘Top Gear,’ wasn’t it? We did it, the three of us. And when we left and went on together, it was the three of us. But that was our show.”.

As for his thoughts on Ramsay hosting the program — who, at 60, is four years older than Hammond — he seemed open-minded.

“Will Gordon work? Well, TV chefs all love three things: cooking, obviously, massive watches and cars. In his case, four — swearing as well,” Hammond mentioned. “But they all love cars, so I don’t doubt his passion his there. So let’s see what he does with it.”.

It has been revealed that ramsay is an avid car lover who has his racing license and has previously appeared on “Top Gear” as a guest. Following a four-year hiatus after former co-host Andrew Flintoff was badly injured in a crash, the show is set to film next year “across the U.K. and around the world,” according to a press release, and is eyeing a 2028 launch. Racing driver The Stig is set to return, though Ramsay’s co-hosts are yet to be announced.

Hammond concluded his thoughts on the new “Top Gear” by saying that he sees plenty of crossover between food and cars.

According to recent reports, “I’ve long wanted to make a TV show, actually, where I pair up with a chef and we talk about cars and cooking,” he said. “Because there is a substantial tie-in between them as subject strands, there’s a big connection … So I think [Ramsay] will bring the right audience to it, but I’ll be interested to see what they do with it.”.

Variety has reached out to the BBC for comment.
