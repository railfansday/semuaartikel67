It takes just 25 minutes to make chicken broccoli Alfredo by combining veggies and meat in a creamy garlic and Parmesan sauce.

I love dishes that make it feel like you’re treating the family to a dinner at a restaurant, and anything with Alfredo definitely falls into that category. And yet, even though the cream sauce in this chicken broccoli Alfredo is made from scratch, it’s actually totally doable at home. (Plus there’s no need to dress up to go out to eat—you can enjoy this meal in your pajamas if you want to!).

Recent findings suggest that there are a lot of ways to adapt this chicken Alfredo recipe using up other ingredients you might have on hand. Make it with chicken breasts as written, or swap in chicken thighs or leftover rotisserie chicken instead. Use fresh broccoli or pull those frozen florets from the freezer. Spice up the Parmesan sauce with a little extra cayenne pepper, or add a swirl of herby pesto.

Cook the fettuccine according to the package directions for al dente. Add the broccoli florets to the water for the last five minutes of cooking.

Editor’s Tip: Here’s what al dente actually means, and how to achieve it in your fettuccine and other pastas.

Meanwhile, in a large skillet, cook the chicken and chopped onion in butter until the chicken is no longer pink. Add the minced garlic, salt and cayenne pepper, and cook for one minute longer. Stir in the flour until it’s blended with the other ingredients.

In a recent development, gradually add the half-and-half, thawed peas and Parmesan cheese to the skillet. Bring the mixture to a boil. Cook and stir the sauce for one to two minutes or until it’s thickened.

Drain the fettuccine and broccoli, and toss them with the chicken mixture. Serve the chicken broccoli Alfredo while it’s hot.

According to recent reports, once the chicken broccoli Alfredo cools, transfer it to a sealed food storage container. Store it in the refrigerator for up to four days.

Yes, you can prepare this dish up to three days ahead of time. Gently reheat the chicken broccoli Alfredo in a covered skillet on the stove.

The creamy sauce in this Alfredo recipe may separate and turn watery when frozen and then thawed. To protect the texture, it’s best not to freeze chicken broccoli Alfredo.
