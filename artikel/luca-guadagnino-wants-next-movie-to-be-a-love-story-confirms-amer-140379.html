Luca Guadagnino wants to return to romance.

Speaking to Variety at the Marrakech Film Festival where he’s presenting “Artificial,” the Italian filmmaker behind “Call Me by Your Name,” “Bones and All” and “Queer” revealed that he hopes his next directorial endeavor will be another love story.

He also confirmed that two of his previously announced projects — a upcoming adaptation of Bret Easton Ellis’ “American Psycho” and the gay romance “Separate Rooms” — are no longer moving forward with him.

In a recent development, “Separate Rooms” was set to be an adaptation of Italian writer Pier Vittorio Tondelli’s 1989 novel about a writer grappling with the death of his lover. Josh O’Connor, who previously worked with Guadagnino on “Challengers,” and Léa Seydoux had been linked to the project since 2024.

Industry observers note that “I don’t think I’m doing a movie with Léa Seydoux, unfortunately. That’s not happening,” Guadagnino said. “But I would love to work with Léa, of course.”.

Recent findings suggest that the director also addressed the demise of his “American Psycho” adaptation, which was first declared by Lionsgate in October 2024. The film, penned by Scott Z. Burns, was intended to be a fresh interpretation of Ellis’ 1991 novel rather than a remake of Mary Harron’s 2000 cult classic starring Christian Bale. “That’s the job of a director,” he remarked of its dissolution. “You direct movies and sometimes it happens, sometimes it doesn’t. It’s very simple and linear and usual.”.

The filmmaker also dispelled speculation that Robert Pattinson had been in contention to play Patrick Bateman, though he mentioned “he’s a perfect choice for James Bond,” adding: “He’s an incredible star, he’s done so many great roles and I wish someday to work with him, of course.”.

Would Guadagnino direct a Bond film? “I would say yes, yes, of course,” he said of the prospect. “I would like to play with icons.”.

The director’s upcoming film, “Artificial,” marks another departure from the sweeping romances that have come to define much of his oeuvre. The Amazon MGM Studios feature dramatizes the turmoil at OpenAI surrounding the ousting and subsequent reinstatement of CEO Sam Altman, played by Andrew Garfield.

The film premiered at Upcoming York Film Festival and screens at Marrakech Film Festival on Thursday night.
