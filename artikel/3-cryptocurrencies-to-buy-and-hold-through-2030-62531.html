Many cryptocurrencies fizzled out this year as the macro headwinds drove investors toward more conservative, higher-yielding investments. That pressure could persist over the next year as the Iran war continues, interest rates rise, and Treasury yields remain high.

Over the next few years, many of the smaller altcoins could fade away. However, several of the top cryptocurrencies should survive that wash-out and head higher as more catalysts kick in. Let's see why three of those tokens -- Bitcoin (BTC -4.94%), Solana (SOL -5.39%), and LINK (LINK -4.73%) -- could generate much bigger gains through 2030.

Bitcoin, the world's most valuable cryptocurrency, is still mined using powerful computing hardware with the energy-intensive proof-of-work (PoW) consensus mechanism. Every four years, those mining rewards are cut in half in scheduled "halvings". More than 20 million Bitcoins have already been mined, and the last token should be mined by 2140.

CRYPTO · As of 6:20 PM ET 11/20/26.

Bitcoin's scarcity makes it more comparable to gold and silver than other smaller tokens. But it's also easier to hold in fractional amounts and transfer than those precious metals, making it a potential hedge against expansionary monetary policies and the debasement of fiat currencies.

Bitcoin's first spot-price ETFs were approved in 2024, and it should continue to gain more retail and institutional investors if the macro environment warms up again. Its next halving in 2028 will be a major catalyst, and more corporations and governments could accumulate more Bitcoins as an alternative to softening fiat currencies over the next few years.

Solana, like Ethereum (ETH -7.80%), is a developer-oriented, proof-of-stake (PoS) blockchain that supports staking (locking up tokens to earn interest-like rewards) and smart contracts (for developing decentralized applications and other tokenized assets).

In a recent development, however, its Layer 1 (L1) blockchain processes transactions at much faster speeds than Ethereum's L1 blockchain. It achieves those higher speeds through its proprietary proof-of-history (PoH) consensus mechanism, which timestamps transactions before they're validated.

CRYPTO · As of 6:21 PM ET 11/20/26.

According to recent reports, solana's higher speeds drew in many software developers and major financial companies such as Circle (NASDAQ:CRCL), Visa (V -0.09%), PayPal (PYPL -5.46%), and Stripe -- all of which are using its blockchain to settle stablecoin transfers, tokenize real-world assets (RWAs), launch new cryptocurrencies, and accelerate their own financial transactions. Solana's token is used to fund those transaction fees, so the expansion of that economy makes it more valuable.

Solana has already grown into the second-largest developer-driven blockchain after Ethereum, and its first spot price ETFs were approved last year. But it's still often lumped together with smaller altcoins rather than "blue chip" tokens like Bitcoin and Ethereum. I believe that perception could change by the end of the decade and drive its price much higher.

According to recent reports, LINK is the native token of Chainlink, the largest decentralized oracle network. An oracle network relies on independent node operators to fetch real-world data -- such as news headlines, weather reports, stock tickers, sports scores, and shipping updates -- and feed that information to developer-oriented blockchains like Solana and Ethereum. The developers on those blockchains use that data to build decentralized apps.

CRYPTO · As of 6:28 PM ET 11/20/26.

Recent findings suggest that chainlink allows its node operators to stake their LINK tokens as collateral to earn rewards. But if they feed false information into the oracle network, those tokens can be confiscated, and their reputation scores (essential for attracting latest requests) will be reduced.

Industry observers note that chainlink is already helping major financial institutions -- including UBS (UBS -3.78%), JPMorgan (JPM -3.03%), and Euroclear -- accelerate their blockchain transactions and tokenize RWAs. It's also modernizing financial platforms -- such as SWIFT (for interbank transfers) and the DTCC (for U.S. stock trades) -- and streamlining their settlements. As that ecosystem grows, LINK should attract more attention and become even more valuable. The first Chainlink ETFs were already released last year, and they could attract more investors through 2030.
