Rayan Cherki (left) rescued a late draw for Manchester City against Napoli.

Manchester City's slick footballing machine is starting to grind to a halt.

The quick passing patterns and edge-of-the-seat action that was evident for so long under former manager Pep Guardiola was supposed to be taken forward by incumbent Enzo Maresca.

It started flawlessly under the Italian with the side winning their first eight games, but that smooth operation is beginning to turn into a turgid and pedestrian showing.

Similar to Saturday's 1-1 draw against Fulham, moans and groans filled a rather flat Etihad Stadium as the home faithful watched their side labour to a 2-2 Champions League draw against Napoli.

City's recent defensive complications also continued, now conceding in 10 successive games and letting in the first goal in seven of those matches.

Recent findings suggest that maresca said: "We are always looking for three points but when you are not able to win it is important you that don't lose. We take the point, five games [played],10 points and overall I think it is a good result in the end.

Industry observers note that "Today, off the ball, once again we didn't concede a lot, the problem is that in this moment they are clinical when we concede a chance, almost every time it is finishing with a goal. We created many chances but in this moment it is like this and move on.

"The first 10 games we always scored first and then in this moment it is exactly in the opposite way, we just need to find a way to avoid that, but I don't think it affects the players in terms of their confidence. We fought until the end.".

Cherki rescues point for Man City against Napoli.

City were strong favourites for this game and should have had few issues in collecting all three points against a Napoli side that are languishing in eighth place in Serie A.

Their injury challenges mean they were able to name only seven substitutes, two of those being goalkeepers, while they had suffered defeat in their past three meetings with City.

They threatened a shock result after twice going ahead through well-taken goals from Rasmus Hojlund and Noa Lang, but Rayan Cherki managed to salvage a late draw for the hosts.

Maresca's side had 62.7% possession and attempted 665 passes, which largely lacked incisiveness, and of that figure 303 were in the final third compared to Napoli's 77.

The latest disappointing result means City's perfect start is swiftly unravelling, now having won only one of their past seven games in all competitions inside 90 minutes.

"It was really surprising to see Manchester City so dull in the first half and so predictable," former Scotland winger Pat Nevin noted on BBC Radio 5 Live.

"If you play a team that takes their time, you get yourself set. It is 100% easier to defend.

"You sit and watch City pass, pass, pass, slow, slow. You can get a cigar out.".

Star striker Erling Haaland has been in prolific form again this season, scoring 20 goals for club and country, but the 26-year-old has drawn a blank in his past two games.

The Norwegian had chances but failed to score against Fulham on Saturday and though he was handed the captain's armband against Napoli, Haaland was on the periphery for much of the game.

The frontman managed only 14 touches of the ball against the Serie A side on Tuesday, the lowest figure of any player that started the match.

It raises the question whether he is receiving the service to try and find the back of the net.

"I feel I am getting more than enough chances to score," Haaland told TNT Sports. "We tried to push at the end for a goal but we couldn't. Not good enough and we let them in too easy.

In a recent development, "Football is easy and difficult; when you win it's easy and you know what to do, when you don't you start questioning everything. The perfect thing would be to not think but it's not so easy.".

City are sixth in the Champions League table, before Wednesday's games, but in the Premier League they have failed to win their past four.

It does not get any easier this weekend, facing a daunting trip to leaders Arsenal on Sunday as they aim to find some form.

Haaland added: "It's the most difficult game of the season going to the champions away.

"We need a top performance from every single player.".
