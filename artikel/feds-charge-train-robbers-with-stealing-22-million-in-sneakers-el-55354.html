The crew targeted freight trains that haul the millions of containers with overseas imports that arrive every year at the ports of LA and Long Beach to destinations in the Central U.S.

According to recent reports, once they had selected a “high-value” train to rob, prosecutors say in the indictment, a group of burglars would board the train at the tracks. At a predetermined point along the tracks, the burglars cut the air hoses that control the train’s brakes, bringing it to an emergency halt.

Other crew members would be ready with trucks and vans to rapidly collect the boxes of shoes and other merchandise from the train and take them to their stash houses.

Some crew members would contact buyers, including one unidentified retailer who repeatedly bought large quantities of Nike sneakers from the train robbers.

The crew wasn’t shy about flaunting their riches in selfies with stacks of stolen merchandise and cash, as well as making videos of themselves while burglarizing the trains.

They also created a group chat on Instagram called “Los Trenes,” where they discussed their thefts as they were happening.

“On July 15, 2026, at approximately 1:57 a.m., defendant Rodolfo Angel posted to the Los Trenes Group Chat, ‘Train still over there?’ At approximately 2:23 a.m., defendant Estrada posted, ‘Stopped it good,’ and defendant Sanchez replied, ‘Whoop,’” prosecutors stated in the indictment.

The trains were stopped and robbed along tracks in industrial areas southeast of downtown LA and elsewhere in Southern California.

The defendants are charged with conspiracy, theft of interstate railroad cargo, possession or receipt of stolen interstate railroad cargo and violence against railroad carriers.
