Tech Report: 2027 Toyota Sienna Now Gets Up To 37 MPG Combined, And It'll Be Better To Drive Too

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If you're still hating on minivans in The Year Of Our Lord 2026, the joke is officially on you. Minivans are exceptionally versatile vehicles that are objectively the best option for most modern SUV buyers, but their lack of a perceived cool factor prevents many buyers from even considering them. We love minivans, and one of the most compelling minivans on the market today is the hybrid-only Toyota Sienna. It's getting even more compelling for 2027, since Toyota has bestowed it with some significant drivetrain improvements including the automaker's sixth-generation hybrid system that uses high efficiency electric motors, a retuned engine, and a more energy efficient lithium-ion battery.

The 2027 Toyota Sienna isn't visibly distinguishable from the 2026 model since no changes were made to its exterior, but minivans have always been about what's beneath the skin, anyway. In addition to the new powertrain, all 2027 Siennas get the larger 12.3-inch infotainment screen and the larger 12.3-inch digital gauge cluster as standard, Toyota's latest suite of active-safety features, an integrated dash cam, a redesigned steering wheel and column, and new LED interior lights.

The Sienna's new hybrid powertrain has been totally recalibrated to operate the 2.5-liter four-cylinder at lower engine speeds when accelerating through the low and mid-range, and all Siennas now have a 1.15-kWh lithium-ion battery and a 166-horsepower electric motor. The powertrain produces a combined 251 horsepower, 6 more horses than last year, and Toyota reckons it's good for a 0.3-second quicker 0-to-60 sprint of 7.5 seconds in front-wheel-drive form. It can still be had with all-wheel drive, which adds a rear-mounted 54-hp electric motor, but its 0-to-60 time is actually a tenth slower than the front-drive model. Toyota now estimates both FWD and AWD Siennas will return 39 mpg in the city, 35 mpg on the highway, and 37 mpg combined, improvements of 3 mpg for FWD and 4 mpg for AWD.

Sources indicate that toyota says the latest steering system is more direct and has better on-center feel while reducing steering effort at low speeds for easier parking thanks to a new steering gear box, motor control unit, and torque sensor. The Sienna's brakes have also been reworked with a new electronic control system, offering improved feel and response with less body roll. The latest Toyota Safety Sense 4.0 suite of active safety features has upgraded sensors and hardware for improved obstacle detection capabilities, and in case that all fails, a built-in dash cam comes standard.

Industry observers note that other little changes include the cancellation of the Limited model, now leaving base LE, XLE, Woodland, XSE, and top Platinum trim levels. Toyota has not yet release pricing for the updated 2027 Sienna, but we expect its prices to increase slightly from their current starting point of $42,915.
