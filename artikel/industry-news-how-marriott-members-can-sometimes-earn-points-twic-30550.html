Industry News: How Marriott Members Can Sometimes Earn Points Twice On The Same Spending

🌐 Source: Viewfromthewing • 📅 17 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (1089 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • A Marriott Bonvoy member reports earning qualifying spend or extra points by charging gift card purchases to the room bill even though Marriott expressly excludes gift card purchases from the spending requirement for Ambassador status.
  • Ambassador takes 100 qualifying nights and $23,000 in annual qualifying spend.
  • An April 4 report on US Card Forum laid out some properties where this worked, and where it didn’t.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A Marriott Bonvoy member reports earning qualifying spend or extra points by charging gift card purchases to the room bill even though Marriott expressly excludes gift card purchases from the spending requirement for Ambassador status. Ambassador takes 100 qualifying nights and $23,000 in annual qualifying spend. Someone staying at inexpensive and even moderately-priced hotels can clear the nights while falling well short on dollars. If a gift card counts when you buy it, you could move future hotel spending into the current qualification year. And if the purchase and the eventual hotel stay both earn points, you’ve earned twice on the same money. An April 4 report on US Card Forum laid out some properties where this worked, and where it didn’t. Property   Reported result Ritz-Carlton Waikiki spa   Gift cards charged to the room counted toward qualifying spend. St. Regis Longboat Key   Two $1,500 cards purchased, then used at checkout; the member reports an extra round of points. Tampa EDITION   Allowed the gift-card room charge; no separate points or spending total supplied. JW Marriott Clearwater   Refused to put the purchase on the room bill. Unnamed hotel, separate commenter   A charge labeled “gc” reached the folio but earned no points. Marriott’s terms (section 4.2.b.v) exclude Marriott Gift Card purchases from Annual Qualifying Spend. The Ambassador help page says the same thing. This comes down to how the hotel records the charge and sends it to Marriott. If a gift card sale goes through as an eligible incidental, the loyalty system credits the amount without recognizing what was actually sold. A properly identified gift card entry gets excluded. There are two places this can fail: the hotel can refuse the room charge, or accept it but keep it out of the amount eligible for points and status. An employee saying “yes, we can charge that to your room” doesn’t tell you whether it will post as eligible spend. I’ve covered another example of hotel level accounting producing more Ambassador credit than you’d expect. In 2024, the Renaissance Dallas advertised a rate with 30% additional qualifying spend for $20 extra per night. That suggested how the property’s handling of a charge can affect the way Marriott credits it. If you’re a Marriott person, who’s going to spend money with them anyway (clearly an Ambassador member does) this is an interesting thing to experiment with but I also wouldn’t hit it too hard with an Ambassador account that I valued. Me, I haven’t been an Ambassador since the end of the Starwood days.
