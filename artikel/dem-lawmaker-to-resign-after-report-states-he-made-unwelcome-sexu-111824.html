A West Coast Democrat confirmed his resignation from the House of Representatives after their bipartisan internal watchdog stated that he could have committed criminal sexual assault against congressional staffers on multiple occasions.

It has been revealed that an explosive report by the House Ethics Committee released on Monday claimed that Rep. Jimmy Gomez, D-Calif., violated congressional rules on sexual misconduct by making "unwelcome" advances toward a staffer for the House, disregarding the power imbalance between lawmakers and aides in the process.

The report also went a step further, warning Gomez could have violated existing law.

In a recent development, gomez has since announced his intent to resign on Dec. 1.

Industry observers note that rep. Jimmy Gomez speaks during a news conference on affordability on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., on Dec. 18, 2025. (Andrew Harnik/Getty Images).

The bipartisan panel found that "Representative Gomez knew or had reason to know that his contact was not welcome" when out with the staffer, called "Witness 1," at a D.C. bar.

"There is also evidence that he knew or had reason to know his repeated advance towards Witness 1 in his congressional office was inappropriate and unwelcome. Accordingly, Representative Gomez’s conduct towards Witness 1 could implicate D.C. laws related to criminal sexual assault, including misdemeanor sexual abuse," the report said.

Gomez has not been charged with any crime and has maintained his innocence. Fox News Digital reached out to his office for comment but did not immediately receive a response.

Industry observers note that gomez was a close ally of disgraced former Rep. Eric Swalwell, D-Calif., who resigned after being accused of sexual assault — which he denied. Gomez is also founder of the pro-family Congressional Dads Caucus.

The bipartisan ethics panel declared in August that it was investigating Gomez over allegations including "inappropriate sexual contact with a House staffer.".

Gomez told Fox News Digital at the time that he "made personal mistakes outside my marriage" but added that his extramarital relations were consensual and that he did not break any laws.

But the lawyers for one of the women interviewed by the committee disputed that in comments to Fox News Digital, accusing the congressman of sexual assault.

Monday’s report shows the committee appears to support her side of the story, and that Gomez’s actions "at various junctures suggests he knew that what he was doing was wrong.".

The staffer is identified as having worked for a congressional leadership office, rather than on Gomez’s own staff.

Before coming out with her accusations, the staffer testified to the committee that she did not want Gomez "to feel upset in any way," and that she was "afraid that I was going to get in trouble at work," the report stated.

"Even if Representative Gomez believed that his actions were wholly consensual, his failure to appreciate the reality of this power dynamic and thus the untenable position he put a young congressional staffer in, is inconsistent with the standards required of a Member of Congress," the report remarked.

The investigation into Gomez began after reports surfaced that he had been seen kissing a congressional staffer at a party attended by lawmakers and House employees.

Rep. Jimmy Gomez, a Democrat from California, sits on the House steps at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., on July 3, 2025. (Kent Nishimura/Bloomberg via Getty Images).

Gomez initially disclosed two relationships involving congressional staffers, describing one as a past consensual sexual relationship and the other as "one sole occasion" of "consensual kissing.".

However, investigators found credible allegations that Gomez made unwanted sexual advances more than once toward the second woman, Witness 1, which the woman claimed was "nonconsensual whatsoever.".

The woman, who was in her mid-20s at the time, testified that Gomez put his hand underneath her skirt and penetrated her with his fingers without consent during a February 2024 gathering at a bar in D.C., and testified to investigators that she fled to the bathroom crying after the incident.

Gomez defended himself against the allegation, telling the committee that the woman "did not say no," and did not "push" him away, while denying penetrating her.

Investigators described a second encounter with the woman in July 2024, claiming that Gomez grabbed her as she attempted to leave his congressional office, pulled her toward him and tried to kiss her.

She described Gomez as "[p]retty forceful" and mentioned she repeatedly tried to push him away, the report said.

Gomez denied the allegation, maintaining that he merely hugged the woman and kissed her on the cheek.

Investigators then refuted his defense after they examined text messages suggesting Gomez was concerned about being seen with the woman by members of his staff, noting that he switched their communications to Signal and enabled disappearing messages. The committee also noted that Gomez initially disclosed only consensual kissing before later acknowledging sexual contact during his testimony.

After reviewing documents and speaking with 10 witnesses, the committee concluded that Gomez violated clauses 1 and 18(b) of the House Code of Official Conduct, which prohibit unwelcome sexual advances toward House employees and require lawmakers to conduct themselves in a manner reflecting creditably on the House.

Investigators concluded that Gomez's conduct caused the woman significant emotional distress and contributed to her decision to leave congressional employment in 2025.

"The Committee reiterates its commitment to investigating allegations and providing accountability to those who face sexual misconduct from Members and their staff. Here, the House lost Witness 1 as a competent and able staffer because of Representative Gomez’s misconduct and because of a broader culture of disrespect and toxic sexual behavior toward staff that permeates life on the Hill and is long overdue for correction," the report mentioned.
