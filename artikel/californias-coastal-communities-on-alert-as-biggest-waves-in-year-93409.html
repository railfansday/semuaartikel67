Hazard warnings issued throughout week after powerful swells and waves of more than 9 metres recorded.

Multiple coastal hazard warnings were issued this week across California as successive areas of low pressure generated powerful swells and large breaking waves for coastal communities. Beaches and piers were closed last weekend as large waves crashed into California’s coastline. Offshore National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration buoys recorded wave heights of greater than 30ft (9 metres) last Friday. The Monterey, California, buoy recorded a wave height of 34.1ft, which would make it the highest reading in 30 years.

Industry observers note that coastal warnings extended from last weekend through this week, with alerts extending until Friday night. Waves of 10-18ft are expected, with local sets of 18-22ft for San Luis Obispo county beaches and Santa Barbara county central coast beaches. These Pacific storm systems are also bringing unsettled conditions across the western US, with heavy rain, mountain snow, and strong winds through the coming days. Already, record-breaking rainfall affected San Fransisco on Wednesday, with heavy rain also causing 32,000 customers to lose power in Sacramento. Into this weekend, a further area of low pressure will bring considerable amounts of moisture for southern Oregon and northern California. By Sunday night, more than 100mm (4in) of rain is expected in places, with further snowfall for areas such as Donner Pass across the northern Sierra Nevada mountains.
