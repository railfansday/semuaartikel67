Environmentalists have long been mystified by the significant proliferation of dams and water transfer projects like pipelines and tunnels across Iran, especially in regions that had no apparent need for them.

Some analysts think they now know the answer after U.S. and Israeli airstrikes on concealed Iranian missile sites this year targeted many of the same areas. These analysts have concluded that the water projects may have been developed to support the country’s military buildup.

It has been revealed that given the secrecy surrounding Iran’s missile program, any connection between it and Iran’s ambitious, decades-old initiative to develop water infrastructure remains speculative. But some experts find the evidence highly suggestive.

Nima Shokri, an expert on water security, and his team at the Hamburg University of Technology, in Germany, had for years tracked an unusually high number of dams across Iran — at least 533 of them, among the most of any country in the region.

Sources indicate that “It was always puzzling to me,” he said. “Why were they building so many dams?”.

It has been revealed that in the spring, Dr. Shokri was perusing news about the war in his home country when he came across a map plotting airstrikes that had targeted suspected military sites. The pattern — a semicircle of dots from north to south — nearly matched that of his own map marking the locations of dams.

His old question, he remarked, was superseded by a new one: “Why does it seem that wherever we have dams, there is a missile strike close by?”.
