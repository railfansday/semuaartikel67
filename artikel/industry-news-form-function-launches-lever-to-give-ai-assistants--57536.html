Form & Function Consulting has released Lever, an AI platform designed to give professional services firms a consistent view of their operations across disconnected systems. Unveiled on November 12, the product combines operational data with company-specific business definitions so leaders can ask questions about staffing, revenue forecasts and project profitability through conversational interfaces.

According to recent reports, the interesting part is not another chatbot answering financial questions. It is the work required before those answers become useful. A sales system can record a promising deal while a delivery team sees insufficient capacity to fulfill it. An AI assistant needs more than access to both databases to explain what that combination means for the business.

Recent findings suggest that according to the launch announcement, Form & Function starts with information from customer relationship management, professional services automation and finance systems. It also gathers documents and the knowledge employees use to interpret those records. Its Lev chatbot supports stakeholder interviews that capture how a firm actually operates.

According to recent reports, that onboarding feeds a context layer describing how work is sold, staffed, delivered and measured. The goal is to connect questions that otherwise fall between departments: whether the sales pipeline supports a hiring decision, which engagements offer the best margins, or how a delayed investment would affect projected cash flow.

This distinction matters in services businesses because similarly named metrics can conceal different assumptions. Available capacity might exclude time spent selling, while a revenue forecast might depend on both a deal’s likelihood of closing and when the resulting work can be delivered. A plausible answer based on the wrong definition can be more misleading than an obvious error.

In a recent development, the manufacturer’s Lever product page demonstrates Lev answering questions, generating visual output and working inside Slack. It also reveals access through other AI clients, including Claude and ChatGPT, using Model Context Protocol, or MCP. Rather than requiring every interface to reconstruct the firm’s business logic, the approach supplies a shared foundation for those interactions.

The launch positioned Lever within operating-partner advisory engagements. The current product page also describes standalone availability. Pricing and detailed deployment requirements are not disclosed there, so the announcement should not be read as a self-service rollout with a published subscription price.

The broader move toward shared business meaning is also visible in Collate’s Ontology Studio announcement. Lever takes a narrower approach centered on professional services operations and consultant-assisted onboarding, rather than treating business context as a generic layer that every organization can adopt unchanged.

Form & Function reports that one client identified additional delivery capacity sufficient to support approximately $1.8 million in incremental annual revenue without adding employees. The release also describes an improvement in projected gross margins. Those are potential operating gains identified through analysis, not evidence that the client has already collected that additional revenue.

Recent findings suggest that the firm’s technical discussion of semantic layers provides more context. In a tech giant-run comparison, it tested five business questions five times each, contrasting access to raw BigQuery data with a semantic model built using Malloy. The structured approach performed better on questions requiring business definitions and relationships between tables.

In a recent development, that is a useful illustration of the mechanism, but it is a small vendor-run test. It does not establish perfect accuracy across arbitrary questions, changing datasets or every customer’s deployment. Defining metrics in advance reduces ambiguity; it does not eliminate the need to maintain those definitions and review consequential decisions.

It has been revealed that lever’s launch highlights a practical shift in enterprise AI: connecting a model to data is only the beginning. Someone must decide what the data means, resolve conflicting definitions and keep that interpretation current as contracts, staffing policies and business processes change.

For services firms, the potential benefit is a shorter path from operational records to a decision about capacity or profitability. The lasting test will be whether that shared context stays reliable as the business evolves, and whether identified opportunities turn into measurable results rather than simply more persuasive answers.
