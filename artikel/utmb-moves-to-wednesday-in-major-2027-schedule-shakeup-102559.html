UTMB Moves to Wednesday in Major 2027 Schedule Shakeup

🌐 Source: Trailrunnermag • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 7 min read (1321 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • The 2027 UTMB week-long festival events will take place on Sunday, August 22 to August 28.
  • There are certain immutable facts in the world of trail running.
  • One of those things will change in 2027, as the world’s most famous trail running race series juggles its long-established order of events, Trail Runner learned from UTMB officials.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The 2027 UTMB week-long festival events will take place on Sunday, August 22 to August 28. The flagship Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc (UTMB) will start on Wednesday, August 25, Trail Runner Chamonix correspondent Doug Mayer confirmed with UTMB officials. This article was written under embargo and has been updated with additional information.

There are certain immutable facts in the world of trail running. Kilian and Courtney are the Greatest of All Time. A trailhead IPA will always be the best recovery drink. And UTMB week at the end of August in Chamonix, France, culminates with a 108-mile lap around Mont Blanc otherwise known as the eponymous UTMB.

One of those things will change in 2027, as the world’s most famous trail running race series juggles its long-established order of events, Trail Runner learned from UTMB officials.

For the past eight years, the alphabet stew of race acronyms comprising UTMB week started with the epic 300-kilometer PTL at the beginning of the week, followed by MCC (40K), TDS (148K), and YCC (a 5-15K youth event), before the three UTMB World Series Finals races: OCC (57K), CCC (108K) and finally UTMB (174K). A 15K event, ETC, was added in Courmayeur, Italy, in 2022.

There are three notable changes for next year’s event:.

Race start times are expected to stay largely the same for the UTMB World Series Final races. Start times for other races will be forthcoming shortly.

Industry observers note that two blazing hot meteors of the global sports world are colliding head on. The International Cycling Union (UCI) Cycling World Championships are taking place in France’s Haute Savoie region—which includes Chamonix. The dates? August 24 to September 5.

And one date looms particularly large: Sunday, August 29, when the UCI elite men’s road race is scheduled to take place in the towns of Sallanches and Domancy, just a handful of kilometers from Chamonix.

After the UCI confirmed the dates for the world championships, President of the French Cycling Federation Michel Callot suggested that UTMB shift its dates.

It has been revealed that UTMB couldn’t move into September, as the Mont Blanc Tunnel that connects Chamonix with Courmayeur, Italy, is tentatively slated to be closed again for maintenance starting on Monday, August 30. The tunnel is critical to the infrastructure of the race series—in fact, it was the reopening of the tunnel in March, 2002, after a tragic fire that forced a nearly three-year closure, that allowed race organizers to host the first edition of UTMB a year later.

Industry observers note that earlier dates presented their own challenges, too. “We would have been right in the middle of the tourist season,” Isabelle Viseux-Poletti, director of UTMB Mont-Blanc, tells Trail Runner. “In terms of visitor numbers and the number of people using the trails, that simply wouldn’t have worked. Everyone around the table agreed on that point.”.

The discussions around the 2027 UTMB Mont-Blanc dates involved official representatives of the prefecture and of municipalities along the races’ courses, notably Chamonix, with its new mayor, François-Xavier Laffin, and directors of the UCI Championships. In the end, they went with the most reasonable option: juggling the flow of the events to better accommodate the regional impacts, and shifting UTMB week a day earlier to avoid a literal collision of events on Sunday, August 29.

Industry observers note that the 2027 schedule was developed in close coordination with the office of Chamonix Mayor François Xavier Laffin. Laffin, who himself raced UTMB in 2007, came into office this past spring promising to review UTMB’s relationship with the town, with an eye towards mitigating the growing impacts of the race on the local population and the valley’s fragile alpine ecosystem.

Those two sporting event meteors have been heading towards a collision for more than a year now.

It has been revealed that this will be the first time the UCI is holding a world championship that brings together all of its disciplines in the same place. That includes 20 events ranging from road cycling to mountain biking, para-cycling, and BMX. The overlap with UTMB in a region about the size of Rhode Island triggered a range of concerns about transportation, safety, and accommodations.

From the very beginning, the UCI worked jointly with UTMB. “We’ve been discussing this for quite a long time,” Viseux-Poletti indicates. ”From the outset, we’ve been working collaboratively to find solutions.”.

But coordinating two significant sporting events is about much more than just aligning the dates, and UTMB officials had to wait until the UCI could share details about its needs, too. “In particular, we needed to understand their requirements regarding road access and law enforcement,” Viseux-Poletti claims. Predictably, some of those decisions took months. And so, it was only in the last several months that coordinating decisions between the entities began in earnest.

Sources indicate that keith Byrne is one person who has watched UTMB race week play out for the last 11 years. An announcer for UTMB English broadcast, he’s taking the calendar shift in stride. “It’s pretty much going to be business as usual for us just with different days of the week,” he says.

Overall, Byrne is hopeful about the changes: “Out of some disturbance and adversity, there’s an opportunity to move forward.” He does wonder if there will be fewer spectators out on the course during CCC and UTMB. “Wednesday and Thursday might not be the most productive days in the office,” he joked. “Because we’re going to make sure they have great live coverage to watch!”.

Recent findings suggest that american Emily Schmitz placed third at UTMB this last year. A year-round Chamonix resident for the past four years, she’s not particularly concerned about lower numbers of spectators out on the course. “There were so many spectators this year. Even if there are fewer, there are still going to be a lot of people out there cheering for you,” she observed. “And, regardless, you’re really focused on your race when you’re out there.”.

If there’s a race winner among these changes, Byrne thinks it’s going to be OCC. And that’s just fine with him. “OCC will have way more eyes on it, falling on a Saturday. That’s great news for OCC runners.” The race has had fierce competition in recent years, with this year’s race featuring a remarkable duel among the top two women, Lauren Gregory (U.S.) and Naomi Lang (U.K.), who finished three seconds apart. “It was the first time in UTMB week history that the women’s finish got more people watching than the men’s finish,” Byrne noted.

For spectators, Byrne found the silver lining. “If you’re a trail running fan and a cycling fan, this year you’ll have two events for the price of one.”.

It has been revealed that could this schedule change-up, which further draws attention to the marquee events of the week, be a harbinger of things to come? UTMB officials are offering no clues.

It has been revealed that it is clear, however, that juggling the schedule of UTMB’s many races opens a Pandora’s box of coordination headaches. There are three countries with whom to deal, plus regional authorities, nearly two dozen towns, and multiple public and private rescue services. Even for onlookers, it’s migraine-inducing.

UTMB officials declined to comment about whether this fresh schedule will remain the same in 2028. But they did say more changes are coming. Soon after the 2026 UTMB World Series Finals ended, UTMB officials and the Chamonix’s Mayor’s office began a series of meetings to address areas of concern raised by the mayor. Both parties have promised changes to address concerns. Viseux-Poletti expects the race series and the mayor’s office to jointly announce the news in about a month.

Doug Mayer is the author of The Race that Changed Running: The Inside Story of UTMB. He lives in Chamonix, France, and is the founder of the trail running tour company Run the Alps.
