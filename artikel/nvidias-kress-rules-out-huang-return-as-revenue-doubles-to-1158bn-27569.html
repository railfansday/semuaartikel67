Nvidia’s Kress rules out Huang return as revenue doubles to $115.8bn

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Colette Kress, Nvidia’s interim chief executive and chief financial officer.

vidia’s board will not bring back Jensen Huang as chief executive, interim CEO Colette Kress mentioned on Tuesday. But support for his return is growing among some staff, CNBC reported, citing people familiar with the matter.

“The board has determined that they will not reinstate Jensen,” Kress told CNBC’s Jim Cramer on Mad Money.

Board chair Harvey Jones made the same point in his opening remarks on Nvidia’s earnings call earlier that day.

“The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” Jones said.

In a recent development, huang noted on Tuesday, in his first public statement since resigning, that he would be willing to return. He had already discussed a possible return, the Financial Times reported. President Donald Trump has also said he would not object.

Inside the company, engineers in particular were deeply devoted to Huang, a former employee told CNBC. TNW has not independently verified CNBC’s reporting on staff sentiment.

Kress, who has been Nvidia’s finance chief since 2013, used her first earnings call as interim CEO to turn attention back to the business. She told Cramer that Nvidia’s customers and employees are staying with the company.

“We’ve engaged with our customers, we’ve engaged with our suppliers, our partners, they’re all 100% behind us,” Kress said on the call.

It has been revealed that the results helped. Revenue for the third quarter more than doubled to $115.8bn, ahead of analyst forecasts of about $109bn. Adjusted earnings came in at $2.68 a share.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia forecast record fourth-quarter revenue of about $130bn. Kress also raised the company’s revenue growth target for fiscal 2028 to 75%, from the 70% Huang gave three months ago. Nvidia added $150bn to its share buyback blueprint.

The shares rose about 3.5% in premarket trading on Wednesday, after falling for four days in a row, CNBC reported.

Separately, Kress mentioned Nvidia is no longer shipping licensed Hopper data centre chips to China, Bloomberg reported.

According to recent reports, huang resigned last week after admitting an affair with Alice Huang, whom US officials suspect of being a Chinese spy. He is due to appear before House panels on 1 December.
