Feyenoord and Porto meet at De Kuip on Tuesday night with both clubs under pressure to revive their Champions League campaigns.

Recent findings suggest that having collected just three points from their opening four league-phase matches, the Dutch side sit 32nd in the 36-team table, while Porto are only slightly better off in 26th place. With the knockout rounds already beginning to drift out of sight for both teams, victory could prove pivotal.

Feyenoord head into the contest in poor form and desperately searching for a positive result after six matches without a win in all competitions.

Their latest setback came at the weekend when they were beaten 3-2 at home by Excelsior, a result made all the more painful by the fact it marked Excelsior’s first league victory at De Kuip in 46 years. Giovanni van Bronckhorst’s side began brightly through an early goal from Givairo Read, but three unanswered goals left Jan Plug’s late strike as little more than a consolation.

The defeat followed a 5-3 loss away to Heerenveen and a 3-0 home defeat to Inter Milan in the Champions League, meaning Feyenoord have conceded at least three goals in each of their last three matches.

Defensive concerns have become a major glitch. The Dutch club have now lost 10 of their last 13 European fixtures and have conceded the opening goal in each of their last seven Champions League outings. That sequence includes defeats against Barcelona, Real Betis and Inter Milan, with their only victory in the current campaign coming against Como.

With memories of last season’s disappointing Europa League campaign, when they finished 29th in the league phase, another defeat would leave their hopes of reaching the knockout stages hanging by a thread.

Anis Hadj Moussa is being assessed after returning from international duty with an ankle injury that forced him to miss the defeat against Excelsior.

Tsuyoshi Watanabe is also a doubt after being substituted at half-time on Saturday with a foot problem.

Jakub Moder and Bart Nieuwkoop remain unavailable through injury, while Thomas Beelen, Jordan Bos and Jerry St. Juste are all expected to miss out as well, leaving Van Bronckhorst short of defensive options.

Former Arsenal winger Reiss Nelson made his first start for the club at the weekend, but Luciano Valente could be recalled on the left flank for Tuesday’s encounter.

Porto arrive in Rotterdam with considerably more momentum despite their difficult start to the Champions League campaign.

Industry observers note that francesco Farioli’s side finally claimed their first victory of the league phase on matchday four, producing an emphatic 4-1 win over Napoli. Jan Bednarek, Gabri Veiga, Jakub Kiwior and Samu Omorodion all found the net as the Portuguese side responded impressively after losing their opening three European matches against Manchester City, Real Betis and PSV Eindhoven.

Domestic form has been excellent. Porto have won all 11 of their Primeira Liga matches and already hold a five-point lead at the top of the table. Their most recent outing was less straightforward, though, as they required a penalty shootout to overcome third-tier Paredes in the Taca de Portugal after a 1-1 draw across 120 minutes, due to an extremely unusual own-goal.

The Dragons are competing in the Champions League main stage for the first time in three years and are hoping to reach the knockout rounds for the third consecutive appearance.

However, their record in the Netherlands offers some encouragement for Feyenoord. Porto have failed to win any of their past four away matches against Dutch opponents, drawing two and losing two. Their previous visits to De Kuip ended in a draw in 1993 and a 2-0 defeat in 2019.

It has been revealed that vasco Sousa remains sidelined with a long-term leg injury. Former Manchester United defender Chris Smalling is struggling with a knee issue and is also ineligible, having not been included in Porto’s Champions League squad.

William Gomes has returned to training after recovering from an ankle problem, but his involvement remains uncertain.

Midfielder In-beom Hwang is set for an emotional return against his former club after joining Porto from Feyenoord during the summer, while striker Santiago Gimenez is also returning to Rotterdam after representing Feyenoord between 2022 and 2025.

Feyenoord’s defensive struggles make it difficult to back the hosts with confidence, particularly against a Porto side carrying significantly stronger form and fresh confidence following their dominant victory over Napoli.

While De Kuip remains a challenging venue and Porto’s historical record in the Netherlands is far from convincing, the visitors appear better equipped to handle the pressure of such a crucial European fixture. Given Feyenoord’s recent performances and recurring defensive issues, Porto look capable of taking advantage and securing an vital away victory.
