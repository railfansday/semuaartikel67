Daily Deal Vouchers Save Money Only When You’d Pay Full Price Anyway

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that before you buy a daily deal voucher, ask whether you’d pay full price for that meal, massage, or class, or one like it, within the next few weeks. If you would, the discount is money you keep. If you wouldn’t, the voucher is newly introduced spending, not a saving.

Take a $25 voucher for $50 of food. If it replaces a $50 dinner out you’d have had this month anyway, it saves you $25, but if you bought it because the deal looked good, the whole night is fresh spending.

The saving also has an end date. Groupon’s terms give its standard voucher two values. The amount you paid doesn’t expire. The promotional value, the extra $25 the deal adds, expires on the date shown on the voucher unless the law forbids it.

According to recent reports, an expired voucher still covers the $25 you paid at that restaurant, so a late visit costs you the full $50. Never use it, and you’ve paid $25 for nothing.

Decide on the dinner or the massage first, then search a deal site for a voucher, rather than buying whatever the day’s deal email offers.

Groupon’s terms say it doesn’t vet the businesses it sells vouchers for or guarantee the best price, so check four more things before you buy.

Recent findings suggest that last, call the business and ask how soon it can book a voucher customer. A first opening after the deal’s expiration date means paying full price, so skip that deal.
