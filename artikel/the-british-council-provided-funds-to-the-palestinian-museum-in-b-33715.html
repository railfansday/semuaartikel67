TERROR’S TREASURY. The British Council has provided public funding to the Palestinian Museum in Birzeit, West Bank, which has been accused of glorifying terrorist attacks against Israeli civilians, The Telegraph reports. Exhibits include posters depicting Dalal al-Mughrabi, who led the 1978 Coastal Road massacre in which 38 Israelis were killed, as a martyr, alongside material celebrating Hamas bomb-maker Yahya Ayyash. UK Lawyers for Israel has called for funding to be suspended and an investigation rolled out. The Charity Commission has opened a regulatory compliance case into Taawon UK, a charity linked to the museum. TEPID RESULT AT CHRISTIE’S. As ARTnews reported, Christie’s 20th-century art evening sale at Rockefeller Center raised $626.6 million including fees. The 74-lot auction was led by a Wassily Kandinsky abstraction, which sold for $85.8 million, followed by a Gustav Klimt landscape at $82.3 million. The sale’s hammer total was $521.1 million, just above the low estimate. Five works were withdrawn before the auction, including a Frida Kahlo painting estimated at $2–3.5 million, which was pulled just hours before proceedings began.

Industry observers note that COLLECTING DUST. A dinosaur fossil that sat in a drawer at London’s Natural History Museum for nearly a century has been identified as a previously unknown species, Futura reports. Named Invictibellatrix amberina, it’s the first megaraptoran discovered in Europe. The three-to-four-metre-long predator lived around 120 million years ago during the Early Cretaceous period and was closely related to tyrannosaurs. Its skull fragment was acquired by the museum in 1924, having been discovered on the Isle of Wight by amateur paleontologist Reginald Walter Tooley. Researchers say the long-clawed, feathered dinosaur would have hunted smaller dinosaurs, lizards, and mammals.
