One of the co-owners of the operators of the “endless” Villa Vie Odyssey cruise hopes that residents are able to buy the ship back so they can continue with their travels.

Back on October 27, 200 travellers on board the Villa Vie Odyssey cruise were told that the “endless” adventure had come to an end. Residents who had paid $120,000 for a spot on board the ship were informed they had hours to pack their stuff and get off in Sihanoukville, Cambodia.

Villa Vie Residences, the cruise’s operator, blamed rising fuel costs for the ship’s “temporary layup,” stating that they would not be comfortable passing on the additional cost to passengers.

The ship has since been seized by authorities in Singapore, with reports claiming that it is “likely” to do with an unpaid fuel bill. However, there are also rumors that wealthy passengers might be lining up a bid for it at auction.

Speaking to CNN Travel, Villa Vie co-owner and executive chairman Mikael Petterson stated he supports the rumored efforts.

According to recent reports, “I support residents who are trying to raise capital and acquire the ship if it goes to auction, and I will assist in every way I can,” Petterson said. “Any successful acquisition would require the resident group to provide the equity and fund the costs of operating the company, using an approach similar to the one used in the past.

“There is no guarantee an acquisition will happen, but I hope they can develop a viable initiative.”.

Petterson added that he also understands why residents “are angry and disappointed” by the situation with being kicked off the ship.

Industry observers note that one couple spent $1 million between buying their spot on the ship and travelling around the world. They even sold their house to boost that budget as well. However, the Cassinghams told CNN they aren’t in any rush to rejoin the cruise if other passengers can rescue it.

Another woman spent close to $64,000 for a trip on the cruise, but didn’t even set foot on board, seeing the journey before her sailing date.

She doesn’t have any hopes about getting the money back, either.
