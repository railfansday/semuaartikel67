FF7 Revelation's story isn't being "shaped by fan reactions," boss notes, but feedback is helping gameplay

Sources indicate that creative decision-making for a sequel can be a tricky push-pull between the devs and fan feedback. Ultimately, the team has to discern what's actionable from player feedback. But that insight can be invaluable. One of the central figures in Final Fantasy 7: Revelation is adamant that, while some of what fans had to say is being used, the game's vision stems first and foremost from the people on the ground who made it.

Naoki Hamaguchi, the director of Revelation and overseer of the Final Fantasy 7 remake trilogy, explained his perspective on Twitter. "We believe it's our responsibility as creators to decide the major direction of the story, rather than having it shaped by fan reactions," he states. "Together with a fresh generation of developers, we're reimagining Final Fantasy 7 for today.".

Sources indicate that his distinction between the narrative elements is crucial here because there's a difference between writing and storytelling, and between the mechanics. The latter can, and I would argue should, be more open to player experience, because you want any given release to be comfortably playable to as much of your desired community as possible.

According to recent reports, we truly value your feedback when it comes to usability and the overall gameplay experience. Hamaguchi states as much in a follow-up tweet, nodding in particular to people who use the accessibility options. "We truly value your feedback when it comes to usability and the overall gameplay experience," he writes. "The feedback we've received from the previous two games has helped shape the experience of Final Fantasy 7: Revelation.".

Such tweaks matter to making sure fans can fully enjoy the game and its narrative the way the devs would like. The more ways to customize the gameplay, the better for everyone.

Industry observers note that and don't forget, this is the big crescendo, where Cloud and his cohorts face Sephiroth for the fate of the planet, Aerith in tow. It's going to be massive, and probably a bit confusing, because of the wibbly-wobbly timeline stuff that's happened so far. Regardless of whether you can make heads or tails of what's going on, you can be sure the combat and exploration in Square Enix's game will be smooth.

After Final Fantasy 7 Revelation, series lead notes Square Enix is "more likely to invest in fresh titles and new games" than more JRPG remakes.
