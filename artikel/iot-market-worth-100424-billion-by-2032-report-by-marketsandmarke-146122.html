IoT Market - Investment & Funding Scenario.

The Internet of Things (IoT) Market is attracting increasing investments as organizations prioritize industrial automation, intelligent connectivity, real-time asset monitoring, smart infrastructure, and digital transformation. Investment is increasingly directed toward IoT platforms, connected sensors, industrial gateways, edge computing, cellular connectivity, and cloud-based device management solutions. Major companies, including Microsoft, Amazon Web Services (AWS), Huawei, Cisco, Siemens, ABB, Intel, and Qualcomm, continue to strengthen their IoT capabilities through technology development, ecosystem partnerships, and strategic investments. For instance, Siemens completed its approximately USD 10 billion acquisition of Altair Engineering in March 2025, strengthening its industrial simulation, digital twin, and data analytics capabilities. Similarly, ABB acquired Sensorfact in February 2025, expanding its industrial energy management portfolio through connected monitoring and software-as-a-service solutions. Among specialized IoT companies, Particle, Blues, emnify, ClearBlade, and Losant address demand for connected-device infrastructure, embedded connectivity, remote monitoring, and scalable IoT applications. These developments highlight growing investment in intelligent connectivity, industrial digitalization, low-power devices, and integrated IoT solutions across manufacturing, healthcare, transportation, energy, and smart infrastructure.

The Internet of Things (IoT) Market is shifting from traditional hardware-centric business models to integrated, software-driven solutions that combine connected devices, cloud platforms, connectivity management, and advanced analytics. Organizations are increasingly adopting subscription-based services, usage-based pricing, managed connectivity, and recurring software licensing models to improve operational efficiency and support scalable deployments. This transition is creating recurring revenue opportunities through IoT device management, cloud subscriptions, remote monitoring, software applications, and connected services. For instance, AWS IoT Core follows a consumption-based pricing structure, while Microsoft Azure IoT Hub offers tiered cloud services for connected-device communication and management. Similarly, Cisco provides IoT connectivity management capabilities, while Oracle, SAP, and IBM support connected enterprise operations through cloud applications, analytics, and integration services. Specialized players such as emnify, Blues, ClearBlade, Ayla Networks, and Losant offer recurring-service models involving IoT connectivity, cloud-based device management, and application enablement. Particle's approximately USD 20 million in annual recurring revenue at the time of its January 2026 acquisition further illustrates the commercial importance of subscription-based connected-device platforms. This shift is encouraging IoT vendors to move beyond standalone hardware sales toward integrated, recurring-revenue models that combine connectivity, software, analytics, continuous monitoring, and long-term enterprise services.

Sources indicate that mergers & acquisitions (M&A) in the Internet of Things (IoT) Market are increasingly focused on strengthening industrial connectivity, edge computing, intelligent automation, connected infrastructure, and software-driven solutions. Major companies such as Siemens, ABB, and PTC are reshaping their technology portfolios through strategic acquisitions and divestitures to enhance operational intelligence, industrial monitoring, and digital transformation capabilities. Recent transactions demonstrate increasing consolidation across industrial software, connected sensors, cloud platforms, and energy management systems. These developments are expanding IoT applications across manufacturing, transportation, smart buildings, energy, and connected infrastructure while supporting recurring revenue growth and integrated technology offerings.
