Inside the Madcap World of Chuyín: ‘Everyone Sings About the Neverending Party. I Sing About the Hangover’

At the 2026 Billboard Latin Music Awards last month, a mob of Chuyíns in white straitjackets turned the stage into a psychiatric ward during a performance of “Pues Ya Ni Pedo.” The show’s closing gag revealed members of Fuerza Regida beneath the oversized masks — a fitting glimpse into the wonderfully chaotic world of the música mexicana breakout star’s May debut album, Los Locos Nunca Mueren (Crazy People Never Die).

But to understand the mysterious masked performer, first enter the void.

According to recent reports, “Before Chuyín, there was nothing, just black space,” the masked singer tells Billboard. According to his origin story, he came into existence on May 6, 2023, at a Fuerza Regida concert in Tepic, Nayarit, where frontman Jesús Ortiz Paz (JOP) “adopted” him. He’s been living with JOP ever since, he says, never once breaking character throughout the interview.

Industry observers note that chuyín is three years old — or 21, if you count in dog years, as he does — and already making his mark on música mexicana. His 16-track debut album, released May 7 via Street Mob Records, debuted at No. 9 on the Top Regional Mexican Albums chart and No. 17 on Top Latin Albums, both dated May 23. In September, “Pues Qué Le Hago?” gave him his first No. 1 on a Billboard chart, topping Regional Mexican Airplay.

On Los Locos Nunca Mueren, which features Fuerza Regida, Calle 24, Luis R Conriquez, Óscar Maydon and Jorsshh, Chuyín takes on the carnal pleasures and intoxicating excesses common to contemporary corridos — then gives them a comedic twist. His protagonist doesn’t escape the consequences.

In a recent development, on “Paniki,” a cheating Chuyín goes to ridiculous lengths to conceal his infidelity, only to get caught when he tries his luck again. On the album opener, “Rehabilitado,” he declares himself rehabilitated while remaining tempted by his vices.

According to recent reports, “Everyone sings about the never-ending party. I sing about the hangover,” he notes. “Everyone sings about partying, partying and partying. I sang about getting sent to rehab.”.

Recent findings suggest that consequences are central to Chuyín’s character, but so is perseverance. “Chuyín is the guy everything happens to, and somehow he gets back up and keeps going,” he explains. “Everything that could go wrong happens to Chuyín, and he gets up with the same smile and says, ‘OK, let’s keep going.’”.

In a recent development, behind the irreverence is an emotionally invested songwriter. Chuyín notes he wrote “Olvídate de Mí” entirely on his own while living through the failed relationship that inspired it. Rather than admit that he’s the problem, he tells his partner to forget him — and run if she knows what’s good for her.

Sources indicate that the song reflects the openness he says defines the album. “If you listen to my music, you know me, and you don’t need to know anything else,” he says. “I give myself to the public completely, emotionally. And I think that’s what connected with people.”.

For Chuyín, concealing his face affords him the freedom to bare his soul. “I feel like I’m mysterious, but I’m very vulnerable in my music,” he states. “It’s a beautiful thing that people can get to know you without you showing your face, because of what you sing about. I always sing about personal things I’ve experienced.”.

Industry observers note that with JOP as his mentor, Chuyín has honed his songwriting chops alongside Street Mob’s wider circle of creatives. “For me, writing music is like therapy,” he indicates. His songwriting extends beyond his own repertoire: He also helped pen Fuerza Regida’s “Tu Sancho.”.

Onstage, Chuyín has experienced both the scale of a Fuerza stadium show and the intimacy of his own performance. Singing with the group at Mexico City’s Estadio GNP Seguros in front of an estimated 80,000 people was exhilarating, he says. But a solo Chuyín show in Chicago, where 800 people had come specifically to see him, felt even more personal.

“Both experiences were incredible, but they were very different,” he says. “Every one of those 800 people came just to see Chuyín.”.

Name: Chuyín. Asked about the person behind the mask, he insists, “I’m Chuyín.”.

Sources indicate that age: 3 — or 21 in Chuyín years. Each year counts as seven because Chuyín is “like a little dog,” he notes.

Born and raised: Tepic, Nayarit, Mexico; Rancho Cucamonga, Calif.

Recommended song: “Olvídate de Mí,” a candid account of romantic self-sabotage that Chuyín says he penned entirely on his own.

Musical influences: His personal Mount Rushmore of música mexicana consists of Alfredo Olivas, Gerardo Ortiz, Natanael Cano and Junior H.

According to recent reports, major accomplishment: Scoring his first Billboard No. 1 with “Pues Qué Le Hago?,” which topped Regional Mexican Airplay in September. “I put all the love in the world into the music,” he indicates. “The fact that the public embraced it and that I made it onto the Billboard charts is crazy.”.

Recent findings suggest that what’s next: Chuyín is working on his sophomore album and exclusively reveals to Billboard that he will embark on his first headlining tour in 2027, with further details to be announced soon. The artist is preparing an immersive live show with one particularly mischievous tradition in mind: “At every show, I’m going to crown a Chuyín,” he notes. “With every show, there will be one more Chuyín in this world.”.
