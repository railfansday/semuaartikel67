UPDATED 11:25 EST / NOVEMBER 16 2026.

Alecto Conveyancing Ltd., a London-based artificial intelligence-native law firm, today announced it raised $20 million in seed funding led by Insight Partners to make the law more accessible to the general public.

It has been revealed that firstminute Capital, Blue Lion Global, Andrena Ventures, FJ Labs, Antiportfolio Ventures, the Rigby Group and Mischon de Reya LLP also joined in the round. Angel investors participating in the round included Octopus Group Chief Executive and founder Simon Rogerson and SwiftKey founder John Reynolds.

Founded in September 2025 by CEO Will Rice (pictured), a serial entrepreneur who previously built fintech mortgage lender Gen H, Alecto’s first service targets the United Kingdom conveyancing market, the legal work required to buy and sell property. Rice drew on his experience building a mortgage company and decided to focus initially on bringing more people into ownership. With that expertise, Alecto aims to use AI to help give everyday people greater access to the law.

Alecto argues that traditional legal services are starved, with too few qualified attorneys and too few hours a day. Billing is based on hours, not outcomes and the service model doesn’t lean toward customers; this leads to unpredictable costs and a process that leaves customers in the dark.

Recent findings suggest that the law firm mentioned it’s using AI and legal experts to explain opaque legal processes in plain language and help clients make informed decisions. The firm intends to target the property conveyancing market, which represents almost $3.3 billion in the U.K. alone, and aims to become the number one leader in property legal services by 2027.

According to recent reports, “For too long, the legal industry has expected customers to work on its terms; now, AI presents the opportunity to put the consumer in control,” Rice said. “People don’t want to buy a lawyer’s time; they want results. Alecto exists to make the law accessible to everyone, giving control back to the consumer.”.

Sources indicate that consumer legal services in the U.K. value growth pushed revenue over £55 billion, around $72.5 billion, in 2026, according to a report from Research and Markets. Business and commercial practices lead with 51% market value, followed by growth in the injury and conveyancing law markets.

“Property is where we prove the model. We’re building the technology and operations to rapidly break into newly introduced countries and new areas of law,” Rice added. “Ultimately, access to exceptional legal support should not depend on how much money you have or how confidently you can navigate the system.”.
