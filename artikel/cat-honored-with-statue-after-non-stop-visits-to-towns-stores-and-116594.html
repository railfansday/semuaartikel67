A town in England has created a bronze statue honoring a beloved cat famous for roaming its pubs, shops, and supermarkets.

Following the story of an orange cat who took a 37-mile solo train journey to London, another adventurous kitty has been making headlines. Meet Susie, a feline with a penchant for exploring her English town of Dorchester.

When she’s not busy napping away, Susie is particularly famous for appearing across the town’s pubs, shops, and other businesses. In fact, the four-legged celebrity is so popular amongst residents that she even has her own Facebook fan page where fans are updated with her latest adventures.

However, Susie’s fame has skyrocketed even further after the local community revealed a bronze statue of the beloved cat that now sits in the town center.

According to the BBC, crowds gathered in the southern English town of Dorchester on November 23 for the long-awaited unveiling.

The bronze sculpture indicates Susie wearing Roman-style beads around her neck, while a tile bearing her paw prints and information about the adventurous kitty sits beside her.

As for the reasons behind the Roman design, it pays tribute to Dorchester’s ancient past, with the English town dating back to a settlement established after Roman forces arrived in the area in 43 AD. A cool little detail for all you history buffs out there!

While Susie didn’t make an appearance at her own statue unveiling (she chose to take a nosy at a local supermarket instead), owner Jenny Graves said the cat’s adventures have brought the community together.

Sources indicate that “It’s completely crazy, but Dorchester has really taken Susie into their heart,” mentioned Graves. “Actually, it’s been such a positive thing, not only for me as I’ve met loads of people I wouldn’t have met before, and I’ve got an entire town looking after the cat.”.

The statue’s sculptor, Ama Menec, hopes the statue will give visitors somewhere to take photos when Dorchester’s most elusive resident cannot be found.

“I really hope people come and have selfies with her, especially the frustrated people who can’t find the real Susie, because sometimes she’s just never to be found,” she said.
