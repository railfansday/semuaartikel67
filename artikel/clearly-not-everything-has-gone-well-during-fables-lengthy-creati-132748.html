📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Fable reboot is finally set to kick the release chicken (I'm not sure that's an established phrase anywhere in the world, but roll with it) early next year, following a not small amount of time in development and multiple delays. Despite that long time in the oven having made it necessary to look beyond just the series' classic entries for RPG inspiration, Playground Games' Fable general manager has said the team's "original vision" of what a Fable game needs to be hasn't changed.

I say this with some pride – tempered against the fact that this has been obviously an enormously long development, so clearly not everything has gone well – but I would say that our original vision of what Fable needs to be has remained unchanged," Ralph Fulton, one of Playground's co-founders, told our suit-wearing siblings at GIBiz.

"There are many reasons that this game development has been long," he added. "It's a fresh team, it's a new genre, a new franchise, new technology – although we're building in an engine that we know well. It's also because a Fable game must have a great story with memorable characters and high-stakes drama, and also it needs to be funny and to have that British wit infused throughout it. Then there's combat, the quest structure, and the world of Albion and its people, and the simulations that underpin it. You'd be nuts to take on all of that in the first project, and yet here we are.".

Recent findings suggest that fulton went on to note that despite that, Playground have worked to ensure their game's scope hasn't grown ever wider as they've made it. One call made early on with the social and economic simming that goes with the likes of landlording and being a dick to entire villages was that the studio "would only model first and second degree ripple effects", due to feeling that "players would only notice or we could only communicate the consequences up to the second degree". That makes sense, not every game can or should be a strategy game like Civ, wherein seeing your actions cause or influence a thousand different tidal waves over time is part of the central appeal. Not every game also has the budget of a GTA 6, which works deep simulation mechanics into a crime adventure, but feels like an exception to the rules even compared to Xbox-backed games like Fable.

On the other hand, Fulton did make clear that Playground have looked beyond the original entries in the series for RPG inspiration, fearing their game might be left behind if not. "Principally, technology has advanced," he said when asked what's changed while the game's been in the works. "In 2015, The Witcher 3 redefined what an open-world action RPG looks like and became the gold standard. And after that bar was raised, the Assassin's Creed franchise changed into this more modern iteration with Origins, Odyssey, and Valhalla, and became like a quasi-RPG.

"All of these games continue to reset player expectations, which means I think looking only to the original Fable trilogy for inspiration would leave us lacking in terms of what the modern audience, the modern marketplace, expects.".

Industry observers note that we've already seen some of the ways in which the reboot diverges from its older forebears. As far as I'm concerned, albeit as someone with only a brief dalliance in the classic Fables, such tweaks could go either way. It is interesting to see Assassin's Creed mentioned alongside The Witcher 3 as having changed the RPG game. A game like Baldur's Gate 3 or even Starfield would strike me as a more obvious candidate to point to, so the AC mention might offer some indiction of the direction Playground have taken with their design.

Sources indicate that fable is, of course, being backed by Microsoft. The tech giant are subject to an ongoing boycott by the BDS movement and have inflicted significant layoffs on their Xbox studios this year. As for whether the latter upheaval has affected Playground or Xbox's backing of Fable, Fulton offered a rather nothing answer here about Forza Horizon being good for the studio and the importance of keeping everyone internally in the loop as to how work on the game's going.
