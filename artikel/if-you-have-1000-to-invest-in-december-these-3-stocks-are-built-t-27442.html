Sources indicate that microsoft (MSFT +0.00%), ASML (ASML +1.08%), and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM +2.32%) (TSMC) are three names worth a closer look for investors looking to hold stocks for the next decade. Each has a powerful competitive edge and exposure to some of the biggest technology trends shaping the future, and all three reported strong quarterly results.

Starting with Microsoft, here's what gives each business its staying power.

According to recent reports, microsoft has spent decades building an ecosystem that businesses and consumers rely on every day, giving it a competitive advantage that goes beyond any single product. And these days, that business model continues to drive growth.

For example, in its fiscal 2027 first-quarter results, Microsoft reported 19% year-over-year revenue growth, with Microsoft Cloud revenue climbing 30% and Azure revenue growing 48%.

NASDAQ · As of 9:33 AM ET 11/18/26.

Plus, Microsoft is integrating artificial intelligence (AI) across its product portfolio through Copilot, while Azure provides the cloud infrastructure businesses need to develop and run AI applications. As a result, rather than relying on a single AI product, Microsoft has several ways to monetize the technology.

However, the risk is that Microsoft continues to spend heavily on AI infrastructure, and investors still want to see it drive revenue growth.

Industry observers note that still, the majority of Wall Street analysts rate the stock a buy, with an average target price of $636. That implies a potential upside of about 21%. On top of that, at a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of about 26, Microsoft doesn't look particularly expensive given its growth opportunities in cloud computing and AI.

Another firm, meanwhile, is supplying something just as crucial: machines that enable advanced semiconductor manufacturing.

Industry observers note that ASML's place in the AI supply chain is extremely hard to replicate. Specifically, the company makes advanced lithography equipment used by manufacturers to produce complex chips that power AI and high-performance computing.

NASDAQ · As of 9:33 AM ET 11/18/26.

Competing with ASML would require decades of research, engineering expertise, and an extensive global supplier network. As a result, that creates a powerful long-term advantage, especially as AI accelerates demand for advanced chips.

The results are showing up in the numbers.

According to recent reports, ASML recently reported record third-quarter net sales of €11.7 billion, as customer demand continued to strengthen amid growing AI spending. In fact, management indicated that customers were already expressing interest in placing orders for 2029 and even 2030.

But the risk now is ASML's exposure to cyclical spending, export restrictions, and geopolitical tensions.

Still, the vast majority of Wall Street analysts covering the stock rate it a buy or strong buy, with an average target price of $2,112, representing approximately 50% potential upside. Plus, the valuation looks reasonable, with the stock trading at a forward P/E of 23 times.

Meanwhile, another company manufactures many of the world's most advanced chips used in the AI build-out.

TSMC runs the world's leading dedicated semiconductor foundry business, manufacturing chips for some of the world's biggest tech companies.

NYSE · As of 9:31 AM ET 11/18/26.

Recent findings suggest that in turn, this business model allows TSMC to benefit from demand across the semiconductor industry. That means it doesn't need to predict which chip designer will win the AI race.

Industry observers note that instead, it can capture growth across the whole industry. Here's how that has shown up in the numbers.

In its third-quarter 2026 results, TSMC reported revenue growth of around 51% year over year, with its advanced manufacturing technologies making up 79% of total wafer revenue. Looking ahead, management pointed to strong demand for leading-edge manufacturing, including the ramp-up of its two-nanometer technology.

But TSMC's biggest risk is its heavy exposure to tensions between China and Taiwan, as well as U.S.-China trade restrictions.

Industry observers note that despite that, 20 of the 21 Wall Street analysts covering the stock rate it a buy or strong buy, with an average target price of about $587, suggesting 57% potential upside. On top of that, it trades at just 18 times forward P/E, making it relatively attractive to investors bullish on its dominant position in advanced chipmaking.

Together, Microsoft, ASML, and TSMC offer exposure to three of the most key parts of the tech industry, making them excellent picks for the next decade.

Industry observers note that ultimately, Microsoft is the safest option because its ecosystem extends beyond any single product. From there, ASML is in the middle, while TSMC is the high-risk, high-reward pick. Overall, for investors with capital to put to work, these three businesses have the staying power to be solid investments over the next decade.
