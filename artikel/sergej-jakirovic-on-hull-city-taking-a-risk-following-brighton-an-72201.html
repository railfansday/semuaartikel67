It has been revealed that the Seagulls started the 21st Century in English football's fourth tier with no history of challenging at the top of the pyramid and no home, but are now second in the Premier League, fifth in the Europa Conference League and looking good for it. They will come to East Yorkshire on Sunday looking to end Hull's unbeaten start to the season at home.

Although they have not won since August, the Tigers are also punching above their weight, 13th in the Premier League and crucially with more points than games played after coming up via the play-offs.

Hull signed 18 players in the summer, 14 of whom were under the age of 25, and another 14 new to the Premier League.

“We have to speak about them because it is not now by accident,” said Hull coach Jakirovic. “They are performing even better every game.

It has been revealed that “They have very significant confidence, an excellent coach (Fabian Hurzeler), system. This club has a spine and very good players – young players with potential.

“If your energy is below 100 per cent you don't have a chance against them.

According to recent reports, “They sold a few good players but they have good scouting. They always bring in young potential players. They pay money for them, then they try to develop these players and then sell again. It is a great system.”.

It is one Hull are trying to mimic.

“Sometimes you cannot afford better players, more expensive players, but I think that we brought young players with good potential,” he said.

“Now it's about developing and how we can improve them because this is a very major step for some of them.

“Especially if you stay in the league, then you have more money, you can invest, you can add more quality players.

“It's about vision. It's about strategy. This is everything with Brighton, Brentford and Bournemouth – they highlighted already how one club who is new in the league can be successful.

“This is maybe an example of what we need to do.”.

Industry observers note that not many of Hull's upcoming 18 have made much impact in the Premier League – yet. Some were always going to take time, and others will not come off at all.

“We have some examples here who joined us too late after pre-season, then you start to play,” acknowledged Jakirovic. “(You pick up) one injury, recovery, second injury. Now it's difficult to get back into the team – especially the first XI.

In a recent development, “They need time and they have to be patient and work very hard every day. So this is risky.”.

Nobel Mendy is fit again after an international break where Hull had a large number of players representing their countries, and Jakirovic watching closely to see how they performed.

“We had 12 players in the building (during the break),” he said. “But we had two good sessions before Thursday when we were finally all together. everyone got back healthy.

“Now it's about fatigue, it's about condition. On Saturday we will decide who will play.

“I tried to watch every game (his internationals played) and if I cannot watch, Josh (Marris, senior analyst) will put it on our computer. Even Qatar v Australia (which centre-back Lucas Herrington played in), even under-21 games, Greece, Norway, with Jens (Hjerto-Dahl).”.
