I’ve tested several Aiper cleaners over the years, and the Aiper Scuba V3 Ultra feels like a natural evolution of the brand’s previous models. I ran it through repeated cleaning cycles in my 8,500-gallon saltwater pool in Florida, where leaves, bugs, fine debris, and the occasional patch of algae give pool robots plenty to deal with. Setup was super simple: The Aiper app connected quickly, the robot updated without any fuss, and I had it in the water about 15 minutes after opening the box.

According to recent reports, what impressed me right away was just how much the Scuba V3 Ultra can do in one cleaning cycle. Plenty of the robotic pool cleaners we’ve tested at Bob Vila handle floors, walls, and the waterline, but only a select few can also skim floating debris from the surface. That matters in my open-air pool, which regularly collects bugs and leaves. Anything the Aiper actually reaches gets cleaned extremely well.

It has been revealed that the MicroMesh filter is another highlight. It traps extremely fine material, including algae and sediment that can slip through coarser filter baskets. Cleaning that ultra-fine layer takes a little more effort—I usually spend a few minutes thoroughly rinsing it with a hose and then leave it out to dry—but that’s a fair trade-off for how much fine material it captures. The app is also among the better pool-cleaner apps I’ve used, and I like getting a notification when the cleaning cycle is finished. The robot then floats neatly near the pool edge for retrieval.

Where the V3 Ultra disappointed me was its claim of shallow-area cleaning. Aiper states it can work in water down to about 7.9 inches deep, but my sun shelf is apparently too shallow for it to clean reliably. I still have to brush debris from the shelf into the main pool and let the robot collect it there. It also won’t clean my pool steps. For a $2,300 machine marketed around near-total pool coverage, those are significant omissions—especially since sun shelves have become so common in newer pools.

Industry observers note that still, I’m a fan of the Scuba V3 Ultra overall. My older Aiper cleaner has been running reliably for close to two years, which gives me more confidence in the brand than I’d have from a short-term test alone. The voice assistant is useful without being obnoxious, retrieval is simple, filtration is excellent, and cleaning performance everywhere the robot can reach is phenomenal. I’m still waiting for the robot that will truly clean every inch of my pool, but the Scuba V3 Ultra gets closer than most.
