Grand Theft Auto VI came out last Thursday, and somehow the most unbearable thing about it isn't the grown men playing the game—it's the couples.

After the release of the game, my feed became a nonstop slideshow of printed contracts, handmade gift baskets, and girlfriends narrating their boyfriends' disappearance into Vice City like it's a deployment. Couples posted printed agreements that grant the player 10 full days of uninterrupted access.

Recent findings suggest that I'm not here to tell anyone not to play a video game. People should do what makes them happy. But why are we all being so weird about a video game?

Recent findings suggest that ten days off from the relationship, so a man can play a game. I'd like to meet the woman who has ever been granted a 10-day leave from her partner, her family, her job, and the emotional labor of every relationship in her life. Women don't get to just disappear to pursue their hobbies.

Some women in relationships are putting together “offerings” for their partner. One viral example involved a GTA-themed gift basket stocked with snacks and drinks for the player's sessions. Another woman posted a Target-run basket of her boyfriend's favorite snacks with the caption, "This is your sign to make your man a GTA 6 basket!".

No. It's not. When was the last time he made you a basket for one of your hobbies?

Sources indicate that on the flip side, some men are gifting their partners a basket, with the idea that women need something in return—a little distraction to keep busy while he's busy. Women are not toddlers who need to be handed a snack and a coloring book while the grown-ups play. We have friends. We have jobs. We have entire lives that existed before his Rockstar account and will exist after it. We'll be fine. We might even enjoy the quiet!

Recent findings suggest that it all begs the question: Why can't men just have hobbies like normal people? Women have hobbies all the time. We go to pilates, we read, we get absorbed in a show, and nobody drafts a legal document. Let’s keep up that energy. Just play the game and also make time for the rest of your life!

According to recent reports, but it isn’t just about people finding the time to play the game. There’s separate discourse among couples about the relationship players maintain with Lucia, the main female character.

Industry observers note that online, some women have expressed feeling jealous of Lucia. Around the game's lead-up, users on TikTok and Instagram were claiming that boyfriends who nurture a virtual relationship with Lucia could be considered cheating. A YouGov poll of 4,000 adults found around 6 percent would consider dating in games like GTA VI to be infidelity. That's a generally small number, but it's still making headway on social media.

In a recent development, lucia is a character. She is pixels. If your relationship can be undone by a woman who exists only to rob a gas station with your boyfriend, the problem was never Lucia.

To be fair, some women have leaned into the joke. One girlfriend gifted her boyfriend the game with a four-rule counter-contract that ended with the line "Lucia is not your real girlfriend." I respect the bit—but it still icks me out.

Recent findings suggest that on the other hand, there have been people claiming that GTA 6 is teaching men to be better partners. I’d like to politely disagree. The theory goes that watching Jason treat Lucia well, and putting effort into that relationship on screen, will make players better boyfriends in real life.

I'm glad they're being attentive to Lucia. I mean that. It’s a nice change to see the video game encouraging men to be decent human beings (though the bar is low). But at the end of the day, they're buying flowers, fixing things up, and doing the emotional work for a woman who doesn't exist, while their actual girlfriends are across the room eating a basket of Swedish Fish.

Can we all get real for a second? What grown man needs a video game to teach him how to treat a partner? Showing up, communicating, and putting in effort are not secret skills only unlocked by a video game. I promise you your boyfriend is not learning valuable relationship skills playing GTA 6.

In a recent development, so to any couples out there being particularly weird about GTA 6: play the game, enjoy it, and please just be normal. Be a person with a hobby. It really isn't that deep.
