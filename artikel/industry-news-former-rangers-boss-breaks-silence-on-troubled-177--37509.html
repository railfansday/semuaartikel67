Former Rangers and Sunderland head coach Michael Beale has broken his silence on his turbulent 177-day spell in charge of Belgian Pro League side Zulte Waregem after resigning last week.

Beale, 46, brought down the curtain on his short time at the Elindus Arena following the 4-1 defeat to Sint-Truidense last week, which made it six consecutive league defeats. Their worst run of results since October 7, 2018, they are just four points above the relegation zone.

His spell in Belgium was plagued by rumours of a breakdown in his relationship with senior figures at the club over Waregem’s direction and strategy. Beale actually started the campaign brightly, going unbeaten in their opening five games. However, their form swiftly deteriorated, and they have failed to register a victory since September 6.

His first managerial role since being sacked after just 12 games in charge at Sunderland almost three years ago, he exited Waregem with an identical record to the one he held at the Stadium of Light, with four wins, two draws, and six losses, meaning he left both sides with a 33.3% win rate after 12 games.

In a short statement confirming his departure, Waregem remarked: “After several internal discussions between the head coach and the club, both parties decided to end their collaboration by mutual agreement.” Zulte Waregem are yet to appoint Beale’s permanent successor, with interim head coach Steve Colpaert expected to take charge of their league game vs Royal Antwerp at the weekend.

Recent findings suggest that A week on from his departure, Beale penned an emotional farewell message to Waregem via his official Instagram page, writing: “Waregem, thankyou. It has been an honour and privilege to manage this club and I want to begin by thanking the fans for their incredible support. From day one, your passion and loyalty stood out and made coaching this club feel truly special. I will never forget the welcome you gave me and I have been overwhelmed by all your messages and kind words over the last few days.

“This community has become a home to me and to everyone I met in the local shops, coffee houses, parks, restaurants and bars: thank you. Many of you have become true friends and you have made me feel completely one of your own. I also want to show my gratitude to the players and staff for their hard work, dedication and the special memories we created together. You have been a pleasure to work with and I wish you every success in the future.”.

The ex-Rangers manager first joined Rangers as first-team coach during Steven Gerrard’s spell as manager at Ibrox. However, he left to join the former Liverpool icon in a similar role when he was appointed at Aston Villa in November 2021. Shortly afterwards, Beale took on his first managerial role with Queens Park Rangers.

Sources indicate that he lasted just six months at Loftus Road before being poached by Rangers as a replacement for Giovanni van Bronckhorst. He struggled in the main role at Rangers, though, and was sacked in October 2023 before joining Sunderland three months later.
