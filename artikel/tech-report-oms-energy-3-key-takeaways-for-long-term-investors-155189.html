It has been revealed that on its first half of fiscal year 2027 earnings call, held on Nov. 25, 2026, OMS Energy (OMSE +1.50%) unveiled that it no longer expects full-year revenue to exceed fiscal 2026 levels as customer delivery schedules slow. Oilfield equipment demand remains tied to customer project timing, but the company holds a record cash balance that shields operations during extended procurement cycles. Three things management said stand out.

Recent findings suggest that customer decision cycles extended in the Middle East, delaying call-off orders, which are delivery requests under existing framework agreements. Slower order placement in Saudi Arabia pushed first-half revenue down to $66.7 million from $82.8 million a year prior, forcing management to lower top-line expectations.

Sources indicate that the manufacturer ended September with $172.3 million in cash and zero bank borrowings, representing about $4.06 per share. That financial buffer allows the manufacturer to navigate market slowdowns and evaluate potential acquisitions without taking on debt.

According to recent reports, management aligned purchasing with active orders, cutting inventory to $7.7 million from $17.2 million six months earlier. That inventory discipline, combined with growth in higher-margin repair and engineering services, lifted gross profit margin to 31.4% from 28.2%.

According to recent reports, order approval cycles are slowing across key international markets, but management is executing on its backlog of $40.9 million in confirmed orders expected within 12 months. The manufacturer maintains long-standing relationships with national oil companies while expanding service capabilities in Southeast Asia, positioning it to capture demand when customer activity resumes.

With zero bank borrowings and substantial liquidity, the manufacturer focuses on capital allocation discipline and organic capacity investment. Management is watching regional procurement timing while seeking strategic growth opportunities across Asia Pacific and the Middle East.
