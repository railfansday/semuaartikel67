Recent findings suggest that in Glen Ridge, Newly introduced Jersey, a red-brick Georgian Colonial has a remarkable backstory. Known locally as the “Titanic House,” it was designed, built, and first owned in 1904 by Henry Blank, who later sailed aboard the Titanic—and survived. When a family of three bought the sprawling home in 2021, its history and architectural pedigree were part of the appeal, and they wanted to honor both while making the house feel like their own.

Sources indicate that for designer Andrew Suvalsky, that meant striking the right aesthetic balance. “The biggest challenge was finding the sweet spot of grandeur and formality that is inherent to the house and its history, while making design choices that were suited to a more casual lifestyle,” he claims.

It has been revealed that over a three-year renovation period, Suvalsky kept priceless details—like the original crown molding, gold architectural details, and the home’s many chandeliers—while giving the rest of the space a much-needed decor upgrade. “I’ve done traditional interiors, but this house is the most elaborate I’ve worked on, and the design had to meet the house on its level,” he notes.

According to recent reports, the renovation touched nearly every room, from the billiards lounge to a fully remade kitchen. He also layered in luxe touches throughout: In the dining room, upholstered Fortuny panels bring a timeless elegance, while the bar’s walnut paneling and ornate cornucopia garland frame the fireplace.

As Suvalsky spent more time in the house, its grand scale began to feel more intimate. “It helped me understand how building a house this size made sense for a family 120 years ago and how it can translate to a family’s life today,” the designer says. He sees the resulting space as meaningfully in dialogue with its deep history while looking ahead to the family’s future. “It’s nice to know that good architecture can truly stand the test of time.”.

In a recent development, this room was designed as a proper sitting area that highlights the view—both of the landscape outside and into the surrounding foyer and bar. The sumptuous jewel tones throughout make for total eye candy, softening the formality of the space. “The plum grasscloth wall covering and dark Wedgwood blue trim are meant to feel dramatic, glamorous, and regal,” Suvalsky adds.

“The bar is reminiscent of the swankiest of lounges in a historic hotel, showcasing high-design elements from periods throughout the 20th century,” Suvalsky notes. Guests can sidle up to the centerpiece surface, comprised of dark walnut accented with blue opalescent tufted leather panels. Overhead, a Schumacher wallpaper provides the right amount of pattern play on the ceiling.

It has been revealed that the Gilded Age grandeur continues into the billiards room, complete with an antique poker table and a pool table that had been in the home for generations. Suvalsky states the saturated baize hues were natural inspiration for the rest of the furnishings: “The deep blues and red and green touches play off the rug and give the feeling of an elaborate speakeasy.”.

“Of all the fresh design elements, I’m most excited about the kitchen,” Suvalsky notes. It had been about 25 years since its last renovation, and it was in need of a layout overhaul to increase flow and keep up with the owners’ love of cooking and entertaining.

Recent findings suggest that with its white marble checkerboard floor and airier atmosphere, the room is an aesthetic departure from the home’s moody lounge areas, making it a more family-friendly gathering area. “The reference point here was a soft approach to a country English style kitchen that feels like it might have been built in the 1920s and refreshed with a midcentury palette and lighting,” Suvalsky notes.

One of the kitchen’s most notable design flourishes is the custom mint-green paint color Suvalsky chose not only for the cabinets and trim, but for the BlueStar range as well. “Clients increasingly want their range to be a major focal point, if not the focal point,” he notes. The cook space customizations didn’t stop there, though: He also relocated the warming drawer from its typical spot below the oven and installed it at mid-height as a regular drawer in the island, “to ensure it would get used daily,” he states.

Recent findings suggest that as Suvalsky puts it, “This is the ultimate high-drama space.” The dining room contains many of the project's biggest investment pieces, such as the custom-designed, all-silk oval rug by Mark Nelson Designs, as well as the Fortuny upholstered wall panels. Bordering the space, gold and lacquered moldings have the look of a gilded confection.

With its French Provincial headboard and Venetian Fortuny ceiling fixture, the primary bedroom is transportive in both time and place. “Softened with European classical details, this bedroom is meant to feel calm and elevate daily life,” Suvalsky says.

Borrowing a similar palette and design sensibility as the primary bedroom, this adjoining dressing room contains “elements with a romantic feeling,” says Suvalsky.

Tucked under the grand main staircase, this powder room is “a beautiful little gem-like space,” according to Suvalsky. Despite its tiny footprint, this half-bath showcases a kaleidoscope of patterns, with geometric tiled floors, swirls of marble on the columnar sink, and sapphire-blue flora-and-fauna wallpaper.

Andrew Suvalsky is the founder of Andrew Suvalsky Designs, located in both Upcoming York City and Montclair, New Jersey. With his team of talented designers and architects, as well as the support from vendors and tradesmen, Suvalsky creates unique interiors that capture his clients' dreams without sacrificing functionality. Though the firm covers a range of design styles, clients can always count on Suvalsky to incorporate both antique and new pieces, as well as bespoke and custom furnishings.
