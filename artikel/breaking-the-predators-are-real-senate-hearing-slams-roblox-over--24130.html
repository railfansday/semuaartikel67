Breaking: ‘The Predators Are Real’: Senate Hearing Slams Roblox Over Child Sex Abuse

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

People who say they were groomed on Roblox testified in front of a Congressional subcommittee Tuesday about safety risks associated with the popular gaming platform frequented by kids and teenagers.

Industry observers note that “Roblox is one of the most popular gaming platforms in the world,” Republican senator Josh Hawley stated in his opening remarks. “If your children are on Roblox they are being exposed in all likelihood to sexual predators … to sexually exploitative content, left, right, and center.”.

The hearing was intended to address the bipartisan legislation introduced by Hawley and Democratic senator Dick Durbin, coauthors of the No Forced Arbitration for Child Sexual Exploitation and Cyber Injuries Act, which would allow families of victims to sue Roblox directly instead of being forced to settle their claims privately in arbitration, as the platform’s terms of service presently dictate.

The process of forced arbitration “strips consumers and workers of their rights,” testified Myriam Gilles, a law professor at Northwestern University who is an expert on forced arbitration. “I never expected to come before this body to talk about its use against 9-year-olds, but that’s where we are.”.

According to recent reports, roblox is currently facing nearly 200 lawsuits from families who allege that their children were groomed by sexual predators posing as children on the platform. According to Hawley, Roblox CEO David Baszucki was invited to testify at the hearing but declined.

“He may think he’s silent, but in fact he’s testifying, and what he’s testifying to is his commitment to money over people,” Hawley said.

Recent findings suggest that in a statement, Nicky Jackson Colaço, global head of public policy at Roblox, told WIRED, “Safety has been central to Roblox since we launched. We require age checks for chat, place users under 16 in age-based accounts with protective defaults, and block images and video in chat. A legal position about arbitration is not a measure of a company’s safety record, and a hearing built around it fails to advance safety. Protecting children online takes stronger protections, better tools, and consistent standards that apply everywhere kids spend time online.”.

Roblox has previously argued that users waive their right to sue in open court in the platform’s terms of agreement. According to Roblox’s own data, 35 percent of its age-checked daily active users are under the age of 13—too young to fully comprehend the terms of the agreement, witnesses argued.

“There is no way an 8-year-old could read those terms and then sign away their right to a moment in court, effectively silencing themselves,” Republican senator Katie Britt said after being presented with a copy of the multi-page agreement.

Two of those who testified before Congress were alleged survivors of grooming on the platform. One was Michael Schlep, a content creator who has advocated for more stringent child safety measures on the platform.

Schlep testified that when he was 12, he met a game developer on the platform who befriended him in a public chatroom, then moved to private chats. He alleges he was exposed to “violent pornography, cartel beheadings, and extreme racist ideologies” that he states was a result of his communication with this adult man, and that his abuser exposed his private information, leading to a suicide attempt. He presently runs a YouTube channel exposing alleged Roblox child predators and criticizing the platform for failing to protect children, which has led to Roblox hitting him with a cease-and-desist.

According to recent reports, “Roblox must be held accountable publicly,” Schlep testified. “They will not voluntarily act to protect children.”.

The hearing also featured testimony from a Marine Corps veteran identified as Woody Doe, who alleged that he was groomed, then sexually assaulted by an adult man he met on Roblox when he was 11 years old. “For years, I carried the shame of what happened,” he testified. He now has a lawsuit pending against Roblox for allegedly failing to warn parents and children of the dangers of the platform.

“I cannot go back and protect the 11-year-old boy I once was, but I can speak for him today … and prevent any other children from ever experiencing the same pain,” he noted.

Roblox is a massive gaming platform, with about 138 million daily active consumers according to recent manufacturer data. As of 2025, 39.7 million daily active users were under the age of 13.

In response to the litigation and to intense media scrutiny, Roblox has adopted what it says are more stringent child-safety measures, including AI-assisted face recognition to estimate a user’s age. But Khari James, senior legal counsel at the National Center on Sexual Exploitation said face-recognition technology isn’t always accurate, and predators could potentially purchase verified accounts to continue to prey on children. Doing face verifications just once, she argues, gives predators “complete access to all the children on Roblox.”.

Sources indicate that “Roblox may be a virtual world, but the children are real,” James stated during her testimony. “The predators are real. The addiction is real. The exploitation is real, and the consequences are real. Children should never have to enter a game marketed to them and wonder whether the person on the other side of the screen is there to play with them or prey on them.”.

Revision, November 17 at 9 pm: The story was updated to correct name and title of the Roblox spokesperson who provided comment.
