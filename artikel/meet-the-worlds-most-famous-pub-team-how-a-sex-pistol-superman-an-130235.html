Meet the world's most famous pub team: How a Sex Pistol, Superman and Vinnie Jones took Hollywood United  beyond Sunset Boulevard via private jet - and even rubbed shoulders with David Beckham's LA Galaxy

They had a World Cup winner in defence, Superman in their squad and a Sex Pistol in charge of recruitment.

There's a clue in the name, but Hollywood United must have been the wildest, most star-studded pub football team of all time.

Ray Winstone, Jason Statham, Mike Myers and Vinnie Jones all turned out for a team that grew out of a gathering of British expats at the Cat & Fiddle pub in the late Eighties.

It being Hollywood, they travelled to matches by private jet and they even took on David Beckham in a charity match.

Now, as the Sex Pistols prepare to embark on a tour marking the 50th anniversary of their debut single, guitarist Steve Jones has revealed how he assembled the extraordinary side on Hollywood's Sunset Boulevard.

Among those who pulled on the shirt were France's World Cup-winning defender Frank Leboeuf, former Scotland captain Richard Gough and Brandon Routh, the American actor who played the superhero in the 2006 film Superman Returns.

Routh was given no special treatment by his team-mates, who would mockingly shout ‘Superman!’ whenever he made a mistake.

Hollywood United is the world's most famous pub team - featuring a littany of high-profile stars.

Created by British expat and Sex Pistols star Steve Jones (right), United have rubbed shoulders with the likes of David Beckham.

Vinnie Jones, meanwhile, had apparently lost none of the competitive instincts that characterised his career in English football.

In 2002, it was reported that he had been sent off just 15 seconds after coming on as a substitute in a Hollywood United friendly.

Anthony LaPaglia, the Australian actor who starred in the hit television series Without a Trace, was their goalkeeper.

There were members of the Sex Pistols, The Cult and Def Leppard, as well as actors such as Ralf Little.

But there were also rather less celebrated footballers.

‘The start of it was with me and Anthony LaPaglia; he was a goalie, and from there it turned into Hollywood United,’ Jones told the Telegraph.

‘We always used to play in a league with a bunch of regular blokes, carpenters and so on, then we got a few more faces who wanted to start kicking about with us like Vinnie Jones.’.

The two Joneses met in a video shop in Los Angeles and the former Wimbledon hardman, who had embarked on a film career with Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels, needed little persuasion to join.

In a recent development, ‘He was up for a laugh, and he knows where to put a ball. Most of the team were amateurs, but we were decent and reached a certain level.’.

In November 2007, Hollywood United took on Beckham's LA Galaxy in a charity match.

In a recent development, england manager Steve McClaren had flown to California to assess Beckham's fitness ahead of a European Championship qualifier against Croatia. It's not clear whether he attended the match, which Hollywood United lost 12-4. Beckham scored twice, as did Leboeuf and over 5,000 spectators raised more than $100,000 for victims of the California wildfires.

Steve Jones, now 71, had himself broken one of football's golden rules by changing his allegiance.

Vinnie Jones (left) and Ray Winstone (right) have turned out for the amateur football team.

Actor Anthony LaPaglia lets the team travel on his private jet to matches across America.

Having followed QPR as a schoolboy in west London and also been associated with Fulham, he became a Chelsea supporter after moving to California and getting sober.

Chelsea's games were available on American television and his lifelong friend and fellow Sex Pistol Paul Cook was already a diehard Blues fan.

Hollywood United played at Griffith Park, beneath the famous Hollywood sign, but their adventures would take them considerably further afield.

LaPaglia would arrange private jets to transport the players to matches.

Gough recalls one trip when the team flew to Vancouver for a charity match before heading straight to the Mexican resort of Puerto Vallarta to play a side of former Mexico internationals.

Vinnie Jones also told the Guardian in 2007 that Hollywood United had fielded nine former international footballers in a single match.

The club's ranks included former Liverpool and Newcastle defender Barry Venison and United States internationals Alexi Lalas and Eric Wynalda.

Winstone, a lifelong West Ham fan, found playing in the Californian heat rather more demanding than he had anticipated.

Sources indicate that he later recalled being so exhausted by the time the ball reached him that he was barely able to kick it. And he wasn't particularly impressed by the willingness of former Chelsea defender Leboeuf to pass the ball, describing the Frenchman as a greedy player.

Among those watching from the touchline was the actor John Hurt, who was in town filming Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull with Winstone.

Some of the rock stars had impressive footballing pedigrees.

Ian Astbury, the lead singer of The Cult, had played boys' club football in Glasgow alongside future Everton and Scotland striker Graeme Sharp.

Gough, meanwhile, struck up a conversation with a team-mate from Northern Ireland without having the slightest idea he was Vivian Campbell, the guitarist of Def Leppard.

Sex Pistol Jones, who has lived in Los Angeles for more than 40 years, is returning to Britain next month for a tour marking the 50th anniversary of the release of Anarchy in the UK.

He still regularly attends football matches whenever he is back in England, although he has little time for the corporate hospitality enjoyed by many of his celebrity contemporaries.

‘I don't care where I sit, but I'm not interested in sitting in some box with a bunch of t***ers.’.
