H&R Block Review – Free for Simple Returns, With Tax Pros Online or in Person

In a recent development, file with H&R Block if your return fits Free Online, which takes retirement, Social Security, and unemployment income with a free state return, or if you want tax pros to answer questions or sign your return. If you need a paid tier but no help, file with a free program instead.

According to recent reports, doing your own taxes costs less than paying a preparer, but it leaves you alone with any form you don’t understand. H&R Block offers several kinds of help in between. Its online software files simple returns free, and its paid tiers include live tax pros who answer questions. For an added fee, a pro will check and file your return, or prepare it for you, online or in an office.

According to recent reports, brothers Henry and Richard Bloch founded H&R Block in 1955 in Kansas City, Missouri, where it’s still based. It has about 11,000 tax offices worldwide, with locations in every state, and nearly 60,000 tax pros. In its fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, its pros prepared 11.3 million U.S. returns, and customers prepared 3.6 million returns themselves in its paid online tiers.

H&R Block sorts its online software into four tiers by the forms a return needs, and the tier sets the price. Any return can start in Free Online, and H&R Block notes it moves you to a paid tier only when your situation requires one.

Free Online charges nothing for federal or state returns. It takes W-2 wages, unemployment benefits, retirement initiative distributions, Social Security, and up to $1,500 of interest and dividends. It also claims the Earned Income Tax Credit, the Child Tax Credit, education credits, and the student loan interest deduction. Its form list for 2025 returns included Schedule 1-A, which claims the new deductions for tips, overtime, car loan interest, and seniors.

In a recent development, it can’t itemize deductions, and it leaves out freelance or business income, rental income, stock sales, foreign income, and health savings accounts (HSAs). H&R Block notes about 52% of filers qualify, without saying how it counts them. A Federal Trade Commission (FTC) order requires its ads for free filing to give that share or say that most filers don’t qualify. Since the 2026 season, the same order has required H&R Block to return a filer who upgrades and then moves back down to the point they’d reached before upgrading, instead of starting them over.

Each paid tier adds forms the one before it lacks, and each charges $49 for every state return on top of its federal price.

Deluxe, Premium, and Self-Employed include unlimited help at no extra charge, and Free Online includes none. AI Tax Assist, H&R Block’s chatbot, answers tax questions at any hour and any step, and live tax pros take the ones you’d rather ask a person. The help is for your questions. For a pro to check, sign, and file your return, you add Tax Pro Review, which costs extra.

Tax Pro Review puts a tax pro on your return without an office visit. When you finish, the pro checks your return and documents for errors and missed deductions or credits, asks for anything more they need, and signs and e-files the return once you approve any changes. H&R Block says the review usually takes up to three days after you send your documents, though timing varies with a return’s complexity and in peak weeks. It adds $75 to Free Online, $65 to Deluxe, and $95 to Premium or Self-Employed.

According to recent reports, A pro can also prepare the whole return, starting at $99 plus a state fee. You can meet one at an H&R Block office, drop your documents off at one, or work with a pro from home through its MyBlock app.

It has been revealed that moving from another program takes an upload of last year’s Form 1040, or H&R Block will transfer the return for you. It notes a return from TurboTax or another tax prep service comes over in as little as two minutes. A federal refund deposited to Spruce, H&R Block’s banking app, can arrive up to five days early.

It has been revealed that the prices in this review are what H&R Block charges for 2025 returns. It usually opens the next season’s online software in November, and its prices for 2026 returns may differ.

It has been revealed that at those prices, a homeowner who itemizes and files one state return pays $114 in Deluxe, $65 plus $49 for the state, or $179 with Tax Pro Review. A freelancer who deducts business expenses needs Self-Employed, which comes to $179 with one state, or $274 with Tax Pro Review. Free Online with Tax Pro Review costs $75, state return included.

Each paid tier’s price includes its help, whether you use it or not. For a filer who won’t use the help, Cash App Taxes charges $0 for federal and state returns, freelance income and stock sales included, though it gives no tax advice. If you’re choosing between H&R Block and TurboTax, price the tier your return needs at both before you start.

It has been revealed that H&R Block’s guarantees depend on who prepared the return. On a return you prepare in its online software, the 100% Accuracy Guarantee reimburses penalties and interest caused by a software error, up to $10,000. On one its tax pro prepares, it reimburses penalties and interest from an H&R Block error, with no cap stated.

It has been revealed that the Maximum Refund Guarantee refunds the software fees if a software error left you a smaller refund or a larger tax bill than you were due, and you can amend the return in the software at no charge. You must claim it during the calendar year the return was prepared. Neither guarantee covers a mistake caused by incomplete or inaccurate information you supplied.

Sources indicate that if the IRS or your state sends a notice about a return you filed online, H&R Block explains it and gives you steps to respond at no extra charge. Someone to represent you at an audit costs extra. Worry-Free Audit Support, which you can buy with any online tier, adds in-person representation by an H&R Block enrolled agent, a tax specialist licensed by the IRS but not an attorney. It pays none of the tax, penalties, or interest you owe, and you have 60 days from the notice to tell H&R Block.

Industry observers note that H&R Block is worth it for two kinds of filers. One has a return that fits Free Online, which files a federal and a state return at no cost for more kinds of income than W-2 wages alone. The other wants a tax pro to ask, to check the return, or to prepare it, online or in an office.

It’s the wrong pick for a return that needs a paid tier but no help, since the tier’s price includes the help either way and a free program costs nothing. Start your return in Free Online and let H&R Block show you which tier your forms need before you pay for one.
