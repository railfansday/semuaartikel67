Xsolla ZK, Inc. is introducing Xsolla Gas (XLA), the native token of Xsolla ZK — a blockchain network built for games. XLA is designed to pay the network fees that arise when players transfer or share eligible digital items on Xsolla ZK.

For players who’d rather not download and set up a separate wallet, Xsolla Gas Station, offered by Xsolla (USA), Inc., can cover those fees. There, players buy XLA-NT, a non-transferable gas credit used only to pay network fees on Xsolla ZK. (Xsolla Gas Station sells only XLA-NT.).

Players want more from the games and brands they follow, and developers want newly introduced ways to engage them. Digital items have evolved well beyond cosmetics — players now collect them from their favorite games. But letting players keep, share, and give away those items has long been complicated.

Industry observers note that most digital items today live on a single game’s backend. Players generally can’t transfer them to friends or carry them into another game, and the items simply vanish if a game shuts down. Blockchain-backed items stay with the players who collect them — but transferring them requires a network fee, which traditionally means setting up and managing a separate digital wallet and acquiring the right token to pay. That friction adds up.

Industry observers note that xsolla ZK is designed to remove that friction. Xsolla’s existing payment, monetization, and distribution products can now support blockchain-backed items — giving developers a newly introduced way to monetize and distribute digital items, and giving players new ways to keep them and pass them on.

Players use Xsolla Gas to cover the network fees required to transfer or share a digital item held in their Xsolla Backpack.

Sources indicate that xsolla Backpack is an inventory for the digital items a player owns. Items issued to the Backpack in transferable form are designed to remain available to the player even if the issuing game shuts down — and can be given to another player.

The intent is to put players in control of what happens to the eligible items they own. Digital items that remain on a game’s own backend continue to work exactly as they do today and can’t be transferred through Backpack.

Sources indicate that players who want to collect these items simply sign in to their account as usual — no seed phrase, no separate wallet setup. They cover network fees by buying XLA-NT gas credits through Xsolla Gas Station, using the payment methods they already know and prefer.

From there, players can collect eligible items they purchase or claim in their Backpack and share them with other players.

“Players should be able to share and gift digital items they own, anytime, anywhere, with anyone they know,” remarked Chris Hewish, President of Xsolla (USA). “Developers gain a new way to monetize their games, and players gain eligible digital items that are theirs to keep. When both sides win, the games do too. We are adding options inside the tools they already use, not asking anyone to change how they work.”.

When a player wants to share or give away a digital item, the transaction runs on Xsolla ZK, the blockchain network, and the network fee is paid in Xsolla Gas. This lets players hold items and pass them on to others, and it’s designed to let them carry items between supported games.

Players can cover the fee themselves in XLA or XLA-NT, while developers and partners can use XLA to pay network fees for their own item activity.
