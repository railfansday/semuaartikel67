In a recent development, before you cancel cable or satellite TV, write down every channel and show your household watches for a week. Then cover each one the cheapest way, with an antenna for local stations, free streaming apps, or one or two paid services. Add a live TV streaming service only for cable channels you watch live and won’t give up.

A cable or satellite package charges for its whole lineup, but whatever replaces it only has to cover what your household actually watches.

Anything still on the list, like games or news on cable channels you watch live, is the only reason to add a live TV streaming service, which carries cable channels as they air. YouTube TV’s main plan costs $82.99 a month, and its Sports Plan costs $64.99. If ESPN’s channels are all you need live, its ESPN Unlimited plan costs $31.99 a month.

Before you cancel, ask your provider what internet alone will cost at your address, equipment included, and when any promotional price on your bill ends.

Strategy the switch for the month that promotion or any contract ends. DIRECTV can charge as much as $20 for every month still left on a 12- to 24-month commitment, so the fee shrinks as the end date nears.

According to recent reports, return every box and anything else the provider rented you, and keep the receipt or tracking number as proof. Xfinity charges an unreturned equipment fee for any of its equipment you don’t send back, under its customer agreement.

Say you pay $150 a month for cable and internet, and your list holds network demonstrates and series from two streaming services, with nothing on cable you watch live. An internet initiative at $60 a month, a $30 antenna, and about $25 a month for those two services come to $1,050 in the first year, $750 less than the $1,800 you’d pay staying put.

Compare the best video streaming services to choose the one or two your list calls for.
