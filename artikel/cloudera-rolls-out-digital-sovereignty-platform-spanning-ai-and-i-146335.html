UPDATED 12:19 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

Cloudera Inc. today declared a digital sovereignty package that it mentioned enables organizations to maintain control over their data, artificial intelligence models and computing infrastructure regardless of where workloads run.

Cloudera for Digital Sovereignty combines the manufacturer’s Anywhere Cloud, Open Data Lakehouse and Unified Data Fabric technologies with open-weight AI models and model customization tools from Mistral SAS, with whom Cloudera recently partnered. The integrated offering supports deployment across sovereign clouds, private data centers and fully air-gapped environments that have no connection to external networks.

Industry observers note that digital sovereignty is becoming a more pressing matter as enterprises move beyond managing data residency to ensuring that the AI models and infrastructure they depend upon remain under their control. McKinsey & Co. reported last year that 71% of the executives it surveyed called sovereign AI an “existential concern” or “strategic imperative.” Cloudera said its own research found that regulation, privacy and data sovereignty requirements rank among the top three barriers to scaling AI.

“As AI becomes more autonomous and embedded in critical operations, enterprises need to know that their data, models, and infrastructure can remain under their control even as technology and regulatory requirements change,” Cloudera Chief Technology Officer Sergio Gago stated in a statement.

Recent findings suggest that the offering provides consistent zero-trust access controls, data lineage tracking and governance policies across deployment environments. Those capabilities are intended to help organizations address requirements imposed by the European Union’s AI Act, Digital Operational Resilience Act and Network and Information Security Directive, among other regulations.

Governance policies also extend to the enterprise data that AI agents retrieve and use in performing tasks. Mistral’s open-weight models can run alongside governed data, enabling organizations to customize models locally and execute agentic workflows without exposing sensitive information.

Cloudera’s modular hybrid cloud architecture enables owners to shift computing workloads between infrastructure providers and private data centers without rewriting applications or migrating or duplicating underlying data. Organizations can also selectively apply sovereignty controls to sensitive workloads without imposing the same restrictions across their entire computing estate.

In a recent development, moor Insights & Strategy Inc. Chief Analyst Patrick Moorhead mentioned AI has raised the bar on data protection. “A organization can meet every line of its sovereignty policy and still not control who accesses its data or who can turn off the models it depends on,” he said in a statement.

In a recent development, cloudera stated it will supplement its Mistral partnership with an ecosystem of sovereign cloud providers and implementation partners. The firm is initially targeting Europe but looks to expand the partner network into other regions and regulatory jurisdictions.
