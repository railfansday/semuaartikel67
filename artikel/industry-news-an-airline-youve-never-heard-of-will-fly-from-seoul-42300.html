Sources indicate that A very small international airline plans to add long haul, international flights to the United States as of next spring, and I feel pretty confident in saying that 99% of people have never heard of this airline (I had to look it up after seeing one of their planes at an airport several weeks back, and I know a thing or two about identifying airplanes). 😉.

Sources indicate that as reported by AeroRoutes, as of May 1, 2027, Parata Air will launch daily nonstop flights between Seoul Incheon (ICN) and Los Angeles (LAX). The flight will operate with the following schedule:.

WE301 Incheon to Los Angeles departing 12:00PM arriving 7:20AMWE302 Los Angeles to Incheon departing 9:50AM arriving 3:20PM (+1 days).

Parata Air will operate the route with an Airbus A330-200, featuring 260 seats, including 18 business class seats and 242 economy seats. The 5,944-mile flight is blocked at 11hr40min eastbound and 13hr30min westbound.

Tickets on the flight are now on sale, and I see fares starting at around $670 one-way in economy, and around $2,500 one-way in business class. Those aren’t exactly terribly attractive fares for a newcomer that appears to have a pretty lackluster onboard product.

In a recent development, for those not familiar with Parata Air, the airline released operations back in 2019, but it has a fleet of just a handful of planes, including three Airbus A330s and two Airbus A320s. This will be the carrier’s first service outside of Asia. As I hinted at in the headline, I imagine this is a fully unknown player to most people, and I had never heard of the airline until a few weeks ago.

In a recent development, random thought — can anyone think of a smaller international airline (in terms of fleet size) that added long haul flights to the United States? I know that’s very specific, but I can’t think of another one… it’s possible I’m missing something, though!

The airline acquired its three A330s secondhand from different sources, with the planes having flown for Garuda Indonesia, Hainan Airlines, and Thomas Cook (though that plane doesn’t have business class).

The Seoul Incheon to Los Angeles market is pretty well served in terms of capacity, but not so well served in terms of competition. That’s because no US carrier flies between Los Angeles and Seoul Incheon, and with Korean Air having acquired Asiana, there’s now only one South Korean flag carrier.

If you look at the market, you’ll see that Korean Air operates up to four daily flights, and Air Premia operates up to two daily flights. Now we’ll see a third airline from the country operating in the market, though with the lowest capacity, and arguably the worst product.

It’ll be interesting to see when we see some competition here on the US side. Delta might operate the route eventually, but that doesn’t actually increase competition, since the airline has a joint venture with Korean Air. American has (smartly, probably) given up on Los Angeles as a long haul hub. So that leaves United, and I could definitely see the airline eventually adding service in the market, as a competitive response against Delta and Korean Air, if nothing else.

In a recent development, as of May 2027, Parata Air will be launching a upcoming nonstop route between Seoul Incheon and Los Angeles, representing the carrier’s first service outside of Asia. The airline will be operating daily in the market with Airbus A330s. With this, there will be three South Korean carriers flying this route, though no airlines from the United States.
