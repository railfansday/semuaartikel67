Still, No One Does Thanksgiving Style Quite Like 'Gossip Girl’s' Blair Waldorf

According to recent reports, with my sincerest apologies to Vogue staffers’ favorite pairs of eating pants, when it comes to Thanksgiving fashion, nothing beats the fresh-off-the-runway and fabulous pieces found in Gossip Girl’s now-legendary Thanksgiving episodes. When there’s a svelte Fendi suit or a tapestry McQueen dress on hand, who needs a forgiving drawstring waistband?

Recent findings suggest that nearly twenty years after the show’s debut, the finer details of those episodes’ familial dramas may be a little murky in memory, but nothing remains as crystal clear as Blair Waldorf’s sharply-defined, uptown girl style. See, for example, the sparkly fall 2007 Marc by Marc Jacobs babydoll dress she dons in season one (episode nine, “Blair Waldorf Must Pie!”). “It’s a defining look for both the character and the show, and I have spent the better part of two decades trying to duplicate it,” claims People Like You author and Gossip Girl fashion expert Tyler McCall. “And if you don’t believe that it’s iconic, try and find that dress on the secondhand market for anything close to a reasonable price.”.

Blair Waldorf-approved Marc by Marc Jacobs dresses may not have quite reached the CBK-Calvin Klein craze, but do a cursory search, and that particular babydoll dress is still incredibly difficult to procure. But when paired with a knit capelet, bow-printed tights, and a dainty pair of pumps—as it was in the episode—Miss Waldorf’s holiday style could be easily mistaken for the secondhand wishlist of many fashion girls today.

Season 6: Fendi suit, Gucci blouse, Chloé overcoat.

Another treasured Thanksgiving look of Blair’s includes season two’s backless green dress from the now-shuttered label Alexandra Vidal, topped off by a blue Sretsis dress (worn as a coat!) and some seriously autumnal Jimmy Choo boots. However, when it comes to rivaling the babydoll dress, it’s season three’s Nanette Lepore belted tweed coat, worn over a brown printed Sariah dress, and—once again—statement tights that handily take second place. Today, thanks to Matthieu Blazy’s Chanel, not-your-mother’s tweed is all the rage.

Sources indicate that while vintage fashion has had us all in a chokehold for quite some time now, and the runways today resemble twenty years ago more than they did ten, McCall also points out how these looks are in lind with certain trends today, including statement tights ,which have re-entered the fashion conversation. (See from the spring 2027 collections: Jil Sander, Marc Jacobs, Lii, and more.) “Festive tights were a Gossip Girl signature, and that’s a trend that’s stuck around for a few winters now, whether it’s a patterned style or a monochromatic moment,” she notes. But still, if you’re looking for something a little less uptown, she recommends channeling one of Serena van der Woodsen’s many silky jewel-toned frocks and tapping into the “lingerie-as-daywear” redux. Somehow, Gossip Girl’s Thanksgiving episodes are a time capsule that captures how all great fashion eventually comes back around.
