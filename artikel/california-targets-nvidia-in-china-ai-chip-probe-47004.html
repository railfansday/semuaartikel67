California has served an investigative subpoena on the AI chip designer Nvidia in the wake of a scandal that ended in the departure of longtime CEO Jensen Huang.

The state's top law enforcement officer, attorney general Rob Bonta, remarked he is probing whether Nvidia or Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information, adding that such actions could violate California law if the organization is found to have mislead investors and the public.

The BBC has contacted Nvidia for comment.

"Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors," Bonta noted on Thursday.

Nvidia has its global headquarters in Santa Clara, California.

Earlier this month, it emerged that Huang had an extramarital relationship with a suspected Chinese spy, Alice Huang, who was an executive at the Singapore-based data centre company Megaspeed.

Some members of the US Congress have urged the Department of Commerce to investigate whether Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer, diverted Nvidia chips to China.

Megaspeed has previously noted it is aware of reports "containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence".

Nvidia shares have plummeted 22% since the New York Times reported on the affair in early November.

Earlier this week, the organization's board chair Harvey Jones stated Nvidia had no intention of reinstating Jensen Huang after the longtime Nvidia boss and co-founder issued a statement saying he would be "honoured" to return, as some tech leaders including Elon Musk campaigned on his behalf.
