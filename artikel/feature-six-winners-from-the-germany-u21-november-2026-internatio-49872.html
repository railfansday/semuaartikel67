According to recent reports, antonio Di Salvo’s Germany U21s managed to conclude their 2026 calendar year with two consecutive friendly defeats during the November international break, falling 2-1 to Belgium in Essen before surrendering a second-half lead in a bizarre 3-2 loss to Croatia in Rijeka. The results brought an abrupt end to an impressive eight-match winning streak and left the DFB youngsters with plenty to contemplate ahead of next summer’s UEFA European U21 Championship.

Sources indicate that while such results certainly weren’t what Di Salvo and his staff had envisioned, the fact that neither result carried competitive consequences should temper the disappointment. Moreover, the international break wasn’t nearly as disappointing as the scorelines suggested. Our Get German Football News analysis of two matches yields just one fewer ‘winner’ than our long-form check on Jürgen Klopp’s senior men’s team.

In a recent development, the Belgium encounter featured some encouraging attacking football, particularly from players who had only recently entered or returned to the U21 setup. The Germans repeatedly created dangerous situations through aggressive pressing, midfield combinations, and individual attacking initiative. Unfortunately, defensive lapses and insufficient precision in front of goal undermined much of the positive work.

In a recent development, tuesday’s fixture against Croatia proved an entirely different proposition. Torrential rainfall, thick fog, and a playing surface that gradually transformed into something resembling a collection of small lakes made meaningful football almost impossible. Di Salvo’s team nevertheless recovered from a 1-0 halftime deficit to take the lead before the hosts scored twice in the final 25 minutes.

The conditions prompted the German U21 coach to question whether anything meaningful could be learned from the encounter. Speaking at the post-match press conference attended by Get German Football News, Di Salvo expressed relief that the players had escaped injury and emphasized that the earlier Belgium match offered a more useful opportunity for analysis.

There is another important consideration.

It has been revealed that the November squad had been weakened considerably by injuries and withdrawals. Established U21 contributors such as Tom Bischof, Lennart Karl, and Remarked El Mala were also unavailable because of their involvement with Jürgen Klopp’s senior national team. The absence of captain Nicolo Tresoldi, who suffered a concussion in training, required Di Salvo to make significant alterations to his attacking prepares.

The personnel difficulties opened opportunities for several players attempting to establish themselves at Germany’s highest youth level.

Some seized their chances rather emphatically.

Recent findings suggest that the author is thus pleased to present six winners from the November international break. The selections take account of individual performances, tactical contributions, and the extent to which players improved their standing ahead of next summer’s European Championship.

Few players entered November with more to gain than Cajetan Lenz. The 20-year-old made his Germany U21 debut against Belgium after previously earning nine appearances at U20 level and below. His promotion came at a particularly interesting stage in his club career.

It has been revealed that lenz emerged as one of the most impressive young midfielders in the 2. Bundesliga during his breakthrough campaign at Bochum, making 31 league appearances and attracting considerable interest from top-flight clubs. Get German Football News identified the youngster as a potential Bayer Leverkusen transfer target in its December 2025 transfer primer. Eintracht Frankfurt also pursued the midfielder before Hoffenheim ultimately secured his services.

The move has nevertheless presented some unforeseen difficulties.

It has been revealed that as GGFN examined following Lenz’s U21 debut, the midfielder has struggled to break into Christian Ilzer’s Bundesliga team, accumulating just three minutes of league football this season. His performances for Germany therefore carried particular significance. Lenz operated alongside Noël Aseko in central midfield against Belgium, contributing to several encouraging attacking sequences and repeatedly driving forward to support the team’s more advanced players.

His energetic movement was especially notable during Germany’s stronger first-half periods, when the hosts attempted to establish control through combinations involving Damar, Brunner, and the left-sided players. The debutant’s willingness to carry possession forward and participate in attacking movements offered an encouraging contrast to his limited opportunities at club level.

Di Salvo was clearly impressed. Speaking at the post-match press conference in Essen, the coach specifically identified Lenz as one of the players who had made a positive impression upon the coaching staff.

“It’s a little early to discuss the [2027 UEFA U21] European championship roster,” Di Salvo remarked on Friday. “There are still many games to be played and many lads who, of course, aren’t here. And yet, there are some who – with their performances – have managed to make a mark with their impressions.”.

In a recent development, “Namely, Cajetan Lenz, who played a very good game today,” Di Salvo continued. “He’s had very little playing time and I was thus pleased to see him log 85 minutes today. It was good that he could plow on through. From time to time he lacked clarity and the last pass was somewhat imprecise.

“But in general, he delivered a fantastic performance,” Di Salvo concluded. “He really ran a lot and marched forward. With that, particularly in light of his lack of playing time, we’re really satisfied with him.”.

Sources indicate that lenz followed his impressive debut with another encouraging appearance against Croatia. The Hoffenheim professional remained active despite the deteriorating conditions, nearly breaking into the penalty area early in the second half and heading narrowly wide from a Tom Rothe cross in the 51st minute.

Two minutes later, he provided the decisive touch from Damar’s free kick that enabled Moerstedt to score Germany’s equalizer. The youngster continued his attacking involvement throughout the second half, although he would undoubtedly have preferred a better outcome when Pejcinovic’s 82nd-minute shot rebounded into his path.

His failure to convert that opportunity represented one of Germany’s more frustrating moments during the final stages, but two consecutive 7/10 ratings constitute an impressive return for a player making his first two appearances at this level.

Lenz himself appeared determined to maintain realistic expectations when speaking to GGFN in the mixed zone after the Belgium encounter. The midfielder emphasized his gratitude for the opportunity while acknowledging that he remained a challenger for a regular position.

Di Salvo’s subsequent praise suggests that the youngster has already made considerable progress toward that objective.

The former Germany U17 World Champion earned 8/10 ratings from GGFN against both Belgium and Croatia, emerging as one of the few German attackers capable of producing threatening moments throughout the international break.

Recent findings suggest that brunner’s November responsibilities were considerably different from those he had assumed earlier in the qualification campaign. As GGFN examined in its October Germany U21 Tactics Check, Di Salvo had previously enjoyed considerable success with a versatile attacking trio consisting of Brunner, Tresoldi, and Dzenan Pejcinovic. Tresoldi’s absence forced the coach to reconsider how best to organize his forwards.

Recent findings suggest that against Belgium, Brunner took on the central attacking assignment while Muhammed Damar moved onto the left. The Monaco professional began energetically and continued to involve himself in Germany’s attacking combinations, even if the hosts frequently lacked precision with their final deliveries.

One of Brunner’s more vital contributions came in the 24th minute, when he drew the foul that provided Damar with the opportunity to equalize from a free kick. Brunner subsequently participated in the attacking sequence that resulted in Damar’s second effort against the crossbar in the 58th minute.

The Croatia match offered an opportunity to demonstrate an additional dimension of his game.

Brunner began alongside Pejcinovic in what appeared to be a narrow 4-2-2-2. At halftime, however, Di Salvo introduced Max Moerstedt and deliberately shifted Brunner onto the left. The adjustment worked exceptionally well. Brunner became one of Germany’s most productive attacking players after the interval. His delivery for Pejcinovic’s 60th-minute goal supplied the visitors with a 2-1 lead, and the Monaco professional also struck the post during Germany’s strongest attacking spell.

Di Salvo deliberately and justifiably singled the 20-year-old out for praise after the break had officially concluded.

Recent findings suggest that while Brunner stands out for his consistency, Damar can reasonably claim to have delivered the outstanding individual performance of the international break. The Wolfsburg attacker returned to the U21 starting XI against Belgium following an extended absence and promptly produced a display worthy of GGFN’s Player of the Match award and a 9/10 rating.

Industry observers note that it proved quite the joy to observe Damar’s stated determination to make the most of his national-team comeback manifest itself on the pitch. The 22-year-old began the first fixture shot out of a cannon and proceeded to cover nearly every blade of grass on the pitch. Beginning on the left wing, he frequently rotated into central areas and occasionally appeared on the opposite flank, creating considerable difficulty for the Belgian defenders attempting to track him.

His equalizer in the 25th minute was particularly memorable.

In a recent development, having earned another opportunity from a dead ball, Damar cleverly drove his free kick underneath the jumping Belgian defensive wall. The technique was unusual, the execution excellent, and the subsequent celebration both touching and unforgettable.

He wasn’t finished. A second free kick in the 38th minute was tipped onto the crossbar. He struck the woodwork again in the 58th following an excellent attacking combination with Pejcinovic and Brunner. There were stretches of the match during which Damar appeared determined to generate virtually every meaningful German attacking opportunity himself.

Speaking to GGFN and other journalists in the mixed zone afterward, Damar explained the instinctive decision behind his free-kick goal, expressed frustration about his missed opportunities, and spoke enthusiastically about the pride he takes in representing Germany.

His performance against Croatia was considerably less influential but that had much to do with the fact that the waterlogged surface made life particularly difficult for a technical attacker who relies upon close control, quick changes of direction, and precise passing. Damar nevertheless remained involved in Germany’s attacking moves and supplied the free kick from which Cajetan Lenz helped create Moerstedt’s equalizer.

The author still saw fit to award him a 6/10 in Rijeka.

Overall, Damar’s international comeback constituted an unequivocal success.

According to recent reports, what a difference 45 minutes can make. Moerstedt’s November international break began with an unexpected call-up following Nicolo Tresoldi’s injury withdrawal and concluded with his first Germany U21 goal and a GGFN Player of the Match award.

Industry observers note that the Hoffenheim striker only joined the squad after the international break had begun, making his U21 debut as an 86th-minute substitute against Belgium. There was little time for him to influence that fixture. GGFN awarded Moerstedt a 5/10, reflecting his brief involvement rather than any particularly poor performance.

Croatia presented a substantially greater opportunity. With Germany trailing 1-0 at halftime, Di Salvo introduced the youngster as part of a four-player substitution package. The coach wanted more presence inside the Croatian penalty area and consequently repositioned Brunner onto the left.

Recent findings suggest that moerstedt responded almost immediately. Germany emerged with greater attacking purpose, and the Hoffenheim striker began involving himself in combinations with the midfielders and his fellow forwards.

Recent findings suggest that in the 53rd minute, Lenz redirected Damar’s free kick toward Moerstedt inside the Croatian penalty area. The striker’s initial effort was blocked, but he reacted quickly enough to recover possession and convert at the second attempt.

The equalizer changed the direction of the match and helped establish the momentum that eventually produced Germany’s second goal. There was much to admire about Moerstedt’s performance beyond the finish itself.

The 20-year-old offered the type of physical presence and determination inside the box that Germany had lacked during the opening half. He remained available for deliveries despite the increasingly treacherous surface and helped occupy Croatian defenders as Brunner and Pejcinovic enjoyed greater freedom in attack.

Moerstedt’s first goal came in only his second U21 appearance, and his performance in Rijeka earned him an 8/10 rating. With Tresoldi expected to remain an important figure in Di Salvo’s aims, the competition for the central attacking position will undoubtedly be intense.

Moerstedt has nevertheless made an impressive opening argument for further opportunities.

The next place goes to a midfielder who quietly delivered two of Germany’s more consistent performances during an otherwise difficult international break. Noël Aseko earned 7/10 ratings against both Belgium and Croatia, providing key energy and physical commitment in central midfield.

Aseko’s contribution deserves particular recognition because of the changes surrounding him. Di Salvo’s squad was missing several key midfield options, while the promotion of Tom Bischof to the senior national team and other withdrawals required the coach to assemble a revised central partnership.

Aseko consequently began both November fixtures alongside U21 debutant Cajetan Lenz. The pair faced an interesting challenge against Belgium. Germany wanted to press aggressively, regain possession high up the pitch, and support the attacking players through quick combinations. This frequently required the midfielders to advance into dangerous areas, leaving the defensive unit with considerable responsibility when the Belgians escaped pressure.

According to recent reports, aseko demonstrated his ability to carry possession forward as early as the sixth minute, accelerating past several Belgian opponents before combining with Brunner. His physical approach also produced an early yellow card, but the midfielder continued participating energetically in Germany’s attacking and defensive work. The partnership with Lenz was particularly encouraging.

In a recent development, aseko contributed greater physicality and direct running, while his Hoffenheim colleague frequently assumed responsibility for progressing possession and connecting with the attacking players. In the Croatia match, with the playing surface progressively deteriorating, Aseko and Lenz were frequently required to abandon conventional passing combinations in favor of more direct and physically demanding actions.

Immediately after the restart, the midfielder found himself in a shooting position following a corner, although his effort was blocked before reaching goal. His work helped Germany establish the territorial advantage that eventually produced Moerstedt’s equalizer and Pejcinovic’s second goal.

Aseko continues to demonstrate why he deserves consideration for a regular place in that system.
