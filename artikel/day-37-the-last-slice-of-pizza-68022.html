Day 37: The Last Slice of Pizza

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I slept like a baby in my curtained-off bunk. At least 9 hours, too, which was way more than I had been getting.

Feeling charged up for the day, I set out into the dawn, ready for whatever the day held.

Sources indicate that as I walked, I stumbled upon a few Italians I knew. The girl was my age, and we discussed the Camino ending and what our lives would be like once we returned home. She was studying to be an actress and wanted to do so for the passion of it and to help people, not for the money or to be famous. She held many side jobs as a way to pay for her education and dream, one of them being teaching theater to children in a church. She spoke of the importance of giving young people a spiritual outlet, in whatever way felt best to them.

The conversation then turned to my job and coaching. After telling her I help people find holistic balance in their life, she curiously asked me:.

“How do you find balance in your life?”.

This started a beautiful conversation about how we each live our lives, the things we prioritize, and our outlook on happiness and peace. A girl I’d met only a few days prior suddenly felt so familiar.

But then up ahead, I saw the brothers.

I yelled to them, and they waited for me to approach. With hugs and excitement, we all decided to go to a local restaurant, A Garnacha, for traditional Galician pulpo (octopus).

According to recent reports, with three orders of pulpo in front of us, we dove into the tentacles with the toothpicks we were given as utensils. Not many words were said as we devoured our plates. The boys asked if I liked it, and the best I could say was,.

“No es mi favorita, pero me gusta.”.

Sources indicate that for the first time in the whole Camino, the three of us walked together. Of course the Italian brother walked ahead, but we were still in close proximity.

The three of us dropped our packs at a pilgrim rest area, and ended up spending over an hour there.

We sang for the pilgrims that passed.

We laughed while we rested all together.

Industry observers note that my Argentinian brother and I were shocked by the fact that our Italian brother sat for that long. Having just bought his plane ticket home, he was feeling the weight of the Camino ending and wanted to slow down over the next few days.

He compared it to the last piece of pizza, and how you want to savor every single bite.

We were all savoring each moment together like it was the last piece of pizza.

It has been revealed that the last part of the walk we did independently. I came up to a stream with a beautiful stone bridge over top. I took the moment to stop and be still, watching the ripples of the water reflect on the bridge. I pulled acorns out of the cold water and made a game of throwing them at a rock.

It has been revealed that simplicity. That’s what I love about this lifestyle, and something I will greatly miss.

When I arrived in the town of Arzúa and made it to the municipal albergue, Xunta de Galicia Arzúa Pilgrims Hostel, I showered and collapsed on my bunk.

In a recent development, my Italian brother came and got me once our Argentinian brother arrived, and we all walked to a restaurant in search of Galician barbecue. They were out of the barbecue we were looking for, so we ordered almost the entire menu instead.

Homemade croquettes, pimientos de Padrón (peppers), mixed salad, raxo, and milanesa de pollo.

Recent findings suggest that there was not a crumb left. We got cheesecake and flan to top it off while the boys talked about the intersection of futbol and politics. I completely zoned out and started to see just how tired we all were.

That became quite evident in the street as we walked to find gelato. There was so much giggling and play, especially because we were delusional with exhaustion. But at one point, I walked behind the two of them as they were carrying on and thought,.

“I love these two so damn much.”.

And with a generous cone of gelato before bed, we all crashed for our last massive day.
