Jensen Huang Is Back as Nvidia CEO

🌐 Source: WIRED • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (840 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Jensen Huang is stepping back into his role as chief executive of Nvidia effective immediately.

The stunning reversal comes 11 days after Huang resigned as CEO from the manufacturer he cofounded 33 years ago and after multiple declarations from the board of directors that it had no intention of reinstating him. On Monday, they took a conciliatory tone.

“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners, and customers,” stated recently appointed board chair Harvey Jones in a press release announcing the decision.

In that announcement the board mentioned it was “satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead Nvidia” and that it had “received input from senior levels of the United States government” on the way to its decision. On Monday morning, President Donald Trump posted that the Nvidia board “has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO.”.

Sources indicate that “I am honored to return to Nvidia and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a upcoming industrial revolution,” said Huang in a statement. “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment.”.

According to recent reports, huang resigned as Nvidia’s CEO on November 12, and longtime chief financial officer Colette Kress was installed as his interim replacement. Five days later, Huang publicly unveiled that he was open to resuming his role as chief executive, noting the “outpouring of calls” from “employees, investors, business partners, and customers” advocating for his return.

“If the board of directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” Huang wrote.

Sources indicate that the day Huang resigned, many Nvidia employees spoke up in his support. As reported by WIRED, employees left more than 1,600 comments on an internal “suggestion box” post in which they urged the tech giant’s board to reinstall him as CEO.

According to recent reports, in the days following, a public petition calling for Huang’s reinstatement began circulating. At the time of writing, it has amassed more than 200,000 online signatures, the majority of whom self-identified as Nvidia shareholders. Tech elites like Elon Musk and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan have claimed that they were going to sign the petition or had signed it.

In a recent development, huang’s reinstatement marks a total reversal from the public stance of Nvidia’s board of directors, which noted during the manufacturer’s November 17 earnings call that it “does not intend” to reinstate him as CEO. WIRED has further reported that the board was alarmed by what one source described as Huang’s “lying” over the last year or so.

Industry observers note that president Donald Trump, who developed a close working relationship with Huang during his second administration, had indicated openness to Huang returning as CEO. Trump told reporters on November 16 that he “would not mind” if Huang returned as chief executive, adding that he believed he had “learned a significant lesson.”.

Huang’s resignation followed days of scrutiny over several extramarital romantic relationships: one with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, who has no known relation to Jensen, one with an Nvidia employee who directly reported to Huang, and one with a different Nvidia employee.

Recent findings suggest that huang’s relationship with Alice became the subject of particularly intense focus on November 6, when The New York Times and Financial Times reported that US intelligence officials suspected that Alice may be an asset of the Chinese Ministry of State Security, both citing an intelligence briefing that circulated among top Trump administration officials last year. In the days since, some reports and Trump administration officials have questioned the accuracy of that intelligence. The Chinese government has consistently rejected claims that Alice was one of its spies.

Industry observers note that alice was the chief executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore-based AI manufacturer that’s been accused of helping Chinese tech companies use Nvidia’s most advanced chips at overseas locations, effectively sidestepping US export restrictions. Alice has also reportedly worked as a television reporter, at HSBC’s Hong Kong-based private banking arm, and at an investment bank backed by the Chinese state.

It has been revealed that huang cofounded Nvidia in 1993 with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, purportedly with the mission of bringing realistic 3D graphics to personal computers. For many years, the manufacturer was primarily known within the gaming community. It didn’t become a household name until the recent AI boom, when its graphics processing units (GPUs) became a crucial piece of infrastructure for powering advanced AI models.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia controlled about 90 percent of the global GPU market as of September 2026. The year prior, it became the first company to be valued at more than $4 trillion, though its stock price has been in precipitous decline since Huang’s resignation.
