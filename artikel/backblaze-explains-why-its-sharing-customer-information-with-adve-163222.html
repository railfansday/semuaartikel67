📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The current policy adds: “Under no circumstances do we rent, trade, or share your address or e-mail address with any other company for their marketing purposes without your consent.” It also makes no mention of advertisers.

This has concerned some owners, but a Backblaze spokesperson told Ars Technica that “Backblaze does not sell or share sensory or surveillance data, such as call recordings, for targeted advertising, and we do not disclose it to advertising or marketing partners.” They added:.

Notably, no “sensitive personal information,” such as social security numbers, credit card numbers, or “precise geolocation,” is shared with advertisers, the policy states. However, identifiers, commercial information, “Internet or Other Similar Network Activity,” and “geolocation data” may be “sold or shared for targeted Advertising”.

Critically, the upcoming policy “only applies to the information collected in respect of your account and not to the content that you store on our system.” Backblaze’s broader terms of service also state that: “Files stored using Backblaze Computer Backup are encrypted prior to being sent to us.”.

Renatta Siewert, Backblaze’s director of communications and brand programs, reiterated to Ars that Backblaze doesn’t share the contents of backup or storage files with advertisers:.

Backblaze takes the privacy and security of our customers’ data seriously. We updated our Privacy Notice to keep pace with the changing legal landscape, which increasingly requires companies to spell out how they collect and use marketing-related information. This upgrade gives customers a clearer, more specific view of what we collect and why. These disclosures are separate from the files customers entrust to Backblaze for backup and storage, which we do not sell or share with advertisers, data brokers, or analytics partners.

Customers can use this link to opt out of the selling or sharing of their personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising.
