VW Will Pay German Drivers To Plug In Their EVs. Even If The Grid Never Uses Their Power

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that plug your electric Volkswagen in at 8 in the evening, unplug it at 6 the next morning, and you could earn €2.40 while you sleep. How much electricity would your car have to send back to the grid for that money? Potentially none. Starting in December, VW’s charging subsidiary Elli will pay eligible German drivers for the hours their car spends plugged in at home, even if it doesn’t actually supply any power during that time.

We already wrote about Germany’s initiative to allow parked EVs to earn money. New rules made it easier to account for electricity stored in a car and later sold back to the grid, even for homes that can generate their own power through solar panels. That left a fairly important question for anyone with an EV in the driveway: How much could you actually earn? Now, Volkswagen has put a number on it.

Elli will pay €0.24 for each hour the EV is plugged in, provided each session lasts at least three hours. The bonus tops out at €60 a month, or €720 yearly. Earning the full amount takes 250 hours a month, a little over eight hours a night. In the app, you tell Elli when you need the car and how much charge you want when you leave.

Elli will handle charging and discharging around your schedule, but to make this work you need its 11-kilowatt bidirectional charger with vehicle-to-grid (V2G) capability. That costs €1,995 without installation, and your home also needs a compatible smart electricity meter for everything to work. Owners can register now, then check eligibility at launch and get an installation quote before ordering.

The thing is, even if you collect every euro of the first-year bonus, that still leaves €1,275 of the charger’s price to cover, plus whatever the electrician charges to install it.

Industry observers note that not all VW EVs have bidirectional charging. It introduced this feature on its European models in 2023, but it notes there are around one million eligible EVs in Europe, about a third of which are in Germany. The offer covers Volkswagen, Cupra and Škoda, and the charger supports 400- and 800-volt vehicles, even if the latter is not present in any of the three brands’ vehicles.

Elli notes it plans to expand into more European markets in 2027 and offer a separate vehicle-to-home system so owners can save rooftop solar power for use after dark. A one-year trial involving 20 German households logged roughly 85,000 combined charger hours online and more than 6,500 charging and discharging sessions, the manufacturer remarked.

Sources indicate that VW calculates that €720 could buy enough electricity to drive an ID.3 Neo nearly 10,000 miles (16,000 km), assuming €0.33 per kWh and the car’s official WLTP consumption. So it may not offset the full cost of the bidirectional charger, but that’s still a lot of free driving for just keeping the car plugged in overnight.
