Romance manhwa comes in many forms, but some of the most memorable love stories begin with two people who would rather have nothing to do with each other. Whether their animosity stems from conflicting ambitions, family rivalries, painful misunderstandings, or circumstances that force them into an uncomfortable partnership, these stories thrive on the tension between their protagonists. Watching two characters gradually abandon their resentment and discover an unexpected connection is one of the most satisfying aspects of the enemies-to-lovers genre.

Sources indicate that the enemies-to-lovers trope is especially popular in fantasy and otome isekai manhwa, where political intrigue, second chances, arranged marriages, and complicated family histories provide plenty of opportunities for romantic conflict. However, the trope isn't limited to historical settings or villainess reincarnation stories. From dangerous games of manipulation to hilarious workplace rivalries and relationships built on long-standing grudges, there's something for every romance fan. For readers who enjoy watching hostility gradually turn into affection, these manhwa are well worth adding to their reading lists.

It has been revealed that beastly Things has a distinct Twilight vibe, introducing readers to a secret society of werewolves. The story follows Ji-u Han, the pack leader's daughter, who harbors an inferiority complex and resents her father for not choosing her as his successor. The plot kicks off when she meets the actor Yuseung Cheo, who's actually a werewolf hunter seeking to exact revenge on the race that killed his parents. However, Ji-u also intends to use him to secure the power she so deeply craves.

Featuring a series of human murders in addition to the conflict between werewolves and hunters, the webtoon's true appeal lies in its two manipulative protagonists on opposing sides, secretly playing games with one another while inevitably beginning to develop feelings. While it features all the hallmarks of toxic relationships, such as obsession, betrayal, and hidden agendas, Beastly Things proves to be an addictive guilty pleasure that's hard to put down.

The Crown Princess Scandal is a different kind of enemies-to-lovers story, as it sets aside the angst and drama in favor of absurd interactions and protagonists whose only reason for hating each other is their clashing personalities. The webtoon begins with Hwa-Yeong, who reluctantly takes her sister's place in a selection process to become the crown princess.

Hwa-Yeong just wants to go home, and the crown prince finds satisfaction in not letting her get away with her looks, with the plot delivering all sorts of hilarious bickering between the two. Although it feels more like a simple comedy at first, the humor doesn't overshadow the romance, and even Hwa-Yeong’s antics serve as a catalyst for the characters' emotional growth. The palpable tension between the protagonists and the way their relationship develops make The Crown Princess Scandal an especially cute and lighthearted love story.

The Hand That Once Fed offers a particularly messy interpretation of enemies-to-lovers romance, exploring what happens when a former relationship deteriorates into resentment and a desire for revenge. The story follows Evelyn Winter, a widowed duchess whose seemingly comfortable life is disrupted when Colonel Gilath Black suddenly returns. Once her devoted servant and lover, Gilath now approaches her with accusations of treason and an entirely different attitude toward the woman he once adored.

Industry observers note that what makes this relationship particularly compelling is the dramatic reversal of their former positions. Gilath, who was once loyal to Evelyn, has become a dangerous presence in her household, while Evelyn must fight to protect her position and navigate the consequences of their shared history. Neither character is particularly straightforward, and their interactions leave readers questioning whether Gilath wants to rekindle their relationship or destroy the woman who once held his heart. With its exploration of betrayal, obsession, and unresolved feelings, The Hand That Once Fed is an excellent choice for readers who prefer their romances with plenty of psychological tension.

Recent findings suggest that betrayal of Dignity is a dark historical romance that puts its protagonists in a complicated relationship defined by manipulation, desperation, and conflicting interests. The story follows Chloe Verdier, a young noblewoman struggling to prevent her family from falling into ruin. When her sister Alice disappears with a mysterious man, Chloe finds herself turning to Duke Damien Thisse, a notoriously arrogant and ruthless military hero. His proposal offers a potential solution to her family's problems, but accepting his help means entering a dangerous arrangement with a man she can hardly trust.

Industry observers note that unlike lighter enemies-to-lovers stories, Betrayal of Dignity doesn't shy away from the uncomfortable aspects of its central relationship. Damien's controlling personality and Chloe's determination to protect herself create an ongoing struggle for power, with neither protagonist willing to surrender easily. Chloe is particularly interesting because she isn't simply swept away by the Duke's wealth or appearance; she has her own priorities and must constantly consider the consequences of her decisions. The result is an emotionally complicated romance filled with questionable choices, courtly intrigue, and a relationship that is anything but straightforward.

According to recent reports, frozen Hearts introduces a supernatural variation of the enemies-to-lovers trope, placing its protagonists on opposite sides of a dangerous conflict. The story follows Seolha, a Snow Maiden demon who has concealed her true identity while living among humans. Her carefully maintained disguise becomes increasingly difficult to protect when she attracts the attention of the Northern Prince, a man infamous for hunting and killing demons. Their circumstances eventually lead to Seolha becoming his bride, creating an unusual marriage between two individuals who should naturally be enemies.

In a recent development, the main appeal of Frozen Hearts comes from the secrets and conflicting loyalties surrounding its protagonists. Seolha must conceal her identity while navigating married life with someone whose reputation makes him particularly dangerous to her kind. Meanwhile, the prince must contend with a relationship that challenges the rigid beliefs underlying his demon-hunting activities. Their situation creates plenty of opportunities for suspicion, emotional conflict, and gradual character development. With its winter-inspired fantasy setting and supernatural premise, Frozen Hearts is an especially appealing choice for readers who enjoy watching characters from opposing worlds develop feelings for each other.

Dear Nemesis offers a very entertaining take on the enemies-to-lovers trope, combining reincarnation, aristocratic politics, and a romance built on a complicated history. The story follows Calmia Phlox, who receives a second chance at life after a previous existence that left her determined to reclaim her family's position and secure her future as Countess Phlox. However, her ambitions become complicated when she must compete with her beloved twin brother while navigating the schemes of those around her. Matters become even more interesting when Rudbeckia Matari, her former archenemy, unexpectedly proposes marriage.

In a recent development, rather than immediately abandoning their differences, Calmia and Rudbeckia approach their engagement as a calculated arrangement, with both having their own ambitions and reasons for accepting the partnership. Their shared history makes it difficult for either to place complete trust in the other, but their political alliance gradually develops into something much more personal. With two intelligent, ambitious protagonists constantly trying to stay one step ahead of each other, Dear Nemesis delivers an especially satisfying slow-burn romance. Its combination of strategic maneuvering, family drama, and gradually developing affection makes it an excellent choice for readers who enjoy seeing former rivals become unexpected romantic partners.

Act Like You Love Me! approaches enemies-to-lovers romance with a considerably lighter touch, combining workplace comedy, celebrity culture, and an unexpected supernatural twist. The story follows Ji-eon, a 25-year-old woman struggling financially while juggling demanding jobs. An unexpected temporary assignment puts her in the position of assisting Doyun Nam, a famous actor whose demanding personality quickly gets on her nerves. After another frustrating encounter, Ji-eon discovers that a seemingly ordinary doll possesses the bizarre ability to control him.

In a recent development, suddenly, the power dynamic between the two changes completely, giving Ji-eon an opportunity to turn the tables on the celebrity who has made her working life miserable. However, having control over someone and actually understanding them are two very different things. As their interactions become increasingly complicated, the hostility between Ji-eon and Doyun gradually develops into something neither initially expected. The series balances its romantic development with plenty of humorous situations, making it a refreshing alternative to the darker, more emotionally demanding stories associated with the trope. For readers who enjoy workplace romances, bickering protagonists, and unconventional premises, Act Like You Love Me! is an entertaining addition.

In a recent development, as its title suggests, Your Eternal Lies follows a deceitful and morally gray protagonist. In the webtoon, Rosen Walker is a notorious criminal who has successfully escaped from prison twice before falling under the supervision of war hero Ian Connor. Thus, Your Eternal Lies mixes the best of the enemies-to-lovers trope with a sort of cat-and-mouse police hunt aboard a ship that keeps readers in suspense about whether she will manage to escape, as well as the real nature of the charges brought against her.

The story features fantasy elements, such as the existence of witches, yet it also offers a grounded reflection on death and the consequences of war, centering on layered characters, each with their own traumas. Beyond the instant chemistry between the protagonists, Your Eternal Lies plays out like a complex game of chess, with Rosen being a wonderfully cunning and strong female lead, making it quite difficult not to root for her to win.

In a recent development, adonis, also known as Reminescence Adonis, puts an interesting spin on the enemies-to-lovers trope. Ianna, an expert swordfighter, has already spent one lifetime being Arhad's enemy. Her only goal was to best him in battle, but she failed to accomplish even this. Though Ianna claims she has no regrets, as she teeters on the brink of death, she hopes that she can be reborn as Arhad's knight and ally instead of his enemy in her next life. Fortunately, Ianna's dying wish is heard, and she finds herself in the body of her younger self when she opens her eyes.

The deep, almost obsessive love that eventually develops between Arhad and Ianna also offers plenty of payoff.

Sources indicate that though Adonis does take its sweet time with the romance, the series' engaging characters more than make up for the slow burn. The deep, almost obsessive love that eventually develops between Arhad and Ianna also offers plenty of payoff, making this series an absolute must read, especially for fans of historical romance manhwa.

Recent findings suggest that an especially impressive take on the Snow White fairy tale, Not-Sew-Wicked Stepmom is a saccharine and wholesome redemption story for Abigail Freidkin, the iconic evil stepmother. It's essentially a blend of isekai and enemies to lovers, where Baekhap Lee, an overworked children's fashion designer, dies from overwork and reincarnates as Abigail. She quickly embarks on a quest to dote on her daughter, Princess Blanche, while evading icy-cool tensions with her estranged, hostile husband, Sabrian.

Not-Sew-Wicked Stepmom is loaded with endearing quirks, such as Abigail's unique villainess quality, where she smiles an inherently malevolent smile even when happy, bewildering her servants and husband. Yet, she muscles through it due to her adoration for her daughter. All the while, Sabrian's hostilities include sabotaging Abigail's gifts for Blanche, believing them to be ill-intentioned, yet maintaining a mere distance from her without outright divorcing or showing intentions to remarry, stoking immediate anticipation for sparks to begin flying between them.
