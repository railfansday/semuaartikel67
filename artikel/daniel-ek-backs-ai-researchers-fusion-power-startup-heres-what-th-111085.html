📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Spotify founder Daniel Ek is backing Europa Fusion, a stealthy fusion power startup founded by an AI researcher who is taking a different approach to developing a fusion power plant.

According to recent reports, the U.K.-based startup was founded in February, though sources told TechCrunch that the founder, Nantas Nardelli, has been working on the idea for longer. The people asked not to be identified because the information isn’t public. Bloomberg first reported Ek’s backing of Europa Fusion.

According to recent reports, nardelli has extensive experience in the AI world, having previously worked at Google DeepMind, Facebook AI Research, and Microsoft Research Cambridge. Recently, he was head of machine learning at Pacific Fusion, the fusion power startup based in Fremont, California.

Sources indicate that pacific Fusion recently broke ground on a demonstration facility in Upcoming Mexico. Like most fusion startups of its size, Pacific Fusion leans heavily on physical devices to test its theories and improve its simulations.

Nardelli apparently didn’t agree with the focus on hardware, instead thinking that fusion startups should invest in a more simulation- and data-heavy approach, a source familiar with his thinking told TechCrunch.

It has been revealed that like Pacific Fusion, Europa Fusion will pursue a pulsed-power approach to fusion. Pulsed-power reactors use massive, perfectly timed surges of electrical current to compress fuel targets until the atoms inside fuse and release energy.

In a recent development, fusion power has the potential to be a significant energy source in the coming decades, but to date, no one has built a power plant capable of supplying electricity to the grid. If fusion power becomes commercially viable, though, it could possibly be a significant source of carbon-free power that draws on nearly limitless fuel supplies. The technology also has the potential to alleviate concerns about AI’s energy consumption.

Fusion’s ability to generate electricity without exacerbating climate change apparently drove Nardelli’s pivot from AI.

In a recent development, “My original background is in electronics and control engineering,” he wrote on his website. “I used to build robots for a living. One day I’ll be back to that, but it’s time to fix climate change for good.”.
