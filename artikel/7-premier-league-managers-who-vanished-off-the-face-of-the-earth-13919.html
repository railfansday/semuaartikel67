Premier League managers are constantly in the spotlight but once they leave their role, sometimes they disappear very quickly into the background.

Plenty of Premier League managers have been on the back pages and appearing on Match of the Day one minute, only to become the answer to an obscure quiz question the next year.

Here are seven former Premier League managers who we have heard little to nothing about since they left the league.

He took the completely unfancied Terriers up to the Premier League and even managed to keep them up, making it one of the best survival stories in the Premier League era.

The team naturally fell apart in the second season with Wagner relieved of his duties in January 2019 but his successive managerial stints have not come close to matching his time in Yorkshire.

Recent findings suggest that he all but took Schalke down and then ignored interest from West Brom to sign with Young Boys. He finished second with the serial league winners before returning to England and joining Norwich.

The German reached the playoffs with the Canaries but was hammered 4-0 and fresh sporting director Ben Knapper handed him the sack.

That was in 2024 but Wagner has been away from the touchline ever since.

Unsurprisingly, winning zero Premier League games and failing to score in your four attempts is likely to put an end to your future hopes of managing in the league.

Frank de Boer is one of the worst Premier League managers in history according to the stats and his career post-Selhurst Park has failed to rebuild his reputation.

According to recent reports, he moved to Atlanta in the MLS and did actually do well, winning two cups in his first and finishing second. He got the boot in 2020 though he fell upwards to the role of Netherlands head coach.

A round of 16 loss to the Czech Republic at Euro 2020 was enough for De Boer to be sacked and his most recent job came in the UAE at Al Jazira. He could only get them to seventh though and was sacked midway through his first season.

Ever since, he has been away from the touchline except in a capacity as an analyst for Dutch TV channel ViaPlay.

Jose Mourinho’s long-time assistant went solo in 2013 as he took over Middlesbrough.

He got them up in 2016 but was always quite combustible and even started having a go at the club’s own fans for not cheering loud enough. That was the beginning of the end and he was sacked in March having fallen out with seemingly everyone at the club.

Recent findings suggest that karanka next joined Nottingham Forest but lost more games than he won and was given the sack a year later. His final appointment in England was at Birmingham, where he lost more than double the games he won.

Next he headed for Spain and Granada where he finally managed to have a positive record but not enough to keep his job and his most recent gig was four months spent as Maccabi Tel Aviv boss in 2023.

Now, he works as the technical director of development for the Spanish football federation, a role he has had since July 2025.

Recent findings suggest that long-time Fergie number two Phelan went out on his own as he took over Hull as caretaker, who had just gone up thanks to Steve Bruce. The fact that Bruce opted to resign rather than manage in the Premier League showed how significant a task awaited Phelan at Hull, who at that time insisted on being called Hull City Tigers.

With a very bare squad, Phelan somehow won games against Leicester and Swansea and even picked up the Premier League Manager of the Month gong for August.

That got him the job on a permanent basis but he was sacked in January as the club slid towards relegation.

After that he went to America for a brief stint as sporting director at Central Coast Mariners but he returned to Old Trafford as coach alongside Ole Gunnar Solskjaer.

Recent findings suggest that when the Norwegian departed in December 2021, Phelan stayed on before three years later he followed Wayne Rooney to Plymouth Argyle to help his former player. Rooney was sacked shortly after and that ended Phelan’s time in management.

Instead, he set up his own company called Mike Phelan Coaching which provides support to grassroots clubs.

Sources indicate that the cards were stacked against Bradley when he arrived in the Premier League. He was the first American to manage in the league and was unfortunately very American.

His Latest Jersey accent rubbed plenty the wrong way, not least the Swansea Supporters’ Trust who owned almost a quarter of the club, and he was seen only getting the job because the club’s owners were American. His performance didn’t exactly quell that opinion.

He lost seven of his 11 games in charge before the Swansea board had to admit their mistake and sacked him the day after Boxing Day.

He left England and returned stateside where he spent 142 games in charge of LAFC. After that, he moved to Toronto but his most recent managerial stint was at Stabaek, a Norwegian side who he had managed previously.

That came to an end in 2024 and he has been out of the managerial game since. He also has gone down the punditry route and was an analyst for FOX Sports during the World Cup.

He was quite forgettable even at the time so perhaps we should not be surprised that former Swansea boss Guidolin has slipped even further from the public eye.

To be honest, we are surprised he is not Palermo manager, having been head coach there on four separate occasions.

Sacked on his 61st birthday, Guidolin left management after Swansea and is instead doing punditry and commentary work for DAZN in Italy.

Hasenhuttl’s work at Southampton (excluding the two 9-0s) felt like the start of a long career in England but not only has he been away from the country for four years, he hasn’t had a job in the last 12 months.

Following his departure from the Saints, he joined Bundesliga strugglers Wolfsburg, saving them from relegation.

He kept them clear of relegation troubles for the beginning of his first full campaign in charge but eight matches without a win, the last of which was a 4-0 defeat to Borussia Dortmund, spooked the board and he was sacked.

As for what he has been doing since, your guess is as good as ours. Not much has been heard of Hasenhuttl since his Wolfsburg departure. It would seem likely that at 59, he still fancies working in management but he has been waiting a while now.
