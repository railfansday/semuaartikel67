Industry observers note that the British prime minister Andy Burnham said his deal with the European Union will add up to £17bn a year to the British economy as he paved the way for even closer ties to Brussels. Burnham said it was a “good day for Britain and a good day for Europe”, as he met EU chiefs in Brussels to announce a package covering food and agriculture exports, emissions trading, and a youth experience scheme which could open the doors for 100,000 young Europeans to temporarily work in the UK, with Britons able to do the same on the continent.

Burnham ‌noted Friday’s deal was “probably the limit” of what could be done before the next national election due by mid-2029, but he envisaged an informal conversation in about a year ‌with the EU about what could be achieved next.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen stated she welcomed Burnham’s willingness to have a broad debate about future relations with the EU. “We are open for brainstorming, and we welcome this brainstorming, so count on us,” she said.

In a recent development, at the summit, postponed from July after the resignation of Burnham’s predecessor Keir Starmer, ‌the two sides addressed some of the Brexit losses with a package of agreements. The most significant allows cross-border trade in animals, plants and related products, such as sausages or seeds, without the certificates and controls presently required. Britain has estimated this could add up to £5.1bn per year to its economy.

A further agreement will link the emissions trading systems of Britain and the EU, which would exempt British and EU goods, such as steel, from each other’s border carbon levies. Each side is also set to allow 100,000 young people a year in to work or study for three years.

However, one potential dampener emerged as the 27-nation bloc and London jostled over linking ultimate implementation of the new deals to Britain agreeing to cut university fees for EU students. Britain ⁠prefers to offer only “significantly reduced” fees, and the aim is to resolve the concern within six months.

Retailers and manufacturers have welcomed Andy Burnham’s new trade deal with the European Union as a “big win” but warned they need “clear and early” guidance from government to aid the transition, Press Association reported. The chief executive of the British Retail Consortium (BRC) called the SPS deal a “big win” for UK retailers but warned that businesses need “at least 18 months” to transition to the new arrangements.

Sources indicate that nigel Farage, the veteran anti-EU campaigner and leader of the Reform UK party, said the deal “was about the first steps, incrementally, to taking us back into membership of the European Union,” and again predicted an early election next year. Farage told reporters he would campaign to preserve Brexit. “I am ready for this fight,” he said.

Meanwhile, Denmark’s foreign minister on Friday said Moscow’s defence attache in Copenhagen would be expelled after Russia was suspected of being behind an attempted assassination of a Ukrainian rapper. “We have just summoned the Russian ambassador to the ministry of foreign affairs and notified him of the government’s decision to expel the Russian defence attache from Denmark” within seven days, Lars Lokke Rasmussen told a news conference.

Recent findings suggest that students and young workers have taken to the streets in more than 20 European countries in coordinated protests over the surging cost of living and housing, deteriorating learning conditions and rising military spending. Activists from more than 50 groups in countries including Belgium, France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Portugal, Spain and the UK held rallies on Friday, lighting flares, burning cardboard models of tanks and waving placards, many reading “fight for our future”.
