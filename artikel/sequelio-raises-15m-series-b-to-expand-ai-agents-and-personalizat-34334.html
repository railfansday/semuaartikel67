Sequel.io Raises $15M Series B to Expand AI Agents and Personalization

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

High Alpha, Album VC and Webflow Ventures joined as new investors. Javelin Venture Partners, Struck Capital, Alumni Ventures and Social Leverage continued as backers.

In a recent development, sequel hosts webinars and video on a customer’s own website instead of sending attendees to a separate event platform. The manufacturer claims this allows it to bring together what a visitor explored before a session, what they watched and asked during it, and what they did afterward, creating a picture of both the individual and their company.

According to recent reports, that on-site activity supplies first-party context, meaning engagement information collected directly on the customer’s website, to Sequel’s three agents. Build plans a video program, Scale turns a conversation into reusable content, and Convert uses audience understanding to support sales-pipeline development. Customers can also build their own agents within Sequel or use its MCP server to connect with an external AI platform of their choice.

Recent findings suggest that co-founder and CEO Oana Manolache described a division of labor in which people lead trust-building video interactions while agents handle program building, logistics, content reuse and personalization. “Our philosophy at Sequel is let people do what people do best, and let agents do the rest,” she wrote.

The personalization engine funded by the round is intended to change what a website displays while audiences are engaging with it. The integration intends include customer relationship management, marketing automation and data warehouses, plus Clay, 6sense and other tools, so understanding created in a webinar can appear in the systems where teams work.

This approach extends Sequel’s original product rather than replacing it. Webinars remain available, while each event supplies activity that can inform later content, follow-up and website experiences.

Manolache previously worked on HP’s global marketing team in London and the United States and was responsible for her business unit’s pipeline target. She said the separation of webinar engagement from website activity motivated her to create Sequel.

In a recent development, sequel began building its product in 2019 and went to market in 2021. It retained the website-hosted approach during the pandemic rather than becoming a standalone virtual-event platform. The engineering work included supporting different website builders and security requirements.

Sources indicate that sequel reports that more than 400 companies use its platform, including ElevenLabs, Datadog, Clay, 6sense, Profound and Athena Health, and that revenue tripled in each of the two years leading up to the announcement. Its enterprise customer base grew fivefold over the year before the announcement.

The funding makes the combined audience record central to Sequel’s expansion: agents draw on it, the personalization engine acts on it during website visits, and integrations carry it into the rest of a customer’s marketing stack.
