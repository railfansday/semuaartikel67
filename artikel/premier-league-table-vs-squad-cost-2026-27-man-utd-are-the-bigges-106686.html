We are 11 games into the Premier League season. Who are the biggest underperformers and overachievers? Manchester United and Hull City, in particular, make for an interesting contrast when you compare their league position with how much their squads cost to assemble.

While squad cost is not a direct assessment of how a team should perform, it does give us an idea of where they should be aiming for.

With that in mind, we’ve taken the current Transfermarkt valuation of each Premier League squad cost and compared it to where each team presently finds itself in the table.

Recent findings suggest that and boy, does it make Manchester United look bad. United are 19th in the Premier League table, despite having the fifth-most expensive squad in the division, giving them a 14-place disparity between their squad cost position and their actual league position.

That’s miles worse than even the likes of Newcastle and Chelsea, who this table doesn’t exactly make pretty reading for either.

In a recent development, at the other end of the scale, Everton are seventh despite having the 16th-most expensive squad in the division. That puts them nine places above where their squad cost would suggest they might be expected to be.

Leeds are also punching well above their weight, sitting sixth despite ranking 17th for squad cost. That’s a difference of +11. Hull are 11th despite having the cheapest squad in the division, putting them nine places above their squad cost ranking.

Brighton (+7), Bournemouth (+6), Coventry (+5) and Arsenal (+5) have also outperformed their squad cost rankings.

Manchester City, meanwhile, are third in the Premier League while having the second-most expensive squad according to Transfermarkt.
