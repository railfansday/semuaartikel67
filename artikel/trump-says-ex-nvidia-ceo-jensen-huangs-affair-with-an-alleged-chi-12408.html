Trump says ex-Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy did not threaten national security

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, U.S. President Donald Trump told reporters on Monday that he does not believe the country’s national security was put at risk as a result of former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

Industry observers note that “It was not put at risk,” Trump said when asked by reporters in light of his own friendship with Huang. “Our country is now stronger than ever before. And you know, China plays games with us, and we play games with China.”.

Trump went on to contradict previous findings from U.S. officials by doubting that Huang’s paramour, former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, is a Chinese spy.

“He probably shouldn’t have been doing on a personal basis what he did … That’s a different story,” Trump added. “But nobody’s proven that she’s a spy. She may not be a spy.”.

It has been revealed that last year, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly oversaw the production of an internal memo, circulated among top policymakers, which warned that Alice Huang was a spy for China’s Ministry of State Security. Trump was reportedly briefed about these intelligence findings at least twice, including once in May aboard Air Force One, shortly before the former Nvidia CEO joined him on a trip to China. But Trump has maintained that he was not aware of these claims.

The U.S. regulates the exports of Nvidia’s top chips to China as a way of maintaining a competitive advantage. But prior to news of Huang’s affair publicly emerging, both the U.S. and Singaporean governments had been investigating Megaspeed for illegally smuggling Nvidia chips into China. Huang has since stepped down from the role he has had for 33 years at Nvidia after more reporting revealed that he also had affairs with two Nvidia employees, at least one of whom was a direct report.

Given Huang’s importance to Nvidia, some prominent figures in the industry — including Elon Musk, Y Combinator president and CEO Garry Tan, and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney — have signed a public letter calling for Nvidia to reinstate Huang.

“A lot of people are asking Jensen — they think Jensen should go back. That certainly isn’t a bad idea,” Trump mentioned. Referring to the former Apple chief executive, Trump added: “I recommended Tim Cook.”.
