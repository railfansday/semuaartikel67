Forget Butter Or Mayo: Spread This Rich Ingredient On Bread For Heartier Grilled Cheese

A grilled cheese is already plenty hearty, but the common tip of using cured meats to increase its satisfying flavor — like in this tasty maple bacon variation — may interfere with your filling's texture if you prefer a smooth, gooey filling. Duck fat is the perfect way around this because it adds meatiness without changing any other ingredients, enriching the bread with umami with just a single smear.

Sources indicate that duck fat is one of the most prized cooking fats in the culinary world because it has a higher smoke point than butter (around 375 degrees Fahrenheit) and a beautifully silky texture rich in subtle, meaty flavors. Just like spreading it on burger buns, using duck fat on grilled cheese can give you crispier bread because you can cook it at a higher temperature. When left at room temperature, it has a consistency similar to softened butter, making it easy to spread or drop in a pan, depending on your preference. Its unique composition of fats and subtle meaty flavor mean you'll notice its richness even in small amounts. Though it has a touch of gaminess, it's quite subtle and serves to elevate creamy and aged cheeses alike with an added layer of complexity.

According to recent reports, using duck fat on grilled cheese is pretty easy, but there are a few tricks that can really maximize your results. Blending it with another fat, using it for some added seasoning, and picking the right pan can all give you the perfect sandwich.

According to recent reports, mixing a fat with a high smoke point with one that burns more easily is a simple way to combine the best of both worlds. The richness of butter is irreplaceable, but you can incorporate some of duck fat's higher-heat cooking properties by swirling the two together before spreading the mix on your bread. Similarly, you can also bring your duck fat to a low, gentle simmer and add herbs and aromatics to flavor it beforehand. Whether you do this when rendering the fat yourself or just for a few minutes before adding your sandwich, a bit of sage can lend tremendous depth, while a smashed clove of garlic can mimic the rich flavor of toum.

It has been revealed that while a nonstick pan is great for butter since it doesn't retain as much heat, you might want to go in the opposite direction for duck fat. A heavy cast iron or stainless steel skillet helps prevent the surface temperature from dropping when you add the sandwich, maintaining that lovely heat that makes duck fat work so well. Plus, animal fats are great for cast iron's seasoning, helping you maintain your pan's coating while you cook!
