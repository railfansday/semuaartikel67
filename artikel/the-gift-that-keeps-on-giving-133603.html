I was running out to grab a cup of coffee and asked my co-worker if I could grab one for her. No biggie, just a common courtesy. I literally didn’t even go out of my way. But when I returned, she couldn't have been more grateful. Sometimes when we do small, simple things, we realize later how much they meant to others.

It could be something like a thoughtful message or a quick check-in where we take a few minutes to listen.

The thing is, we often underestimate the impact of these simple gestures. We are all so busy these days, caught up in daily responsibilities and checking all the boxes to complete everything on our list, that we tend to forget how much we can effortlessly offer to others.

Recent findings suggest that of course, in this fast-paced world, we are always striving for more from life. And, at the same time, it's important to pause and recognize what we have and how it can be shared with those around us.

In a recent development, just take a moment to think about some of the people who make a difference in your life. Perhaps it was that friend who checked in on you during a difficult time or a supervisor who encouraged you. They may not have given their generosity much thought, but I bet you remember how it made you feel, right?

Recent findings suggest that let me share with you a research study that supports this idea. A 2023 study in the Journal of Experimental Psychology found that people frequently underestimate how positive acts of kindness impact others. In one study, participants who gave strangers hot chocolate thought the recipients would be happy, but in reality, the recipients were incredibly grateful for the gesture.

It has been revealed that generativity, a concept developed by psychologist Erik Erikson, describes how a person wants to contribute to the lives of others and make a difference for future generations. This is evidenced in the things that we do, like mentoring others, supporting a friend or family member, or sharing lessons.

It’s notable to mention that the research demonstrates a strong connection between helping people and well-being. In 2020, Psychological Bulletin published a meta-analysis of more than 200 studies and found a consistent relationship between helping people and positive well-being.

It has been revealed that it’s vital to mention that giving to others doesn't mean we are neglecting our needs. It just means that we are also helping one another build relationships. And the research also indicates that a sense of connection really matters.

Sadly, we are often so busy managing all that we have to do that we don't think about how we can support others.

You will both benefit if you reach out, offer a little encouragement, listen, or just let someone know they are on your mind. The point is that it doesn't have to be this significant grand gesture. Small acts of kindness go a long way.

It has been revealed that life's most meaningful moments happen with people who took the time to listen, remember, or encourage, or helped us feel less alone. Don’t wait for more time or money, or until you've figured everything out, before you try to make a difference. Just start with where you are.
