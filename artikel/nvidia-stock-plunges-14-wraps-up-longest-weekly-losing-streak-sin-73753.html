Nvidia stock plunges 14%, wraps up longest weekly losing streak since 2008 financial crisis

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The last time Wall Street was this down on Nvidia the chipmaker's flagship product was the GeForce graphics processor for high-end PC video games, and markets were reeling from the global financial crisis.

Nvidia shares tumbled 14% on Friday, sliding for a seventh straight day as investors grew increasingly concerned about life after co-founder Jensen Huang. The stock closed lower for a seventh consecutive week, the longest such stretch since 2008.

According to recent reports, shares of Nvidia are down 46% over the period, pushing the company's market cap down by about $2.6 trillion. The stock is now trading at roughly 8.4 times forward earnings, while Advanced Micro Devices is valued at 29 times profit for the next 12 months.

On Friday, Reuters reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is exploring potential accounting problems at Nvidia, pushing the stock down further.

Once the most valuable company in the world, Nvidia now has a market cap of $3 trillion, putting it fourth behind Apple, Microsoft and Alphabet.

Nvidia is reeling from the removal of Huang, who resigned on Nov. 12, after multiple reported affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman who was allegedly a spy.

Harvey Jones, Nvidia's newly appointed board chair, mentioned earlier this week that Huang had resigned after "disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the organization's code of conduct, standards and cultural norms.".

But the chipmaker's losing streak started before Nvidia was thrown into crisis as investors worried over the sustainability of the AI buildout, particularly after the midterm elections, and emerging competition in AI chips from Google and AMD.

According to recent reports, even a better-than-expected earnings report, which included an increase to fiscal 2028 guidance, didn't stem the selling. Interim CEO Colette Kress, the firm's longtime CFO, projected revenue growth next fiscal year of 75%, and said that customers aren't going anywhere.

On Tuesday's earnings call with analysts, Kress signed off by saying, "we've got this." In a manufacturer meeting the next day, executives told staff that it was moving its yearly review process, which can come with more stock, from March to December.

It has been revealed that for investors, one major question is whether Nvidia can continue to innovate and sustain its commanding lead in the AI chip market without its visionary leader. Almost the entire manufacturer now is built around the AI trade, a phenomenon that began taking hold just four years ago with the launch of OpenAI's ChatGPT.

It has been revealed that before that, Nvidia's primary business was selling graphics cards used for video games. In mid-2008, the last time the stock was this deep into a weekly losing streak, Nvidia was generating less than $1 billion per quarter. Now it pulls in more than $1 billion per day. At the time, the entire market was in freefall due to the worst financial crisis since the Great Depression.
