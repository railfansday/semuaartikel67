Sources indicate that the difference between success and failure in this division is measured in hundreds of millions of pounds. To the three promoted sides comes the financial mushroom cloud of Premier League cash – a veritable explosion to enable survival in the top tier – but to those who fail to climb out of the Championship – especially those with big budgets – comes financial obscurity and the very real risk of further budget slashing just to stay afloat.

It has been revealed that last season, Championship teams between them lost a worrying £300 million plus: An unsustainable model for football club survival and West Ham, with hugely diminished parachute payments, would face further swinging cuts to stay solvent. So promotion at the first attempt is crucial.

The Hammers would do well to take a leaf out of competitor Wolverhampton Wanderers’ books. Like West Ham, Wolves have stuttered without hitting top form and yet there they sit, third in the Championship. As footballleagueworld.co.uk report, they’ve ground out an impressive sequence of results:.

“if Wolves haven’t always impressed with their performances, they have found a way of getting results, and they head into the Derby game unbeaten in seven, picking up 17 points in that period.”.

In a recent development, “Wolves are just getting it done. Even when they haven’t been turning up (performances), they’ve been doing enough. So, it worries me that if they turn it on, and click into gear, and reach another level, they could be the ones.”.
