American Airlines Replaces Its Rebooking Cards—Agents Now Must Do More Than Hand Them To Stranded Passengers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, american Airlines is replacing the cards it hands passengers when their flights fall apart. The newly introduced Travel Assistance Card points customers to rebooking, baggage tracking, eligible vouchers and other help. And employees are being told to do more than hand over the card and send passengers away. An internal memo set this week as the start date at domestic airports. Use the Travel Assistance Card as a guided customer support tool, not simply as a handout. That’s a small change in what American hands customers, with a much bigger expectation for what employees are supposed to do. What Replaces The RICAP Card I’ve written about American’s RICAP cards and the special phone number printed on them. The name stands for Reservations Irregular Ops Customer Assistance Program. Airport agents gave them to customers affected by delays and cancellations, providing a way to get rebooked when airport lines were long. American periodically changed the number. They didn’t want it becoming everyone’s everyday reservations shortcut, and employees had to throw away old cards so they weren’t sending stranded passengers to a disconnected line. The old cards already had QR codes. The change here is a broader set of resources and instructions for how employees should use the card. The new version provides web addresses and QR codes for: Rebooking and baggage tracking. Eligible vouchers and refund information. Answers to common questions, chat support, the American Airlines app and additional travel assistance. Employees are told to offer it during cancellations, significant delays, missed connections, weather events and other operational disruptions. They’re supposed to explain how to scan the code or use the web address, then help the passenger identify which resources address their problem. Domestic mainline and regional stations are instructed to switch once their new cards arrive and discontinue all previous RICAP cards. International stations keep using RICAP. American Has Been Building More Ways To Recover From A Disruption This fits a series of changes at American: January’s disruption screens brought clearer status banners alongside rebooking, bag tracking and digital vouchers in the app and on the website. Agents gained the ability to issue meal vouchers to an entire eligible flight, rather than processing everyone individually. In June, automatic meal vouchers expanded beyond elite members to all AAdvantage members. Earlier this month, American added standby inside its disruption rebooking tool. Passsengers can try for an earlier nonstop while keeping their confirmed replacement flight until standby clears. The latest hotel and transportation update described automatic offers for qualifying overnight delays and an Uber Central rollout allowing authorized agents to arrange rides without passengers downloading Uber. American’s January app screens show the resources this card is meant to help passengers find: This Is Part Of Becoming An Airline People Will Pay More To Fly American has been adding better seats, lounges and food. Their premium push also depends on what happens to passengers whose flights don’t run on time. Robert Isom included disruption recovery technology in his plan to reclaim American’s premium reputation. And Chief Customer Officer Heather Garboden has put the value of a one point improvement in net promoter score at $50 million to $100 million in revenue. A passenger who gets rebooked rapidly and into a hotel can still be willing to buy American again. Spending hours in a line, then discovering that nobody can help, undermines the rest of the money they’re spending on the product. They’ve also been trying to reduce the disruptions themselves. American spread Dallas–Fort Worth’s schedule across 13 smaller connecting banks instead of nine. Management reported missed connections down 50% in the early April results. American’s overall reliability still doesn’t outperform though. In the September performance figures I covered, American arrived on time 77.11% of the time, compared with 78.65% for United and 82.52% for Delta. And American has been working against itself in how it handles passengers. I covered the loss of routinely staffed customer service counters at Washington National, where passengers were directed to digital tools and phones, with live help brought out during major disruptions. The new instruction to guide passengers through the card makes sense. Someone who can’t get the app to offer a workable itinerary still needs an employee’s help. At a gate with one employee, though, boarding the next departure and walking several stranded customers through their options compete for the same person’s time. This memo gives that employee another task. It doesn’t add anyone else to help.
