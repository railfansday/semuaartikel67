Toyota is set to reveal its first extended-range electric vehicle (EREV) on November 27, and from the first look, it could be based on its flagship Highlander SUV.

The EREV is Toyota’s fifth “electrified” vehicle, a term that now spans pure electric vehicles (EVs), plug-in hybrids (PHEVs), hybrids (HEVs), and fuel-cell electric vehicles (FCEVs).

Unlike fully electric vehicles, an EREV also uses a gas engine that serves as a generator to recharge the battery when it gets low, thereby extending the driving range.

Sources indicate that EREVs use a smaller battery than a pure electric vehicle, while the engine is typically smaller than a PHEV. While they are quieter and often cheaper than a plug-in, EREVs are also less efficient than a pure EV.

A Nikkei report in September claimed that Toyota was preparing to begin production of its first EREV in China in Spring 2027.

It appears the rumors are true. Toyota’s joint venture in China, GAC Toyota, posted a preview on its Weibo account with the caption #Toyota doesn’t rush but range-extended.

Toyota didn’t confirm the name, but all signs point to it being the Highlander EREV. The blurry preview looks like the Highlander, and when translated into English, a part of the text below it reads “Cash in on the classics. Global flagship SUV. Not a quick fix, but a range extender.”.

GAC Toyota will reveal the fresh EREV at the Guangzhou Motor Show on November 27. Check back for the full details.

According to the Nikkei report, Toyota aims to produce 200,000 EREVs in 2027. In 2028, it expects production to double to around 400,000 units.

While Toyota likely won’t bring the extended-range EV to the US, it is set to launch the fully electric version of its three-row Highlander in early 2027 (shown above on the left).

The all-electric Toyota Highlander BEV will be available with front-wheel-drive and all-wheel-drive powertrains with two battery packs: 77 kWh or 95.8 kWh. The smaller (77 kWh) battery provides up to 287 miles of range, while the larger (95.8 kWh) battery provides up to 320 miles in FWD models.

Inside, the electric Toyota Highlander EV offers three rows of seating with room for up to seven passengers.

The three-row electric SUV will be built at Toyota’s manufacturing plant in Georgetown, Kentucky.

Like most foreign brands, Toyota is struggling to keep up with domestic brands like BYD, Geely, and Chery in China’s brutal auto market.

However, it’s Toyota’s gas and hybrid cars that are getting crushed by the competition. In the first nine months of 2026, Toyota sold just over 1.05 million vehicles in China, about 19% fewer than in the same period last year.

After sales plunged nearly 20%, marking its eighth consecutive month with lower year-over-year sales, Toyota noted the market in China “remained weak due in part to rising gasoline prices, while the market for gasoline vehicles and hybrid vehicles was particularly affected.”.

Meanwhile, BYD introduces a self-driving SUV with 600 miles of range that recharges in 10 minutes. These guys are toast.

Toyota looks to avoid the intensifying price war with pure electric vehicles in China. An EREV can potentially be offered at a premium, especially as a flagship SUV.

A organization executive mentioned (via Nikkei) that “Competition in China is intensifying as the market fixates on battery EVs, leading to price wars among automakers,” adding, “We need to take steps to avoid being dragged into that competition.”.
