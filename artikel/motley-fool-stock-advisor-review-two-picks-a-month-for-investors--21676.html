Join Motley Fool Stock Advisor if you’ll buy dozens of its picks over time and hold each one at least five years. The Fool’s record beats the S&P 500, but it averages every pick since 2002 rather than tracking any member’s portfolio. If you want the market’s return, buy an index fund.

Sources indicate that stock Advisor, The Motley Fool’s cheapest membership, sends two stock picks a month, and the Fool’s advice is to buy 50 stocks and hold each at least five years. Motley Fool Options isn’t sold on its own anymore, and options strategies come only with Epic Plus, at $1,999 a year, and the tiers above it.

Recent findings suggest that stock Advisor earns its fee for investors who’ll buy its picks for years and invest $25,000 or more in them. Investors content with the market’s return can skip it for an index fund, and those who research their own stocks will get more from Seeking Alpha Premium.
