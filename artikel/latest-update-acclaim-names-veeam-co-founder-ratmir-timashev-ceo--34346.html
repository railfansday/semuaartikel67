Latest Update: Acclaim Names Veeam Co-Founder Ratmir Timashev CEO for U.S. Voice AI Expansion

Acclaim has appointed Veeam co-founder and former CEO Ratmir Timashev as chief executive, bringing one of its existing investors into an operating role as the voice AI organization expands in the United States. The November 17 announcement also names Frank Schneider, co-founder of Speakeasy AI, as general manager for North America and chief revenue officer.

Sources indicate that the appointments put enterprise software experience behind a focused proposition: AI agents that handle customer conversations and complete defined tasks in regulated industries. Financial services, healthcare, and collections are the initial priorities for the U.S. expansion, according to the organization’s announcement.

Timashev already had a substantial relationship with Acclaim. He led its $34 million Series A and served on its board before becoming CEO. The appointment is therefore a move from financing and oversight into day-to-day leadership, rather than an executive arriving without prior involvement in the business.

Industry observers note that his background includes building Veeam, the data protection firm acquired by Insight Partners in 2020 in a $5 billion transaction. He also founded OH.io, a venture firm whose model places experienced sales and marketing operators inside growing AI companies. That commercial background is relevant to Acclaim’s next phase: turning a specialized technology platform into a larger enterprise business.

In a recent development, schneider will oversee U.S. enterprise operations, go-to-market strategy, and customer-facing product direction. He previously led Speakeasy AI, a conversational AI and orchestration manufacturer acquired by Verint, and subsequently served at Verint as a vice president and AI evangelist. His remit connects product requirements with the work of selling and deploying voice systems inside large organizations.

Recent findings suggest that acclaim’s platform is built around completing specified customer-service tasks rather than sustaining unrestricted conversation. Its public product documentation describes agents for account servicing, payment reminders, customer retention, and related communication-heavy workflows. Voice is central, but the platform also supports other channels, allowing a conversation to move between speaking and written interactions.

Sources indicate that the company describes configurable guardrails, simulation before launch, and a self-service Agent Studio for managing agents. It also offers on-premises and private-cloud deployment options, with data, model weights, and conversation logs retained within the customer’s environment under those configurations. These are vendor-described capabilities, not independently measured results from this announcement.

The distinction between a fluent response and a completed workflow is important. A customer may need a request recorded, an approved action taken, or a conversation passed to a person with its context intact. That makes integration, escalation, and traceability part of the product, rather than optional additions to speech recognition and generation.

The leadership announcement follows Acclaim’s October enhancement on its SaaS offering. The organization noted its cloud platform had completed a SOC 2 Type II examination and obtained ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification, with both conducted by A-LIGN. It describes SaaS and customer-controlled infrastructure as alternative deployment models.

Recent findings suggest that that means Acclaim should not be understood as an on-premises-only product. Data location and operational control depend on the deployment selected. The October patch says detailed assurance reports and data-flow documentation are available to qualified prospects and customers under confidentiality arrangements; the leadership announcement itself does not provide those underlying materials.

Industry observers note that for the latest management team, the practical challenge is converting these options into repeatable deployments. A voice agent operating within an approved workflow has a narrower job than a general-purpose chatbot, but it still needs reliable integrations and clear limits on its actions. Timashev and Schneider’s appointments strengthen Acclaim’s commercial leadership; evidence of how consistently the platform performs across additional U.S. customers will determine the significance of that expansion.
