Antonio Conte reveals what changed to win the Premier League at Chelsea, the Arsenal player that ‘killed’ them, his funny reaction to meeting N’Golo Kante, and why Cesc Fabregas was ‘destined’ to be a coach.

The coach is at the moment waiting for his next role, after walking away from Napoli with the Scudetto and Supercoppa Italiana in his two seasons at the Stadio Diego Armando Maradona.

He sat down with the Cronache di Spogliatoio podcast and discussed his long career as a coach, including the experience at Chelsea from 2016 to 2018.

In that spell at Stamford Bridge, he won the Premier League title immediately, then the FA Cup, but admits it required a radical rethink of his Juventus tactics.

“I had this sort of 3-3-4 formation at Juventus, so when I went to Chelsea, I started with the idea of a 4-2-4 formation, but we didn’t have the players at that moment to make it work,” noted Conte.

“I had Diego Costa, Eden Hazard, Wilian and Pedro as the wide men, and Oscar as a second striker, so I used a 4-2-3-1 with all of them and kept some of the same movements.

According to recent reports, “We had started well, but after every game I told the staff, we are winning, but I don’t feel this is my team. We were top of the table, but after the break for international duty we took on Francesco Guidolin’s Swansea and it was chaos.

In a recent development, “Anything could’ve happened, it was 2-2, but when there is a sense of instability, these are not my teams. I need sides who control the game. I felt that I didn’t have complete control.”.

The turning point came from a painful defeat to local rivals Arsenal.

“It was my first season in the Premier League, but there were Pep Guardiola, Jurgen Klopp, Jose Mourinho, all the best managers. We lost 2-1 at home to Liverpool, then were 3-0 down at half-time to Arsenal.

“Hector Bellerin was killing us down the left, it was one of the few times I was ashamed,” stated Conte on the Italian language podcast.

“I realised that was the time I had to change, because I needed to give more protection to Oscar.

“I put Ivanovic on the right with David Luiz and Cahill, Azpilicueta on the right, basically a 3-4-3. It was the turning point, as the next game against Hull City I put Azpilicueta in the back three, he is a soldier and works wherever you put him, and Moses who Chelsea had wanted to sell.”.

Conte also revealed that some of the meetings with Chelsea players didn’t quite go as planned, but they worked out in the end.

In a recent development, “It was not a transfer session worthy of Chelsea. Kante had won the Premier League title with Leicester City, I asked for two players, Wanyama and Kante. I’d seen Kante play several times, with him it was as if we were playing with 12 or 13, he never felt tired.

According to recent reports, “He walked in behind his agent, I couldn’t even see him. Kante is not a tall man, but he looked like a child, with this big smile. I thought, I expected him to be taller….

According to recent reports, “I hoped I hadn’t made a mistake asking for this player, the Premier League they were all huge and physically strong, but the fear only lasted a minute. I spoke to him, Kante is one of the best players I ever coached, and an exceptional lad.”.

Conte reveals the aim at Chelsea that season was for a top 10 finish, but they ended up winning the Premier League.

Recent findings suggest that “We won 13 games in a row, that is when I felt this was my team, it was in control. Before that, playing three at the back was practically blasphemous in England, but now it has become a cult and many teams do it.”.

Cesc Fabregas was in that Chelsea team too, and has now gone on to become a coach in his own right at Como, qualifying for the Champions League.

“His curiosity, his desire to learn, to modify, Cesc was destined for greatness,” insisted Conte.

According to recent reports, “He was a thinker on the field too, so that helped. He could see the move before he got the ball, was in the right position to receive it. When you get these players who think, they are destined to be coaches.”.
