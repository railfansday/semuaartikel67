Industry observers note that anchored in a Mediterranean harbour nearly 2,000 miles from Moscow, the Captain of Russia's corvette Merkury might well have thought his ship was safe from attack. It wasn't.

The audacious and successful night-time attack in Benghazi, co-ordinated by Ukraine's SBU security agency and its armed forces, has raised the obvious question: is the Ukraine war spreading well beyond its borders?

The answer is yes, in fact, it was already spreading. But this attack, which Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky called "an exemplary operation" will inevitably raise the stakes another notch.

Russia, which is currently engaged in a non-stop campaign to batter Ukraine's civilian population into submission with daily missile and drone strikes, has called it "a dangerous escalation".

It follows the suspected Ukrainian drone attack on a Russian dry-cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland at the weekend, which the Kremlin described as an act of terrorism.

Ukraine's long-range drones have also previously struck Russia's Baltic oil terminal at Ust-Luga.

Some of Ukraine's more audacious long-range drone attacks â what Zelensky likes to call "long-range sanctions" â have also struck targets thousands of kilometres beyond Ukraine's borders with Russia, deep inside Siberia.

The most notable of these was last year's Operation Spider's Web, which involved a specially adapted lorry that drove close to a Russian strategic nuclear bomber airbase and released a "swarm" of explosive drones that damaged or destroyed several expensive bombers.

And Ukraine has also been active further south in Africa where its forces have targeted Russian mercenaries from the Africa Corps, the successor to the infamous Wagner Group that was accused of carrying out numerous human rights abuses.

In a recent development, it's not just Russia that will be alarmed by this latest Ukrainian operation in Benghazi harbour. The United Nations mission in Libya has already expressed its "deep concern".

Libyan officials point out that their country is not a party to the Russia-Ukraine war, although the Merkury and an accompanying cargo ship that was also hit were berthed in the eastern part of Libya controlled by the pro-Moscow warlord and self-styled "Field Marshal" Khalifa Haftar.

How Ukraine drone attacks on Russian ships unfolded.

Throughout this year the International Maritime Organisation (IMO) has condemned all attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and today the global shipping community will be wondering whether this attack does indeed set a dangerous precedent.

If Ukraine can proposal and execute such a bold attack on Russian ships in a nominally neutral harbour so far from home, where does this stop?

As the fifth anniversary of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine approaches, this war has already been spreading, notably in other parts of Europe.

On Friday, several Russian projectiles landed well inside Poland's borders, just 60 miles from an Anglo-French exercise of forces intended to be deployed following an eventual ceasefire in Ukraine.

This, stated Poland's PM Donald Tusk, was "a deliberate provocation" by Moscow.

It's not the first time Russian munitions have landed inside Poland, and therefore inside Nato territory, but observers have pointed out that Russia's probing of Nato's borders is becoming ever more brazen and some are calling for a firmer response.

But Russia is already heavily sanctioned by the West, with Europe having reduced much â but not all â of its energy imports from Russia, so the options for retaliation are limited, short of military confrontation, which no one wants.

Both Romania and Moldova have found themselves frequently struck by overflying Russian drones targeting Ukraine's Black Sea ports, while Bulgaria and Romania have suffered maritime drone attacks on commercial shipping just offshore in the Black Sea.

In the case of Romania, a Nato member, fast jets have had to be scrambled to intercept incoming Russian drones and there is a profound suspicion that these incursions are not all accidental.

There have been a number of anonymous attacks on military industrial facilities in these countries, blamed on agents acting on Moscow's behalf.

Russia, for its part, suspects Estonia of being complicit in Ukrainian drone attacks on the Baltic involving overflights through Estonian airspace.

Hybrid warfare isn't new but it is on the rise.

It refers to the practice, by either side, of carrying out largely anonymous and unattributable operations using methods like cyber attacks, the burning down of warehouses supplying military equipment and the dispatching of drones, both for surveillance and loaded with explosives.

Germany's Leipzig-Halle airport, which is used as a resupply hub by Ukraine's cargo aircraft, was targeted in August by three explosive drones that were discovered before anyone was hurt.

Recent findings suggest that there have been other incidents involving explosive packages sent across Europe targeting Germany, Poland and the UK. Moscow denies any involvement.
