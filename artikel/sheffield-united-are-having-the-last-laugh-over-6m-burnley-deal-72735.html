Sheffield United endured a tricky start to the Championship campaign, but Chris Wilder’s side have fought their way up the table in recent weeks and are having the last laugh regarding one transfer saga.

Following the sales of Andre Brooks, Callum O’Hare, and Gustavo Hamer in the summer, Sheffield United appeared to be in a spot of bother, especially with parachute payments running out.

The Blades had no choice but to rely solely on loans and free transfers, bringing in the likes of Ben Brereton Diaz, Romelle Donovan, Jordan Ayew, and Matt Doherty to subdued fanfare.

With almost no money to spend, Chris Wilder’s side saw themselves priced out of numerous deals, including one that would have reunited a former fan favourite with Bramall Lane.

Sheffield United were interested in bringing Bosnian centre-back Anel Ahmedhodzic back to the club over the summer, after Feyenoord made it abundantly clear that they were looking to sell the 27-year-old.

Ahmedhodzic initially joined the Blades in the summer of 2022 from French outfit Bordeaux, and across the next three years, he would make 114 appearances across all competitions for the South Yorkshire outfit, including 31 in the Premier League.

After Sheffield United were defeated in the 2025 Championship play-off final by Sunderland, Ahmedhodzic, along with the likes of Vinicius Souza and Kieffer Moore, left the club the following summer, with the Bosnian heading to Rotterdam to link up with Feyenoord.

However, after just one year at De Kuip and 36 appearances across all competitions, the defender was made available for transfer this summer, but it rapidly became apparent that the Blades could not afford him, as a division rival swooped in under their noses.

Following their relegation from the Premier League and departures of Maxime Esteve, Joe Worrall, and Axel Tuanzebe, Burnley were in the market for defensive reinforcements.

Then under Nicky Hayen, the Clarets completed deals for Max Alleyne, Igor Julio, and Ahmedhodzic, with the latter heading to Turf Moor in a deal worth around £6 million.

It was viewed as a real coup for Burnley at the time, with Ahmedhodzic having ample Championship experience under his belt and at a decent age, but so far, he has struggled to justify that fee.

Amid Burnley’s struggles, the Bosnian has not helped stop the rot spreading in Lancashire, with the Clarets sitting in the relegation zone after 15 league games with just one win, and, with just six league appearances, he has found himself on the bench and out of the squad entirely in recent weeks.

Certainly, this is not how he would’ve imagined his return to England going, as those at Bramall Lane must be laughing.

With Ahmedhodzic joining Burnley, this didn’t stop Sheffield United’s own pursuit of defensive reinforcements, as with a deal for Leicester City’s Harry Souttar looking too expensive, Wilder turned to the loan market.

Coventry City centre-back Liam Kitching, following the Sky Blues’ promotion to the top flight, found himself out of Frank Lampard’s prepares at the CBS Arena, and Wilder moved quickly to secure a deal for the Irishman.

Following his late move to South Yorkshire, Kitching has been an almost ever-present in the Blades’ back line alongside Japhet Tanganga, with ten league appearances so far, and reminding everyone why he was a crucial cog in Coventry’s promotion to the Premier League.

Having not paid a penny for Kitching, Sheffield United and Wilder must be laughing now after Ahmedhodzic's struggles with Burnley, as an expensive transfer bullet may have been dodged.

With Burnley and Ahmedhodzic struggling at the foot of the Championship table and Sheffield United within touching distance of the play-offs, Wilder will surely be pleased with the way things have panned out.

Of course, had the Bosnian returned to Bramall Lane, then things could have gone differently, and he could be a key part of the side in South Yorkshire now.

But avoiding a £6 million fee and signing someone some people will argue is better, the Blades have come out on the right end of this saga, much to Burnley’s dismay.
