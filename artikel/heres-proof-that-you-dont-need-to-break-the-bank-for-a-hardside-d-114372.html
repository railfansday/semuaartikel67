Growing up, I never really hunted out of a blind. Here in the Northeast, we traditionally hunt from treestands or just pick a good spot on the ground with natural cover. Sure, I watched plenty of hunting shows where they sat in a hardsided blind over a food plot, but that just wasn’t an option for me. That is, until recently, when I started planting my own food plots here in Pennsylvania.

In a recent development, once I figured out the planting cycle, I knew it was time to get my hands on a hardsided blind. But the absurd prices of some of the higher-end models really turned my stomach. Then, I heard about the Maverick XL-V and knew it’d be a great fit for my latest food plot.

In a recent development, this six-panel blind provides a near 360 degree view with large, vertical windows that are perfect for archery hunting. It’s also quite spacious, easily allowing two people to sit and hunt comfortably. But the best part is, it’s a fraction of the price that you’d pay for some of the competition out there. Here’s what you need to know about this versatile hunting blind.

Sources indicate that after tending to my food plot for the better part of the summer, the clover was flourishing and my Maverick XL-V had arrived just in time to build it a month or so ahead of the season. This allowed the deer to get used to the blind’s presence prior to the season getting underway in October.

According to recent reports, the first true test of the blind was setting it up. Maverick talked up its six-panel lock-in system, and I was excited to see how it all came together. Unfortunately, the first shipment I received was missing the door panel. After a quick call to customer support, the missing door panel arrived about a week later, but that panel was missing the window. Rather than wait any longer, I opted to simply replace the window with a piece of plexiglass cut to fit.

The good news is that the lock-in system works flawlessly. The panels all fit together quickly and seamlessly, making it easy to get the blind placed on our homemade platform with just two people. (For some of the bigger, spendier hard-sided blinds, you need a tractor or forklift.) Once it was set up, all that was left was to wait for our archery season to open.

On the opening weekend of archery, my girlfriend and I had an amazing first evening sitting in the XL-V blind. With several does and a few small bucks under 20 yards away, they never had any idea we were there. It was spacious enough for two blind chairs, our hunting packs, and our bows to fit with plenty of room to spare.

Recent findings suggest that since then, I’ve spent several weeks in and out of the blind, with deer in the plot almost every time. I have yet to see a target buck arrive in daylight, but I have a feeling the skunk will be knocked out of the blind in no time.

Industry observers note that after spending several weeks in the XL-V, I can say I’ve grown quite fond of this blind. The windows offer a great view and operate on friction hinges, with three easy-to-use latches to keep them shut. Thus far, the windows open and close silently, which is obviously incredibly notable to avoid spooking any deer in range.

It has been revealed that the door offers two easy-to-use handles, too, and is large enough to fit through without having to contort my body to squeeze into the blind. Aside from the missing components with delivery, the set up was easy and straightforward. The panels with the lock-in system were intuitive and functioned as expected. I honestly expected to struggle getting it set up on our homemade platform, but was pleasantly surprised to be proven wrong.

Obviously, the concerns I experienced with the missing door and then the missing window were not ideal. It’s hard to say if that’s a one-off and I simply got unlucky, or if there’s a larger issue at play there. Aside from that, there are a few other areas I think the XL-V could improve.

Sources indicate that first, the near-360 degree view is great, but it leaves you backlit on sunny days. I’d love to see included covers for the windows behind you to avoid the backlighting issue. On top of that, this blind is not airtight, meaning you need to be aware of where your scent will be going just like hunting from a treestand.

According to recent reports, while the XL-V might have some room for improvements, it’s worth keeping in mind how affordable this hardsided blind is compared to others on the market. It might lack some of the high-end features those blinds offer, but it still gets the job done for a fraction of the price.

Once getting it set up and deployed in the plot, I’ve thoroughly enjoyed every sit. It’s provided me with some incredible hunts, plenty of room to stretch my legs throughout some long sits, and ensure I have all of the gear I need. If you’re in the market for a budget hunting blind, don’t overlook the Maverick XL-V hunting blind.
