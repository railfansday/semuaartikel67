Industry News: FAA Orders Nationwide Ground Stop

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Flights were grounded across the U.S. Friday afternoon over suspected air traffic control communications issues.

The FAA issued a highly unusual mass ground stop shortly after 3 p.m.

Sources indicate that “Due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety, attention all aircraft operators, by order of the Federal Aviation Command Center all airports are ground stopped,” the agency remarked in an advisory. “All departing traffic should remain ground stopped until further notice. Inbound international flights will be managed on a case by case basis.”.

In a separate statement, the FAA remarked a communications issue affecting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers is to blame. Agency technicians are now investigating the issue.

According to ABC News, this is only the second time since the 9/11 attacks that departures across the country have been stopped. Flights at the moment in the air are believed to still be in contact with ATC.

National media outlets highlighted footage of commercial airplanes queued up but not moving at numerous major airports, including Los Angeles, Boston, Reagan National, New York-LaGuardia, Houston Bush, Dallas/Fort Worth, and Raleigh–Durham.

The ground stop will almost certainly cause a cascade of cancellations and delays lasting into the weekend.

In a recent development, the first Cabinet-level official to comment in person on the incident was Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, who spoke to reporters outside the White House. Mullin remarked there is no indication that the outage is a cyberattack, or connected to the recent Microsoft outage.

The situation is close to “unprecedented,” the secretary added, but there is no threat to public safety or reason to panic. Mullin remarked it is still too early to say when the problem will be resolved.

Industry observers note that it was not immediately clear if the outage is connected to ongoing upgrades on ATC systems and infrastructure. The FAA has been moving rapidly to replace outdated equipment, such as old radios and floppy disks, and install more automated technology. The agency is also ripping out old copper wiring and replacing it with fiber optic cables.
