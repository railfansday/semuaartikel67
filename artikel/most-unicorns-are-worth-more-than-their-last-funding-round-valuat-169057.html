Most Unicorns Are Worth More Than Their Last Funding-Round Valuations, Crunchbase Estimates

🌐 Source: Crunchbase • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (826 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • The more than 1,900 startups on the The Crunchbase Unicorn Board have a combined valuation of roughly $9 trillion, based on their last reported valuations.
  • Most unicorns are estimated to have gained value, even as others have lost ground, our analysis shows.
  • According to recent reports, the majority of companies on the board are valued higher by the Current Valuations model than they were when they last raised funding.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The more than 1,900 startups on the The Crunchbase Unicorn Board have a combined valuation of roughly $9 trillion, based on their last reported valuations. But Crunchbase’s upcoming Current Valuation Model, which estimates how private-manufacturer values have changed since their last funding rounds, puts that figure closer to $11 trillion.

Most unicorns are estimated to have gained value, even as others have lost ground, our analysis shows. Among the biggest potential gainers are AI startups Thinking Machines Lab, whose estimated value has climbed from $12 billion to $40 billion, and Sierra, from nearly $16 billion to $31.5 billion.

According to recent reports, the majority of companies on the board are valued higher by the Current Valuations model than they were when they last raised funding. Only around 236 companies — roughly 12% of the board — are down by more than $100 million in value compared to their last reported valuations.

Industry observers note that the valuation model estimates that a select group of 24 companies have gained more than $10 billion in value since their most recent funding rounds. They include Mira Murati’s AI research lab Thinking Machines Lab, which was last valued at $12 billion in its 2025 seed funding but has a current valuation projected at $40 billion.

Industry observers note that similarly, Bret Taylor’s customer oriented AI agent builder Sierra was last valued at close to $16  billion in a May 2026 funding round. It is now estimated to be valued at $31.5 billion, per the Crunchbase model. (On Monday, Bloomberg reported that Sierra is raising a newly introduced round at a roughly $30 billion valuation.).

Physical Intelligence, a organization building AI for robotics, was valued at $5.6 billion in its Series B. Crunchbase’s Current Valuation model estimates its valuation now at $18.5 billion.

Another 53 companies are estimated to have current valuations that are $5 billion to just under $10 billion higher than those achieved in their most recent funding rounds. They include Unconventional AI, which is focused on energy efficiency for AI; Lila Sciences, which is building artificial intelligence for science; AI cloud company Vultr; AI research lab humans&; stablecoin payment platform Tempo; and Yann LeCun’s Advanced Machine Intelligence, which is modeling physical world intelligence.

In a recent development, A total of 484 companies are estimated to have current valuations that are $1 billion to $5 billion higher than their most recent funding valuations. More than 1,100 companies have values that were flat or up to $1 billion higher than their most recent funding valuations.

Only around 236 companies are estimated to be below their most recent valuation, by more than $100 million. Collectively, their current valuations are down by $326 billion from their most recent fundings, Crunchbase estimates.

According to recent reports, the Crunchbase Unicorn Board reached its highest value in 2026 as three companies approached or exceeded $1 trillion valuations — unprecedented in the board’s history. The board peaked at $10 trillion in advance of the Anthropic IPO this past week. The company’s public-market debut removed close to $1 trillion from the board. This followed another trillion-dollar exit from the board earlier this year, when SpaceX — last valued at $1.3 trillion in a secondary financing this year — before it went public in June at a value of $1.8 trillion.

In a recent development, all told, the unicorn board this year is at the moment up by $1.5 trillion from its $7.5 trillion value at the end of 2025. With its closest rival now on the public markets, OpenAI is currently the most highly valued private company at an official valuation of $1.25 trillion.

Sources indicate that the unicorn board has picked up pace this year. Around 360 companies joined the board so far this year, the second highest count in a single year after 2021 when 621 companies joined.

A faster pace of new entrants and rising valuations have helped replenish the board, while current estimates suggest substantial additional value has accumulated between funding rounds.

The next test is how closely these estimated values align with prices investors are willing to pay. Future funding rounds, secondary transactions, public listings and M&A will reveal how much of the board’s estimated $2 trillion uplift translates into realized value.

Industry observers note that current valuations are based on the upcoming Crunchbase Current Valuation Model, which prices companies using a proprietary machine learning model that starts with a firm’s most recent post-money valuation and continuously estimates how its value has shifted since that funding event.
