A fresh film about the historic World Cup quarter-final clash finds new angles for a new generation.

“Is there anything newly introduced to say about it?” Gary Lineker asked the producer Flora Fernandez-Marengo when she first approached him to take part in The Match, a documentary about the 1986 World Cup quarter-final between England and Argentina. Flora told an audience at the London film festival premiere of the film that she played her best card then and showed Lineker an image of Maradona with Queen in 1981, during the band’s tour of Argentina. Maradona wearing a union jack T-shirt and Freddie Mercury an Argentina strip. It worked. Lineker said yes.

In a recent development, the rock’n’roll angle, with Brian May as a talking head, is one of the very few details the film-makers added to Andrés Burgo’s book, The Match, on which the documentary is based. The directors Juan Cabral and Santiago Franco play with the timeline, hopping between past and present across 91 minutes, the exact duration of the match itself. Anecdotes that now form part of Argentina’s football folklore are spliced with excellent archive material.
