My trip to Newly introduced York on Air France began with a short domestic flight on the Embraer 170 in business class from Basel to Paris, a short and pleasant journey.

Years ago after my La Premiere flight from Los Angeles to Paris, I continued on to Basel on an Embraer 190 in what Air France marketed as business class. But the service onboard was no different than economy class: there was no blocked middle seat, meal, or beverages served in glass..it was just regular economy class service.

But Air France has come a long way and I feel like even on a short domestic business class flight it really has really raised the bar in terms of service.

I booked my ticket from Basel to New York JFK via Paris for 60,000 Flying Blue miles and $453 in business class, booking just 36 hours prior to departure (so yes, Air France does release last-minute award space).

Basel Airport straddles Switzerland and France. I entered on the French side and checked in for my flight.

Recent findings suggest that on the check-in level you can walk back and forth freely between the French and Swiss side, but on the lower level and upper level there’s a physical barrier. For example, I went out to the terrace on the upper level and there’s a fence separating the two nations.

After visiting the Skyview Lounge, now operated by Aspire, I headed to the gate for boarding at 1:45 pm, which began right on schedule.

Stepping onboard, I found my seat in 2F.

In a recent development, the aircraft has 76 seats in a 2-2 configuration. Each seat has 31 inches of pitch and is 18 inches wide. Seats recline three inches. There are individual air vents, but no power of any kind: neither USB or a universal power socket.

Industry observers note that business class can be adjusted according to demand, the “genius” of having the same seat. On today’s flight there were three rows of business class, but only three passengers: we each had our own row. Air France now blocks adjacent seats in business class, so that is a big advantage versus economy class.

The only other business class distinctive was that each seat had a pillow and water bottle:.

At only 250 miles, there wasn’t  much time for a meal service.

Sources indicate that air France nevertheless offers business class passengers a small meal box on its shorthaul network, part of a catering concept introduced in 2024 and developed with Servair Corporate Chef François Adamski, a Bocuse d’Or winner and Meilleur Ouvrier de France. For flights departing after 10:00 am, Air France serves what it calls a “signature” sandwich along with a French sweet or dessert, with the selection changing regularly. It is served in a sturdy cardboard box.

On my flight, the meal consisted of two finger sandwiches with smoked salmon and a creamy herb filling on brown bread, along with a small pecan tart and a chocolate…a very nice treat. The airline also serves wine and Champagne in proper glassware, though I just had sparkling water on this flight.

Flight attendants onboard were very polite and efficient: meals and drinks were offered immediately and refills offered after they had completed service in economy class and again before landing, all on a very short flight.

This flight has no internet and no in-flight-entertainment, but Starlink is coming to these jets.

However, it was a beautiful day tempearture wise and I enjoyed looking out the window, especially as we flew over Paris (thought it was a bit hazy) as we neared CDG.

Business class passengers have a dedicated lavatory in the font of the cabin. It was small but clean.

We landed on time in Paris where I ran into a fairly complicated connection to Terminal 2E Hall M…but it was a short but lovely flight.
