Industry News: Buy a Home Sized for Everyday Life – Extra Rooms Cost You Every Year

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Mortgage interest, property taxes, insurance, energy, and upkeep all rise with a home’s size, every year you own it. Size your next home for the rooms you use each week, not for holiday guests, and if you already own more space than you use, add up what those empty rooms cost you.

Industry observers note that A bigger house is easy to justify with holiday guests or a someday you haven’t planned for. Its extra rooms cost money all year, whether anyone sleeps in them or not.

Take two houses, 2,400 and 3,200 square feet, with the bigger one priced $80,000 higher. Say you borrow the difference at 7%, pay property tax at 1% of the home’s value, and set aside another 1% for upkeep, the low end of Fannie Mae’s rule of thumb of 1% to 4% a year. Put insurance at the $4.90 per $1,000 of home value that Freddie Mac’s average borrower paid in 2023.

It has been revealed that the bigger house then adds about $5,600 in first-year interest, $800 in property tax, $800 in upkeep, and $400 in insurance. Heating, cooling, and powering the extra 800 square feet add a few hundred dollars more, by our math on the Energy Information Administration’s figures for household energy spending by home size. Together, that’s close to $8,000 a year for two spare bedrooms you’d use about two weeks a year, before the furniture to fill them.

If you already own more house than you use, run the same numbers on your empty rooms. Whether selling pays is a separate calculation, since agent fees, moving costs, and the rate on a new mortgage can eat into the savings.

For your next home, blueprint for changes you can see coming, like a baby on the way or a job going remote, and favor rooms that do two jobs, such as a guest room that doubles as an office.

In a recent development, put holiday guests up in a nearby hotel instead. At about $160 a night, CoStar’s 2025 U.S. average, two hotel rooms for those two weeks cost about $4,500, against close to $8,000 a year for the spare bedrooms. Your guests sleep down the road instead of down the hall, and you keep more than $3,000 a year.

Before you tour, count the bedrooms your household used last week, and set your listing search to that number plus one flexible room.
