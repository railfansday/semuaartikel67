Russian bank VTB began letting brokerage clients buy and sell bitcoin in its investing app on Friday, a day after the Bank of Russia approved the knowledge test every Russian crypto buyer now has to pass.

According to recent reports, starting Nov. 27, clients of VTB’s brokerage can trade cryptocurrencies in the VTB My Investments app, the broker mentioned. For now, the trades happen over the counter rather than on an exchange, and access is limited to qualified investors, the legal term for Russians who meet wealth or experience thresholds, who also pass the new test.

Sources indicate that “In our model, access to cryptocurrencies will require qualified investor status and passing special testing,” Andrei Yatskov, head of brokerage services at VTB, said. He said the test would appear in the app on Nov. 27 and work like the bank’s existing tests for other instruments.

VTB handles the buying, storage and record-keeping itself on Russian infrastructure. To trade, clients need to open a separate digital account at VTB’s digital depository, where the crypto is held, on top of their regular brokerage account. The bank received its digital depository and crypto exchange licenses on Oct. 6.

“At the start, we will focus on buying and selling cryptocurrencies, and then we will introduce additional services as regulation develops,” Yatskov noted.

In a recent development, the launch came quickly after the regulator moved. On Nov. 26, the Bank of Russia approved the first basic standards for brokers and crypto exchange offices, setting the rules for the test. Every investor has to take it, qualified or not, and it is free.

In a recent development, buyers must answer at least seven of eight questions correctly. The standard lays out eight topic blocks, from regulation, technical basics and information security to stablecoins, low-liquidity coins, address labeling and blocked transactions, and volatility, with one question drawn at random from each.

Investors who fail can try once more the same day, with different questions. Brokers are barred from sharing the correct answers with clients or telling them which questions they missed. A passing result stays valid for one year from the test date or the latest trade, whichever comes later.

Industry observers note that the test was the missing piece for Russian banks. When retail buying became legal on Nov. 17, T-Bank said it was waiting on a market standard for testing, and VTB said it was waiting on the remaining rules. Under the broker standard, the industry’s self-regulatory organization has three working days after publication to send brokers the answer options and correct answers.

According to recent reports, ordinary investors still face tight limits once they pass. Non-qualified buyers can only purchase bitcoin, ether and Tether’s USDT, and no more than 300,000 rubles a year through a single intermediary.

Exchange trading is next. SPB Exchange said Friday it will open spot trading in bitcoin, ether and USDT against the ruble on Dec. 1, after the Bank of Russia added it to the registry of digital depositories on Nov. 27. Trading will run from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Moscow time, with same-day settlement in rubles.

Investors won’t all get in on day one. SPB Exchange said brokers will first use the market to set up their systems, and access will open as each broker is ready. TRX and USDC are also approved, but only for qualified investors, with a start date still to come.

Russian exchanges have been allowed to run organized crypto trading since Nov. 22, once the central bank signs off on their rules. Moscow Exchange has also been aiming to open bitcoin trading on Dec. 1.

“We expect that in 2027, as brokers become ready, investor access to the spot market will expand and fresh products and investment services will appear,” stated Evgeny Serdyukov, SPB Exchange’s chief executive.
