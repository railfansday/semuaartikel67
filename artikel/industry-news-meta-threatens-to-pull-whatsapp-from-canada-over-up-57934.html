Meta has warned that it could pull WhatsApp and other encrypted services from Canada if the government moves forward with a controversial surveillance law.

The tech giant is pushing back against Bill C-22, also known as the Lawful Access Act, which would require technology companies to provide authorities with greater access to information during criminal investigations.

According to Canada’s state broadcaster, Meta representatives raised concerns during a Canadian Senate committee hearing on November 16, arguing that the proposed legislation could compromise users’ privacy and security.

Meta’s director of privacy and public policy, Robyn Greene, warned lawmakers that complying with the bill while maintaining secure, end-to-end encrypted communications would not be “technically feasible.”.

When asked whether Meta could withdraw services such as WhatsApp from Canada, the company’s head of Canadian public policy, Rachel Curran, mentioned it would have to “review all of our options.”.

She confirmed that withdrawing services would be something Meta would have to consider.

Bill C-22 would make it easier for Canadian police and intelligence agencies to access people’s information, while also allowing the government to secretly order tech companies to make certain technical changes. These could include installing surveillance-related equipment, developing tools to help authorities access data, and storing metadata about people’ communications.

Meta fears these changes could weaken encryption and put the privacy of Canadians using services like WhatsApp at risk.

Although the Canadian government has amended the legislation to strengthen encryption protections, Meta argues the changes do not go far enough.

“They have protected just the core and not the outside layers that are an integral part of the encryption process,” Curran explained.

The government, meanwhile, maintains that the legislation would not force companies to create encryption backdoors or give police unrestricted access to private messages.

According to recent reports, meta isn’t alone in its opposition. Encrypted messaging platform Signal has also warned that it would leave Canada rather than compromise its consumers’ security, while Apple has expressed concerns about the potential privacy risks.

According to recent reports, this isn’t the first time Meta has considered removing its services over a legal dispute. Earlier in 2026, the tech giant warned it could withdraw Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp from Fresh Mexico amid a legal battle over child safety.

Canada has also been considering separate restrictions on social media access for users under 16.

For now, WhatsApp remains available in Canada, and Meta has not announced any aims or a timeline for shutting down the service.

Bill C-22 has already passed the House of Commons but remains under consideration in the Senate, where lawmakers could introduce further amendments before it becomes law.
