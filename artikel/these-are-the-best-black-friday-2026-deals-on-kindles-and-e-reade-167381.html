Industry observers note that amazon's Black Friday sale is in full swing, which means it's a good time to score some of the year's best prices on e-readers. Timing your purchase is now a bigger deal than ever (so to speak), as Amazon recently raised the price of basically every Kindle by $50 or more due to the ongoing RAM crisis.

Industry observers note that because of this, the prices aren't nearly as low as they were last year, but now is still the time to get a decent discount on a Kindle Paperwhite or Colorsoft—but if you'd rather read differently, you can also save big on a few worthy Kindle alternatives. Here are your e-reader best bets in the lead-up to Black Friday and Cyber Monday.

The Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition is the best e-reader for most people. An upgraded version of the basic Kindle, the Paperwhite adds a larger screen, temperature-adjustable lighting, water resistance, and better battery life—and if you opt for the Signature Edition, you also get double the storage and no lock screen ads. Earlier this year, it jumped in price to $250, even before the tech giant introduced a new model—but you can get the 2024 model for $179.99, 28% off the new list price.

If you want a great Kindle but you don't care about paying extra for more storage or getting rid of lock screen ads, the non-Signature Edition of the Kindle Paperwhite is also a great buy right now. The 2024 model has been discounted to $129.99 from the list price of $199.99. It's available in standard black, Jade, or Raspberry colors.

If you don't need the bells and whistles of the Paperwhite, you can also pick up the standard Kindle. It's still a very capable device, with good lighting, 16GB of onboard storage, and the ability to hook up your Bluetooth speakers to listen to Audible audiobooks. The standard Kindle, which now retails for $150, is marked down to $119.99, making it the cheapest Kindle option. Though I think it makes more sense to spend $10 more on the Signature Edition, this is the way to go if you want the cute Matcha color.

If you like to read comics or you just appreciate looking at your book covers in full color, the 2024 Kindle Colorsoft is also at a strong 41% discount for Black Friday, bringing it down to around $170. For $10 less than the Paperwhite Signature edition, you get all the same perks (including an ad-free experience) and a color e-ink screen that's great for reading magazines, graphic novels, and manga. Color e-ink isn't for everyone, but if it's for you, the Colorsoft has the best color screen on the market. For an extra $10, the Colorsoft Kids Edition adds a case, a yearlong subscription to Kindle Kids+, and an extended warranty.

If you're looking for a combination e-reader and e-notebook, the Kindle Scribe is a great option. With a larger screen than Amazon's other e-readers, it offers all the benefits of the Paperwhite but adds support for the excellent Premium Pen stylus, allowing you to take notes in your Amazon e-books or create handwritten documents that you can later convert to text using the onboard AI tools. The 32GB black-and-white version of the Kindle Scribe is currently $349.99, while the Colorsoft version will run you $449.99. Both of these are the newest models, released in 2025.

The upcoming Kindle Scribe Colorsoft has all the same great features of the black-and-white version discussed above, but adds a full-color e-ink display. Combined with the large 11-inch screen, this makes it a great device for reading comics or magazine articles as they were meant to be read, or marking up PDFs using the included Premium Pen. This device usually costs a hefty $630, but the current Black Friday sale cuts $180 off of that price.

If you want to step outside of the Amazon ecosystem, the Kobo Libra Colour is a good bet. Discounted $30 to $229.99 at Best Buy, it's presently pricier than the Kindle Colorsoft, but you get a bit more for your money. While most of the specs are similar (they both use the same Kaleido 3 screen and offer 32GB of storage), the Kobo supports a stylus, so you can jot down notes in your books or keep a digital journal. It also has physical page-turn buttons, which the Colorsoft lacks.

If you don't want to be tied to the Amazon ecosystem and/or you're looking for a device that's a bit more portable, consider the Viwoods AiPaper Reader, presently marked down to $200, its lowest price ever. It's got a 6.13-inch screen, so it's the same size and shape as your phone, and it has a crisp e-ink screen with a strong (if not temperature-adjustable) front light and 128GB of onboard storage. The best part is, it runs on an open Android system, so you can install multiple reading apps, including Kindle, Nook, Kobo, and Libby, and keep all of your books in one place. (Check out my review of the Viwoods AiPaper Reader for more—it's a nifty little device.).

Industry observers note that if you don't have a large Kindle library, you're moderately tech-savvy, and you praise portability above all else, I can't recommend Xteink's line of e-readers more highly. The 4.3-inch Xteink X4 Classic is at its lowest price ever, coming in at just under $55, around $34 off the list price. Meanwhile, the smaller 3.7-inch Xteink X3 is about a dollar more; that's a less-generous 19% discount. I own both, and while the Classic is definitely worth the current sale price, I actually prefer the form factor of the smaller X3 (at the moment $55.72), which is about the size of a library card.

Sources indicate that for about $30 more than the X4 Classic, you can upgrade to the Xteink X4 Pro, which happens to be Lifehacker's e-reader Gift of the Year pick. It offers all the perks of the other Xteink devices, with two critical quality-of-life upgrades: A touchscreen for easier navigation, and a temperature-adjustable front light so you can read it in a dark room. (Less obviously, it also has a slightly faster processor that makes the whole thing feel a little snappier.) Right now, the X4 Pro is at its lowest price ever when ordered directly from Xteink: $84, or around 15% off the usual $99 price.

If you're feeling tentative about trusting a seemingly no-name Chinese tech brand, read more about the devices in my review, or listen to the collected wisdom of the tens of thousands of people in the bustling r/xteink Reddit group.
