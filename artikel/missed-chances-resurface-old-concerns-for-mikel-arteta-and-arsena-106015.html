It isn’t yet a real problem for Arsenal, but it may just breathe a bit of life into the number of teams looking up at them: a sudden inability to kill games.

And while that usually refers to finishing - in the sense of scoring - it is here as applicable to their ability to strangle matches.

Because, for the second match in two, and third in eight, Arsenal have allowed a lead to fritter away and a seemingly cast-iron win to become a frustrating draw, this time at home to Borussia Dortmund. That shouldn't be cause for huge alarm given they are the Bundesliga leaders, and a very good side up against an Arsenal team with changes. Mikel Arteta's team were also generally excellent, which he did praise them for.

It is, however, a talking point.

It has been revealed that viktor Gyokeres’ brilliant opening goal wasn’t enough. Gyokeres himself may not be enough, but more aggravating for Mikel Arteta here was how their backline was just opened for Maximilian Beier’s equaliser and countless other earlier chances.

So, a previously formidable place in the Champions League top eight becomes that bit more fragile, in the same way the Premier League lead isn’t what it could have been.

This latest example immediately follows that 2-2 draw at Newcastle United, having also happened in their last Champions League game against German opposition: another 2-2 at Bayern Munich.

That fixture obviously carries a different expectation, but it’s still the same issue. It’s now happened three times in eight games, creating an unusual angst around a team that had previously been playing with the assurance of champions.

And that is the greater quandary for Arteta, at least until January.

It’s long been discussed that this attack isn’t as strong or as deep as it should be, and that Arsenal will look at left-sided forward options in January.

That isn’t really a problem if Arsenal don’t concede, as had been the case for most of this campaign. Before Newcastle, they’d only conceded in three league games, even if one of those was a 3-0 beating to Brighton.

Generally, though, the old 1-0 to the Arsenal had become a death sentence for the opposition.

In a recent development, if they went ahead, you didn’t get in behind. Not so much lately.

It has been revealed that even this match, if admittedly with half the team changed, saw Borussia Dortmund repeatedly get at them through the centre. That was the source of Maximilian Beier’s tidy equaliser, and elements of it had similarities to Harvey Barnes’ equaliser on Saturday.

Arteta, after seeing his team concede three goals in two games, will now be fixated on tightening up.

There, it was perhaps some surprise that he didn’t give William Saliba minutes. Ezri Konsa doesn’t exactly deserve to lose his place, though, and it was Gabriel turned for the equaliser anyway.

Either way, their usual machine of a defensive structure isn’t as well calibrated right now, and that then puts a focus on that forward line.

It has been the lingering doubt over a season that looks set to have little substantial title challenge, and offers a strikingly good chance for the club to retain the title for the first time since the 1930s.

Is it at all possible that they just fail to score enough that games that should become wins instead become draws, doubt is created and others are allowed back in?

Gyokeres, as ever, almost encapsulated this: a forward that has qualities but not enough to really be the starter.

When given space to shoot in the first half, he emphatically highlighted that quality. When required to score in a more pressured situation, he missed the chance of the game.

In a recent development, this is just another frustration for Arteta. Despite again giving up a lead, they had more than enough chances to reclaim it. Gyokeres wasn’t the only attacker culpable. Noni Madueke, recently on such fine form, passed up a brilliant chance just before half-time. Arsenal generally just looked rushed rather than ruthless.

That decisiveness just wasn’t there, though.

It has been revealed that many might say Bukayo Saka wasn’t there, but Arteta has most of his options back. If he is really serious about winning this Champions League, in the way he dreams, Arsenal surely go back into the market in January.

Recent findings suggest that it isn’t the only question for Arteta right now, though. He needs his defence tightened.

He needs his team to recover that recent assurance.

This isn’t yet a real problem, after all. It’s just a recent one, which suggests it might weigh over them for the next spell.

And next up in the league are Manchester City, in that home fixture freighted with so much narrative.

It has been revealed that next up in the Champions League are Real Madrid, who don’t even need Vinicius Junior to show their killer instinct. They’ve got it almost everywhere in attack.

Arsenal had a look in the summer, but now need to look elsewhere - as well as within the team.
