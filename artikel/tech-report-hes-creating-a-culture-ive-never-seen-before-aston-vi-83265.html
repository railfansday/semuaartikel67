By TOM COLLOMOSSE, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 06:01 EST, 18 November 2026 | Updated: 11:56 EST, 18 November 2026.

When most footballers are recovering from a serious injury, the stadium is the last place they want to be. If the team are winning, they feel left out. In difficult times, they feel helpless and frustrated. So the best thing to do is stay away and avoid even watching matches on TV.

Sources indicate that amadou Onana is different. The Aston Villa midfielder has not played since sustaining a serious knee injury during Belgium’s last-16 tie with the United States at last summer’s World Cup and still has some way to go before he can feature again.

Industry observers note that still Onana is playing his part. Dressed in eye-catching clothes, he is often pitchside at Villa Park before and after matches, geeing up the crowd or offering advice and encouragement to younger team-mates. He does the same in the dressing room, where his command of five languages is invaluable. To help upcoming goalkeeper Zion Suzuki settle, Onana is trying to learn basic Japanese.

Villa have missed Onana this season and this week indicated their faith in him by handing the 25-year-old a two-year contract extension through to 2031 on Wednesday – a rare gesture to a man who will not play again for some time.

‘It’s a big one for me,’ says Onana, taking a break from his recovery to speak to Daily Mail Sport in Villa’s giant indoor training facility known as ‘The Barn’.

‘When I got injured during the World Cup, (director of football) Damian Vidagany came all the way to Los Angeles to see me. They indicated massive faith in my ability and who I am as a person.

Aston Villa midfielder Amadou Onana has signed a latest five-year contract, extending his existing deal by two years and committing his future to the club alongside manager Unai Emery.

It's a show of faith in the Belgian, who has been absent since suffering a serious knee injury at the World Cup.

It has been revealed that ‘Very few players renew their contract while they’re out with such a serious injury, so it’s very gratifying. As for the recovery, it’s going to blueprint.

‘The start is the hardest bit. Now I’m back on my feet, in the gym and moving weights so I feel like myself again. I’m happy with how it’s going.

Industry observers note that ‘There is no precise timeline. It’s just a question of seeing how I’m progressing. I’m pretty chilled about it and I know I have to take my time because with such a serious injury, if you come back too early you might re-hurt yourself.

It has been revealed that ‘Not many people talk about it but football can be lonely in some aspects. I know we earn a lot of money and do an incredible job, and I’m very grateful for it.

Recent findings suggest that ‘But there are downsides to it that people have to bring to light. If you go home and it’s total silence – just you and you – it can be very complicated. Throughout my injury, it’s a lonely and dark place at times. Luckily the family and the people around me make a huge difference.’.

Recent findings suggest that onana is an unusual modern footballer. Coaches regularly complain about how few vocal leaders there are among the current generation yet Onana is never afraid to speak up.

He first wore the captain’s armband for Belgium at 21, after Kevin De Bruyne was substituted during a friendly against Germany in March 2023.

Villa need those qualities more than ever. Last summer they lost Emi Martinez, Ezri Konsa, Youri Tielemans, Lucas Digne, Morgan Rogers and Ollie Watkins, who were all crucial in the dressing room. Even though skipper John McGinn is still around, this is a new Villa and some of their performances this season have reflected that lack of experience.

A summer exodus of experienced players means Onana's leadership is more key than ever at Villa.

Recent findings suggest that ‘Going to the stadium for the first time wasn’t easy at all,’ Onana admits. ‘Even now when I’m watching games and I can’t help my team-mates, it’s frustrating. But I understand my responsibility at this club as well.

‘I’m not a "young talent" any more. I am part of the leadership group with Belgium and I know what that means. We have plenty of significant personalities and leaders but whenever they need me, I show up.’.

Communication certainly helps. Onana was born in Dakar, Senegal, and grew up speaking Wolof, the most widely spoken language in that country. He has also mastered French, Dutch, German and English.

‘Because I’m not in the dressing room all the time, I have an outside perspective on it,’ Onana explains. ‘I am into one-to-one talks, making sure everyone feels OK and comfortable within the team.

It has been revealed that ‘I can speak to Johan Manzambi, Nicolas Jackson, Ibrahim Mbaye and Modou Keba Cisse in French, and I was pleased we signed Leon Goretzka so I can speak to him in German and maintain my level. Japanese is the next one.’.

The messages Villa players really need to heed, though, are the ones Emery delivers.

It has been revealed that players rarely have it easy during their early weeks at Villa. Rogers adapted almost instantly after joining in January 2024 but he is the exception that proves the rule.

Recent findings suggest that ‘He made me feel uncomfortable,’ Onana recalls. ‘He took me into areas where I wasn’t ready to go and indicated me aspects of my game I needed to be better at.

In a recent development, ‘He has challenged me over and over again in training and games, and he’s made me a better player, certainly on the ball. He doesn’t care about losing the ball or making a mistake. He wants me to be brave and play.’.

‘He has challenged me over and over again in training and games, and he’s made me a better player,' indicates Onana of Emery.

The working relationship should continue for some time yet. Emery revealed earlier this month that he had discussed a new contract with Villa co-owner Nassef Sawiris, which would likely make him one of the two highest-paid Premier League managers along with Arsenal boss Mikel Arteta.

The start to this season has been bumpy at times but Onana is convinced Villa are on the right track.

Sources indicate that ‘Unai Emery is the best manager I’ve had and we are lucky to have him,’ indicates Onana. ‘(Emery's possible new contract) is a massive one for the players, staff and fans.

‘He played a substantial part in me renewing my contract as well which indicates the faith and belief he has in me. What he’s done here, not many managers have done it.

Industry observers note that ‘We’ve played in Europe four seasons in a row now. We won the Europa League last year. He’s creating a culture that I’ve never seen before. Now we are asking ourselves what else we can achieve. He pushes us to the limit and it seems to work.’.

It has been revealed that onana, you sense, needs to keep himself busy. There is not much peace at home these days: his son recently turned one and keeps his parents on their toes, especially as he has just learned to walk. ‘It’s the best job in the world,’ Onana reflects.

All things being equal, Onana should have at least a decade left as a player but he is already considering the next steps and is studying for his UEFA B coaching licence.

Industry observers note that ‘I think it can make me a better player and give me a better understanding of how coaches see the game,' he states. 'A coach’s job is underestimated. People can’t fathom the time it takes or how much thinking you have to do just to prepare a training session.

‘I’ve been working with 16-to-17-year-olds and it’s a heck of a challenge. It’s pretty interesting trying to work out what coaches are thinking and why they proposal certain sessions.’.

In a recent development, onana's son recently turned one and keeps his parents on their toes. ‘It’s the best job in the world,’ he reflects (Source: Instagram/its_onana).

According to recent reports, emery has long been a master at this and demands total commitment. Many times he has urged his players to think solely about their careers, with other considerations coming later.

It has been revealed that onana could not have reached this level without serious dedication, yet he appreciates that life exists outside football. ‘There were times last season where I would see my wife only twice a week due to all the travelling,’ he reveals.

In a recent development, ‘Now I can blueprint my weeks. Sometimes I can plan a weekend away, be a dad, be a husband. That’s important too. It’s all about balance. Too much of something is never good. I don't think a plumber goes out in the day, works and then comes home and tries to do some plumbing at home.

‘If I’m only remembered as a footballer, then I will have failed. Football is my job and I'm very, very dedicated to it, but it's not what defines me. I’m trying to make a positive impact in the world in very different aspects. When I’m gone, that’s what I want people to remember from who Amadou Onana was.’.
