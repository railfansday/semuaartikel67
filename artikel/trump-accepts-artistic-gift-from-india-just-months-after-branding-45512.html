Trump Accepts Artistic Gift from India Just Months After Branding it a ‘Hellhole’ Country

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that traditional Indian art and crafts were in the spotlight as Indian prime minister Narendra Modi arrived at the ASEAN Summit and meeting of Quad leaders, which concluded yesterday in Manila, the capital of the Philippines. It was organized by Association of Southeast Asian Nations, which includes 11 countries in the region, whose populations total almost 700 million.

It has been revealed that modi gave out gifts to all the leaders in attendance. Philippines president Ferdinand R. “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. got a hand-knotted Kashmiri carpet. His wife, Marie Louise “Liza” Araneta-Marcos, received a Pashmina shawl in a papier-mâché box. Donald Trump took home a golden Pichwai painting from Nathdwara, Rajasthan, while Australian prime minister Anthony Albanese is the proud latest owner of a piece of Jaipur blue pottery. And the gift-giving went on. Modi gave a Katan silk stole in a Sadheli box to Japanese PM Sanae Takaichi. He bestowed a rose quartz elephant with gold work upon Lao PM Sonexay Siphandone. To Singapore’s PM he conveyed a traditional gond painting. He delivered a Chola bronze sculpture to Indonesian president Prabowo Subianto.

The Nathdwara school of miniature painting, of which Trump was given a representative gift, flourished during the 18th and 19th centuries in the village of the same name in southern Rajasthan, as the Times of India wrote in 2008, on the occasion of an exhibition organized by the Archer Foundation at Amdavad-ni-Gufa, a gallery in Ahmedabad, India. A group of priests had moved a treasured idol of Shrinathji to a newly introduced temple there in 1672, explains the Times, after Mughal emperor Aurangzeb destroyed a number of the Goswamis’ temples. Shrinathji is considered a form of Krishna.

Industry observers note that in the large example given to Trump, visible in an Instagram post by the Indian government, eight women in gowns surround an extravagantly dressed central figure sporting a halo, against a backdrop of highly stylized trees and sky. Gold, seemingly Trump’s favorite color to judge by his Oval Office decor, is abundant.

Trump received his offering even though he arrived late, only on Tuesday, as the summit was wrapping up, and, according to the New York Times, “he is not expected to make any significant policies or diplomacy moves.” This, indicates the newspaper, leads to a “widespread belief” among ASEAN leaders “that the United States is not a reliable partner.”.

Industry observers note that but Trump got his gift just the same. He is also not one who is known for his attitude toward preserving the arts and culture, having, for example, initiated Elon Musk’s Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) campaign, which resulted in the gutting of the Institute of Museum and Library Services, and having moved more than once to completely defund organizations like the National Endowment for the Arts.

But he is a man known to love gifts from foreign leaders, even a superjumbo jet from the nation of Qatar meant to serve as Air Force One, despite its not being technically outfitted for the job and despite that country’s history of support for the Muslim Brotherhood. During the midterm election campaigns, Trump attempted to smear Minnesota’s then–Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed with association with the group, calling him a “jihadist.”.

He also accepted the gift despite calling India a “hellhole” in April on the Savage Nation talk radio show.
