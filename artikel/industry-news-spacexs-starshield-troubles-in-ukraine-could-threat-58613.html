The representative in the US House expected to chair the House Intelligence Committee in January, Jim Himes (D-Conn.), has threatened to withhold support for funding government-owned systems from SpaceX amid the ongoing inquiry into the use of Starshield in Ukraine.

Sources indicate that “SpaceX’s potentially unilateral modification of a U.S. Government contract, combined with its lack of responsiveness to congressional oversight, calls into question every contract the U.S. Government has with SpaceX,” Himes wrote in a letter to SpaceX founder Elon Musk on Friday.

CNN first reported the letter’s existence. The network also reported that Himes sent a classified communication to Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton with a similar message that it would be difficult for Congress to authorize funding for systems with SpaceX as the primary vendor.

According to recent reports, spaceX has developed the consumer Starlink service over the last decade, which provides high-speed Internet from low-Earth orbit to user terminals on Earth. For government customers, SpaceX has also developed Starshield, a suite of satellite services that includes a militarized version of Starlink communications. The US government has purchased Starshield services and extended access to Ukraine, although the terms governing that access are largely classified.

During the last several weeks, a controversy has erupted over interruptions to Starshield service in parts of Ukraine, cutting off connectivity to drones used to defend against Russian attacks.

According to recent reports, on October 19, for example, Ukraine President Volodymyr Zelenskyy stated of Starshield on social media, “Unfortunately, we have established that the equipment stops functioning at speeds of 130–161 kilometers per hour in various sectors of the front. This is slowing down our frontline logistics and limiting the use of various types of drones, particularly mid-strike drones and interceptors of jet-powered ‘shaheds.’” Additional disruptions were reported a week ago.

It has been revealed that the central question is who decided to curtail the performance of Starshield assets in Ukraine. Accounts have variously implicated Musk, SpaceX, the Pentagon, and the White House. Musk has stated that US officials control Starshield access, while defending SpaceX’s right to restrict the use of commercial Starlink service in war.

“My ask to you is straightforward,” Himes wrote to Musk. “Explain the changes that were made to the Starshield service in October 2026, why those changes were made, and whether those changes were made unilaterally by SpaceX or were directed by a U.S. Government official.”.

The communications from Himes suggest that Congressional Democrats, who will control the House and Senate in January following decisive victories in the Midterm elections, are prepared to not just investigate the disruption of these SpaceX services in Ukraine, but to take action that could threaten SpaceX’s broader national security business.

“It is difficult for me to continue supporting the authorization of funding for these SpaceX-developed, government-owned systems, until I receive satisfactory answers to my questions about the Starshield service in Ukraine,” Himes wrote in his letter to Musk.

According to recent reports, various options are open to Congressional Democrats, as well as some Republicans who have raised similar concerns, including demanding classified briefings and documents and restricting funding through appropriations to the US Department of Defense and other federal departments. Notably, the US Space Force awarded SpaceX a $2.29 billion contract earlier this year to develop the Space Data Network Backbone intended to support the Golden Dome missile defense initiative. That program, along with SpaceX’s work building intelligence satellites for the National Reconnaissance Office, could become a focus of congressional scrutiny.

Industry observers note that it seems unlikely that the US government would sever ties with SpaceX for space-based data services or launches, as the manufacturer is presently providing capabilities that no other US company can match. But Democrats are clearly serious about putting restrictions on Musk’s ability to influence geopolitical conflicts that have traditionally been the purview of governments rather than corporations.
