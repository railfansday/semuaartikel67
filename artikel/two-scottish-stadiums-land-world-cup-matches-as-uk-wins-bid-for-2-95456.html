Hampden and Easter Road are set to host Women’s World Cup matches after England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales were confirmed as the joint hosts of the 2035 edition of the tournament.

The four-nation bid was given the official seal of approval in a vote by acclamation at an Extraordinary FIFA Congress on Monday, having been the sole valid bid in contention for the event.

It will be the first men’s or women’s World Cup finals to be staged in the UK since the 1966 men’s tournament was held in England.

Recent findings suggest that nineteen stadiums are proposed, with 14 in England, one in Northern Ireland, two in Scotland and two in Wales in a tournament which will feature 48 teams. One of those venues – Old Trafford – has been included in its current guise but any rebuilt stadium is envisaged to be a host.

A further three stadiums could be added – Birmingham’s newly introduced Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham’s redeveloped Racecourse Ground and a potential new Chelsea stadium – which would take the tournament to 16 cities and 22 stadiums across the four nations.

Scotland have two Candidate Stadiums - the national football arena Hampden (capacity 52,032), which was used as part of the delayed Euro 2020 men’s tournament in 2021, and Easter Road, the home of Hibs with a capacity of 20,093, that has never been used for a major tournament.

The bid team projected the tournament would attract 3.5 billion viewers worldwide, and that 4.5 million tickets would be sold.

FIFA’s evaluation of the bid, published earlier this month, rated it 4.3 out of five.

The Congress also approved the joint bid by Mexico, the United States, Costa Rica and Jamaica to host the 2031 finals, which will be the first to feature 48 teams.

Commenting on confirmation that Scotland will co-host the FIFA Women's World Cup 2035, First Minister John Swinney stated:.

Recent findings suggest that "This is fantastic news for Scotland. We have proved time and again that we are a world-class host, and I am delighted that the FIFA Women's World Cup 2035 will be held in Scotland alongside England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

“The timing is ideal. Project 2035, our upcoming long-term plan to grow the girls' and women's game, aims to establish Scotland as a leading nation for women's football by 2035. Our newly established girls’ and women’s football taskforce will help to make this happen, capitalising on the World Cup and leaving a lasting legacy for communities across the country.

Recent findings suggest that “Scotland is building a remarkable reputation for hosting major events, and the FIFA Women’s World Cup joins the Tour de France Grand Départ, The Open, the World Rally Championships and UEFA EURO 2028 in promising to showcase what our country has to offer. We will make sure each one of these amazing sporting events delivers for our economy, supporting jobs, businesses and tourism.”.

English Football Association chair Debbie Hewitt addressed the Congress after the bid’s success had been confirmed.

“Thank you for your trust and confidence in awarding us the FIFA Women’s World Cup 2035,” Hewitt said.

“We’re deeply honoured by your support and grateful for the opportunity to host the pinnacle of women’s football on behalf of the global football family.

“As we said throughout our bid, we want this to be your FIFA Women’s World Cup, a World Cup for everyone.

“Together, we will celebrate the power of women’s football, inspire the next generation, and unite fans from every corner of the world. We look forward to welcoming you to the United Kingdom in 2035 and just as importantly, to working closely with you all in the years ahead to deliver an exceptional FIFA Women’s World Cup that the entire football community can be proud of.”.
