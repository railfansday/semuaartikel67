Jamal Musiala will be sidelined “until further notice” after suffering a muscle injury to his right hamstring, in another setback for the 23-year-old.

Musiala has struggled with repeated injury issues over the last two years as well as a neurological condition which has received enormous scrutiny in recent weeks.

He suffered a muscle tear in April 2025 and began suffering from absence seizures, which cause a brief and sudden lapse in awareness, the same month, two months after signing a contract extension with Bayern Munich.

The condition was not made public knowledge until this summer, with Musiala experiencing two of these seizures during pre-season games. Absence seizures can be managed with daily medication, and other elite athletes, including England rugby player Tommy Freeman, have been able to continue professional sporting careers while experiencing the condition.

Over the same period Musiala has endured repeated injuries: he broke his fibula during the Club World Cup in 2025 and suffered a subsequent ankle injury, then had a metal plate removed from his foot after this summer’s World Cup.

Recent findings suggest that he has only recently returned from an absence induced by a hamstring tear, coming off the bench against Koln in the Bundesliga after two months out. He made an appearance as a second-half substitute in Bayern’s surprise 2-1 defeat to Lille in the Champions League this week, but has now suffered another setback.

Bayern Munich revealed on Friday: “FC Bayern’s medical department diagnosed Jamal Musiala with a muscle injury in his right thigh today. Musiala will be out until further notice. Get well soon, Jamal!”.

How Bayern have handled Musiala’s neurological condition has been heavily criticised after highly-publicised comments by several members of the club’s hierarchy.

Former board chairman Karl-Heinz Rummenigge cast doubt on the midfielder’s future, saying the “hefty sums of money” Musiala is paid are “not justifiable any longer”, and honorary president Uli Hoeness suggested his contract - which now runs until 2030 - should be reviewed if his illness and injury record prevents him returning to top form.

The comments sparked backlash, with players’ union Fifpro saying it was “deeply concerned” by the “pressure” placed on Musiala by such public remarks.

Stand-in captain Joshua Kimmich said after the Lille defeat: “What I don’t understand is the way this is being communicated in public. It must never happen that the burden is placed on a player. The club is obviously the top priority and bigger than a player, but it’s not bigger than a person. That’s an absolute no-go and it is not Bayern-like.”.

Bayern have a raft of top players out with injuries including star striker Harry Kane, who tore ligaments in his ankle during the Koln game, as well as Michael Olise, Manuel Neuer, Luis Diaz and Serge Gnabry.

They sit second in the Bundesliga and 15th in the Champions League group phase table.
