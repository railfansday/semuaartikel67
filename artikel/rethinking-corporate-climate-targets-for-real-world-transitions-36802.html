Rethinking corporate climate targets for real-world transitions

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, corporate greenhouse gas reduction targets are central to climate action, but they often fall short of driving meaningful business transformation. Despite progress in standardization, current target-setting approaches remain weakened by methodological problems, including inaccurate emissions accounting, excluded emissions sources and limited comparability across companies. More importantly, many corporate targets are disconnected from the real-world sectoral transitions required to meet the Paris Agreement’s temperature goal. Drawing on evidence from four editions of the Corporate Climate Responsibility Monitor and recent literature, we argue that target-setting frameworks should place greater emphasis on systemic changes in business models, technologies and value chains. We propose transition targets as a practical complement to traditional greenhouse gas reduction targets. These targets would incentivize, guide and measure progress towards sector-specific milestones, such as electrification, low-carbon material uptake and the phase-out of emissions-intensive technologies. Embedding transition targets in accountability standards, and eventually in regulation, could help close the gap between corporate climate commitments and real-world action. It would also align corporate target-setting with the physical transition benchmarks introduced by the Paris Agreement’s Global Stocktake. The scientific community has a key role in developing robust, actionable frameworks for corporate decarbonization.
