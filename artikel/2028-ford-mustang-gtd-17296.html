It has been revealed that the Blue Oval's most famous stallion has historically been a sports car for the masses, but the Ford Mustang GTD isn't for the faint of heart or pocket. For all intents and purposes, the GTD is an IMSA GT3 racecar for the road, but with far more power. Sandwiched between its flared-and- vented front fenders, a supercharged V-8 sends 815 racehorses sprinting through an eight-speed dual-clutch automatic transmission. For reference, the Mustang GT3 race cars that compete in IMSA and at Le Mans only make around 550 horsepower.

Earth-quaking power is only one of the GTD's tricks, though, and with advanced active aerodynamics, a racing-derived pushrod rear suspension setup, and carbon-fiber bodywork, Ford's super pony can beat all production cars but Mercedes's AMG One hypercar around the Nürburgring Nordschleife. While the racetrack is undeniably the GTD's natural habitat, it's also surprisingly civilized on the road. That combination of traits, and an ultra-limited production run, makes the GTD a rare breed with a six-digit price tag to match.

Expert Tip: If you're after a lightning-fast palomino but can't quite justify the GTD's retirement-draining price tag, the Ford Mustang Darkhorse SC is well worth looking into. The SC might not have the GTD's trick suspension, but it features a virtually identical supercharged Predator 5.2-liter V-8 that churns out 795 horsepower.

Ford has a few minor changes in store for the GTD in 2028. After encountering manufacturing concerns earlier in the GTD's build cycle, magnesium wheels are finally available. The company claims that the new wheels save 50.3 pounds of unsprung mass, which should positively impact handling.

According to recent reports, in addition to the new wheels, the GTD's paddle shifters have been redesigned and are now 3D printed from titanium. An additional stripe design has also been added to the order sheet, and the Liquid Carbon special edition now comes with teal accenting.

Sources indicate that with 815 horsepower from its supercharged Predator 5.2-liter V-8 engine, the roadgoing car has far more power than the Mustang GT3 race car it shares styling with. That track-only model instead uses a 5.4-liter V-8 with around 550 horsepower. Cog swaps for the GTD come courtesy of a rear-mounted eight-speed dual-clutch automated transaxle, similar to the type of gearbox used in the race car.

Recent findings suggest that A Mustang GTD we tested blew us away with its performance. Its shockingly civilized on-road ride quality surprised us as well. We would welcome more steering feedback, but the supremely balanced handling and staggering levels of grip make for a world-class driving experience.

In Comparison: The Mustang GTD is quick, but two other rear-drive rockets, the Chevrolet Corvette ZR1 and Porsche 911 GT3 RS, outran it to 60 mph in our testing, completing that task in 2.2 and 2.7 seconds, respectively.

Recent findings suggest that the Ford Mustang GTD prioritizes lap times over fuel economy, as the roar from its mighty supercharged V-8 swiftly reveals. As a result, the GTD only manages a pitiful 10 mpg in the city, 17 mpg on the highway, and 12 mpg combined, according to the EPA. For more information about the Mustang GTD's fuel economy, visit the EPA's website.

Sources indicate that first Row: Though it looks every bit like the Mustang GT3 purpose-built race car on the outside, the GTD's cabin looks a lot like any ol' Mustang's. Suede, leather, and carbon fiber are the main ingredients of the GTD's interior that upgrade it from its lesser kin—though not by much. Recaro bucket seats hug both driver and front passenger, but the GTD jettisons the rear seats of other Mustang models. The paddle shifters are 3D-printed from titanium, same for the rotary dial gear selector and serial placard. Ford claims it sourced the material for these components from retired Lockheed Martin F-22 fighter jets.

Cargo Capacity: The trunk is a treasure box of suspension and cooling components, but there is room for some small pieces of soft luggage where the rear seat used to be.

In a recent development, the Mustang GTD packs a 12.4-inch digital gauge cluster and a 13.2-inch touchscreen infotainment display. Apple CarPlay and Android Auto are standard equipment. Though the system is largely the same as that of other Mustangs, it does include additional GTD-exclusive performance menus.

As you'd expect of a car like this, the GTD doesn't have many driver-assistance features, but it does have a lane-keep assist system and automatic emergency braking.

For information about the GTD's crash-test results, visit the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) websites.

The Mustang GTD has a solid but average warranty. However, unlike rival Chevy, Ford doesn't provide a complimentary scheduled maintenance visit.
