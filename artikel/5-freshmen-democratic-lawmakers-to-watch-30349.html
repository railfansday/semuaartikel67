Here are five incoming House Democrats who could end up being just as formidable presences in the next Congress, especially on foreign policy and national security.

In a recent development, retired Army Lt. Col. Fred Wellman rode the blue tsunami to topple a longtime Republican lawmaker. His background as a helicopter pilot with multiple combat tours followed by work in political communications and veterans’ advocacy could position him to be a thorn in the side of the Trump administration and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

Industry observers note that wellman served for 22 years in the Army and saw combat four times, including during the first Gulf War and three tours in Iraq. Formerly a lifelong Republican, Wellman switched parties after becoming disgusted with Donald Trump’s disparagement of former Sen. John McCain’s prisoner-of-war status. He would go on to be a veterans’ affairs advisor and later the executive director of the Lincoln Project, a political action committee that drew national attention in the 2020 presidential contest for its acerbic ads that lampooned Trump’s character and record.

Wellman has been a fierce critic of Trump’s decision to launch a war against Iran without congressional authorization. He supports continued military assistance to Ukraine and the Block the Bombs Act, legislation popular with progressives that would prohibit the export of some of the United States’ most powerful bombs to Israel.

Eileen Laubacher, a retired Navy rear admiral, is another former Republican who flipped a deeply red district. Laubacher narrowly unseated one of MAGA’s most prominent and polarizing House members, Rep. Lauren Boebert, in Colorado’s reddest district.

Laubacher’s distinguished 34-year Navy career, which focused on intelligence matters and South Asia, could propel her to assignments on Capitol Hill’s national security committees, such as House Armed Services or House Intelligence, where her insider knowledge of U.S. surveillance technology and the defense-industrial base bureaucracy is likely to be valued.

According to recent reports, in her final years of military service, Laubacher served as defense attaché to India and then on the National Security Council during the Biden administration as the point person for South Asia. During her stint as the top defense official at the U.S. Embassy in Fresh Delhi, Laubacher was credited with playing an crucial role in facilitating U.S. support for the Indian military during a series of violent 2020 standoffs with the Chinese army along the two countries’ disputed Himalayan border.

Her campaign focused on affordability issues and connecting Trump’s tariff policies and the Iran war to higher cost of living, as well as improving care and services for veterans. She is a member of the Blue Dogs, a caucus of moderate and conservative-leaning House Democrats. Laubacher wants an end to U.S. taxpayer-financed military support for Israel but supports the transfer of weapons to the country on a “case by case basis.”.

Industry observers note that it wasn’t just Iran. Israel, tariffs, and immigration also loomed large in voters’ minds.

Also hailing from Colorado but from the opposite wing of the Democratic Party is Melat Kiros, a democratic socialist who at 29 years old will become the youngest female lawmaker in Congress in January. Kiros immigrated as a baby with her family from Ethiopia.

As a junior corporate attorney, she was fired from her job at a prominent law firm for refusing to take down an open letter she published defending law students who expressed pro-Palestinian views in the weeks after Hamas’s Oct. 7, 2023, attack on Israel. Kiros, who has accused Israel of committing genocide, drew connections to her family’s own experiences, having lost family members to what she called the recent “genocidal war against Tigrayans by the Ethiopian and Eritrean governments.”.

Sources indicate that this summer, Kiros successfully challenged from the left the 15-term incumbent progressive lawmaker Rep. Diana DeGette to win the Democratic nomination for the solid blue Denver-area congressional seat. She argued that DeGette and other establishment Democrats had been ineffective in combating Trump. During the primary, Kiros made Israel a distinguishing issue between herself and DeGette. She supports an arms embargo to the country that would cover even defensive systems such as the Iron Dome missile shield.

In the House, Kiros is expected to align with other far-left young progressives such as Ocasio-Cortez. Although Kiros received critical primary campaign backing from the Democratic Socialists of America, she has mentioned she does not share the national organization’s position that the United States should withdraw from NATO.

Like Kiros, Brad Lander successfully ran to the left of an establishment Democrat in a deep blue district where support for Israel was a key dividing matter. But Lander’s Jewish faith and victory over a pro-Israel incumbent in one of the most Jewish districts in the United States (New York’s 10th) could make him a potent Capitol Hill foil for the Israeli government.

Lander, a former New York City councilman and comptroller, is a political ally of New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani. And he has already been mixing it up online and offline with members of both Israel’s political opposition and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s far-right government. Like many other progressive House Democrats, Lander, whose district includes Lower Manhattan and Brooklyn, supports the Block the Bombs Act.

According to recent reports, when former Israeli Prime Minister Yair Lapid wrote on X shortly after the U.S. midterm elections that his country’s next government should prioritize repairing ties with moderate Democrats in Congress as well as embark on a public relations tour of the United States, Lander pushed back hard.

In a recent development, “[Y]ou’re not listening to what American voters—Jewish and non-Jewish—are saying,” Lander wrote on X in response. “We aren’t starving for a barnstorming tour. We’re hungry to see respect for international law and human rights.”.

Just a few weeks earlier during a solidarity visit to the West Bank to help Palestinian farmers harvest their olive crop, Lander was detained for several hours by the Israeli military, which mentioned it was because his trip presented a “security risk.”.

“Yet one more element of escalating, and very clearly state-sponsored ethnic cleansing,” Lander wrote of the experience.

Speaking of potent foils, Hegseth may face one of his own making when he is eventually called to testify before the House oversight committees.

Sources indicate that nancy Lacore is another retired Navy admiral who narrowly flipped a solidly conservative district in the midterms. But Lacore, who won her South Carolina House seat by just over 3,000 votes, is also one of the highest-ranking officers to be ousted by Hegseth. Her 35-year Navy career, including her most recent position as the chief of the Navy Reserve, would seem to make her a strong candidate for membership on the House Armed Services Committee.

In a recent development, lacore has said she doesn’t know why Hegseth fired her, but she went on to make her opposition to his leadership a central tenet of her campaign. “Pete Hegseth is the most unqualified secretary that this nation has seen,” Lacore said in an interview with CBS News after winning election. “He has caused significant damage to us as a country and put us at risk and really damaged the readiness of our entire military force.”.

According to recent reports, the retired three-star vice admiral was a registered Republican until 2016, when Trump became the party’s leader. In January, she will become the highest-ranking female military officer ever to serve in Congress.
