Industry observers note that for the 17th year, the Board of Governors of the Motion Picture Academy gave out Honorary Oscars at the Governors Awards inside the Ray Dolby Ballroom in Hollywood, which Sunday night was crammed with the usual Oscar networkers. Honorary Oscar winners Ridley Scott (“Gladiator”), eight-time Oscar nominee Glenn Close (“Fatal Attraction”), and famed Disney animator Floyd Norman (“The Jungle Book”) represented the old Hollywood guard, while Killer Films’ Christine Vachon and Pam Koffler (“Carol”), accepting the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award, embodied the ongoing vitality of the independents.

It’s telling who shows up for these awards, which offer an opportunity for talent and craftspeople to appear in front of many Oscar voters (and media) at once. All week, contenders have been flowing into the city from far and wide to gather for this star-studded evening. They have hope. Or their studios are willing to spend the money to get them there. Matt Damon, who has been working overtime this week on a series of “The Odyssey” panels (he moderated one), introduced Oscar honoree Ridley Scott and a stunning clip reel including “The Duellists,” “Blade Runner,” “Alien,” “Gladiator,” “Napoleon” and, of course, Damon’s “The Martian,” which he almost turned down because the role of an abandoned astronaut stranded in space seemed too similar to “Interstellar.” “Nobody gives a shit,” remarked Scott. Damon was glad he made “The Martian.” Scott, who is 88 and still working, sent his pre-recorded video acceptance from London. In it, he honored his late brother, “Top Gun” and “Crimson Tide” director Tony Scott, who died in 2012. “His great work would certainly have earned this recognition,” Scott said.

Of course, it didn’t hurt for Damon to charm the room as he vies for a Best Actor Oscar for his role as wily Odysseus in “The Odyssey.” Chairman of NBCUniversal Entertainment Donna Langley’s table was packed with Damon and fellow contenders Christopher Nolan, Emma Thomas, Tom Holland, Zendaya, casting director John Papsidera, John Leguizamo, and more.

According to recent reports, and it didn’t hurt for John Malkovich to honor his old “Dangerous Liaisons” costar and Newly introduced York theater buddy Glenn Close at the same time that he is vying for Best Actor for Martin McDonagh’s “Wild Horse Nine” (Searchlight). “Numerous screenwriters and playwrights have uttered that phrase over the years: ‘made better by Glenn Close,'” he mentioned. “Glenn’s got the goods. She can do the major stuff, the little stuff, and everything in between.” Her list is long and includes eight nominations. She broke out in “The World According to Garp,” followed by “The Big Chill,” “The Natural,” “Mars Attacks,” “Albert Nobbs,” “The Wife,” “Hillbilly Elegy,” and more. The story of casting her in “Fatal Attraction” is legendary. After the studio resisted, fearing she was too wholesome, Close insisted on auditioning and blew Paramount chief Sherry Lansing and Michael Douglas away. On Sunday, Close was visibly moved accepting her prize, wiping away tears, while acknowledging past co-stars like Robin Williams and Malkovich, and departed champions such as her “World According to Garp” director George Roy Hill. “Look George, I got one,” she said, holding her Oscar up toward the heavens.

Last year’s Honoree Tom Cruise showed up to support not just box-office disaster “Digger” ($50 million worldwide, stratospheric budget), he told me, “but everyone.” Cruise still goes to see movies in theaters, sneaking in, he said, cap lowered. When he screens in his private screening room, it’s often when he wants to scrutinize a film and play it repeatedly. For his part, Oscar-winning director A.G. Iñárritu (“Birdman”) felt “confused,” he said, by the range of reactions to “Digger” (Metacritic: 46), which is not ranking high on Gold Derby charts. Production Design and Cinematography seem to be its best shots.

It has been revealed that the newly introduced Hollywood world order was on view. Early in the evening Langley was chatting with CAA’s Bryan Lourd but didn’t want to linger near the Warner Bros. table, where Skydance’s freshly minted Co-CEO Ynon Kreiz was eventually seated, while Denis Villeneuve presided over the “Dune: Part Three” table with Timothée Chalamet, who walked the red carpet with Kylie Jenner. Why show up? It means he’s interested in getting back into the Best Actor derby after losing back to back Oscars for “Marty Supreme” and “A Complete Unknown.” The movie is just starting to screen to meet critics’ groups’ deadlines.

Tom Quinn headed Neon’s table, which was front and center; producers David Heyman and Jennifer Fox, director Luca Guadagnino and stars Andrew Garfield and Yori Borisov represented Neon pickup “Artificial” (December 25). According to Fox, “Artificial” tested well before it was dropped by Amazon/MGM. Certainly the success of Silicon Valley documentary “You Can See Everything” (A24) bodes well. Quinn isn’t worried. And everyone has been asking Heyman about the hunt for the next James Bond. “We’ll know soon,” he said wearily. ENHANCEMENT: Indeed, Villeneuve’s “Dune: Part Three” star Rob Pattinson was declared Monday morning.

Industry observers note that james Gray sat with his “Paper Tiger” stars Adam Driver and Miles Teller, who snuck out before the end of the show. “Fjord” star Sebastian Stan hung with his director Cristian Mungiu, who is running an English-language film festival right before Cannes in Romania.

Netflix’s Ted Sarandos and latest content chief Peter Rice (who said he will not hire anyone to replace Dan Lim) commandeered several tables for its top contenders, “La Bola Negra” and “Possible Love.”.

It has been revealed that during the cocktail hour, I asked a couple of documentary branch members if still-buzzy “You Can See Everything” will land a nomination for their branch. “It’s competitive,” said one, citing “Naza” and “Once Upon a Time in Harlem” as shoo-ins. “They’ll get shortlisted.” Also in the mix is “Musk” director Alex Gibney, who flew in from Amsterdam — these days awards campaigns are international — and greeted his date, who had also flown in from Europe, musician David Byrne, who wrote a song for the movie. Gibney asked me to photograph him with his doppelganger, elegant “Wild Horse Nine” star John Malkovich. “People confuse me for him all the time,” said Gibney.

Many movie stars hugged and kissed, among them Garfield and Sophie Okonedo, who stars in two well-reviewed festival favorites this season, “Mouse” and “Clarissa.” Sandra Hüller, star of “Rose,” “Fatherland,” and “Project Hail Mary,” asked to be introduced to the belles of the Academy Ball, Killer Films producers Christine Vachon and Pam Koffler.

Recent findings suggest that there was warmth in the room for Scott, Norman, and Close, but the real whoops and cheers were saved for Vachon and Koffler. Filmmaker Todd Haynes, who met Vachon at Brown, introduced the duo along with Killer Films vet and Oscar-winner Julianne Moore (“Still Alice”), who is the current frontrunner for Best Actress for Jesse Eisenberg’s musical “The Debut.”.

“The Debut” opens December 11th, and will likely add to A24’s amazing year at the box office, with “The Invite,” “You Can See Everything,” “Club Kid,” “Primetime,” “The Drama,” “Tony,” and “Backrooms.”.

The Killer film list is long and varied and ranges over many transgressive subjects, including “Kids,” “Boys Don’t Cry,” “Hedwig and the Angry Inch,” “Far from Heaven,” “Still Alice,” “Carol,” and their first best Picture nomination, “Past Lives.” I nabbed a t-shirt listing their films in small type at their Saturday night Soho House party, which drew an impressive crowd: Sony Pictures Classics co-chiefs Tom Bernard and Michael Barker, John Turturro, Cinetic’s John Sloss, Academy documentary branch governor Roger Ross Williams, producers Cathy Schulman and Jon Kilik, Mubi’s Laura Kim, Strand’s Marcus Hu, filmmakers Lisa Cholodenko and Wash Westmoreland, and many more. (See our Killer Films story here.) Among the many collaborators they thanked at the Governors Awards for helping them over the years was PR stalwart Jeff Hill.

It wasn’t lost on the Academy crowd that while “The Odyssey,” “Project Hail Mary” and “Spider-Man: Brand New Day” kicked ass at the 2026 box office, the indies are flourishing at the moment, shooting original rockets into the system. Killer Films represents that energy. “Indie films, as we’ve seen this year, are as vital as ever,” Academy CEO Bill Kramer told me. “And Christine and Pam have led the way for decades and decades, and they’ve told stories that we had not seen before, and everybody’s responding to them again and again and again. These are films with staying power.”.
