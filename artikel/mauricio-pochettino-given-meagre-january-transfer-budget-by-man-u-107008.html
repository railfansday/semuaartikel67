Manchester United fans should not expect much transfer business to be done this winter, with Mauricio Pochettino expected to be given a meagre budget to work with in January.

The Argentine has arrived as the newly introduced Red Devils boss and took charge of his first game on Sunday, which did not go well at all as Liverpool romped to a 4-0 win over their old rivals at Anfield.

Leny Yoro was shown an early red card, leaving Pochettino’s looks in tatters as defeat left Manchester United 19th in the Premier League table.

With just one win and 23 goals conceded in 11 league outings, there have been calls for signings to be made, especially in defence.

Before Pochettino was appointed, club legend Gary Neville even urged him to turn down the job unless the hierarchy promised to sign two defenders over the winter transfer window.

That may still happen, but it will have to be some canny business as the Daily Mail reports that Manchester United will have ‘well under £50 million’ to spend in the next transfer window.

Having focused on midfield signings over the summer, United want a centre-back, left-back and striker, but obviously that cannot be done to any level of quality on such a meagre budget.

Brentford centre-back Nathan Collins has been touted as a target, but the 25-year-old would not be a cheap option.

The Bees paid £23m for him when they signed him from Wolves three years ago and he remains under contract until 2029.

The Republic of Ireland international has been a mainstay in the team since moving to Brentford, although is currently out with injury.

Summer targets at left-back included Manchester City’s Rayan Ait-Nouri and Barcelona’s Alejandro Balde, both of whom could be options again.

Monaco striker Folarin Balogun has been linked with a move to Old Trafford, having worked with Pochettino in his stint as USA manager.

The former Arsenal man came close to joining Everton in a £40m deal over the summer before the deal collapsing at the last minute.

Manchester United may look to make a loan signing to keep costs down, although could stretch the budget if they feel that relegation is a genuine threat.

According to recent reports, neville does not think it will come to a relegation scrap, despite United’s woeful form, saying after the defeat to Liverpool: ‘I don’t believe they’re in a relegation fight. They’re second-bottom in the league, it’s as bad as it’s been for 55 years. I do think they’ll come out of it and move up the table.

However, he added: ‘You could arrogantly look at Coventry at home, or Sunderland at home, and think United will be alright. But the performance levels we’re seeing at the moment don’t give you much confidence.’.
