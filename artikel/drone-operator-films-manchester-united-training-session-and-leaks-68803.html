A drone operator filmed English soccer team Manchester United’s first training session with new coach Mauricio Pochettino and shared the “unethical” footage on YouTube.

Pochettino officially left the U.S. men’s national soccer team (USMNT) to become head coach of English Premier League club Manchester United. On Thursday, he oversaw his first training session with Man United’s first-team squad ahead of Sunday’s Premier League game against Liverpool at the club’s Carrington training ground.

According to recent reports, however, a drone operator who runs the YouTube account CC Overview — which describes itself as sharing “drone footage of construction at football stadia and events affecting our environment” — flew his aerial device over the facility during Pochettino’s first training session. He then posted a 12-minute segment on YouTube on Thursday.

Sources indicate that according to a report by The Athletic, Pochettino and his players, who were split into three groups, were identifiable in the aerial footage, which amassed over 600,000 views on YouTube. The footage demonstrated Pochettino leading a training session with one group of players, while his assistant Jesus Perez oversaw two separate groups. The video, filmed at United’s Carrington training base, also captured audio of the YouTuber apparently controlling the drone saying “let’s get closer,” before zooming in on the upcoming head coach.

It has been revealed that the drone operator has since apologized for a “total error in judgment.” He states the aerial footage was legal under U.K. drone laws but has removed the “unethical” video from YouTube.

“A total error of judgement on my part led to me filming some of the Man United training session at Carrington, whilst the flight was legal in terms of drone laws I fully accept it was unethical on my part,” the drone operator writes in a post on YouTube.

According to recent reports, the drone operator describes aiming to film Pochettino in the club’s training kit for the first time, but adds he had no intention of recording a “meaningful training session.” The YouTuber indicates he was operating under an incorrect assumption that it was open to media outlets. He also insists he acted independently and was not working on behalf of Liverpool or any other football club.

“I would like to apologize to Manchester United, Mauricio Pochettino and the players for my actions and sincerely hope those actions do not affect the outcome of any match,” the drone operator notes.

Man United prohibits the use of drones at its Carrington training facility, where signs warn that anyone operating a drone could face civil action or prosecution.

In the U.K., capturing aerial footage using drones is permitted provided operators comply with Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) safety regulations, as well as privacy and data protection laws.

CAA guidance on privacy for drone use details: “If you use these devices where people can expect privacy, such as inside their home or garden, you’re likely to be breaking data protection laws.” It adds that drone operators should “let people know before you start recording or taking pictures”.
