It's not been the loan spell at Bradford City that QPR expected Kieran Morgan to have so far this season, with the 20-year-old struggling to get regular League One minutes under both Graham Alexander and Michael Duff.

Morgan was thrust into first-team action at Loftus Road as a teenager after arriving from Tottenham Hotspur in the summer of 2024.

He made 59 league appearances over two years, but after signing Glen Kamara to partner Nicolas Madsen and Jonathan Varane in the engine room this past summer, and with Ronnie Edwards able to anchor the midfield, the club sent Morgan out on loan to aid his development.

Sources indicate that joining Bradford seemed a great opportunity, with the Bantams coming off a third-place finish in their first season back at third-tier level in six years last season. It was the top-end League One move that could help bridge the gap towards regular Championship football upon his return.

Morgan has hardly had a look-in, though, and all signs point to the 20-year-old being sent elsewhere once the January transfer window opens.

Industry observers note that morgan arrived at Bradford late in the window, a few days out from Deadline Day. It's always difficult for young players to dislodge a settled partnership in the middle, especially after not being with the club for pre-season, and the QPR loanee has struggled to do so.

Recent findings suggest that the majority of his minutes have come in the Jersey Mike's Trophy, with starts in the FA Cup and EFL Cup, too. In League One, he's played just 41 minutes, being unused from the bench five times. Granted, he has come off the bench more under Michael Duff, who has used him in the last two.

Nevertheless, QPR will definitely have wanted more from Morgan from this loan spell, and FLW's Hoops fan pundit, Louis Moir, "wouldn't be surprised" if the 20-year-old was recalled.

"I wouldn't be surprised to see the club recall Morgan, purely just to send him somewhere where he's guaranteed to play," he said.

"He's got the ability to play consistently in League One, for sure. I think he got more experience than he could have imagined for us in the Championship and probably deserves to be playing a lot in League One.".

Moir did concede that it would have been difficult for Morgan to get into a Bradford side who, despite now sitting outside the top six, have been well-drilled and difficult to beat. A lot of that comes down to Antoni Sarcevic and Callum Connolly in the middle.

"He's joined Bradford, who are doing fairly well, so it's probably been difficult for him to go there and automatically play every week, so he's probably getting frustrated," he continued.

"For his own development, he'll likely be recalled and go to a club that agrees to play him most weeks.".

According to recent reports, moir went on to admit that Morgan's career at QPR has, so far, been quite peculiar. It's not often that an 18-year-old gets thrown into the action at Championship level and then, two years later, is sent out on loan to League One.

Ultimately, the midfielder can't control the number of injuries the Hoops have had, especially last season, which have given him minutes, but the fan pundit feels that his standing in Julien Stephan's side could be much better if his loan spell came before his time in the first-team fold at his parent club.

According to recent reports, "I wouldn't say he's too good to be warming the bench in League One. He's a decent player, but I think he was overhyped a little bit with us when he came and played in our first team," Moir admitted.

It has been revealed that "It revealed in his last few appearances for us. He was a bit lightweight and gave the ball away a lot, I noticed. He was probably rushed into the team with us and should have gone on loan a lot sooner.

Sources indicate that "His career has almost gone the opposite way. He should have been on loan last year and then may have been good enough for us now, but yeah, if he doesn't improve at Bradford, bring him back and send him out somewhere where he'll play and go with that until the summer.".

Industry observers note that morgan hasn't played for QPR yet this season, luckily enough, so the Hoops will be able to send him to play elsewhere if they so wish. Moir hopes he gets the game time he needs, as he feels his chances of playing regularly at Loftus Road again are slipping away.

"I'll be surprised if he makes it properly at QPR, making the first team week in, week out," he concluded.

It may be a bit dramatic to say, given Morgan is only 20 years old, but with QPR improving rapidly over the last three months or so without the ex-Spurs academy man in the team, worries are seeping in that they may have outgrown him.

In a recent development, morgan was a useful squad option at Loftus Road both in the 2024/25 campaign and in Julien Stephan's first season in charge last year. Being sent out on loan this year would have been a massive opportunity for the 20-year-old to prove that he's more than just a sporadic option.

It hasn't worked out at all, though, and if Morgan can't get into the Bradford side regularly, the chances of him coming back and dislodging Madsen or Kamara, if the Finnish international stays past his loan spell, are very slim.

It has been revealed that the 20-year-old needs game time not only for his own development, but for a chance to prove himself to his parent club, too. If he's going to get back into the QPR picture, a big January decision will need to be made.
