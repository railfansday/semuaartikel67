I am not the Ironclad, nor was meant to be; am an attendant lord, one that will do to bind a necro, Exhaust a scene or two, advise the Regent; no doubt, an easy tool, Defective, glad to be of use, Politic, Silent, and Impervious; full of high synergy, but a bit obtuse; at times, indeed, almost ridiculous— almost, at times, the Fool.

It has been revealed that it has been a while since I played Slay the Spire 2. I return to find the forums full of grief concerning a recently introduced sixth character - a minstrel of sorts, dressed in carpets, who shoots arrows out of a harp.

In a recent development, the short version is that the Fool was wildly overpowered when Mega Crit added her to the early access deckbuilder's beta branch, with a base arrow-shooting mechanic that lets her dish out at least 21 damage in her first turn, and a Repeat Shot card that allows for upwards of 80. She ain't so vicious anymore, because they've just released a "mostly Fool-centric" patch that nerfs a couple dozen or so cards.

It's not all nerfs – they've buffed Cho Chorus, Quietus and Show Off, among others, while reworking Tune, Flourescence and Rapid Shot. There's a upcoming Rare card, Optimism, which exhausts all Sadness in your Hand, while drawing a card for every card exhausted.

Industry observers note that some context: Sadness is a special colourless card, earned by way of cards like Mourning Blow. It remains in your hand between turns and can't be played, making it an immediate hindrance, but you can transform it and use it as fuel for certain cards. Just like real sadness! Best of all, you can turn it into Joy, which grants you Energy. Reading about this complex neurophysical alchemy is making me tired. Shall I give the beta build a go? Do I dare to eat a peach? Are you sick of ham-fisted Prufrock references yet?

Sources indicate that read the full patch notes here. The Fool is an interesting creation, eyebrow-raising damage output aside, and I look forward to her addition to the game's main Steam branch. She can play various flavours of music to modify other cards.

Industry observers note that I do like a weird bard, and I broadly approve of the practice of releasing fresh early access characters with all the sliders pushed to max, waiting for the cacophonous whining to reach a certain pitch, and then jocularly chopping back their stats.
