Cloud infrastructure stocks have been hammered lately as investors have been rotating out of this sector for multiple reasons.

According to recent reports, inflationary conditions led the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates in September, and it could make a similar move in December. A high-interest rate scenario doesn't bode well for companies investing heavily in artificial intelligence (AI) data center infrastructure, as they will have to contend with higher borrowing costs.

According to recent reports, this explains why investors have been booking profits in AI infrastructure stocks. DigitalOcean (DOCN -3.09%) has also borne the brunt of the broader sell-off in cloud stocks, with shares of the organization losing 55% of their value over the past six months. However, a closer look at DigitalOcean's growth and prospects indicates that the sell-off may be overdone.

DigitalOcean offers on-demand cloud infrastructure services to small and medium-sized companies, developers, and start-ups. DigitalOcean's customers can rent computing resources to run AI workloads in the cloud without investing in expensive infrastructure of their own. The company also offers platform-as-a-service (PaaS) solutions, enabling users to build, customize, and deploy AI applications.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

So, DigitalOcean provides a full-stack cloud infrastructure platform that integrates hardware and software. Customers can build AI agents, run business analytics, detect fraud, create chatbots, summarize documents, and perform other tasks using its AI-native cloud platform. The good part is that DigitalOcean's AI services are in solid demand.

The company released its third-quarter results earlier this month, and it became clear that AI is a key catalyst. Its Q3 revenue increased by 40% year over year to $321 million. The company's annual run rate revenue (ARR) also increased by a similar magnitude to $1.28 billion. However, what's worth noting is that DigitalOcean's ARR from AI customers increased by 288% year over year to $376 million.

The robust adoption of DigitalOcean's AI services explains why it expects its revenue growth rate to ramp up next year. DigitalOcean expects its revenue growth to accelerate to 45% year over year in the current quarter. Even better, it sees a 60% jump in revenue next year, which is well above its prior guidance of 50% growth.

Recent findings suggest that another crucial point worth noting is that DigitalOcean's software stack is boosting its ARR. I already pointed out that DigitalOcean doesn't make money by just renting out hardware. The firm points out that customers are using its software solutions to build and deploy agentic AI applications, which is driving up DigitalOcean's revenue per megawatt (MW) of cloud computing capacity it deploys.

DigitalOcean is on track to end 2026 with 75 MW of active data center capacity. That number is poised to double next year, and it aims to further increase its capacity in 2028. This explains why analysts expect DigitalOcean to clock solid revenue growth in 2028 as well.

Importantly, the increasing adoption of its software offerings is poised to drive significant acceleration in earnings per share (EPS) growth.

The impressive revenue and earnings growth that DigitalOcean is poised to deliver is likely to send its shares soaring, according to Wall Street.

Recent findings suggest that digitalOcean's median 12-month price target of $160 implies potential upside of 123%, according to estimates compiled by CNN. It is worth noting that 16 of the 18 analysts covering DigitalOcean rate the stock as a buy.

Sources indicate that it is easy to see why analysts are bullish about its stock price trajectory over the coming year. DigitalOcean's growth rate is picking up, and it trades at an attractive 8 times sales right now. The U.S. technology sector has an average sales multiple of 5.6, and the slight premium that DigitalOcean enjoys is attributable to its improving growth profile.

I won't be surprised to see DigitalOcean trading at a slightly higher sales multiple after a year, driven by a stronger top-line jump. Assuming it trades at 10 times sales, and its top line reaches $1.96 billion in 2027, the organization's market cap could increase to $19.6 billion. That's 130% higher than the current market cap, suggesting that DigitalOcean indeed has the potential to live up to Wall Street's expectations and skyrocket next year.
