Industry observers note that leaker Ice Universe says Apple is currently developing four models of the 20th anniversary iPhone which we’ve been referring to as the iPhone 20. We’re expecting next year’s flagship models to be a radical new design with an all-glass appearance.

Why four models rather than two is unclear, but it seems possible Apple is testing two versions of both the Pro and the Pro Max to determine just how ambitious it can be with the final product ….

Didn’t the iPhone 6 or the iPhone 11 already have a curved front glass?

It has been revealed that ice Universe notes that two of the four models have quad-curved glass backs and two have flat glass. They indicate that Apple has already had to ramp back its ambitions with the curvature.

Apple is at the moment developing four models in total: two with flat glass backs and two with quad-curved glass backs. Apple has been prototyping this quad-curved design for three years, making several revisions along the way—particularly regarding the glass curvature, which is still being refined. The earliest version had a very pronounced curve; it was later reduced somewhat.

It’s standard practice for Apple to develop multiple competing prototypes of new devices, evaluating them for everything from usability and durability to ease of manufacturing.

In a recent development, we’re expecting Apple to use a combination of ultra-thin bezels and curved glass to create the illusion of no bezels at all – a more sophisticated version of the effect seen on the Apple Watch. Today’s report states the bezels are around 1mm thick.

Sources indicate that the curved edges on the quad-curved version are approximately 1mm wide. However, because the glass curves around all sides, when viewed straight on from the front, the bezel becomes nearly invisible, creating a near‑borderless appearance. The actual visual effect could be quite stunning.

According to recent reports, we yesterday saw an AI-generated render of what the design could look like. It’s expected to be the biggest redesign of the standard iPhone since the iPhone X.
