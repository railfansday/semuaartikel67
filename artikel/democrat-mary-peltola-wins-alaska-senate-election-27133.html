Democratic candidate Mary Peltola won the Alaska Senate election, capping a midterm cycle that already was one of the best for Democrats in more than a half-century, the Associated Press reported Tuesday evening.

Industry observers note that peltola defeated incumbent Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan in a race that was surrounded by controversy over an independent candidate who shared his name.

Recent findings suggest that peltola had faced accusations that her campaign recruited ultra-longshot candidate Dan J. Sullivan to the race to confuse voters. The Justice Department filed a lawsuit seeking to delay certification of the election if the incumbent Sullivan lost in order to conduct an investigation into whether voter confusion tainted the outcome.

According to recent reports, the Justice Department is appealing the dismissal of that civil suit. The department is also conducting a criminal investigation into the second Sullivan's decision to enter the race.

Peltola's victory gives Democrats a solid majority of 56 seats in the Senate beginning in January.

Democrats this cycle flipped nine Senate seats that are at the moment held by Republicans.

That outcome, which was not considered remotely likely at the beginning of the election, came as part of a massive Democratic wave this year, which saw the party also flip majority control of the House of Representatives by a decisive margin.

Alaska was the last Senate race to be called in the midterm elections. Peltola, a former House member, joins Senator-elect Peggy Flanagan as the first two Native American women elected to the Senate.

Recent findings suggest that the race was called two weeks after Election Day, and six days after Peltola's fellow Democrat Scott Colom pulled off perhaps the most stunning upset of the 2026 cycle by defeating incumbent Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in the Mississippi Senate race.

As of 10:39 p.m. ET, Peltola had 50.1% of the vote, and Sullivan had 45.8%. Another Republican, Gerald Heikes, had 2.1% of the vote, while Daniel J. Sullivan had 1.6% of the vote.

If no candidate exceeded 50% of the vote, the election would have been decided through a ranked-choice method, in which the two bottom finishers would have been eliminated, and the second choices on the ballots of voters who selected them would be allocated to the remaining two candidates.

"From Juneau to Utqiagvik, the message from Alaskans is clear: the status quo in DC is not working for Alaska," Peltola said in a statement.

In a recent development, "Over the course of this campaign I've spoken with elders in tears about empty fish racks; families struggling to find housing they can afford; and parents in the Bush faced with the impossible choice of heating their homes or putting food on the table," Peltola said. "Today we take a monumental step forward in addressing those concerns and putting Alaska first."As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my life to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first," she said. "Thank you Alaska. Let's get to work.".
