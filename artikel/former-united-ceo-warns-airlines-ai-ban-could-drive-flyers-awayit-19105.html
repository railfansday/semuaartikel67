Sources indicate that former United Airlines CEO Oscar Munoz indicates customers who want an AI assistant to book their flights will choose an airline that lets them. That’s a warning to the airline he led, which just moved to ban those bookings. But the carrier’s own recruiting ad offers a revealing look at the future they’re preparing for: AI agents with access to shopping systems, under United’s control. Speaking with Scott McCartney on the November 15 special edition of Airlines Confidential, Munoz said: If travelers want an agent booking their trips, they’re going to find the airline that lets them do that.

They said the restrictions would reach outside agents using United’s own systems as well as travel agencies and aggregators. As I wrote when United unveiled the ban, their contract language reaches even agents whose actions a customer supervises or approves. An assistant that becomes the starting point for travel shopping can gain enormous bargaining power. It decides which options to recommend. Eventually, its owner might demand payment for access to customers. United would rather customers start with United where they can sell ancillaries as well as the ticket, control messaging to the customer, and manage costs.

According to recent reports, A United assistant could be excellent at finding a United flight, explaining the seats and handling a schedule change. That would be valuable. But I wouldn’t expect it to suggest that I should buy a competing airline’s nonstop instead. An assistant working for me should know whether I’d pay more to avoid a connection, whether a better seat is worth the difference, and whether spending miles makes sense for this particular trip. It finds the best trip on the right airline to match my preferences and tradeoffs. OpenAI’s Sam Altman says “If ChatGPT were accepting payment to put a worse hotel above a better hotel, that’s probably catastrophic for your relationship with ChatGPT.” He argued that a booking transaction fee would be acceptable if ChatGPT recommended the best choice independently and took the same cut regardless of what choice the customer books. My bet is that United eventually accepts more outside agents after negotiating terms, because customers will increasingly expect someone else to do this work.
