Surviving The Worst-Case Scenario: Higher Inflation, Lower Asset Values

🌐 Source: Financialsamurai • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 12 min read (2370 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Sources indicate that for the past several years, most of us made an unspoken deal with inflation.
  • Recent findings suggest that post midterm elections, inflation is still running hot, but stocks, commodities, crypto are now going the wrong way.
  • If you felt rich in October and feel poor today, you're not alone.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that for the past several years, most of us made an unspoken deal with inflation. Yes, food costs more. Yes, tuition and health insurance went up again. But our stocks, our homes, and our private investments rose even faster. We felt richer, so we shrugged.

Recent findings suggest that post midterm elections, inflation is still running hot, but stocks, commodities, crypto are now going the wrong way. Real estate is usually the last domino to fall. We are living in the worst-case scenario for anyone building or preserving wealth: higher prices and lower asset values at the same time.

If you felt rich in October and feel poor today, you're not alone. My public equity portfolio is overweight tech, and it's taking a beating. I'm down around 20% from the highs, or more than three years of living expenses, and it hurts.

The only silver lining is that FIRE and debt don't mix, which has prevented me from going on margin and losing even more.

Recent findings suggest that say a household has $1 million in investments and spends $120,000 a year. With 3.7% inflation, its cost of living rises by about $4,400 a year.

Recent findings suggest that when that portfolio was up 20%, the household gained $200,000. That's 45 years' worth of extra inflation costs. Nobody complains about $4,400 when they're up $200,000.

It has been revealed that now the portfolio is down 12%. That's a $120,000 loss, and the extra $4,400 a year in living costs hasn't gone anywhere. The household didn't just lose its cushion. It's paying more to live while owning less.

Recent findings suggest that that's why the mood flipped so fast. The wealth effect works in reverse, and it works faster on the way down. Let's look at how we got here, where stocks could go, and how to survive until the bargain gets restored.

After Democrats won both the House and the Senate in the midterms, investors began pricing in a slower AI data-center buildout. The market began to correct.

Then Nvidia, due to the Jensen Huang debacle, and the rest of the AI complex kept sliding. The QQQ just finished its 9th-worst week since its 1999 launch, down 10.5%. That's the worst week since March 2020.

It has been revealed that the S&P 500 closed at 6,910 on Nov 20, about 11.6% below its Oct 6 closing high of 7,819. Another ~9.5% decline to roughly 6,255 and we're officially in a bear market.

Sources indicate that whether you cheered or groaned on election night is irrelevant to your portfolio. The market reprices to future expectations.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, inflation refuses to cooperate. The last annual CPI reading was 3.7% in September. October prices rose 0.4% month-over-month, hotter than expected. Gasoline is up about 37% from a year ago. And the market is pricing in a Fed rate hike in December.

A Fed hiking into a falling stock market is not what the doctor ordered. But it is what we've got.

Sources indicate that here's what makes this downturn different from 2000 or 2008. Corporate earnings are still projected to grow at a healthy clip. Goldman Sachs forecasts S&P 500 earnings of $375 for 2026, $415 for 2027, and $460 for 2028.

It has been revealed that when stocks fall while earnings rise, the only thing that's breaking is the multiple investors are willing to pay. The S&P 500's forward P/E has collapsed from roughly 23x a year ago to 16.5x today. That's a ~29% de-rating in about 13 months. We're also lower than we were during Liberation Day in April 2025.

According to recent reports, in other words, the market isn't saying companies are making less money. It's saying it no longer trusts how fast AI profits will grow, and it wants a bigger margin of safety for the risk.

Industry observers note that here's one of several rosy forecasts by Wall Street firms for year-end 2026 and 2027. I hope it comes true so that my wife and I can keep our FIRE membership card.

According to recent reports, at 6,910 and 16.5x, the implied forward earnings are about $418, which lines up neatly with Goldman's $415 estimate for 2027. So let's run the math using $418 in forward earnings.

In a recent development, the first takeaway is counterintuitive. At 16.5x, the S&P 500 is already cheaper than its 5-year and 10-year averages. If valuations simply mean revert to the past decade, the index has 16-20% of upside.

Recent findings suggest that the bear case says the last decade was the anomaly. Go back to 1990 on the Goldman chart and the aggregate index spent most of the 2003-2015 period between 13x and 17x. Revert to ~15x and the S&P falls another 7-8%. Revert to the 12.8x lows of the post-financial crisis era and we're looking at another ~23% drop.

In a recent development, but the bigger risk isn't the multiple. It's the earnings. Here's what happens if AI spending gets throttled and forward earnings fall 10% to ~$376, essentially flat versus 2026.

Sources indicate that the nightmare scenario requires both lower earnings and a lower multiple. That combination gets you to 5,600-5,700, or roughly 28% off the peak. Painful, but in line with a garden-variety bear market, not a 2000-style wipeout.

In a recent development, my base case is that we're closer to the bottom than the top on valuation, but earnings revisions over the next two quarters will decide whether this is a correction or a bear market. Watch the earnings estimates, not the headlines.

Sources indicate that when stock prices fall, CEOs get nervous and CFOs get busy. The easiest way to defend earnings per share is to cut the biggest expense line: people.

Recent findings suggest that the layoffs were already happening before the selloff. Through October, tech companies confirmed 171,501 job cuts, 21% more than the same period a year earlier, and AI has been the top stated reason for cuts this year. Now add a 10%+ market correction and a Fed that's raising rates. I'm certain more mass layoffs are coming in Q1, after the bonuses are paid.

I lived through roughly 20 rounds of layoffs during my 13 years at Goldman Sachs and Credit Suisse. The people who got cut first were rarely the least talented. They were the least liked, the least visible, and the most expensive relative to their output.

So please be on your best behavior if you want to keep your job. Get in first, leave last, volunteer your time, and support your boss's charitable causes. It's corporate theater, but theater keeps the paychecks coming.

In a recent development, if you don't want to deal with corporate BS anymore, start planning to negotiate a severance package now. The people who get laid off first tend to get the best severance packages. They also get a head start on the hundreds, if not thousands, of employees who will be looking for jobs later.

That's what I did in 2012. Instead of quitting, I negotiated my way out with a severance check that helped fund my first years of early retirement. I lay out the whole playbook in How To Engineer Your Layoff. Leaving a job you no longer like with a severance package feels incredible.

It has been revealed that most downturn advice assumes inflation is falling and the Fed is riding to the rescue with rate cuts. Not this time. When prices go up and asset values go down, you have to defend on four fronts at once.

In a bull market, your portfolio can earn more than your salary. In this environment, your paycheck is the only asset that isn't getting marked down every day. Your job is now your most valuable asset, so treat it that way.

Recent findings suggest that be the person who uses AI to do the work of two people, not the person AI replaces. Build goodwill with your manager and colleagues before the layoff list gets made. And if you were thinking about quitting, don't. Stick around, collect your paychecks, and see if you can negotiate a severance when cuts inevitably hit your group.

If you don't have a second income stream yet, start one now. It doesn't need to replace your salary. It just needs to cover your groceries, which feel like they cost a lot more than they used to. The key to financial freedom is generating enough passive income to cover your basic living expenses.

Industry observers note that if you locked in a low fixed-rate mortgage, congratulations. Inflation is quietly shrinking your debt in real terms. This is not the time to aggressively pay it down and give up liquidity.

Recent findings suggest that with the Fed hiking, cash in a money market fund or Treasury bills finally pays you to wait. Earning over 5% risk-free in US Treasury bonds is a beautiful, beautiful thing.

Favor assets that can reprice with inflation. Rents and replacement costs tend to rise with prices, which is one reason I've always kept a large chunk of my net worth in real estate. Meanwhile, attack the variable expenses that are inflating fastest: gas, food, insurance, and subscriptions you forgot you had.

According to recent reports, personally, I'm eating less due to rising food costs, which is great for my dad bod challenge. I'm also just fixing my car instead of buying a newly introduced one. Finally, I've cut many superfluous items such as cable from our budget.

It has been revealed that instead of guessing the bottom, tie your purchases to the forward P/E. At ~$418 in forward earnings, 16x is about 6,690 on the S&P 500, 15x is about 6,270, and 14x is about 5,850. If we get down to these levels, buy a slug at each level and let the market come to you.

Sources indicate that before you buy anything, run a Financial SEER check. It's a way to quantify your risk tolerance, calculating potential loss in terms of time.

Industry observers note that for example, if you have $500,000 in stocks and save $5,000 a month, another 20% drop costs you $100,000, or 20 months of work. If that makes you sick, you're already too aggressive, and buying more won't help you sleep.

It has been revealed that above all, avoid selling your best assets to fund a lifestyle that inflation made more expensive. That's how a temporary loss becomes a permanent one. Always stay disciplined when there's a chance we're heading into a zombie apocalypse.

According to recent reports, if you're retired, this is a good time to lower your withdrawal rate and ride it out. As I wrote recently, FIRE in a bear market can actually be comforting. When Return On Effort falls, it's the perfect time to work less, not more.

Industry observers note that if you have kids, a downturn is a gift. use corrections to help make your kids millionaires. During this selloff, I transferred $23,000 into each of my children's custodial accounts and bought stocks. The more the market fell on Friday, November 20, the more I transferred. And I will continue to transfer cash to their accounts and buy if the market goes lower.

Personally, I've accepted that I can't make money in the stock market right now. Every rally gets sold, and checking my portfolio feels hopeless. So I've stopped trying to fight it. I'm tired of the volatility.

It has been revealed that instead, I'm spending more time with my kids and fixing things around the house. For a couple of years, thick, heavy wallpaper had been peeling off in our downstairs hallway. It looked terrible, so I finally hired professionals to strip it, patch and sand, then prime and paint the walls. Then I spent an hour filling in imperfections and painting as well.

It looks great now. When the market won't reward your effort, put your money and energy into something you can actually see improve. This is why having an internal locus of control feels so good. You're not helpless.

Recent findings suggest that and please keep living your life. The market doesn't care how miserable you are, so you might as well enjoy the holidays with your family.

It's easy to forget during a selloff, but downturns are temporary and time in the market is what builds wealth.

Bear markets transfer wealth from the impatient to the patient. They also reveal who was swimming naked with too much risk, too little cash, and too much confidence in their job security.

You don't need to be a hero. You just need to survive with your job, your cash flow, and your sanity intact. Do that, and the next bull market will take care of the rest.
