Gondor calls for aid, because Lego Minas Tirith just got its first discount as part of the Black Friday sale.

Recent findings suggest that while it's not a massive price cut, it's the first time we've seen any kind of reduction on the mega set since it introduced in June of this year. And because it has a 'Black Friday deal' sticker, I sincerely doubt it'll drop any further. If you want Lego Minas Tirith, you should ride with all the haste of Shadowfax to Amazon as a result. It's definitely one of the most eye-catching early Lego Black Friday deals, that's for sure.

We've never seen this set for any less than it is now. In fact, Lego Minas Tirith has never enjoyed any kind of discount so far as I can tell. Sure, it may 'only' be £59 or 10% off. But the fact that it's never been any less than this is big news. Plus, the Black Friday deal badge means it's highly unlikely to drop any more as the sale rumbles on.

I've been busily building this set myself for a while now, and even partially complete, it's impressive. Lego Minas Tirith (which recreates the capital city of Gondor that features in The Return of the King) is massive, with over 8,200 pieces and a grand height of 23.4 inches from top to bottom. Throw in 10 minifigures for an incredibly stuffed model that's larger than many of the best Lego sets combined.

Industry observers note that this kit doesn't just recreate the White City, though; it's a very clever bit of toy architecture by the design team over at Lego. That's because it seamlessly uses multiple scales in one set. While the bottom wall is minifigure scale, the city gets gradually smaller as it rises with some incredibly effective forced perspective.

Recent findings suggest that there are multiple hidden scenes around the back, too. While the throne room takes pride of place, Gandalf and Pippin's lodging from Return of the King, as well as the archives room where Gandalf first learns the secret of the One Ring are included. And that's to say nothing of numerous other Easter eggs tucked away all over the place (I won't spoil these as discovering them yourself is part of the fun).

Will this absolute monster of a set be for everyone? No - it's a huge undertaking to build, and it takes up a lot of space on display too. But the effort is worthwhile if you're up to the challenge, because there are few models that can drop the jaw more when seen in person. It certainly gives my all-time favorite model, Lego Rivendell, a run for its money.

Of course, there are plenty of other kits on offer if the scale or price of Lego Minas Tirith makes your blood run cold. The Lego Balrog set has tumbled down to £71.99 at Amazon instead of £110, for example, while Sauron's Helmet is just £43.99 at Amazon rather than the full £65. Basically, you've got options if you want to take a slice of Middle-earth home.
