New Balance SC Elite V5 + FuelCell Rebel V5 Running Shoes.

Black Friday sales are notorious for flooding inboxes with endless discounts on triathlon gear you might not actually need. Triathlete’s editors are no exception: for the past two months, they have received a deluge of notifications teasing Black Friday deals from just about every triathlon brand.

Recent findings suggest that but our staff, who test and review gear year-round, know not every sale is worth shopping. After all, what good is a massive Black Friday discount on cheap triathlon gear if it requires replacement in a few months?

To separate the signal from the noise, we asked our very own Triathlete editors to share the items sitting in their online shopping carts this holiday season. From reliable daily trainers and high-end smartwatches to cold-weather layers and budget-friendly pool essentials, here are the Black Friday deals our team is actually buying with their own money.

Sources indicate that have you ever found that pair of shoes where it was immediate love at first stride? You just knew you’d found yourself in a new, long-term relationship, and for the next several weeks, you were in the honeymoon phase – enjoying running more and convincing yourself that running in carbon shoes was fine because this was the only shoe you wanted to run in. Forever.

That was me with the Newly introduced Balance SC Elite V4. That might surprise a few people, as it’s not the fastest or flashiest of shoes – and very few pros wore it. Yet it just ticked all the boxes: a wide toe box, stable, grippy, and breathable. They’re the not-intimidating super shoe I recommend to everyone looking to try super shoes. And to top it off, they lasted two seasons and far more miles than you’re ever supposed to put on them.

Industry observers note that that’s why the SC Elite V5 is on my shopping list. It’s not only well past time to replace my current pair, but the new version promises to be lighter and faster, with a couple of small challenges, like the short tongue, addressed. Alex Yee and Laura Philipp both wore this shoe to great effect, and now I think it’s my turn.

According to recent reports, but I can’t just run in these for every session. Sigh. I must also shop for a daily trainer that matches the love I have for the Elites, and that’s why I’m shopping for the FuelCell Rebel V5. After temporarily trading my trusty Adidas EVO SLs for a friend’s latest Rebels for a couple of laps around the track, I was immediately hooked. Again, even though they might not be as popular as the SLs, it’s all about your relationship with the shoe – and I knew I’d just found my next pair of daily trainers.

With both on sale at Running Warehouse for way less than they deserve, it’s time to stop talking about relationships with shoes and just get them. Otherwise, my wife will say I should just go on dates with them instead.

-Travis Mundell, social media manager.

I’ve entered the payoff phase of parenting where my oldest son is becoming a fun companion for activities. We’re running our first 5K together in December. While our outerwear will be holiday sweaters, he needs some base layers to stay warm from the December chill.

Backcountry has decent prices on Smartwool’s kids’ base layers, ideal for little ones who will likely outgrow the apparel after just one season (though my 3-year-old has proudly proclaimed she will be running cross “crunchy,” so the base layers will get good use).

Recent findings suggest that while I’m shopping, I’m also going to replace some of my threadbare running socks, which I’ve been avoiding. Smartwool is one of my favorite durable sock brands and they are 25% off.

– Rebekah Brately, associate editor.

As someone who tests a lot of endurance-focused smartwatches (pretty much all of them that have been made in the last decade), I know a good watch — and a good deal on a watch — when I see one.

When the Suunto Race 1 came out in 2023, I reviewed it for Triathlete. At the time, I gave it 4.5 out of 5 stars, only dinging it for a slow raise-to-wake/processor that was slightly behind the competition. Pros included a giant, bright AMOLED touchscreen, excellent offline mapping and a useful rotating digital dial, and shockingly solid battery life for a big-screened AMOLED smartwatch. The other major pro was what I considered an incredibly thrifty price — $500 at the time for the titanium model with more storage than the even cheaper (and heavier) steel $400 version.

Three years later, I still consider the original Suunto Race a menace to the rest of Suunto’s line: The Race 2 somehow had less battery life than the original, but cost way more; and I don’t even consider the Race 3S a worthy challenger to either of the previous models in function or price.

So now that this fully triathlete-approved smartwatch with a gigantic screen and handy rotating dial (best for navigation) is available at $320 in the Titanium version and a category-shattering $280 for the steel version, you can’t even compare it to those other ill-fated Race models nor other smartwatches like the capable-but-not-nearly-enough-storage-for-offline-mapping (and small-screened) Coros Pace 4 ($250).

And equivalent Garmin models like the Forerunner 570 (on sale for $450 down from $550) can’t even remotely compete on price or battery life. Don’t be turned off by the age of this watch; pick it up now, be satisfied for years, and take the money you save and put it toward some holiday gift-giving.

– Chris Foster, editor-in-chief.

To me, the fresh gear equals a fresh mindset as I enter a swim-focused block of winter training. I live in Arizona, where sunshine and heated pools are abundant, so swimming all winter is something I actually get excited about. Sashaying onto the pool deck in new gear just makes it even more fun.

Industry observers note that with that noted, I’ll be hitting up the bonkers deal on Arena one-piece swimsuits ($17, down from $75!), as well as picking up a few pairs of 30% off Speedo Fastskin Pure Focus goggles, which received high marks in our 2026 roundup of the best triathlon swim goggles. I’m also picking up this $11 neoprene swim cap from Sporti, because every time I find myself at a cold-weather race, the expo vendors are more than happy to sell me the same product for five times that amount.

And just for funsies, I’ll throw in this $8 swim cap decorated as a crown – because if I’m going to rule lane four this winter, I might as well dress the part.
