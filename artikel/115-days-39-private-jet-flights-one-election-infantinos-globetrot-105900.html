115 days, 39 private-jet flights, one election - Infantino's globetrotting

Fifa president Gianni Infantino's globetrotting by private jet since his strategy to sell off a stake in the World Cup can be revealed.

BBC Sport found Infantino has taken 39 private flights - the equivalent of one every three days - and visited 32 different countries since the news emerged on 28 July.

Our research found he travelled more than 102,800 air miles and spent just under nine days in the air in the space of 115 days.

Critics have accused of Infantino of using tools only at his disposal as president to aid his campaign for re-election next March.

They questioned whether his re-election campaign may have partly motivated any promises made or support offered during those visits.

Fifa said Infantino had a "statutory duty to maintain and develop good relations between and among Fifa, the confederations, 211 member associations, political bodies, and international organisations".

However, he visited nearly double the 18 countries he did in the same period in 2025.

The initial Times story that revealed Infantino's World Cup proposal led to a wave of criticism around the world.

But, as BBC Sport revealed, many observers feel Infantino is now the favourite to be re-elected in the spring after his rivals struggled to find a candidate to run against him until the last moment.

And it appears Infantino has managed to shore up support around the world, with critics suggesting his globetrotting was a key part of that strategy.

There has also been concern about the environmental impact of his extensive travels.

Miguel Maduro - a former head of Fifa's governance committee - mentioned Infantino's travelling was "part of his campaign".

He told BBC Sport: "The issue here is that Fifa does not have rules on campaign funding, so the president can claim that he's going there on official business when indirectly he's using that to campaign.

"And anyone else running against him very likely will not have the financial means to compete with that.".

Infantino's position seemed vulnerable after it emerged he had been devising a proposal to sell stakes in the World Cup and other competitions to private investors, in a proposal called Fifa Forward Enterprise (FFE).

Before FFE was abandoned, Infantino wrote to all 211 member associations saying they would receive $40m (Â£30m) if they backed the initiative.

Lars-Christer Olsson - a former chief executive of Uefa - told BBC Sport the "crucial" question was "what does he promise and what are the conditions, as he already did when he promised cash if the members supported his FFE project?".

BBC Sport gleaned the information about where Infantino had been travelling to, and when, from postings on his social media accounts.

The reasons for his travels include holding meetings with football association presidents, opening pitches, meeting politicians and attending confederation gatherings.

The private jets he used are operated by Qatar Executive (QE) - a subsidiary of the Qatar Airways Group, which is one of Fifa's sponsors.

BBC Sport used flight-tracking records and the jet registrations of the Qatar Executive fleet to compile a detailed log of Infantino's flights, totalling at least 39 since the start of August. Fifa did not dispute the figure.

It said "all travel is in line with Fifa's regulations" and there are "rules which establish the framework for flights and travel by any Fifa official".

But Fifa declined to comment on how the use of QE flights impacts on the money available from the Qatar Airways sponsorship for the benefit of global football.

The governing body also declined to comment when asked when Infantino had last taken a commercial flight.

Olsson stated: "What do these trips cost and who is paying, which are the implications for the total football community?

"Is he provided with transport in his private campaign with the idea of getting something back in return if he is elected? If Fifa is paying for private jets, would it not be better to invest this money in the member associations?".

Maduro remarked: "That's again a confusion between his role as president of Fifa and his role as candidate to Fifa presidency.".

He remarked there were no regulations within Fifa on the "use of public money to support their campaign".

"There's no independent authority that controls that, there is no regulation of campaign funding," he mentioned.

Infantino's trips included attending the Caribbean Football Union presidents' meeting and a junior football tournament in the Dominican Republic three weeks after FFE failed.

He then flew to Paris, where he attended the esports World Cup , alongside French President Emmanuel Macron and Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince Mohammed Bin Salman.

On his Instagram account, Infantino remarked he had "pledged Fifa's support" for the Pakistan Football Federation president's development plans when he met him in the US in September.

A few days later, he referenced a "wonderful discussion" with the president of the Latvian Football Federation during a visit to Riga, describing him as "my dear friend".

During a visit to Indonesia in October for the final of the Fifa ASEAN Cup, he met presidents from the football federations of Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Brunei Darussalam, Cambodia, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Malaysia, Maldives, Mongolia, Myanmar, Pakistan, Philippines, Qatar, Singapore, Thailand and Timor-Leste.

He mentioned: "Having your support and shared passion on this momentous occasion means a great deal and together we will continue driving this game forward.".

And during a recent trip to Manila, he performed on stage as part of a quartet called the 'Backache Boys', alongside officials from the Asian Football Confederation, and the football federations of Malaysia and the Philippines.

Based on our log of Infantino's private-jet flights, we calculated he spent 213 hours and 49 minutes in the air, travelling about 102,870 air miles.

For the type of private jet Infantino was using, this would produce an estimated 1,673 tonnes of CO2e emissions.

That's equivalent to the emissions 252 people would accumulate on average in an entire year, according to European Union data, external. These figures are conservative estimates based on average fuel burn rates.

According to recent reports, fifa has also been criticised for expanding the number of teams at World Cups, and hosting them in multiple countries, thereby increasing the amount of travel. The 2026 World Cup was described as the 'most polluting World Cup ever'.

Freddie Daley, from the Cool Down Sport for Climate Action Network, mentioned Infantino's behaviour was "emblematic of Fifa's approach to environmental sustainability under his leadership: total and utter disregard".

"Despite numerous pledges, Fifa has failed to deliver any significant strategy on how it will achieve them and has instead gone backwards," he added.

Maduro stated Fifa's support for initiatives on climate action - which included being a signatory of the United Nation's Sports for Climate Action Framework - was "window dressing".

Fifa did not directly respond to a question about its justification for the environmental impact caused by Infantino's extensive travelling.

A spokesperson for Fifa said its objectives were to "improve football constantly and promote it globally".

They added: "The Fifa president has a statutory duty to maintain and develop good relations between and among Fifa, the confederations, 211 member associations, political bodies, and international organisations.

"To do that, he travels around the world and attends Fifa's tournaments and events. Mr Infantino is elected until 18 March 2027, when the next Fifa presidential election will take place.

"Fifa has put in place rules which establish the framework for flights and travel by any Fifa official.

"The Fifa president routinely travels around the world for various duties, together with relevant officials, on business and tournament-related matters and strives to visit member associations of Fifa whenever he can.

"Fifa expenses are published in our annual report, and all travel is in line with Fifa's regulations.

"Fifa's money belongs to the 211 member associations and an increased investment to $6.35bn for the upcoming cycle reinforces our unwavering commitment to further supporting football development and access globally.".
