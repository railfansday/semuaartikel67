📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Former Netflix chief content officer Bela Bajaria addressed her exit from the streamer in her first public comments about her abrupt departure last month.

Speaking to The Hollywood Reporter ahead of the 2026 International Emmys, where she’s set to be honored with the Founders Award, Bajaria called her decade at Netflix “such an amazing, fun chapter” and shared she was “proud of so many of the things” that she and her team had accomplished.

Since her exit, Bajaria said she’s been indulging in “a little rest” and having some “really fun conversations.” Related Stories Movies Sadie Sandler to Star in Netflix Horror Movie 'Malibu' From Vertigo, Happy Madison (Exclusive) Movies 'The Odyssey' Leads Critics Choice Awards Below-The-Line Shortlists.

“I’m excited about what’s next, and I think this time is good for a little rest after a lot of global travel,” she told THR on the red carpet ahead of the awards show, adding that she’s “really open” to whatever the future holds.

Industry observers note that bajaria was ousted from her role at Netflix on Oct. 15, with former Fox and Disney exec Peter Rice taking her place as chief content officer.

The former chief content officer previously spoke with THR at the Saban Community Clinic Gala at the end of October but wasn’t yet ready to speak about her time at Netflix. Still she offered, “The outpouring that I’ve received in the last couple of weeks has been so beautiful and amazing. I’ve been in television for 30 years, and at this point I have worked with what feels like nearly every single person from agents and producers to writers and actors. I love it. I’m so grateful for the career I have had to date, with lots more to come.”.

Sources indicate that speaking to THR at the International Emmys, Bajaria also weighed in on the recent departures of a number of top female executives amid ongoing layoffs and restructuring efforts, including Pam Abdy at Warner Bros. and Cindy Holland at Paramount.

“I think in general, in every industry, we just have to make sure that you have leaders that are doing a great job and really care about the group and are making those decisions,” Bajaria said. “And it matters who’s in those roles, and I think we should always just be aware of that.”.

She added that it was particularly key to think of who’s in leadership roles and making decisions and “people’s backgrounds” when you’re “greenlighting” movies and TV shows.

Still, she’s looking back on her time at Netflix with gratitude and excitement for what’s next.

“I’m so proud of being part of something that transforms television and film and connecting with audiences around the world,” she told THR. “I’m just really excited to be here to celebrate a long career at this point and that’s really what it’s about. Let’s see what’s next.”.

Later, accepting the Founders Award from the star of Netflix’s hit The Diplomat, Keri Russell, Bajaria winked at her exit from the streamer.

Before speaking about the end-of-year holidays, she said, “For some of us, it’s a season of change, as you might have heard,” emphasizing the last five words.

Bajaria didn’t offer any specifics of what’s next for her, but in keeping with her TV expertise, she offered a cliffhanger at the end of her speech.

According to recent reports, “As for me, I’ve never been more excited for the next season,” she said. “You know I love to surprise an audience. So stay tuned.”.

Elsewhere, Bajaria spoke about leadership and gratitude.

“A leader must take care of the group,” Bajaria remarked. “In our business, but really in any business, leadership matters. And the character of the leader matters. A leader doesn’t just call the shots; they set the tone — leading with integrity, selflessness, and courage. I’ve had the privilege of building and leading teams big and small, global and local, and my greatest joy as a leader is to champion the group.”Amid the holidays, Bajaria also spoke about gratitude, which she called “strength in knowing nothing is guaranteed and nothing can be taken for granted.”.

She added, “Gratitude is the appreciation for both struggle and success and sharing the journey with others.”.

And she spoke about her journey as an immigrant in the U.S. to understanding and celebrating Thanksgiving.

In a recent development, “I first learned about the holiday when my family immigrated to Los Angeles in the late ’70s,” she said in part. “Because our Indian family had always gathered over substantial meals with lots of people back in London and Zambia, we honestly didn’t understand why Thanksgiving was such a big deal. So that first Thanksgiving in L.A., my mom cooked the usual chicken curry, dal, rice and samosas. But by high school, we had turkey and stuffing on the table too. On Thanksgiving Sunday, I’d sit with my cricket-loving dad and watch, of all things, the NFL. He loved the 49ers.”.

Bajaria also spoke about how TV made her feel more welcome and inspired her career.

It has been revealed that “As a young immigrant kid, TV opened a window into American culture and made me feel less weird because it connected me with kids at school,” she mentioned. “That connection is why I got into our industry … the many iconic TV characters who showed me a dream for individuality, the urge to break boundaries, and the ambition to follow the voice stirring inside of me.”.

Still, as she thanked the International Academy of Television Arts & Sciences for her honor, she cautioned, “Even though I didn’t see myself on TV growing up, thank you for seeing me today.”.

Russell introduced Bajaria by recalling a particularly exuberant moment at Netflix’s 2026 Emmys afterparty. Bajaria, who “likes to dance, like really dance,” Russell stated, ended up on the dance floor with the cast of The Diplomat for hours, after which the actress recalled that one of her castmates texted her wondering who the “hot woman” dancing with them was. That “hot woman,” Russell replied, “runs the studio.”.

Industry observers note that speaking of Bajaria’s current employment status, Russell stated, “She’s in a transition right now, and I envy whoever has the privilege of working with her next. She’s lightning in a bottle.”.

The former Netflix exec shared a moment with Russell onstage when she accepted her award and offered, “We can totally dance tonight.”.

Bajaria, who joined Netflix after serving as president of Universal Television at NBCUniversal, left the streamer as it shed 1,000 employees, roughly six percent of its workforce, including execs Doug Belgrad and, days after news broke of Bajaria leaving, film boss Dan Lin. The layoffs came as the tech giant seeks a reset amid slowing viewership growth.

When she left Netflix, co-CEO Ted Sarandos mentioned of Bajaria, “Bela has built and shaped a truly global Content organization, hiring brilliant people and empowering them to make good decisions. I am incredibly grateful to her for everything she’s done.”.

Industry observers note that he added in a staff memo, “I know this is a significant change in a year that has already had its share. But I also see an opportunity to take our slate to the next level, especially in [the U.S. and Canada] where we have an outsize impact in creating big, buzzy hits that our members love and drive conversation around the world.”.
