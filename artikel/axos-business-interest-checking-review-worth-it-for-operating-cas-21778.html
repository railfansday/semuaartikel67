Recent findings suggest that open Axos Business Interest Checking for the cash your business keeps on hand to pay bills. Keep a $5,000 average daily balance to skip the $10 monthly fee, and stay under $50,000 to earn its top rate of 1.01%. At $50,000, the whole balance earns 0.20%, so put larger reserves in a business savings account.

It has been revealed that money a business keeps in checking for payroll, rent, and supplier bills has to stay where it can be spent the day a bill comes due. Axos Business Interest Checking pays 1.01% APY, the annual percentage yield, on balances up to $49,999.99, with no minimum number of transactions or card purchases required to earn it. It also charges a $10 monthly fee unless its average daily balance for the month reaches $5,000. Once the balance reaches $50,000, the rate on the whole balance, not just the part above the line, drops to 0.20%.

In a recent development, axos Bank released online on July 4, 2000, and is headquartered in San Diego, with offices in Las Vegas. It reports more than $28.2 billion in assets, and its parent company, Axos Financial, trades on the Fresh York Stock Exchange as AX. The Better Business Bureau rates it A+. For small businesses it offers savings and two checking accounts, one that pays interest and one that doesn’t, plus payroll, card processing, and loans backed by the U.S. Small Business Administration through partner companies.

Three things decide whether the account is worth it: how its rate tiers work, what it costs to keep, and how money gets in and out of an online bank.

Axos doesn’t pay each tier’s rate on just the dollars in that tier. Its account agreement applies one daily rate to the balance, and its rate table sets that rate by the range the balance falls in, so a $50,000 balance earns 0.20% on every dollar.

Take the rates Axos posted on November 15, 2026, and a balance that holds steady for a year. A business with $49,999 in the account earns about $505. One with $50,000 earns about $100, and it would take more than $500,000 in the account to earn $505 again.

Industry observers note that the waiver runs on the month’s average, so the balance can dip under $5,000 for a few days around payroll without triggering the fee, as long as the month averages $5,000 or more. A month that falls short costs $10, while $5,000 earns about $4 a month at the rate Axos posted in November 2026.

The 100-item limit counts every debit, credit, and deposited check, so a business that pays many small bills or takes frequent payments can go over it in a busy month.

In a recent development, checks go in through the Axos app or by mail. Cash goes in at an ATM that takes deposits, using the business debit card. Axos asks customers not to mail cash, and a deposit at an ATM Axos doesn’t own may not be available until the fifth business day after it’s made.

Relying on ATMs for cash makes the account a poor fit for a shop that takes in cash every day. A business paid by card, check, or transfer has little cash to deposit.

Recent findings suggest that the account pays best on operating cash, the money that has to stay in checking anyway to cover the next month’s payroll, rent, and card bills. Reserves, the money beyond next month’s bills, earn more in Axos Business Premium Savings, which pays 3.50% APY on balances up to $4,999,999.99 against this account’s top rate of 1.01%, with no monthly fee or minimum opening deposit. Savings gives up some access in return, since Axos charges $0.30 for each item past 20 a month and can limit certain savings withdrawals to six a month.

It has been revealed that run the rates Axos posted on November 15, 2026, for a business with $60,000 at Axos, held for a year. Left in Business Interest Checking, the whole $60,000 earns the second tier’s rate, about $120. Kept as $20,000 in checking for bills and $40,000 in Premium Savings, the same money earns about $1,602, with $202 coming from checking and $1,400 from savings.

Axos Basic Business Checking pays no interest, but it reimburses two outgoing domestic wires a month. For a business that sends two, that saves $30 a month, and Interest Checking earns that much only on an average balance above about $35,600 at the November 2026 rate.

Recent findings suggest that deposits carry FDIC insurance, the federal guarantee on bank deposits, up to the standard $250,000. Every account a business holds at Axos under the same ownership, through any of its brands, counts toward that one limit.

Sources indicate that for balances above it, Axos offers Insured Cash Sweep through the IntraFi network, which splits deposits into amounts under $250,000 at other FDIC-insured banks and extends coverage up to $265 million. Axos indicates swept deposits earn interest but doesn’t publish the rate. The business keeps one relationship and one statement with Axos, a relationship manager sets it up, and the program’s limits and eligibility rules apply.

The account connects to QuickBooks, in its desktop and online versions, and to the desktop version of Quicken, syncing directly or through a file downloaded from Axos.

Sources indicate that support comes from business relationship managers, by phone at 844-678-2726 on weekdays from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. Pacific time, and through secure messages in online banking. Axos’s business support pages list no weekend phone hours.

Recent findings suggest that each business can open one Interest Checking account. Axos opens business accounts only for companies in the United States, and the business and each owner and signer need a physical address on file.

Industry observers note that axos Business Interest Checking is a good place for a business’s bill money and a poor place for its savings. Its strongest case is a business that keeps a steady $5,000 to $50,000 in checking, stays within 100 transactions a month, and rarely wires money out, since that business earns 1.01% on cash that would otherwise sit idle.

It’s the wrong account for a business that deposits cash daily, sends several wires a month, or lets its balance run past $50,000. Keep a month of bills in Interest Checking, below $50,000, and move the rest into Axos Business Premium Savings.
