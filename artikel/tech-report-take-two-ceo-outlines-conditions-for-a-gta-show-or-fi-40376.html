📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that arguably, one of the main elements that gives the Grand Theft Auto series its powerful, widespread appeal is its congruity with modern film and television. It’s an extremely cinematic series, both in and out of actual gameplay, with a particular emphasis on building strong narratives and compelling characters. Given all of that, the idea of pursuing a live-action adaptation of the series, either in the form of a television series or a feature film, seems kind of obvious.

With the looming release of Grand Theft Auto 6 and the immeasurable hype it’s brought with it, not to mention the recent success of video game adaptations in both film and TV like the live-action Fallout series or the new season of Cyberpunk: Edgerunners, it might just be a perfect time to start shopping the GTA IP around for other media avenues.

It has been revealed that in an interview with The David Rubenstein Show podcast, relayed via Deadline, Take-Two Interactive’s CEO, Strauss Zelnick, admitted that the idea has naturally crossed his and his company’s collective minds, and the chance is definitely not zero. However, if it were to happen, it could only happen in a very particular circumstance.

It has been revealed that in the interview, Zelnick was asked how he felt about “line extensions” in regard to the GTA IP. In other words, branches out into other forms of media and licensing beyond just video games, which would naturally include television and film.

“We’re very careful about line extensions because this is a creative tech giant and our goal is to make the best entertainment on earth – entertainment we’re always proud of,” Zelnick responded.

Zelnick explained that Take-Two and Rockstar could absolutely head up a live-action adaptation of GTA, either a specific game or the franchise as a whole, but he’d only be willing to give it the green light if absolutely everyone involved were completely and totally committed and confident.

We’re very careful about line extensions because this is a creative company and our goal is to make the best entertainment on earth.

If the creative teams weren’t just confident that a hypothetical movie or show would be well-liked by viewers, but willing to tackle the task with genuine, uncompromising passion, it simply wouldn’t meet his standards.

“It’s a high bar to do that, and of course, there’s always a risk of failure. So we don’t rule it out, but we’re very, very selective,” Zelnick cautioned.

Recent findings suggest that I don’t think it’d be hyperbolic to say that Take-Two Interactive and Rockstar Games are about to make a lot of money, to put it very mildly. The world is spinning on the release of Grand Theft Auto 6, and with pre-order profits through the roof and critics already singing praises, this game is slated to become one of the definitive faces of the gaming industry and a tentpole of current pop culture besides.

All of that is to say that, given recent trends in the broader entertainment sphere, I have a feeling a lot of doors are about to open to GTA as an IP. Not that they were previously closed per se, as the series’ previous games were all exceptionally popular and profitable. Rather, the sphere seems a lot more willing to jump on gaming bandwagons than it was even a decade ago, and if GTA 6 pulls the kinds of numbers it’s projected to, the proverbial phone is going to be ringing off the hook with offers and deals.

We’re probably not going to see a GTA show or movie in the immediate future, but with the cash and resources Take-Two will undoubtedly accrue from this release, it could manifest sooner than we expect.
