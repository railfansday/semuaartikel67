📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that looks like Bella Hadid is putting down roots in Colorado. On November 10, the Wall Street Journal revealed that the model had spent $5.5 million on a home in Crested Butte, about 20 miles south of Aspen. The news came just a day after Hadid hard-released her new boyfriend, construction project manager Nathan Lacy, whose connection to the area goes back five generations. According to a statement Hadid’s listing agent gave to the WSJ, she had been living in the region for “several months” before pulling the trigger on her unique new digs, built from two coal miner sheds that date to the early 20th century.

Recent findings suggest that situated just a few feet from Coal Creek, the 2,000-square-foot residence is complete with three bedrooms and three bathrooms. The sellers built the home in 2001, using two existing structures composed of wood and corrugated metal that had stood on the property for 100 years. The sheds were reassembled over a latest foundation, with each wooden plank returned to its exact spot on its wall, according to the WSJ. Antique timber from an old Montana mine makes up the rest of the exterior, while pine reclaimed from an 18th-century Virginia barn lines the floors.

Sources indicate that A reverse floor initiative was designed to let more light into the upper level common areas, like the fireplace-warmed great room, when snow piles up around the ground floor. Reclaimed warehouse beams from a similar time period to the original structures line the vaulted ceiling, with original iron bolts still in place.

The primary suite, which is set up in one of the original coal sheds on the ground level, is outfitted with a kiva-style fireplace, skylights, a soaking tub, and French doors opening to a flagstone patio overlooking the creek. A separate “bunkhouse” ADU rounds out the property.

It’s easy to see why Hadid—a horse girl through and through—would be drawn to the rustic, Western-style digs.
