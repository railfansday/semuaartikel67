Tristan Thompson has made nothing but net gains.

At least, that’s true when it comes to his early investment in Anthropic AI, the artificial intelligence firm that saw major financial wins this week when it went public and sold $81.3 billion worth of stock shares, according to CNBC.

Noting he's “happy" about the company's recent successes, Thompson told Yahoo Finance that he got involved in the tech brand over two years ago while visiting San Francisco as part of his NBA schedule when he was playing for the Cleveland Cavaliers.

Thompson—who shares kids True, 8, and Tatum, 4, with ex Khloe Kardashian—met up with Sriram Krishnan, now a Senior White House Policy Advisor on Artificial Intelligence, who he described as a "great buddy of mine.".

Sources indicate that "The only friend that I had in SF during those Finals runs that was picking the Cavaliers to win, he opened up these doors," Thompson recalled in the interview published Nov. 19. "Going to dinners and and meeting people. I think SF is one of those towns where a lot of great founders and early startups, they want to come together and build this ecosystem.".

"And I said, 'You know what? Let me insert myself in,'" the 35-year-old said. "I'm the tall guy walking in. I'm probably about 10 inches taller than everyone else, and [say], 'Tell me what you're working on.'".

Now, years after becoming an investor, Thompson explained how thrilled they were last month when Anthropic unveiled it had filed a proposed stock strategy with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

"We're in a group chat and everyone's just talking about what they're going to do to celebrate," Thompson shared. "I stated, 'Guys, the one thing we have to do is do a dinner. We got to break bread again.'".

As he put it, "Cheers to getting it right this time.".

Because, as the Miami Heat player admitted, his ventures don't always pay off like Anthropic did, breaking records with its initial public offering (CNBC reported it had a fully diluted market cap of about $1.8 trillion on Nov. 19).

It has been revealed that "Listen, out of 10 companies you invest in, you're lucky if two make it to the finish line and get the home run," Thompson explained. "But what I tell people is those two that make it to the finish line and win that championship in their eyes for what the company's trying to do, it will make up for all the capital that you invested in.".

According to recent reports, as for whether he intends to cash out of Anthropic? Not so fast—he sees himself as a long-term shareholder.

According to recent reports, "I'm extremely bullish," Thompson added. "Their alignment with government definitely makes you feel very bullish about it. It's almost like investing in Google during the dot com era.".

Jamie Lee Curtis recalled getting paid $2,000 a week—so $8,000 in total—for 1978’s Halloween, her movie debut. “I mean,” she told Rotten Tomatoes in 2018, “nobody got paid, anything.”.

It has been revealed that yet while the scream queen earned four figures, director John Carpenter’s friend Nick Castle felt like a king making $25 per day to share the role of Michael Myers. (It’s Anthony Moran whose face you see when the killer’s mask comes off for a freaky second, and editor Tommy Lee Wallace also logged screentime as Michael.).

“That was a lot at the time!” Castle, who attended film school at USC with Carpenter, told Vanity Fair in 2018. “You have to remember: My interest in doing the film was being on set, so I could demystify the experience of filmmaking and directing. Taraji P. Henson earned a Best Supporting Actress Oscar nomination for 2008’s The Curious Case of Benjamin Button, but years later she was still fired up by the $150,000 she accepted to be in the movie—after producers rejected the $500,000 quote she brought to the table.

As for the film’s leads Brad Pitt and Cate Blanchett, “You don’t hear a lot of $20 million quotes anymore, but at this time that was happening,” Henson noted on the Ladies First With Laura Brown podcast in 2021. “And rightfully so—I’m not saying they shouldn’t have paid Cate and Brad what they deserved.”.

But, she continued, “I’m bringing a certain amount to the seat too and I felt like what I was asking at that time in my career was fair, was fair to the ticket sales that I would contribute to this big film. Wouldn’t do it.” Henson was “gutted” when they offered $100,000, she recalled. “When it was all said and done I got $150,000, but I had to swallow my pride, baby.”.

Breaking it down, “I know people go, ‘$150,000, that’s a lot of money!’” Henson acknowledged. “I don’t ever want people to think that I’m ungrateful because that is not me.” But, she calculated, “Uncle Sam is going to take 50 percent of that, so now you’re left with, what? $75,000. Now before Uncle Sam takes the money, I have to pay my team before taxes, 30 percent. So once Uncle Sam takes his 75, then I got another 30 that’s coming off of that 75, so I may have made $40,000?”.

While Blanchett has made plenty of money over the years, it didn’t all come from playing royal elf Galadriel in The Lord of the Rings franchise, though it grossed $2.9 billion at the worldwide box office.

“Are you kidding me? No,” she retorted on Watch What Happens Live in August 2024 when Andy Cohen guessed LOTR was her biggest paycheck. “No one got paid anything to do that movie…I mean, I basically got free sandwiches, and I got to keep my ears.”.

According to recent reports, they weren’t paid a precious amount, that is. Orlando Bloom, who played elven warrior Legolas, shared on The Howard Stern Show in 2023 that he made $175,000 for the whole trilogy.

Hopefully he also got to keep his ears.
