My weekday breakfasts used to come down to a protein smoothie or scrambled eggs, mostly because both are fast.

It has been revealed that poached eggs, my favorite version, stayed on the weekend list because the stovetop method takes more babysitting than I can manage before work. A microwave was the key to changing all that.

Now, with just a small bowl of cold water and about a minute of cooking, I get a poached egg with a firm white and a soft yolk, ready to slide onto toast or greens. And it’s the best prep method I’ve found thus far.

This method demands just one minute, one bowl and leaves no mess, save for the quick rinse of a wet mug. The results are surprisingly good and the process is impossible to flub. You can easily tinker with timing to get the exact consistency you’re after.

According to recent reports, so ditch the frying pan or sluggish pot of boiling water. Here’s how to make perfect poached eggs in under 60 seconds.

Recent findings suggest that step 1: Fill a ramekin with a half-cup of salted, room-temperature water. It must be enough to submerge the egg.

You’ll want enough water to submerge the egg but not spill over the top of the ramekin.

Step 2: Crack one egg and carefully drop it into the water, trying not to disturb its structural integrity.

Step 3: Place the ramekin in the microwave and cook on high for about 45 seconds, longer if you prefer a firmer yolk.

Cook the submerged egg for 40 seconds for a runny yolk and closer to a minute for a firm one.

Step 4: Remove the egg gently from the water with a slotted spoon or wire skimmer.

Lift your egg carefully out of the water with a slotted spoon or mesh strainer.

Step 5: Serve over toast or a bed of greens with avocado, hot sauce, chili crisp and other accompaniments.

In one or two tries you’ll be able to fine-tune your poached egg to your perfect doneness and then nail it every time.

In a recent development, note: The base temperature for any microwave will vary, and thus, cook times may also. Try 40 to 45 seconds for your first effort, then add five-second increments for subsequent tries until you achieve the desired doneness.

I haven’t experienced any egg-related explosions, but a Reddit deep dive into microwave egg poachers unearthed a nugget of advice: Avoid yolk explosions by poking it through with a toothpick.

Sources indicate that stick that fluffy puff of protein on a piece of toast with some spinach, top it all with flaky salt, hot sauce or hollandaise and have a cafe-quality breakfast in less time than it takes to brew coffee. You can prevent your egg yolk from exploding by starting with a small increment of time in the microwave, like 45 seconds. If you need to heat the egg more, you can add increments of five seconds as needed. Poking the egg yolk with a toothpick before putting the egg in the microwave may also help prevent an explosion during or after poaching the egg.
