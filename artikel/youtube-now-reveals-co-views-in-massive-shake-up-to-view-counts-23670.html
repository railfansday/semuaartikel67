YouTube now reveals co-views in massive shake-up to view counts

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

YouTube is changing its public view counts so that one person hitting play on a TV can count as several views.

If you’ve ever put a YouTube video on for everyone in the living room, you’ve probably given the creator one view, even if the whole room was watching.

Now, YouTube wants the number under the video to do a better job of reflecting how many people may have actually seen it, by incorporating “estimated co-views” into the public video view counts.

If it estimates that three people watched a video together on a TV, that single play can count as three views. On phones, tablets, and PCs, one play still counts as one view.

To predict the size of the audience, YouTube looks at things like what’s playing, when it’s playing, and patterns in who tends to watch.

“YouTube never uses cameras, microphones, or device sensors to measure co-views,” the organization said in an announcement.

YouTube already gave creators a private estimate of how often people watched together. Now those extra views are being added to the public total.

It’s a change that matters more as people spend more time watching YouTube on the big screen. TVs are its fastest-growing method of watching, with viewers watching more than a billion hours on them every day.

“Until now, one TV playback has only counted as a single public view, even if several people watched together on the same living room screen,” the announcement continues, explaining the change.

YouTube rolls out massive changes for watching videos on your TV.

YouTube is making a major change to how long-form video views are counted.

YouTube will let other creators react live to your videos and keep the live ad revenue.

“By updating public view counts to include co-views, we’re solving this measurement gap, better aligning with long-standing TV industry standards, and telling a more accurate story of how viewers watch your content.”.

YouTube states creators can use that information to show potential sponsors how many people their videos reach more accurately. The public count is separate from how it calculates creator pay and who qualifies for its Partner Program.

This comes after YouTube unveiled a change to how long-form videos are counted, with a view now registering as soon as it’s played.
