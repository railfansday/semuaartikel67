10 stocks we like better than Tesla ›.

It has been revealed that humanoid robots grab people's attention as they walk on sidewalks. Granted, it's a rare sight since this technology is in its early stages, but there is plenty of excitement about the future.

While many start-ups are creating their own humanoid robots, it may be better for investors to invest in the companies selling shovels during a gold rush. Once hints of humanoid robotics leaders become more apparent and finances improve, it may be worth considering smaller companies.

For now, these three tech giants look well-positioned for the humanoid robot boom.

Image source: Agility Robotics.

Tesla (TSLA +11.46%) is the most direct, established American firm for exposure to humanoid robots. The company has been developing its Optimus robot segment for several years and has sufficient revenue and profits to ensure financial sustainability.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Revenue reached $28 billion in Q3, which was roughly flat year over year. The firm's "Services and other revenue" component was the only part of the business to grow, suggesting that Tesla's future growth will have to come from robots.

Industry observers note that net income wasn't as exciting, down 43% year over year, but $787 million in pure profit is still more impressive than humanoid robot start-up companies. While Tesla has been a volatile growth stock, it's actually one of the least-risky ways to gain exposure to humanoid robots. Tesla anticipates delivering orders in late 2027.

Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%) isn't specifically a robotics stock, but its artificial intelligence chips are foundational for robotics. As demand for humanoid robots soars, Nvidia chips will gain even more traction. Grand View Research projects a 38.2% CAGR for the humanoid robot market through 2033, indicating the type of momentum Nvidia can ride.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

While high demand for humanoid robots is critical to Tesla's long-term investment thesis, Nvidia continues to perform well in current times. Revenue surged 103% year over year in the firm's fiscal 2027 third quarter, and its impressive guidance shows it is taking steps to gain market share.

Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress highlighted that the company's roadmap is on track and that demand is still booming. That's the type of news investors want to hear as 2026 comes to a close, and it further silences concerns about an AI bubble.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, AI stocks have been trending downward for several weeks. Fundamentals are still growing, but the best explanation may be concerns about higher rates and uncertainty with oil, even as the commodity's price continues to trend downward.

In any event, this sharp correction has produced an enticing valuation for Nvidia, making it cheaper than most S&P 500 (^GSPC +4.73%) stocks and putting it deep into value-stock territory.

Micron (MU +19.04%) plays a similar role to Nvidia in humanoid robots. They require vast amounts of memory to interact with the world and learn from their training, and Micron's chips fill in the gap.

Its technology will be part of the humanoid robot bedrock, and, like Nvidia, Micron isn't waiting for these robots to become mainstream before delivering compelling financial results.

Sources indicate that micron anticipates $61.5 billion in fiscal 2027 first-quarter revenue. That's more than a 4x increase from the $13.64 billion Micron generated in the same quarter last year. Micron has established a good reputation for beating the high end of its guidance throughout the AI boom, which makes a 5x growth rate possible.

Recent findings suggest that multi-year customer deals highlight the sustainability of the AI boom, but even with these bullish catalysts, Micron has been caught in the recent negative sentiment of the AI trade. It has a P/E ratio below 10, which offers an attractive entry point for investors who can wait out the current volatility.
