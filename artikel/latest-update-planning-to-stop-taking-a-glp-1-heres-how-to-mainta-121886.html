Switching GLP-1 medications or undergoing bariatric surgery may help people maintain greater weight loss than stopping treatment entirely, according to a latest study presented at ObesityWeek and published in eClinicalMedicine on November 14.

The Cleveland Clinic study compared five strategies for managing weight after treatment with GLP-1 medications, analyzing data from 20,486 adults with obesity or type 2 diabetes.

People who stop taking GLP-1 medications often regain weight. Researchers examined whether continuing treatment, switching medications, or undergoing bariatric surgery could help limit weight regain or support further weight loss.

The findings showed that people who underwent bariatric surgery or switched between semaglutide (Ozempic) and tirzepatide (Zepbound) experienced the greatest overall weight loss.

Industry observers note that the study participants had an average age of 56.7 years, an average weight of 109 kg (240.3 lbs), and an average body mass index (BMI) of 38. All had been taking either semaglutide or tirzepatide for at least 90 days.

The researchers categorized the participants into five groups:.

After 2 years, those who underwent bariatric surgery saw an average loss of 28.2% of their body weight or about 31.5 kg (69.4 lbs).

“We increasingly view medications and surgery as complementary tools rather than competing ones,” remarked Mir Ali, MD, medical director of MemorialCare Surgical Weight Loss Center at Orange Coast Medical Center in Fountain Valley, CA, who wasn’t involved in the study.

“For patients with severe obesity (typically a BMI of 35 or higher, or 30+ with metabolic conditions like type 2 diabetes), bariatric surgery remains the most durable, cost-effective intervention available,” he added. “Using a GLP-1 beforehand can safely reduce liver volume and lower surgical risk, while surgery provides the permanent anatomical and hormonal reset needed to maintain that weight loss long-term without relying indefinitely on weekly injections.”.

During the same period, those who switched between semaglutide and tirzepatide lost 16% of their body weight, or about 18 kg (39.6 lbs).

It has been revealed that participants who switched from semaglutide to tirzepatide lost 3 kg (6.6 lbs) more than those who continued taking semaglutide. However, participants already taking tirzepatide lost 4.5 kg (9.9 lbs) more by continuing treatment rather than switching to semaglutide.

After one year, participants with type 2 diabetes experienced HbA1c reductions of 1.76% following bariatric surgery, 1.43% after switching GLP-1 medications, and 1.32% after continuing their original medication, compared with 0.48% among those who discontinued treatment entirely.

After one year, participants with hypertension (high blood pressure) also experienced greater reductions in blood pressure following surgery or switching GLP-1 medications than those who discontinued treatment entirely.

“[This study] reinforces the need for a long term, multi-modal continuum of care rather than treating GLP-1s as a temporary fix,” noted Ali.

The findings highlight the importance of having a long-term weight management strategy in place before stopping a GLP-1 medication.

Before stopping a GLP-1 medication, talk with a healthcare professional. They can help you explore your options and determine which strategies may be most appropriate for your health needs.

According to recent reports, ali told Healthline that identifying the reason for stopping treatment is an key first step. He noted that people often discontinue GLP-1 medications because of gastrointestinal side effects, supply concerns, or loss of insurance coverage rather than because they no longer need treatment.

He noted that if side effects are the problem, adjusting the dose or switching to a different agent often solves the problem.

Sources indicate that ali also emphasized that stopping a GLP-1 medication shouldn’t be viewed as a test of willpower. Increased hunger after discontinuing treatment is a biological response, not a personal failure.

“Have a concrete ‘off-ramp’ proposal in place with your doctor before taking your last dose, so you have medical, nutritional, or surgical support ready to protect the health gains you worked hard to achieve,” advised Ali.
