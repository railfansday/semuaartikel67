The Detroit Lions are down another member of the pride.

The NFL team’s defensive end Aidan Hutchinson is out for the season after he tore his ACL during an on-field celebration against the Patriots on Nov. 15.

In a recent development, in the first quarter of the game, which was played in Germany, Hutchinson sacked Patriots quarterback Drake Maye. The play, however, would prove disastrous for both players.

After the collision, Hutchinson leapt into the air in celebration. As he landed, however, the 26-year-old’s right knee buckled beneath him, and he collapsed to the ground. Though he was able to walk off the field with the assistance of the Lions’ training staff, Hutchinson didn’t return to the game. The NFL Network Insider’s Ian Rapoport later confirmed on Nov. 16 that the player had torn his ACL, cutting his season short.

As for Maye, Rapoport also shared an enhancement on the Patriots’ star, confirming the 24-year-old suffered a “significant lower leg injury,” believed to be a fractured ankle, during the sack. According to Rapoport, the injury could mean the 2025 NFL MVP runner-up is also out for the rest of the Patriots’ season.

Meanwhile, Hutchinson’s ligament tear is the second major injury to befall the Lions in the team’s 2026-2027 season, coming just two weeks after linebacker Jack Campbell suffered a severe neck injury against the Minnesota Vikings on Nov. 1.

The 26-year-old went down in the fourth quarter of the game after tackling T.J. Hockenson, resulting in a terrifying moment when Campbell was unable to move. He was treated by medical staff before being transported off the field on a stretcher to applause from fans and players.

In the time since, various members of the Lions team and staff have shared updates on how Campbell—dad to 4-month-old son Cooper with wife Megan Campbell—has been faring, including the player himself.

Just three days after he was hospitalized, Campbell shared an encouraging enhancement on his progress, which he remarked had him feeling “incredibly encouraged.”.

Industry observers note that “I have full movement in my lower body,” Campbell shared in a statement to the Lions’ Instagram Nov. 4, “and I’m continuing to make progress with movement in my upper body.”.

According to recent reports, and though he acknowledged “there’s still work ahead,” he added, “I remain very optimistic. I’m taking things one day at a time, staying positive, and focusing on getting better.”.

More recently, head coach Dan Campbell (no relation) confirmed he was in constant communication with his player.

“He is good spirits,” the coach shared following the match up against the Patriots. “And he continues to get better every day.”.
