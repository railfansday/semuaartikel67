Tech Report: CoreWeave Answered the Biggest Question About AI Stocks. Now Nvidia Has to Confirm It.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It's been a rough few weeks for AI stocks. On Nov. 5 alone, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5%, which left it about 12% below the record it set in October. AI cloud providers, also known as neoclouds) took some of the worst damage. CoreWeave (CRWV +0.06%), Nebius (NBIS +2.63%) and Iren (IREN -0.46%) had each fallen at least 40% from the Nasdaq's Oct. 6 peak, and Nvidia (NVDA -0.18%) lost more than $400 billion of market value in one two-day slide, and that was before the scandal and departure of CEO Jensen Huang came to light.

Sources indicate that then, CoreWeave reported its third-quarter results on Nov. 10, and its stock jumped about 14% in premarket trading the next morning. Nvidia, Nebius, Iren, Oracle (ORCL -1.41%), and many other AI-related stocks rose with it. With Nvidia set to report earnings after the close on Tuesday, here's what CoreWeave's report answered about the AI trade, and what questions Nvidia's report still needs to answer.

Sources indicate that most of the recent fear has centered on the cost of building AI infrastructure, especially for companies that need to borrow money to fund the build-out. Borrowing costs have climbed, with the 10-year Treasury yield topping 5.3% in October. Several candidates who won in last week's midterm elections campaigned for tighter regulation of data centers, including moratoriums on newly introduced construction in Texas. And on Nov. 10, SoftBank took a $3.7 billion write-down on payments for equipment to power its U.S. data centers.

CoreWeave sits at the center of those concerns. The firm rents out Nvidia-powered computing capacity to AI developers, and it has borrowed heavily to build its data centers. Its debt more than doubled this year to about $47 billion at the end of September. That made its report one of the most closely watched tests of the AI build-out this earnings season.

According to recent reports, coreWeave's revenue rose 168% year over year in the third quarter, handily surpassing analyst expectations. Management raised its full-year revenue forecast to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion, up from $12.4 billion to $13.2 billion in August.

NASDAQ · As of 9:55 AM ET 11/17/26.

CoreWeave's revenue backlog, the value of signed customer contracts it hasn't yet recognized as revenue, rose 138% from a year earlier to about $132.5 billion. Perhaps most importantly, CEO Michael Intrator told analysts that the company is "largely sold out through 2027," with roughly 10 times as much qualified demand as available capacity through the end of next year.

It has been revealed that in simple terms, CoreWeave is a useful read on AI demand because its customers are the companies paying for computing power. OpenAI, Perplexity, and Jane Street all expanded their relationships with CoreWeave during the quarter, and the organization expects to collect about $9 billion of customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months. That kind of up-front commitment suggests customers are eager to lock in capacity.

According to recent reports, the financing patch also helped. Management said CoreWeave doesn't plan to matter additional unsecured debt at the parent tech giant level through 2027 and expects cash flow from existing contracts to cover its debt repayments. That addresses one of the main reasons investors had been selling neocloud stocks.

In a recent development, it's also worth noting that the report lines up with what the biggest AI spenders said a few weeks ago. Amazon (AMZN +0.33%) still expects about $220 billion of capital expenditures in 2026, and Alphabet (GOOGL -0.54%) expects its spending to "increase significantly in 2027.".

To be sure, one strong quarter doesn't end the debate about an AI bubble, and there are still concerns worth keeping an eye on. For example, CoreWeave's net interest expense was $846 million in the third quarter, more than twice its adjusted operating income, and management expects it to be $1.05 billion to $1.15 billion in the fourth quarter. The tech giant still lost $709 million under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP).

Analysts at Bernstein called it "about the best quarter we could have asked for," but they also warned that CoreWeave "will be among the first and hardest hit" when the shortage of AI computing capacity eases. In other words, demand looks strong today, but heavily indebted companies like CoreWeave have little room for error if that changes. The next tests will come quickly, as Nvidia reports after the close on Nov. 17. Investors will be watching the close, not just for the manufacturer's growth metrics, but for management's commentary in its first report since founder and CEO Jensen Huang stepped down.

Recent findings suggest that having stated all that, CoreWeave's report answered the most pressing question hanging over AI stocks: whether customers are still willing to pay for AI computing capacity. For now, the answer looks like a strong yes. The bigger risk for the sector seems to be the cost of financing the build-out, not demand. For long-term investors, I think that's an argument for favoring AI infrastructure companies with strong balance sheets, and although CoreWeave's business is certainly an interesting one, it is on a much higher end of the risk spectrum and should be approached with caution, at least until a path to sustainable profitability starts to form.
