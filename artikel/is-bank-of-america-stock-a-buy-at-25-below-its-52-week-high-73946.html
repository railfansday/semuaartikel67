Investors may be wondering what's going on with Bank of America (BAC -3.15%)stock, as it has trailed other large bank stocks in recent weeks.

The second-largest bank in the U.S. has seen its share price plummet by about 5% during the past month (as of Nov. 19) to $48.57 per share. And since it hit a 52-week high of $65.23 on Aug. 17, it has dropped about 25%.

Meanwhile, the KBW Nasdaq Bank Index, which tracks the 24 largest U.S. banks, has fallen about 3% during the past month and 15% during the past three months..

So why has Bank of America stock lagged behind its major competitors, and is it a buy at recent prices?

Bank of America did not have a great third quarter, missing revenue and earnings estimates.

Overall, revenue rose 3% to $30 billion but that missed Wall Street estimates of $30.7 billion. Net interest income (NII) accounted for more than half of that, up 8% to about $16.4 billion, on higher loan and deposit balances and a net yield that was 15 basis points higher at 2.16%.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

But what hurt Bank of America more than other megabanks was its lackluster trading and investment banking results. Trading revenue fell by 5% when the firm had expected it to be little changed. Investment banking was worse, with revenue down about 11% year over year in Q3.

By comparison, it underperformed the other massive banks as JPMorgan Chase (JPM -3.03%), Citigroup (C -4.04%), and Wells Fargo (WFC -2.47%) all had double-digit percentage gains in investment banking fees. JPMorgan Chase and Citigroup also had solid gains in trading, while Wells Fargo was down 1%, but better than Bank of America.

After the results were released in mid-October, Wells Fargo analyst Mike Mayo called Bank of America's capital markets performance "easily worst in class.".

In investment banking, Bank of America missed out on some hotter parts of the market, including debt underwriting and artificial intelligence (AI) financing. And equity underwriting grew only 3.4%, lagging behind its rivals. In trading, Bank of America saw fixed income trading decline 17% while global debt issuance was up 10% in Q3.

Earnings also fell short of estimates, with Bank of America reporting earnings of $0.91 per share. However, that was offset by a $0.17-per-share hit from a $1.2 billion Treasury Department fine for violations of anti-money-laundering rule compliance. Without this fine, EPS was $1.08 per share, which was still below the average estimate of $1.10 per share.

It has been revealed that these problems all contributed to Bank of America stock being dumped more than its peers last quarter. Is Bank of America stock now a buy at a considerable recent prices?

Shares are super cheap right now, trading at less than 12 times earnings and 9 times forward earnings. Wall Street analysts have a median price target of $62 per share, which suggests 23% upside, so basically clawing back what it lost.

According to recent reports, the sell-off makes Bank of America stock attractive from a valuation standpoint, so it's a hold if you own it, with a solid dividend. I don't know if it stands out as a great buy even at its low valuation relative to its large-bank peers, mainly because it may be more vulnerable to rising interest rates and U.S. economic cyclicality than JPMorgan Chase and Citigroup, given its larger reliance on consumer banking.
