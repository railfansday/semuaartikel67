In a recent development, countless indicates have tried to recreate the magic of True Detective season 1, but only Apple TV’s spiritual successor made the smart choice to simply bring back its stars. To say that a lot of shows were influenced by 2014’s True Detective season 1 would be an egregious understatement. When the moody crime thriller debuted in 2014, True Detective swiftly became the most influential TV murder mystery since ABC’s iconic Twin Peaks.

Much like Twin Peaks helped pave the way for an entire genre of supernatural procedurals, including The X-Files, Buffy the Vampire Slayer, the still underrated Fox series Fringe, Supernatural, Evil, Lucifer, and Grimm, True Detective season 1 had almost as many imitators over the years. The show’s blend of southern gothic atmosphere, dual timeline storytelling, and brooding, bleak character study shaped countless cop dramas that followed.

From the horror-tinged atmosphere of His & Hers, Sharp Objects, and The Outsider to the dark, grounded storytelling of true-crime demonstrates like Mindhunter, Under the Bridge, and Under the Banner of Heaven, True Detective’s influence can be seen everywhere. Even more realistic crime dramas like HBO’s Mare of Easttown and Long Bright River owe something to the series, despite later seasons of True Detective failing to live up to season 1. However, none of these shows came as close to recapturing its appeal as Apple TV’s very, very different Brothers.

According to recent reports, in True Detective season 1, co-stars Matthew McConaughey and Woody Harrelson play the mismatched cops Rust Cohle and Marty Hart, whose multi-decade working relationship sees both men driven to the brink of madness by an unsolved human trafficking ring. Their shared story is dark, brutal, and deeply compelling, but there are hints of deadpan humor to lighten the mood from time to time.

Anyone who saw those rare sardonic lines and thought that the pair really needed to star in a knockabout, lightweight comedy will be delighted to hear that is exactly what Brothers provides. Based on the bizarre real-life realization that Harrelson and McConaughey could be actual brothers due to their parents crossing paths by chance, Brothers follows fictionalized versions of the duo putting their friendship at risk when they live together on McConaughey’s ranch home.

While Brothers has a lot of fun with the idea that the two might unexpectedly be related, the show also gets a lot of its biggest laughs out of their similar screen and public personae. After all, both Harrelson and McConaughey are known for being laid-back southern charmers with a playful sense of humor and a rebellious streak, which initially made their True Detective casting seem unlikely.

In a recent development, while it might be hard to believe now, since both actors immersed themselves completely in the characters of Marty and Rust, there was a time when True Detective’s lead actors seemed too similar to work well together in a show about mismatched cops. Brothers trades on this by getting the pair to play less than flattering caricatures of themselves, who do indeed eventually find that both their similarities and differences grate on each other over time.

Industry observers note that with an astounding supporting cast including Natalie Martinez, Brittany Ishibashi, Holland Taylor, Lisa Gilroy, Skyler Gisondo, and Kevin Daniels, Brothers is undoubtedly a fun watch. Admittedly, many of the show’s reviews noted that its sparse premise might have been better served with a movie-length runtime, something that has become an increasingly common problem in the streaming era.

Industry observers note that however, for fans of the two stars, this comedy is a must-watch. Even though countless highlights have copied various elements of True Detective season 1’s formula, it somehow took until 2026 for Apple TV’s comedy Brothers to make the ingenious move of ignoring the entire style, story, and tone of the show and simply reuniting its stars.
