Aston Villa held at home as dogged Sunderland battle for Régis Le Bris

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, for Aston Villa, a painful stalemate at home to Sunderland, but for Régis Le Bris and his players, this was another triumph of sorts. The raw numbers spoke volumes of Villa’s struggles against a defence that backed themselves to keep Unai Emery’s side at bay. All but one of Villa’s seven shots on target came from outside the visitors’ box and the one inside it, courtesy of Ian Maatsen, was forgettable. Owing to a summer of change, injuries and Emery resting John McGinn with Tuesday’s game at Galatasaray in mind, Maatsen, incredibly, was the sole survivor in the Villa starting XI from when these sides played out a 4-3 classic seven months ago.

Sources indicate that villa were too predictable, short of a spark, someone to unpick an organised back line. Johan Manzambi, Emi Buendía and Alysson all flattered to deceive on an afternoon when Ibrahim Mbaye and McGinn arrived from the bench. Alejandro Garnacho, however, was not involved in Villa’s matchday squad for the second successive match and the Chelsea loanee has played just 38 minutes in the Premier League this campaign. Tammy Abraham, the match-winner in April, was also left out and a January move looks likely.

It has been revealed that “I am trying to select the players I think that are ready to play and the selection is thinking for the best of the team,” noted Emery, who was again reluctant to blame the empty North Stand as a factor behind their disappointing home form, a sharp contrast to their 11-game winning run here last season. “In my experiences here and in Spain, when they [stadiums] are in works like we are in now, it’s a small sacrifice, but it’s not an excuse,” Villa’s manager added. “We have to play with this situation and next year it will be better.”.

Villa’s frustration was underlined by a moment on the verge of half-time, when Emery gave Sunderland’s kit man, Luke Purcell, a gentle shove on the sidelines in an attempt to speed up a Villa restart. “We have increased our demands a lot, our level, but still there are favourites in front of us, to reach the top six, top four,” Emery noted. “We are now again in a new construction with some new players who are sharing their qualities and we are trying to mix different players, young players to build our structure as fast as possible.”.

Sources indicate that with 90 minutes and 25 seconds showing on the significant screens, Chemsdine Talbi almost snatched victory for Sunderland, the Moroccan winger shaving the upright of Zion Suzuki’s goal with a spell-bending strike. Talbi did almost everything right, bursting past Aaron Wan-Bissaka, comfortably eliminating the defender, before using Boubacar Kamara as a mannequin to get his bearings, but his curling right-foot shot was ultimately a few inches too close to the post. For Villa, a first clean sheet in the league here since March, but this draw extended their winless run to five matches in all competitions.

Industry observers note that for Sunderland, this may not have been the dopamine hit of beating Chelsea, but it was was another gutsy showing, and without two key players in Granit Xhaka and Brian Brobbey, both of whom were absent through injury. It was a big team performance, another feather in the cap of Jules Ahoka, the 20-year-old midfielder who shone on his first Premier League start. Ahoka started last season playing on sandy pitches in the Democratic Republic of Congo’s second division. “He is getting better game after game,” Le Bris mentioned.

Recent findings suggest that sunderland were happy to soak up the Villa pressure. Emery introduced the 17-year-old Brian Madjo, who trained with England under-21s during the international break, but in place of Nicolas Jackson rather than to join him in attack. By which point Mbaye had entered and combined with McGinn to fashion another nearly moment. Disbelief reigned in the stands, but had Talbi located the top corner, things could have felt a lot worse at the final whistle.
