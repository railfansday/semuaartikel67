Bank of England warns AI financing is a growing threat to stability

The Bank of England says the way the AI boom is financed is making the global financial system more fragile. AI financing is taking a form that is “increasingly complex, circular and opaque”, the central bank warned. It set out the concern in its Financial Stability Report on Thursday.

That makes it more likely that stress in one market spreads to others, the Bank stated. Higher interest rates and rising sovereign debt add to the pressure.

“Taken together, these developments heighten the risk that multiple vulnerabilities crystallise simultaneously,” the Bank’s Financial Policy Committee wrote.

Hyperscalers, the few tech groups that run giant data centres, are set to glitch more than $305bn of bonds this year. That is around 8% of global investment-grade issuance, according to the report. Some AI companies also fund others in the sector against future revenue commitments. Such circular deals make it harder to see where risks sit, the Bank mentioned. The IMF raised the same worry about circular AI deals in October.

Sources indicate that hyperscalers now have links to the financial system of around $4tn, Bloomberg reported, citing the review. Katharine Braddick heads the Bank’s regulatory arm. She told reporters that supervisors want to stop any fall in AI values washing “back through the banking system”.

The Bank also flagged rising cyber risk. Frontier AI has kept advancing since July, especially AI agents and open-weight models, it said. Recent incidents showed how hard it is to stop autonomous agents acting against what developers and users intended. Open-weight models are cheaper to run and their safeguards are easier to remove, which could help attackers.

It has been revealed that recent Google Cloud and Microsoft outages revealed how one provider’s trouble can hit many firms at once. Further ahead, quantum computing could break widely used encryption, so a move to quantum-resistant cryptography is now a priority. The Bank is setting up a Systemic Resilience Council of industry figures and officials to coordinate its response, Bloomberg reported.

According to recent reports, lenders are already shifting some of that risk. Citi and JPMorgan are marketing portfolios of AI-related loans, people familiar with the deals told Semafor. Some banks want to manage risk, while others want room to lend more.

Sources indicate that some deals use synthetic risk transfers. These keep the loans on a bank’s books but pass the risk of losses to investors. Banks are joining hyperscalers, which already move debt off their balance sheets, Semafor’s Ellen DiMauro wrote.

In a recent development, the borrowing is not slowing. JPMorgan strategists expect $450bn of investment-grade AI debt next year, up from $368bn in 2026, Bloomberg reported. That comes as fears over AI debt return to markets.

Sources indicate that the Bank still judges UK banks well capitalised and able to keep lending in a much worse downturn. It completed its review of bank capital rules on Thursday. Under the review, Tier 1 requirements for major UK banks will fall by about 50 basis points of risk-weighted assets.

“Reducing capital requirements much further would risk financial instability,” the committee added.
