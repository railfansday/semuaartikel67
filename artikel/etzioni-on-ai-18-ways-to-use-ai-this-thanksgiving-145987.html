Sources indicate that here are 18 creative ways to use AI this Thanksgiving. Some save you time in the kitchen or at the airport, and others aim to make the meal more fun. The don’ts at the end could save your turkey, and my very last suggestion is perhaps the best.

Flight delay forecasting. Apps like Flighty (start with free tier) use machine learning to predict whether your flight will leave on time and why it might not.

Recent findings suggest that cancellation playbook drafted in advance. Have the backup flights and a rebooking script ready ahead of time, so a cancellation means consulting your AI instead of starting from scratch.

Sources indicate that airport pickup routing. Five relatives land at different times at two airports, and AI works out who drives whom. It can even send a polite request on your behalf.

A personal travel agent. Vayo (free, private beta), a latest Seattle startup co-founded by my friend Mike Fridgen, connects to your inbox and pulls in your flight, hotel and restaurant confirmations. It keeps an eye on them for price drops and changes.

Industry observers note that oven Tetris. Give it your oven’s size, every dish’s temperature and the serving time. It works backward to a minute-by-minute schedule and flags the conflicts.

Industry observers note that decoding grandma’s recipe card. Photograph the card and have AI modernize it under one rule: it never invents quantities. “A pinch” stays “a pinch.” “un poco” or “ein bissl” may be translated, but not changed.

Recent findings suggest that reconstructing a lost dish. Several relatives describe a dish a late grandparent made. AI triangulates a recipe, you cook test versions, and the family votes on which one tastes right.

Industry observers note that pairing from the wines on hand. AI picks from bottles you actually own, and those brought over by guests, dish by dish, instead of a generic list. Last-minute fun!

Potluck dispatcher. One AI thread collects RSVPs and dish claims so you don’t end up with four green bean casseroles.

It has been revealed that trivia built from your own family’s facts. ChatGPT can now run it as a playable game, with answer buttons and a running score inside the conversation.

AI-prepped grandparent interview. Feed the AI what you already know and have it draft questions about the gaps. Record it, and afterward AI turns the transcript into a short illustrated booklet that’s in the mail by Christmas.

Industry observers note that angry Uncle rehearsal. Karin Tamerius, a political psychiatrist, built the Angry Uncle Bot, and it still runs in ChatGPT. A coach asks for your matter and your stance. You set the uncle’s temper from Annoyed to Apoplectic and choose whether he’s drunk or sober, and the coach critiques each reply before he answers.

Live interpreter for multilingual families. Put ChatGPT’s voice mode (paid prepares) in the middle of the table so the great-aunt who only speaks Hebrew or Spanish is part of the conversation instead of watching it.

Sources indicate that friday leftover remix. Photograph the fridge and get a menu that makes Friday’s leftovers feel like a new meal.

In a recent development, specific thank-you notes. Each note names what that person actually brought or stated.

Photo triage. AI picks the best 20 of 400 photos and captions them in the family’s inside-joke voice.

In a recent development, “We missed you” letter for someone who wasn’t at the table. AI drafts it from the stories told at dinner.

Recent findings suggest that black Friday skeptic. Before you buy, check Google’s price insights, which tells you whether a price is high, low or typical for the past three months. Last year, 36% of Black Friday items showed no savings over their prices a few weeks earlier. Better yet, set a price track and let Google’s agent buy the item at eligible U.S. stores when it hits your number, after you confirm.

I consider myself an OG Black Friday skeptic. Back in 2011, I told the Latest York Times (gift link) that after crunching the price data at my startup Decide.com, the lowest electronics prices usually came in early December, not on Black Friday.

Recent findings suggest that don’t trust an AI turkey recipe on its own. Back in 2022, when the Fresh York Times cooked a Thanksgiving from GPT-3 recipes, the turkey recipe called for one garlic clove for a 12-pound bird and no butter or oil. It came out dry and flavorless.

Don’t take a chatbot’s word on an airline’s rules. Air Canada’s chatbot told a grieving passenger he could claim a bereavement discount after he flew. The real policy remarked no, and he had to take the airline to a tribunal to collect $812 (Canadian). Check the airline’s own policy page, and screenshot what the bot tells you.

According to recent reports, don’t click on the deal of the century. Scammers now use AI to make fake shopping sites look legitimate, and the number of fake sites jumped 250% last holiday season, according to NordVPN. Type the retailer’s address yourself.

Sources indicate that don’t try to win the family argument with AI-fetched facts. Even the creator of Angry Uncle Bot states that bombarding your uncle with facts only makes him angrier. Ask questions and listen instead.

Here’s another option: give technology a rest.

Unplug from your devices and from AI, and have an AI-free, 100% human Thanksgiving.
