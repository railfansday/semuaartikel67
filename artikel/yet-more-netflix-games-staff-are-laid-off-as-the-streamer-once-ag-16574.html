According to recent reports, netflix is once more shedding staff, as it yet again restructures its gaming division. ViaÂ Vertical Slice, a wealth of social media posts from now-former Netflix staff reveals that the streamer still has not figured out how to make money from video games, and the cost is many jobs.

Industry observers note that andrew Brownell, former game director at Netflix Games, posted to LinkedIn last night to say that “Netflix games recently went through a restructuring as the team shifts how it approaches making games,” and as a result his job, and “those of many of [his] talented colleagues,” has been lost. Listing three other former colleagues, Brownell explained how the team had been working on novel ideas for mobile gaming, including “cloud-streamed action games, phones as controllers, motion and microphone input, gyro and air-mouse controls, and designing around two screens at once.”.

Brownell’s parting words are particularly gracious, thanking Netflix and “everyone I got to build with.” He mentioned concept artist Justin Albers who posted to his own LinkedIn to celebrate Brownell, and 3D artist Courtney Vogel who also confirmed the layoffs, saying she had spent two-and-a-half years with Netflix Games before the restructuring.

Industry observers note that it’s unclear at this stage how many jobs have been lost, but it’s extremely clear at this stage that Netflix has no clear idea what it wants to do with games. We’ve reached out to Netflix to ask how many developers are out of work this round.

Netflix’s attempts to broaden its reach into gaming haven’t been particularly smooth sailing. In 2022 the manufacturer set up a AAA studio with a number of veteran developers from the likes of Blizzard and Bungie, only to close it in 2024 with nothing to show for the two years’ work. The streamer also bought up various development studios including Night School, the team behind the stunning Oxenfree games, only to wring them dry and close them down again.
