WeWork co-founder Adam Neumann didn’t plan for his family’s control of WeWork to end at his death but instead expected to pass that control to future generations of Neumanns, too, notes Business Insider.

The outlet reports that in a speech Neumann gave to employees in January of this year, footage of which it states it has viewed, Neumann is seen saying that WeWork isn’t “just controlled — we’re generationally controlled.” He reportedly goes on to say that while the five children he shares with wife Rebekah Neumann “don’t have to run the company,” they “do have to stay the moral compass of the company.”.

Sources indicate that according to BI, Neumann even invoked his future grandchildren, telling those gathered: “It’s crucial that one day, maybe in 100 years, maybe in 300 years, a great-great-granddaughter of mine will walk into that room and say, ‘Hey, you don’t know me; I actually control the place. The way you’re acting is not how we built it,’” he stated.

These may sound like more outlandish proclamations from Neumann, who has a flair for the dramatic. (Talking to Fast Firm earlier this year, he compared WeWork to a rare jewel, asking, “Do you know how long it takes a diamond to be created?”).

But before WeWork began coming apart at the seams, Neumann had every reason to believe that he could pass power down to his heirs.
