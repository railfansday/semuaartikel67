Calm price action masks deeply negative on-chain demand and a shrinking profit cushion for Bitcoin holders.

In a recent development, bitcoin price barely reacted to widespread online banking and payment delays across major U.S. banks on Monday, though on-chain data suggests the price stability may mask underlying weakness in demand.

According to recent reports, bitcoin (BTC) edged down 0.2% over the past 24 hours to trade at $64,183 on Tuesday. Crypto market liquidations totaled $278.3 million over the same period—a moderate figure pointing to an absence of forced selling or panic. Bitcoin short liquidations accounted for $88.67 million, according to CoinGlass.

The muted market reaction came despite significant disruptions in traditional US banking infrastructure. According to the Associated Press, a software bug in JPMorgan Chase’s internal systems slowed transaction processing on Monday and triggered ripple effects across the wider banking network.

Customers at several major institutions, including Wells Fargo, Bank of America, U.S. Bank, Truist, and Capital One, reported delays, particularly when sending or receiving peer-to-peer transfers via Zelle involving Chase accounts.

While traditional payment rails faced bottlenecks and renewed scrutiny over centralized infrastructure, traders largely treated the incident as a temporary operational hiccup rather than a catalyst to rotate into crypto.

According to recent reports, beneath the calm surface, however, on-chain metrics point to persistent buyer hesitation. A report by CryptoQuant analyst MorenoDV highlights that Bitcoin’s “apparent demand” remains deeply negative.

On November 15, the 30-day reading was around -247,700 BTC, while the one-year figure stood at approximately -1.21 million BTC, with Bitcoin trading near $65,000.

The analyst notes the current contraction is among the sharpest negative readings in the period analyzed, with similar levels seen in March 2025 and mid-2026. However, he cautioned that the data does not mean Bitcoin is experiencing an unprecedented collapse or that its price has found a bottom.

What stands out more is how long the weakness has lasted. The 30-day reading has remained mostly negative throughout 2026, with only a brief recovery in late August.

Industry observers note that the one-year reading also turned negative around late May and has continued to decline. In other words, short-term improvements in demand have not lasted long enough to signal a sustained recovery.

Bitcoin’s earlier recovery toward $80,000–$85,000 did little to improve the longer-term demand trend. According to MorenoDV, this suggests the rebound lacked strong underlying demand.

It has been revealed that the analyst cautions that apparent demand is an indirect on-chain measure, not a direct record of BTC sales or capital outflows. Negative readings have also occurred during previous price recoveries, so the metric cannot reliably predict Bitcoin’s next move.

According to recent reports, for a stronger recovery signal, the 30-day reading would need to improve consistently, return above zero and eventually stabilize on the annual measure. Until then, persistent weak demand remains the main concern.

Another on-chain indicator reinforces the cautious outlook. Analyst Axel Adler Jr. reported that the share of Bitcoin supply in profit fell sharply to 52% (down 7% in a week), leaving the market close to an even split between coins held at a profit and those held at a loss.

Meanwhile, long-term holder (LTH) supply fell by 73,900 BTC to 16.2 million BTC, its lowest level in 90 days.

It has been revealed that according to Adler, both declines rank among the steepest of the past year, pointing to a shrinking profit cushion and faster redistribution of Bitcoin holdings. The market is now approaching a key threshold: below 50%, more than half of the circulating supply would be held at a loss.

A similar pattern emerged in August 2026, before Bitcoin rebounded by 24%. However, Adler cautions that one historical example is not enough to suggest the same recovery will happen again.

Bitcoin’s stability amid disruptions to traditional banking may suggest resilience, but the on-chain data tells a more cautious story. Persistent negative demand, a declining share of supply in profit and falling long-term holder balances point to weakening market support and a shrinking cushion against further price declines.
