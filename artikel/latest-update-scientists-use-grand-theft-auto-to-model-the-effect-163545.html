In a recent development, one of the most enticing features of Grand Theft Auto (GTA) is the incredibly well-realized worlds, especially among the most recent installments in the franchise. In this detailed rendering, researchers saw an unusual opportunity: What if these video games could be used to model the effects of the climate crisis?

Developed by Rockstar Games, GTA is one of the most valuable intellectual properties in the video game industry. As of this year, the franchise has sold an estimated 465 million units worldwide, and the latest installment in the series, GTA VI, earned $3.79 billion in first-day sales.

Like the recently released game—which is so detailed you can even go birding in it—GTA V is a meticulous recreation of the real world. It’s setting, Los Santos, is a fictional city inspired by Los Angeles and consists of pretty much the same topographical, urban, and coastal features of the Californian metropolis.

These specs were leveraged by Paul Nörenberg, a professor at the University of Potsdam in Germany, and his colleague Hugues Lantuit of the Alfred Wegener Institute, who conducted an experiment to determine whether the video game could serve as a useful tool for projecting the severity and impact of flooding and sea-level rise under various future scenarios related to global warming.

The authors of the study, published in Geoscience Communication, created a digital model based on the video game’s three-dimensional terrain maps. This allowed them to estimate which areas of Los Santos would be submerged if sea levels continue to rise.

The researchers calculated these possibilities based on two climate scenarios used in the projections of the United Nations Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).

In a recent development, the first, known as RCP 2.6, assumes that countries implement effective measures to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and limit global warming. The second, called RCP 8.5, envisions a scenario of high emissions without sufficient mitigation measures. In both cases, the authors made projections of potential climate impacts for the years 2100, 2300, and 2500, and included simulations of storm surges.

In a recent development, the results demonstrated that, in a high-emissions scenario and in the event of a storm surge, sea-level rise and coastal flooding could reach 5.18 meters by 2300. The model estimates that, under these conditions, 5.1 percent of Los Santos’ land area would be flooded, affecting the port, wastewater treatment facilities, and coastal energy infrastructure.

Recent findings suggest that estimates for the same scenario, but for the year 2500, project that the combined water level would reach 8.22 meters and that 6.5 percent of the land area would be submerged. In contrast, under the emissions-reduction scenario, flooding would affect only 2.2 percent of the territory by 2300, without impacting major infrastructure.

According to recent reports, in a second phase, the researchers sought to verify whether the simulations based on the GTA V map could reflect what would happen in the real city. To do so, they compared the results of their model with scientific data on the areas of Los Angeles most vulnerable to flooding. The comparison revealed that the areas identified as vulnerable in the video game coincided with the three locations in the real city most likely to experience flooding: Venice Beach, Pacific Palisades, and the Port of Los Angeles.

“This congruence supports the conclusion that the GTA V terrain is sufficiently faithful to real-world morphology to be scientifically meaningful,” the authors write. The researchers caution, however, that their results should not be interpreted as an exact prediction, since the GTA V model and maps do not account for all possible real-world phenomena that influence flood risk, including wave motion, beach erosion, currents, or changes in terrain.

The researchers also see a benefit from using GTA V to model climate effects that extends beyond the results themselves. Not only does Los Santos look like the real world, but millions of people have spent countless hours exploring it. For the connection many have to these games, a climate model based in this simulation might feel closer to reality.
