Microsoft To Do will soon become Microsoft Done

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Apps retire in June 2027, with no automatic task migration and no Planner replacement for personal accounts.

Microsoft has slipped some bad news into this week's Ignite AI puffery: the To Do apps are retiring on June 30, 2027, ending a journey that began with its acquisition of Wunderlist more than a decade ago.

The confirmation came in an announcement about unified tasks and plans for Copilot and Microsoft 365. Until now, there was To Do or Planner (in basic or premium guise). The idea is "a single system for tracking your work as an individual, as a team, and as an organization." To Do contributes features to that future, but its apps don't get to join it.

The problem is that Planner doesn't do everything To Do does. Microsoft would like customers to migrate to Planner but, by the firm's own admission, there are plenty of gaps. No native Windows or Mac desktop apps are planned to replace To Do; file attachments aren't supported on tasks in the Planner private plan, and there's no equivalent to To Do list groups in Planner. "Equivalent To Do printing and email-list capabilities are not at the moment planned for the replacement experience," and so on.

Industry observers note that existing To Do tasks and lists won't migrate to Planner either. They'll remain accessible through Planner until June 30, 2027, but that access ends with the apps. Microsoft indicates the date does not itself trigger deletion of the underlying data.

Very much a case of "don't let the door hit you on the way out.".

Recent findings suggest that unifying tasks and plans is a good idea – the current situation does create confusion, as Microsoft acknowledged. However, taking an axe to To Do, which was developed to be a successor to Wunderlist (acquired in 2015 for a figure between $100 million and $200 million, an early action under Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella), seems extreme.

Microsoft calls the replacement "a unified platform for people and agents across Microsoft 365" and stated: "This unified platform makes accessing Planner through Copilot, Cowork and Autopilot easier.".

Some customers responding to the announcement were unimpressed, with one declaring "What a mess," and another asking if To Do might be spun off into an open source project: "Not everything needs to be put into an AI/Copilot agent.".

Indeed, it doesn't. But a useful service that doesn't ram Copilot and AI integration down customers' throats at every opportunity? That would never do.

The message for To Do people appears to be: Get with the Plan(ner) or just get out. ®.
