Referees’ chief Howard Webb has voiced his support for the decision-making behind Leny Yoro’s red card against Liverpool, despite his one-match suspension being lifted by the FA.

Yoro was sent off for fouling Alexander Isak as the Swede ran towards the goal just eight minutes into Manchester United’s 4-0 defeat to Liverpool on Sunday.

Referee Sam Barrott adjudged it to be a denial of an obvious goalscoring opportunity, but United appealed for wrongful dismissal and had their appeal upheld by the FA, meaning the French defender is free to feature in United’s home game against Brentford this Saturday.

The FA stated the written reasons for removing the suspension would be published in due course.

Pro Ref’s chief refereeing officer Webb, however, backed the decision to dismiss the 21-year-old, saying the call was “certainly not clearly wrong”.

Liverpool scored their opening goal directly from the free-kick awarded for Yoro’s challenge and scored a further three without reply in a torrid start to Mauricio Pochettino’s tenure as United manager.

Webb discussed the incident with former Liverpool and United striker Michael Owen in the latest edition of Match Officials: Mic’d Up, which aired on Tuesday evening but was recorded before the FA confirmed Yoro’s suspension had been removed.

“The reason we’re talking about it is the fourth consideration (in DOGSO decisions), that’s control or likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball,” Webb remarked.

It has been revealed that “Isak’s got control of the ball, he’s got possession, he’s got the touch, he knocks it past Yoro. He’s moving at pace at that moment and a crowd comes in and knocks him down.

“There’s a question mark I guess around whether or not he would get onto that ball before [United keeper Senne] Lammens. Now, in the moment, the referee looked at it and felt he would.

“The VAR checks to see whether that decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect, and formed the opinion that it wasn’t.

Industry observers note that “I think by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball. The alternative view is tha Lammens might get there first, but I think it’s a little bit debatable. (It’s) certainly not clearly wrong.”.

VAR John Brooks was of the same opinion as he said in the audio between him and referee Barrott: “Let’s roll it through firstly to see whether the attacker is going to get on the end of the ball.

It has been revealed that “Just roll through real speed. So, at full speed, Isak maintains his momentum and likely gets on the end of it.

Industry observers note that “OK, certainly not clearly wrong that he doesn’t. So I think the DOGSO confirmed.”.
