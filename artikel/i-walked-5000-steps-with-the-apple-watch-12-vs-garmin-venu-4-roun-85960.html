I walked 5,000 steps with the Apple Watch 12 vs Garmin Venu 4: Round 2 — and we have a upcoming winner

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I walked 5,018 steps over roughly 45 minutes with the Apple Watch Series 12 on my left wrist and the Garmin Venu 4 on my right. This is the second time I’ve pitted these two similarly priced and capable wearables — two of the best smartwatches in 2026 — against one another in a test of fitness tracking accuracy supremacy.

Last time these two met, Garmin eked out a win over its Apple rival, but not by a huge margin. For this test, as in the past, the primary metric we’ll use to compare overall performance is total step count.

While step count isn’t necessarily the most important workout data point, it is one I can easily manually count (with some help from my trusty, clicky tally counter) and use as a primary control. For other metrics, including pace, distance, and climb, I logged the walk using Strava on my iPhone 16 Plus.

Find the results of my Apple Watch 12 vs. Garmin Venu 4 walk test: Round 2 below, along with five takeaways from the data and the overall winner.

The Garmin Venu 4 is one of the brand's most stylish and well-rounded smartwatches, balancing impressive workout features with useful smart features. It's also on sale for a sizable discount ahead of the 2026 holidays.

Sources indicate that the Apple Watch Series 12 is the brand's latest flagship model, released only a few months ago. Despite that, it just dropped to its lowest-ever price for Black Friday 2026.

17 mins 50 secs per mile (Strava).

During my 45-minute walk around hilly, leafy Seattle, Washington, I counted exactly 5,018 steps, a tally Stava matched precisely.

Industry observers note that when it comes to judging overall accuracy for a walk of this length, I consider any step count tally that's within 100 steps of my actual count to be excellent. This is to say, both the Apple Watch 12 and Garmin Venu 4 did exceptionally well here.

It has been revealed that but Apple was a little bit closer, with a total that's only 12 steps off from my actual one compared to 22 steps for the Garmin. On the flip side, Garmin's distance tally is a tad closer to the control (Strava), but again, both watches were within a reasonable margin for error.

In a recent development, even the best Apple Watch models sometimes inflate climb data, at least in my experience, and that appears to be the case here. Still, I'd rather have my Apple Watch consistently overcount by around 20 feet during my walks than undercount by that amount.

Garmin came a lot closer to matching Strava's climb data — a predictable outcome — with a total that's off by only seven feet.

Current Apple Watches don't record an average moving pace, unlike Garmin and Strava, only an elapsed or total average pace for your walks/runs/bike rides/etc. For this walk, Apple's average is a little slower than Strava's, while Garmin's is considerably slower.

According to recent reports, that said, when comparing Strava and Garmin's moving pace metric, the two align much more closely. Strava logged 17 ins 22 secs per mile compared to 17 mins 29 secs for Garmin.

Recent findings suggest that garmin's average and maximum heart rate recordings are noticeably higher than Apple's, which is a bit perplexing. Ultimately, this was a fairly leisurely walk, with only a few blocks of steep hills. Which is to say, Apple's heart rate data looks right on the money to me, while Garmin's suggests a higher intensity than I expected.

Despite that, the Venu 4 noted fewer overall calories burned during my ~45-minute walk than Apple.

Ladies and gentlemen, we have a newly introduced champion! With a more accurate step-count total and average elapsed pace data, the Apple Watch Series 12 beats the Garmin Venu 4 in this second round of fitness-tracking performance supremacy.

This, of course, means that a round three is in order. Stay tuned for that! In the meantime, which of these two watches do you prefer? Let me know in the poll below.
