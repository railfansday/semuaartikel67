Sources indicate that keeping your cryptocurrency on an exchange is convenient, but it comes with risks. If the exchange gets hacked, freezes withdrawals, or runs into financial trouble, accessing your funds can become difficult. Using a self-custody wallet gives you direct control over your private keys and, ultimately, your crypto.

Whether you are holding Bitcoin for the next decade, trading memecoins on Solana, or exploring decentralized finance (DeFi), choosing the right wallet matters. Some wallets are designed to keep your assets as secure as possible, while others make it easier to trade, manage tokens, and connect to Web3 apps.

To help you find the right option, we’ve put together a list of seven popular crypto wallets, covering what each one does best, its main advantages, and what to consider before choosing it.

*Prices change frequently, so always confirm on the manufacturer’s official website.

If your primary goal is long-term wealth preservation, Ledger remains the industry benchmark for cold storage.

Unlike mobile apps that stay connected to the internet, Ledger devices store your private keys inside an offline, certified Secure Element chip, the same class of chip used in passports and credit cards (check Ledger’s website for the current certification details).

Through the Ledger Live companion app, you can buy, swap, and stake thousands of coins and tokens. Your private keys never leave the device. Ledger Live runs on your connected phone or computer, but transactions are signed on the Ledger itself.

In a recent development, for those who live by the motto “don’t trust, verify,” Trezor is the gold standard. Contrary to the competitors that keep parts of their code proprietary, Trezor’s firmware is fully open-source, meaning independent security researchers constantly audit it for vulnerabilities.

The Trezor Safe 5 pairs this transparency with a vibrant color touchscreen, haptic feedback, and an EAL6+ Secure Element. It also offers a dedicated Bitcoin-only firmware option for maximalists who want to minimize attack surfaces.

When it comes to interacting with decentralized applications (DApps), decentralized exchanges (DEXs), and NFT marketplaces, MetaMask is the undisputed gateway to Web3.

Available as both a browser extension and a mobile app, MetasMask connects seamlessly to virtually every Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) network, including Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon, Base, and BNB Chain.

Modern updates have expanded MetaMask beyond Ethereum via MetaMask Snaps and allow people to add support for non-EVM ecosystems like Bitcoin and Solana directly inside the same interface.

Originally built as the flagship wallet for the Solana ecosystem, Phantom has evolved into one of the fastest, cleanest multi-chain wallets on the market. Today, it seamlessly supports Solana, Ethereum, Base, Polygon, and Bitcoin in a single unified interface.

In a recent development, what sets Phantom apart is user experience. Transaction simulations show you exact balance changes before you hit approve, and its automated spam filter hides malicious airdrop NFTs so beginners don’t accidentally click phishing links.

If you want one smartphone app that holds virtually every cryptocurrency in existence without manual network configuration, Trust Wallet is built for you.

Supporting over 100 blockchains and millions of digital assets, it removes the technical friction of adding custom RPC networks or hunting down token contract addresses.

It also includes a built-in Web3 browser, straightforward in-app staking for major proof-of-stake coins, and an integrated Security Scanner that flags risky smart contracts.

Many newcomers confuse the main Coinbase exchange app with Coinbase Wallet, but they are completely separate products. Coinbase Wallet is a true non-custodial wallet—meaning you hold your own 12-word recovery phrase, and Coinbase cannot freeze or access your funds.

Note that the app’s branding has changed recently: Coinbase renamed it “Base App” in July 2025 and restored the Coinbase Wallet name in September 2026. It has since shifted toward multichain trading, including perpetual futures, prediction markets, and tokenized stocks, so some of its features are now aimed at more active traders.

It has been revealed that its biggest advantage is frictionless onboarding. You can link your exchange account to transfer funds into self-custody in seconds, claim a free human-readable username instead of copying long hexadecimal addresses, and explore Base and Ethereum DApps.

Recent findings suggest that traditional hardware wallets require cables, batteries, and carefully guarding a 24-word paper backup. Tangem reimagines cold storage as a sleek, waterproof smart card—roughly the size and thickness of a standard bank debit card.

It has been revealed that to approve a transaction, you simply tap the card against the back of your NFC-enabled smartphone. Tangem comes in sets of two or three cards that act as physical backups for one another, allowing you to skip vulnerable paper seed phrases entirely (though a traditional seed option is still available if you prefer it).

Understanding the difference between hot and cold storage saves you from overpaying—or worse, leaving your funds exposed:.

A common rule of thumb among experienced crypto investors is to use both, for example keeping around 90% of long-term holdings in a cold wallet and about 10% in a hot wallet for everyday trading and Web3 exploration. This is a guideline, not a standard – adjust it to your own situation.

With crypto, there’s no bank to call if something goes wrong. Transactions are usually irreversible, and if an exchange freezes withdrawals or goes bankrupt, your funds could be stuck for months or even years.

It has been revealed that that’s why choosing the best crypto wallet matters. It gives you more control over your money and lets you manage your funds without relying on an exchange.

It has been revealed that your cryptocurrency is not stored inside the physical device—it lives on the blockchain. If your Ledger, Trezor, or Tangem card is lost, stolen, or broken, you can buy a replacement device and restore full access to your funds in minutes using your 12- or 24-word recovery phrase (or backup Tangem card).

While no system is 100% immune, cold hardware wallets cannot be hacked remotely because your private keys stay offline. Most wallet “hacks” happen when users accidentally sign a malicious smart contract on a phishing website or store their seed phrase digitally where malware can find it.

Software wallets like MetaMask, Phantom, and Trust Wallet are completely free to download and hold crypto in. Hardware wallets require an upfront purchase (typically $55 to $250). Regardless of the wallet you choose, you will still pay standard blockchain network (“gas”) fees whenever you send or swap tokens.

According to recent reports, with a custodial wallet (like a standard exchange account), a third-party company holds your private keys and can freeze or restrict your withdrawals. With a non-custodial (self-custody) wallet, only you hold the private keys—meaning nobody can block your transactions, though you are solely responsible for backing up your recovery phrase.

In a recent development, no. Unlike traditional bank accounts, funds in a self-custody crypto wallet are not covered by government deposit insurance like the FDIC or SIPC. Because you act as your own bank, protecting your recovery phrase and verifying transaction details before approving them is your primary safeguard.
