If your calls and texts aren't going through, try these recommendations.

The modern electronic device has made a lot of strides in ensuring that it lets you know when something has gone awry. Things like computers, game consoles and tablets will often find a way to alert you to an glitch, which makes it easier to troubleshoot any potential problems that may arise.

Sources indicate that on your phone, you may notice one of these alerts in the form of a notification that states, "No SIM card installed." This can be alarming, as it's possible that you may not know what a SIM card even does. Or if you do know what the SIM card does, it can lead to some worrying conclusions as you try to troubleshoot it.

Fixing a SIM card error is a task that requires some diagnostic detective work, or if you're extra unlucky, some actual physical work. Thankfully, there are a lot of tools, both in your phone's settings and in the real world, that can assist with a potential fix.

When a SIM card error leaves you without service, the phone will still work and power on; it will just be without its main features. The only thing that needs to be done is to take it out and put it right back in. This is usually caused by dropping the phone or ithe card not being properly seated when it was serviced for you, but you can use a SIM ejector tool to take it out. If you don't have one, something as simple as a paper clip or safety pin will usually do the trick.

In a recent development, it's always worth finding out if it's something as simple as a carrier outage, which is entirely possible. If you have access to another device on a different carrier and it can connect to the internet or make a phone call, that could indicate a network outage specific to your carrier. Asking a local friend or family member using the same service whether their phone is working properly can confirm a carrier outage or eliminate it as a possibility.

Your phone may be using an eSIM card, which replaces the physical card with a digital SIM. These are great, but they tend to have unique problems with international travel. If you're using an eSIM card, the way you access settings on an iPhone is slightly different from Android. On an iPhone, eSIM cards are located in Cellular or Mobile Data in the Settings menu, while Android stores them in a setting called SIM Manager. By turning on data roaming, you may fix any sort of problems you're having.

A SIM card or "no service" error can sometimes be something at a software level as opposed to something being wrong with the card. One of the more common fixes is simply toggling airplane mode on and off. Airplane mode can be found by swiping down from the top of your phone and tapping the airplane button.

Industry observers note that you may also be able to do a quick network reset, which sounds bad but has zero effect on your personal files. Doing a network reset on an iPhone is largely similar to the process on Android. It should be noted, however, that the iPhone's network reset is in the Transfer or Reset section of Settings, while Android stores it in Reset Options, also in Settings. You will need to sign back into your Wi-Fi, however, along with some other connections.

In a recent development, we all get busy in life, and if you haven't paid your bill, then your SIM card will simply stop connecting to your carrier's network. It may seem like a simple thing, but sometimes the simple solutions are blindingly obvious. If you pay your bill online, check your account to see if you've missed a payment or if your service hasn't been suspended for some other reason.

Sources indicate that getting no service on your phone may not even have anything to do with a bad SIM card or an inactive account. You may just be in a place with limited to no service available, or a cellphone tower may be blocked by something. Move around and look for someplace that you actually can connect, as the error may be a result of poor service in that area.
