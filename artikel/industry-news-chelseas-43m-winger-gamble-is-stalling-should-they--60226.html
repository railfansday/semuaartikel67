Chelsea transfer news: Birmingham Live reports that Alejandro Garnacho’s future needs resolving, because the winger has not started a match for Aston Villa and a permanent move depends on certain conditions being met. Chelsea sent the 22-year-old to Villa Park on a season-long loan in July, with a reported £43m obligation to buy next summer. Neither club has revealed the trigger, and reports link it to appearances.

The numbers explain the concern. FotMob lists four Premier League outings, all from the bench, without a goal or assist, while Football365 counts only 95 minutes across seven substitute appearances. Football Insider claims Villa are prepared to back out of the deal if his form doesn’t improve.

Garnacho’s strengths haven’t changed. His pace, directness and willingness to take on defenders earned a £40m move from Manchester United in 2025. The weakness is end product, and it followed him to London with eight goals in 43 games for Chelsea, only one of them in 24 Premier League appearances. Unai Emery left him out of the squad for the Manchester United game.

Chelsea have no obvious use for him either. Xabi Alonso told him in July that regular football wasn’t guaranteed, and the 3-4-2-1 Alonso favours uses Morgan Rogers and Cole Palmer behind João Pedro, with Pedro Neto and Estêvão competing for the wide roles.

Sources indicate that fotMob values him at about €29m (roughly £25m), far below the €71m peak recorded in August 2024, and Manchester United are reportedly entitled to 10 per cent of any future fee. If the clause isn’t triggered, he returns in July 2027 on a contract running to 2032.

Sources indicate that yes, and soon. Chelsea built the deal to protect £43m, but the clause relies on appearances from a manager who isn’t picking him, so the price drops with every unused substitution. The sporting case for a return is thin. Alonso’s system asks wide players to defend as wing-backs, while Garnacho’s value lies in isolating a full-back, and the £117m Rogers signing already covers the left-sided attacking role.

According to recent reports, A mid-£20m sale, even after United’s cut, would still return more than half of the £40m Chelsea paid and clear a contract running to 2032. Chelsea should open talks with Villa in January about a lower fixed fee, or line up other buyers for the summer. A clause that depends on a manager picking a player he has benched was never a safe way to protect £43m.
