Welcome to Foreign Policy’s Southeast Asia Brief.

The highlights this week from the ASEAN summit in Manila, Philippines: Trump stops by for 24 hours; Myanmar is still shut out despite junta lobbying; and Canada forges two newly introduced trade deals in the region.

Sources indicate that so keen was the Philippines to have U.S. President Donald Trump attend the annual leader’s summit of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) that it changed the dates just for him. Originally scheduled to run five days earlier, it was shifted to run Nov. 15 through Nov. 17, the better to accommodate the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, which is running yesterday and today in Shenzhen, China.

ASEAN can also take pleasure in the fact that Trump has now attended two years running. Under previous administrations, the nonattendance of the U.S. president or senior U.S. officials was a running complaint.

It has been revealed that after all the hoopla, then, what did the visit add up to? Not much, it seems.

It has been revealed that last year, when Kuala Lumpur hosted, the world was still gripped by questions about tariff negotiations. And Trump also got to play the peacemaker between Thailand and Cambodia—even though fighting between the two resumed the next month.

In a recent development, this time, no single glitch defined the U.S. president’s visit, which lasted less than 24 hours. Trump proclaimed that the United States and Philippines had signed their biggest “ever” trade deal. But in typical Trump style, few details were forthcoming and nothing has actually been signed.

On a symbolic level for Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., Trump’s arrival is a nice sign of the continued close relationship between the two countries.

According to recent reports, manila’s previous president, Rodrigo Duterte, was deeply skeptical of the United States, flirting with ending the two countries’ alliance in favor of a deal with China. Marcos has spun the other way, doubling down on the security relationship with Washington to push back against Beijing.

A few other deliverables were also duly delivered upon.

Sources indicate that A set of deals involving anti-scam initiatives was finalized. A “trade deal” with Thailand—which, like the Philippines deal, was not actually a final agreement—was announced.

Sources indicate that the leaders of the other Quad countries—Australia, Japan, and India—got a grand total of 55 scheduled minutes with Trump. The announcements that came out of that mainly repackaged old agreements.

And Marcos arranged for some food from Jollibee—the Philippines’ top fast-food chain and a national icon—to be delivered to Trump in the interest of “providing extra energy for all the work that you have to do.”.

According to recent reports, at the state dinner, where Jollibee was not served, the U.S. president delivered a typically Trumpian speech. He assured ASEAN that he would support “partners and allies 100 percent,” adding that “when there’s a problem, you’ll call me.”.

He also took the opportunity to praise Marcos for his “very good genetics” as well as calling on Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul and Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto to stand up for compliments.

According to recent reports, some had expected announcements on Pax Silica, the United States’ global AI initiative. The United States is planning to build enormous semiconductor and data center facilities on land that used to house one of its military bases in the Philippines. But no announcements were forthcoming.

It has been revealed that the Philippine government is keen on the idea as a way to boost its chips and electronics industry, as well as develop its defense industrial base. But the project is also controversial, spurring worries about the environmental impact and displacement of local people.

Protesters took to the streets over Trump’s arrival to make their displeasure heard. Police reacted by blocking marches and detaining people. Nearly 300 demonstrators were arrested, according to protest organizers.

Sources indicate that don’t read too much into this, though. The Philippines is one of the few countries in the world where Trump is still popular. A poll by Pew Research Centre found that 68 percent of Filipinos trusted Trump to do the right thing, the highest of any country surveyed.

According to recent reports, overshadowing all of this was Trump’s next set of meetings in Shenzhen. Will he meet Russian President Vladimir Putin in a trilateral with Chinese President Xi Jinping? Maybe. Will he meet North Korean leader Kim Jong Un? Also maybe.

So—at the end of the day—did Trump just stop by Manila for a pit stop, a photo op, and some Filipino fried food? Maybe.

Recent findings suggest that not yet… Myanmar isn’t back in ASEAN, yet. Despite determined lobbying from Thailand, junta leader Min Aung Hlaing still wasn’t invited to attend the ASEAN summit.

Since its 2021 coup, ASEAN has expressed its displeasure with Myanmar by not allowing junta leaders to attend summits. Senior civil servants get sent instead.

However, a leaders’ statement adopted on Sunday declared that ASEAN would pursue “calibrated engagement with Myanmar” and “may consider the gradual restoration of Myanmar’s full political representation.”.

The widespread assumption is that come 2028, when Thailand is the chair of the bloc, the final barriers will be removed.

The argument for reengagement is that, like it or not, the junta is there. Therefore, ASEAN countries need to engage—especially Thailand, which shares more than 2,000 kilometers of border with Myanmar.

According to recent reports, critics will counter that engagement doesn’t seem to yield much. After the ASEAN summit, Min Aung Hlaing went on a rant in which he noted that the statement on Myanmar was unreasonable. He also accused three unnamed member states of prejudice.

Meanwhile, in late October, fighting between junta forces and rebel groups on the Myanmar-Thia border saw explosives and shrapnel spill over to the Thai side. One Thai woman was wounded, and buildings were damaged.

In a recent development, not quite… The draft “Code of Conduct on the South China Sea,” which the Philippines pushed so hard for, wasn’t finished. According to Marcos in his press conference at the end of the summit, it’s 85 percent done, and all that’s left is the defining of terms.

With the actual terms as yet unknown, there is, on the summit sidelines, a good deal of cynicism. Even if the agreement is finished next year, many see two decidedly nonthrilling options.

One is that in order for China to sign the agreement, it will have to be extremely thin, with little upcoming or binding terms in it. Second, if some reasonable guidelines are set out, China will just ignore them when it suits it.

An analyst from a country with its own massive claim on the waters suggested to me that a code of conduct could even be a disadvantage. If China breaks the rules, then other more rule-abiding countries might find their options to respond constrained by the agreement.

Not on the agenda… Remember the Thailand-Cambodia conflict? ASEAN doesn’t, it seems.

Following last summer’s fighting, ASEAN made an effort to address it. A Trump-sponsored peace deal was signed at the summit in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. Following renewed fighting in December, an ASEAN observer team was unveiled. And in Cebu, Philippines, earlier this year, the two countries met on the sidelines for Philippine-brokered talks, even if the results were limited.

This time around? Not a squeak. Partly to blame is perhaps that Cambodian Prime Minister Hun Manet didn’t attend. He was in Phnom Penh hosting the Francophonie Summit—see below.

Sorry for the secondhand smoke. During the ASEAN summit, Prabowo apologized to Malaysia and Singapore for how they have been affected by the haze from Indonesia’s vast forest fires last month.

He then rather undercut it by adding, “It is not our fault, but once again we apologize.”.

In a recent development, singaporean Prime Minister Lawrence Wong also proposed that ASEAN should establish a joint task force to probe companies linked to such fires. This would expand on the current bilateral Singapore-Indonesia task force established last month following some diplomatic spats on the topic.

Trade deals. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney dropped by on Saturday for the announcement that two trade deals were nearly done. Both an ASEAN-Canada and a Canada-Philippines trade deal have been forged with the aim of getting both signed next year.

Sources indicate that how much free trade will actually be in these agreements? It’s hard to tell with no text published, but past experience suggests that any changes will probably be more incremental than sweeping.

Wow, everything’s computer. On Saturday, ASEAN signed its Digital Economy Framework Agreement, which should come into force in early 2027. The proclaimed aim is to create an “interconnected ASEAN digital marketplace.”.

According to recent reports, what does this mean in practice? The text’s not out yet, but this Singaporean government announcement lays out some broad strokes—mainly, accepting electronic trade documents, clearer rules on cross-border data flows, and a guarantee of no taxes on them for now, as well as cooperation on artificial intelligence and financial technology.

According to recent reports, oil stockpile. Indonesia, Japan, the Philippines, and Thailand agreed to set up shared stockpiles of diesel and gasoline to help manage future energy crises. The actual facilities will be built in Indonesia and Thailand.

In a recent development, cambodia: Cambodia hosted the Francophonie Summit from Saturday to Monday, gathering countries from across the French-speaking world—loosely defined. Prime Minister Hun Manet skipped ASEAN for this event and used it to do some diplomatic lobbying for Cambodia over its border dispute with Thailand, which infuriated the Thai government. French President Emmanuel Macron called for the investigation of the killing of a former opposition lawmaker and French Cambodian dual citizen Lim Kimya in Bangkok in 2025.

According to recent reports, indonesia: Police raided a data center owned by Megaspeed and found empty server racks. Prabowo is expected to pick a loyalist from his own party to be the next VP. Online phenomenon iShowSpeed demonstrated off his Indonesian-speaking skills at an awards ceremony and then on a phone call with former U.S. President Barack Obama. And Nikkei has published a great article on Indonesia’s runners-for-hire who will burnish your Strava for you.

In a recent development, malaysia: The governing coalition faced another setback with a loss in the Melaka state election. And Macron visited on Tuesday and pitched cooperation on rare earths, weapons, semiconductors, and nuclear energy to reduce dependence on the United States and China.
