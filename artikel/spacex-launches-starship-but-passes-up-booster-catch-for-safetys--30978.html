SpaceX sent its Starship on a flight that was meant to feature a first-ever retrieval of both rocket stages, but a launch-pad catch of the first-stage booster had to be canceled due to safety concerns.

Instead, the Super Heavy booster was diverted during its descent to make an offshore splashdown. Observers at SpaceX's Starbase on the South Texas coast could hear the sonic boom and see the fiery plunge that took place minutes after launch at 4:47 p.m. CT (2247 GMT) today. "Wow, you could feel the rumble of that splashdown there," launch commentator Jessie Anderson mentioned.

SpaceX has caught Starship's booster during three previous test flights, but this would be a first for a second stage.

Hours after launch, SpaceX launch commentator Kate Tice said the booster's control system triggered an abort of the launch-tower catch because the boostback engine burn ended early, apparently due to a low fuel mass alarm. SpaceX's team was still troubleshooting the root cause of the alarm, she said.
