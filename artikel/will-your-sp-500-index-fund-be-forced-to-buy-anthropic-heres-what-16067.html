Will Your S&P 500 Index Fund Be Forced to Buy Anthropic? Here’s What the Rules Say.

  • Industry observers note that that raises a question for the millions of people who own an index fund in a retirement account.
  • If you own an S&P 500 index fund like the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -0.15%).
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anthropic is about to become one of the largest public companies in the world. While we don't know exactly what the IPO price will be, or what its stock price will be when it starts trading, at the top of its $70 to $78 price range, the tech giant would be valued at roughly $1.4 trillion. That's bigger than all but a select few U.S. companies.

Industry observers note that that raises a question for the millions of people who own an index fund in a retirement account. If you own an S&P 500 fund, will you automatically end up owning Anthropic? The short answer is no, at least not for a while. But there's a lot more to unpack.

If you own an S&P 500 index fund like the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -0.15%). The S&P 500 isn't simply the 500 largest U.S. companies. If it were, Anthropic would clearly qualify. A committee selects its members and has eligibility rules. A company generally has to be public for 12 months; it needs positive earnings under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) in its most recent four quarters and in its latest quarter, and sufficient shares must trade publicly. In June, S&P Dow Jones Indices specifically stated it would keep those rules in place for megacap IPOs such as SpaceX (SPCX +2.22%), OpenAI, and Anthropic.

Anthropic fails the profitability test by a wide margin, at least on paper. It reported a $104 billion net loss for the first nine months of 2026. Most of that, about $94 billion, came from non-cash charges tied to the rising value of its convertible notes and similar securities, and its operating loss was a much smaller $1.26 billion. Even if its GAAP results improve rapidly after the IPO, the one-year waiting period means the earliest it could join is late 2027.

Sources indicate that on the other hand, if you own a Nasdaq-100 fund like the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ -0.17%), the answer is probably yes, and soon. Nasdaq adopted a fast-entry rule in May that tests a large latest listing on its seventh trading day and allows it to join the index after about 15 trading days. That's how SpaceX joined the Nasdaq-100 less than a month after its June debut. Anthropic is listing on Nasdaq, so it could be in the index by early December.

Let's be clear. None of this tells you whether Anthropic is a good investment, and newly public companies tend to be volatile in their first year. Index inclusion can trigger a burst of buying from funds that must own the stock, but that's a short-term effect, not a reason to own a manufacturer for the long run.

Having said all that, the takeaway for most investors is simple. If you want to own Anthropic in its first few weeks after going public, you'll need to buy it directly or own a Nasdaq-100 fund once it's added. If you'd rather wait and see how it performs as a public firm first, your S&P 500 fund will keep you on the sidelines for at least another year.
