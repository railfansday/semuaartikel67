Will Wright's next simulation will use AI to mine content from your memories

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The computer gaming world hasn’t heard much from Will Wright (SimCity, The Sims) since the underwhelming release of Spore in 2008 and Wright’s subsequent departure from Maxis owner EA. Now, well over 15 years later, Wright is starting to promote his next digital simulation, which will use generative AI technology to craft a game-like experience from players’ memories.

In a recent development, myMems, which is slated to release next year, differentiates itself from Wright’s previous projects by “turn[ing] the simulation inward,” as Wright’s Gallium Studios and partner Artificial Agency put it in a press release this morning. The game will mine memories provided by the player to create digital people that can then form teams to solve puzzles in the game world.

“If my Grandmother ended up in a Grand Slam match against Serena Williams, who would win?” Wright says by way of example in the release. “If I had to build a bridge across a short chasm, which three people from my life would I choose to help me?”.

Industry observers note that in an accompanying announcement video, Wright talks about how the “plausible behavior” in a game like The Sims still didn’t effectively capture the nuances of people he encounters in real life. “It was nowhere near them understanding my claustrophobia or why my sister and I fought so much… We’re actually taking parts of your life, your memories, your experiences, people you know, and turning that into a game that’s very personal.”.

Wright said MyMems will be able to extract generalized themes from your memories into self-contained areas that Wright compared to the various parts in a theme park: Sports, vacations, injuries, memories, and deaths could all be the basis for their own zones to play in. Players will also have the option to create worlds based on shared memories with family, friends, and co-workers, for example.

According to recent reports, since this is 2026, it shouldn’t be surprising that MyMems is making heavy use of generative AI (with a “proprietary behavior engine” on top) to transform your typed-up memories into simulated locales and characters. In the announcement video, Wright praises the “entirely open-ended” AI systems Gallium is using “to extract from the stories and whatnot based upon the memories you put into the game.”.

But Wright’s focus on mining memories for simulation content predates the current generative AI boom. What has now become MyMems started off as Proxi, a mobile simulation that was first unveiled in 2018 as “a game of self-discovery, a game where we actually uncover the hidden you.” But after years of pre-production work (as outlined in an excellent Vulture piece earlier this year), Wright’s Gallium Studios finally ran out of funding in late 2024, leading to mass layoffs for the small team and an uncertain future for the project.

It has been revealed that now the idea has been revived thanks in large part to a partnership with AI middleware firm Artificial Agency. Until now, that company has mainly been focused on agentic systems that can create “virtual players” to playtest a game or serve as a partner for human players.

Now, Artificial Agency’s marketing claims the firm’s AI tool will be used to create “a personal behavior model and a knowledge graph” from players’ memories, drawing some unsettling comparisons to significant tech surveillance.

“Meta knows what you click,” the company writes. “Netflix knows what you watch. Nobody knows what you remember. MemDNA learns from your memories locally and only with your permission.”.

Industry observers note that wright noted the partnership with Artificial Agency will let Gallium benefit from “off-the-shelf” AI solutions without having to “become an AI research company” in its own right. He also said the collaboration will help limit costs by providing a cheaper option than the “really expensive” frontier models that might be overkill for a project like this.

“Right now AI has opened a vast fresh design area and I’m enjoying, you know, exploring that as a designer,” Wright noted.
