Before she ever became a luxury real estate agent at The Oppenheim Group or appeared on the Netflix hit “Selling Sunset,” serial entrepreneur Emma Hernan had hired Jason Oppenheim to help her buy a home in West Hollywood, where she has since released several successful businesses.

The 1940-built Colonial-style estate that Hernan bought in 2017 was a hot commodity, seeing as Oppenheim had initially hoped to snap up the property for himself, Hernan, 34, reveals to Realtor.com in the latest Celebrity Sanctuary.

In a recent development, “Funny enough, Jason really wanted to buy this house but he had just purchased another home, so he wanted someone that he at least knows to get it. I ended up buying it off market with Jason as my broker,” she explains.

Throughout the house hunt, Oppenheim encouraged Hernan to learn the real estate ropes so she could go from being his client to becoming his coworker and costar. After getting her real estate license in 2018, she “immediately hung [her] license with The O Group … and ended up on the show.”.

Though Hernan left “Selling Sunset” in 2025 after six seasons as a main cast member, she still works with Oppenheim and other former costars—just without the added conflict that comes with filming reality TV.

“I would describe it in one word: Peaceful,” Hernan says about her experience in the office now that she’s not on the show.

However, Hernan still credits the series for giving her a platform to pursue other ventures alongside luxury real estate, such as her frozen seafood and empanada brand, Coast to Coast Kitchen, which officially debuted in 2025 but had been in the works for decades.

Industry observers note that hernan “grew up passing out crab cakes at demos and supermarkets” because her grandfather was the founder of the Massachusetts-based seafood firm, Yankee Trader Seafood. When Hernan’s mother and aunt took over and needed capital to keep the enterprise going, Hernan lent them money she had earned from modeling, nannying, and investing in the stock market.

Sources indicate that beyond providing funding for equipment and employees, Hernan even bought a manufacturing facility in Hingham, MA that still services her family’s manufacturer as well as other food brands. She also owns a house in Norwell, MA next door to her brother’s abode, and is often in the area because of business and her boyfriend.

“Obviously, Boston is very special to me, but L.A.—I created Coast to Coast out of that kitchen with family,” she shares to illustrate why her West Coast residence holds a special place in her heart.

Recent findings suggest that in this edition of Celebrity Sanctuary, Hernan points out the potential she’s always seen in the 3,000-square-foot, four-bedroom, four-bathroom, two-story dwelling she shares with her two dogs and eight rescue cats. Plus, she opens up about how the familiar New England feel of her home kitchen helps her feed every passion.

Sources indicate that I purchased the home in 2017 for $2.7 million and … was renting it out. I had it as an investment property because it was a bit major for me by myself. When I started filming the show, my house is super conveniently located to the office, so I moved in right when I started filming.

This specific house and street, it's super conveniently located. It’s in the heart of West Hollywood. The office is right there. The location is a 10 for sure.

There's a lot of modern homes out there. I was looking for a while and didn't fall in love with anything. Then when I walked in here, it reminded me of Latest England and my family's home.

According to recent reports, it was built in 1940. [The previous owners] did a small remodel [in] 1948 and then right before I purchased it, they did another small remodel, updated [the] kitchen and all that.

In a recent development, I redid the pool. The flooring is original and that's something that I go back and forth with changing, even though I do want to re-do the floors and warm them up. They're wood floors, original from 1940.

The house has so much potential, which is one of the reasons why I bought it. I didn't want to buy a house that was completely done. I wanted to be able to add value and make some money off of it. I actually just had a couple contractors come by and [give a] quote.

According to recent reports, I'm definitely gonna do a pretty massive remodel. It's very home-y, but it needs updates and I'm super excited to do that. I’m gonna do the full landscaping. It needs newly introduced floors, windows, all the things, but I think it'll be a really fun project.

I haven't really made it my own besides the kitchen, which I would say is my sanctuary: somewhere that feels safe, homey, and peaceful. That's really crucial. My life is very public and having that peace and a place that feels home-y and safe, that's definitely what [sanctuary] means to me.

Industry observers note that “Selling Sunset” [was] different than [selling] real estate because [reality TV is] a little bit more drama heavy, so … a lot of times when I would come home from filming, cooking and creating meals for my friends and my family really did help separate that scene that we just had that was so crazy and so hectic. Coming home and forgetting all about that, chopping the vegetables that I was gonna serve for dinner, and really being able to separate the two—I did that all in the kitchen.

It has been revealed that the kitchen, when I bought it, was definitely one of my favorite things about the whole house. It reminded me about New England and my family. I added some family photos. I’m from Scituate, MA. I added this cool little Scituate plaque.

It’s bright and white, but there's a lot of wood in there, too, which warms it up. Granite countertops, they're white. It's a very warm and bright kitchen. There's a lot of light that comes through. It really is my favorite part of the whole home.

It has been revealed that the dining room area … I have a really beautiful [Restoration Hardware] dining room table that I love. And then I have this little countertop area.

I've created businesses there. I’ve closed deals there. I've had family gatherings there. I’ve had Thanksgiving there, Christmas there, and so many fun little taco nights with my friends that now became my family. I’ve gone on QVC live from the kitchen. I have been in glam for “Selling Sunset.” So many of my interviews—everything's from my kitchen. It’s really special to me.

Recent findings suggest that the stove was literally where I created half of my recipes for Coast to Coast. So many meals are created in there. I love to cook. It's such a passion of mine. It’s therapeutic for me and that's why I was like, “I need to start my own organization.

Sources indicate that I'm gonna get a newly introduced oven, a new SubZero, and all of that. The one thing that it's lacking is storage. I had this open space area where there's glasses, but there's not enough cabinetry, especially for the amount of cooking that I do.

According to recent reports, I definitely tend to overcook because I'd rather have more than enough food than not enough. I love to cook and have many options for people. It's funny because my mom and my dad are like, “Do you need help? What do you need help with?” And I really don't need help. I love doing it. I love making every dish, making it special. I love plating it and making it look really good.
