There’s a fresh billionaire in town — and this one’s ready to party.

Todd Graves, founder and CEO of chicken fingers chain Raising Cane’s, has swiftly gained a reputation for hosting Hollywood’s hottest events, splashing out for A-list gatherings around major sporting events and awards shows.

During last summer’s World Cup, the 54-year-old Louisiana native rented out stadium suites nationwide, throwing parties with guests including Woody Harrelson, Sacha Baron Cohen, Mick Jagger, Halle Berry, Jamie Foxx, Ashton Kutcher, Adam Brody, Florence Pugh, Alix Earle, Tate McRae and Matt Damon (during opening weekend of The Odyssey, no less). He also enlisted Tiffany Haddish to serve chicken fingers and host Raising Cane’s-branded interviews backstage at the Emmys, persuaded Adrien Brody to paint a custom mural for his Times Square store and is getting the biggest winners across sports and awards season to come by his restaurants immediately after their victories, establishing a tradition to rival the post-awards In-N-Out run. Recent findings suggest that how exactly did a brand serving $15 chicken finger meals — whose nearest competitor is Chick-fil-A — become such a popular collab magnet for the rich and famous? Graves says the secret sauce is his own up-by-the-bootstraps backstory, which he sees as the ultimate icebreaker: Having worked as a boilermaker in California oil refineries and as a salmon fisherman as a young man, he went on to launch his fast food empire — which 30 years later, according to Forbes, has landed him a net worth of $20 billion.

Graves says he does not pay celebrities to attend his events but instead provides a high-end, all-inclusive outing. “I want it to be like, ‘I want to go hang out with Todd and go to this event.’ It’s first-class, great hotels, transportation, security,” Graves says of his pitch. “No one in there wants anything from them but friendship, hanging out.”.

He adds that on top of the fact that “the dinners are great, the parties are fun, there’s DJs and performers that they like,” it’s important to him to take out the corporate element, with no business partners in his rooms “hitting people up or asking for selfies.”.

There is a catch: Graves does ask his guests to post on their social media channels, telling them up front that ” ‘It helps me get a return on this and we can keep doing great events,’ and they’re all cool about doing that.” Haddish and Snoop Dogg have been particularly enthusiastic posters for the brand, while on the Raising Cane’s channel, Tom Brady declared its Fanatics Fest event (which featured a dunk tank of Cane’s sauce) “my favorite part of the entire week.”.

It has been revealed that graves confirms that the company measures impressions after every event, and “it’s amazing for sales,” he adds. “You see long-term loyalty because [customers] are like, ‘The person that I really love and respect loves Cane’s and actually wants to hang out with the founder.’ “.

The CEO, who this year bought a home in the Hollywood Hills for a reported $23 million, says he doesn’t do cold calls to invite people he doesn’t know. “It looks like a branded thing,” he says, which, to be fair, it very much is, judging by the ubiquity of the chain’s logo in celebrities’ posts. Instead, he fills his events with stars with whom he is friendly. That helps him expand the Hollywood circle in a more organic way, through word-of-mouth. Damon, for instance, came to the Raising Cane’s World Cup celebrations because Casey Affleck, a friend of Graves’, vouched that the events were good.

Graves’ friend group also includes Adrien Brody — who highlighted off his artwork while Graves was hanging out at the actor’s home, leading to the idea to paint a custom piece for the Latest York store — and Teyana Taylor, who dropped by a Cane’s for a photo op after winning at the Golden Globes earlier this year. “We want to be part of culture when it happens,” he states, having organized the blueprint with Taylor before the awards show, getting her to come by before her night of parties. (These photo moments are executed as more traditional brand deals.).

In a recent development, the Raising Cane’s founder says he was on the road 260 days in 2025, and he doesn’t seem to be slowing down as the brand expands into Europe and begins to plan international events. (Baron Cohen is helping connect him to contacts in London, Graves says.) He also notes that despite his connections and financial status, he doesn’t have any aspirations to produce projects or be actively involved in Hollywood — though he does think another friend, Leonardo DiCaprio, should play him in the story of his life.

Industry observers note that but L.A. remains as much of a home base as Louisiana. “The town has so accepted me. [It’s] so diverse, so rich culturally. I love it,” Graves claims. “So for me, [the goal] is just to keep meeting these great people. L.A. is so full of all these amazing people that I get inspired by.”.
