Sean “Diddy” Combs isn’t skipping out on Turkey Day.

After all, the rapper—who is serving a 50-month prison sentence for prostitution related charges at FCI Allenwood Low in Pennsylvania—will enjoy a full-course meal come Nov. 26, a law enforcement official at the prison confirmed to People.

Combs' Thanksgiving menu includes a choice between turkey and tofu stir-fry, they told the outlet, along with sides like mashed potatoes and candied sweet potato casserole.

And it appears the music mogul will be able to enjoy all the fixings as the menu also features green bean casserole, sage and onion stuffing and dinner rolls, the official confirmed. Combs will also have his choice of gravy and cranberry sauce for the meal.

And what’s Thanksgiving without dessert? This year, the 57-year-old will satisfy his sweet tooth with pecan pie.

It has been revealed that E! News has reached out to Combs’ rep for comment but did not hear back.

It has been revealed that but not every inmate will be getting the same spread. In fact, the Federal Bureau of Prisons (BOP) does not offer a standardized holiday menu across all facilities. Rather, some have more flexibility with their menus than others.

Recent findings suggest that holiday meals “reflect available food items, local considerations, and the cultural diversity of the inmate population,” a spokesperson told Oxygen. “This allows institutions to incorporate menu enhancements and provide meals that recognize the specific holiday.”.

The statement added, “The same goes for any planned activities at each facility.”.

Though this Thanksgiving marks Combs’ third behind bars, it’s his first at FCI Allenwood Low, where he was transferred in October after sustaining a head injury while incarcerated at Latest Jersey's FCI Fort Dix.

Recent findings suggest that "His transfer from one low-security facility to another is an crucial distinction, and while we cannot speak to the BOP's specific reasons for the move, it is consistent with a decision made with safety and security in mind,” Combs’ rep told E! in an Oct. 16 statement. "We are grateful for the attention being given to his well-being and hope he can continue serving his time without unnecessary disruption.".
