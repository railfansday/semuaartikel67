By ALEX RASKIN, US SPORTS NEWS EDITOR.

Published: 15:41 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 23:53 EST, 20 November 2026.

Zohran Mamdani squared off with Newly introduced York media and now the city's mayor will need a few months to recover.

As he revealed Friday while announcing intends to replace Rikers Island jail, Mamdani was playing in a soccer match against the local press corps at an indoor pitch in lower Manhattan when he tore his right Achilles tendon. Consequently, Mamdani is now using crutches and a walking boot, which he expects to be in for the next six to eight weeks.

'I scored a goal soon after getting on the pitch and I forgot that I was 35,' Mamdani told gathered media on Friday at City Hall, where he was forced to rely on crutches.

Mamdani hopes to be back playing soccer again in nine months.

Industry observers note that local media may not be to blame here. As Mamdani told the Upcoming York Post, he arrived late to the game and began playing without properly stretching. What's more, the Post reported that Mamdani was overheard asking his police guards which reporter tackled him, only to be told he went down untouched.

Despite Mamdani's injury, team City Hall beat the local media, 8-4.

As he revealed Friday while announcing intends to close Rikers Island, Mamdani was playing  against the local press corps when tore his Achilles. Now he's in a boot (pictured).

Zohran Mamdani and wife Rama Duwaji are seen during a Brazil-Morocco World Cup match.

Born in Uganda and raised in Manhattan's Morningside Heights neighborhood, Mamdani played cricket and soccer in his youth. He even suited up for Bowdoin College's JV team during his days at the Maine school. Then later, as an adult, he became a shareholder of Spanish second-division club Real Oviedo.

But his biggest impact on the sport came over the summer, when New York welcomed thousands of tourists for the World Cup action at MetLife Stadium in nearby East Rutherford, New Jersey.

Upset with ticket prices for the matches, Mamdani organized more than 100 free watch parties around the city. The events were well attended and helped locals engage with the tournament even though no actual matches were played in New York.

Zohran Mamdani, center, joined by, from left, United States soccer star Tim Weah NYC World Cup Czar Maya Handa, and New York council member Yusef Salaam back in May.

'We said from the beginning that the World Cup belongs to New Yorkers. This summer, we proved it,' Mamdani said in a statement. 'In the most international city on earth, you didn't need thousands of dollars to be part of the world's game.

It has been revealed that 'A kid in Corona, a nurse in Bay Ridge and a small business owner in Little Haiti could all celebrate together — for free or at a price they could afford,' he continued. 'That's what we mean when we talk about a city for the many.

Industry observers note that 'This summer, strangers became neighbors on Open Streets and in free Fan Fests across the city because government made room for people to come together. And long after the confetti is swept away, Newly introduced Yorkers will keep what we built: safer streets, faster buses, stronger protections, closer neighbors and the memory of a summer when the whole world felt at home here.'.
