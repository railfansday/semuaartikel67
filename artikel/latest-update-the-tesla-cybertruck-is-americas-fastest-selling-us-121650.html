Latest Update: The Tesla Cybertruck Is America’s Fastest-Selling Used Truck

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Even though it was recently canceled, the Tesla Cybertruck is the number-one-selling used car in America. That’s based on analysis of over 1.1 million used-vehicle sales from October 2026 by iSeeCars.com. It includes the fastest-selling trucks and the 25 fastest- and slowest-selling vehicles overall.

In fact, EVs take 25 percent less time to sell than gas vehicles in the used market, and among those EVs there are three Teslas in the top 25, including the Cybertruck, Model 3, and Model Y. And 16 of the 25 fastest-selling used vehicles are EVs.

According to recent reports, “Why do so many electric vehicles, including the recently canceled Cybertruck, sell so rapidly on the used market? The combination of sustained high fuel prices and a dramatic drop in market value essentially ‘flips the script’ on EVs when they transition from latest to used status,” said iSeeCars Executive Analyst Karl Brauer.

Industry observers note that here are some numbers: EVs average 45.6 days on the market compared with 54.5 days for hybrids and 61.0 days for gasoline vehicles, according to iSeeCars. Those 45.6 days are 15.4 days less than gasoline vehicles and 8.9 days less than hybrids. The overall industry average is 59.5 days. Used EVs spend 25.2 percent less time on the market than gasoline vehicles, while hybrids spend 10.7 percent less time on the market than gasoline models.

According to recent reports, and despite the fact that many electric pickup trucks have recently been axed, EV pickups in general are particularly well represented among the fastest-selling used trucks, the study indicates. Four of the top 10 trucks are electric, and three of them rank among the top four fastest-selling pickups.

Industry observers note that the Tesla Cybertruck leads the rankings at 39.1 days on the market, selling in 26.4 fewer days than the average used pickup. The Ford Maverick hybrid ranks second at 43.3 days, followed by the fully electric Rivian R1T at 45.2 days and the Ford F-150 Lightning at 47.4 days.

Gasoline-powered trucks account for five of the top 10 positions, led by the Toyota Tacoma in fifth place at 50.1 days. The Ford Maverick hybrid ranks second, outperforming all electric trucks except the Cybertruck.

Recent findings suggest that however, not every electric pickup sells promptly. The Chevrolet Silverado EV ranks last among all 24 trucks, averaging 107.5 days on the market. That’s 68.4 days longer than the Cybertruck and 42.0 days longer than the average truck.

EVs also perform strongly in the broader used market, per iSeeCars, where they claim 16 of the 25 fastest-selling model rankings.

The Audi A4 Allroad is the fastest-selling used vehicle overall, averaging just over a month on the market, or 33.7 days, followed by the Lexus RX 450h+ plug-in hybrid at 35.0 days and Audi Q4 Sportback e-tron at 36.3 days.

Electric vehicles occupy six of the top 10 positions, including the Audi Q4 Sportback e-tron, Audi Q6 e-tron, Hyundai Ioniq 5, Lexus RZ 300e, Kia EV9, and BMW i5. All six average fewer than 39 days on the market, compared with the overall used vehicle average of 59.5 days.

According to recent reports, the Tesla Cybertruck ranks 16th among all used vehicles, averaging 39.1 days, while the Tesla Model 3 ranks 18th at 39.5 days and Tesla Model Y ranks 24th at 39.8 days. Electric models from Kia, Rivian, Subaru, Toyota, and Volkswagen also appear in the top 25.

It has been revealed that what are the slowest sellers on the used car market? The Cadillac Escalade IQ is the slowest-selling vehicle in the study, averaging 134.3 days on the market, more than twice the overall industry average. The Ford Escape plug-in hybrid ranks second at 127.3 days, followed by the Lincoln Corsair hybrid at 114.0 days.

The Silverado EV ranks fourth at 107.5 days, making it both the slowest-selling electric pickup and one of the slowest-selling used vehicles overall.

Two additional electric vehicles appear among the slowest-selling models: the electric Dodge Charger, averaging 98.2 days, and the GMC Hummer EV SUV, averaging 84.9 days.

Industry observers note that “There’s clearly a big shift from latest to used status for electric vehicles, especially when fuel prices have remained elevated for several months,” mentioned Brauer. “Used shoppers are all about value, which normally plays against electric vehicles. But when consumers see their fuel budget jump 20 to 30 percent, the value of not visiting a gas station leads straight to used EVs.”.
