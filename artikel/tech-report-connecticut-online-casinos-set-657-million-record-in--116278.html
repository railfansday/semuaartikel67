In a recent development, connecticut's online casino revenue reached a fresh high in October 2026, the first month taxed at a higher rate. Growth in the state sped up while the national market has been cooling.

In a recent development, connecticut’s two online casino operators, DraftKings and FanDuel, beat the previous record of $59.7 million, set in March, by about 10%. October revenue was also nearly $10 million higher than September’s total.

It has been revealed that wagering grew as well. Online casino handle topped $2 billion for the first time and was up 10% from October 2025. Revenue grew faster than handle in the month. That is consistent with operators keeping a larger share of each dollar wagered.

According to recent reports, the October result runs against a cooling national picture. US iGaming revenue grew 12% year over year in Q3 2026, the slowest rate on record, SiGMA World reported. Connecticut grew at that same 12% pace in the quarter.

Industry observers note that the American Gaming Association’s tracker reveals US iGaming revenue hit a record $3.05 billion in Q3 2026, up 13.5%. That was still the slowest quarterly growth since 2018. Connecticut had been part of the stronger phase. Its iGaming revenue reached $707.5 million in 2025, a 31.7% increase from 2024.

The jump from single-digit growth in September to a latest high a month later stands out. It suggests the national slowdown is not uniform across states. One month, however, does not set a trend.

Operators paid the state 18% of online casino gross gaming revenue through September 2026. They owe 20% for every period after that, the Department of Consumer Protection’s dataset notes show. That lower rate covered the five years after online casino gaming began on October 12, 2021.

In a recent development, the state’s take was already sizable before the increase. Online casino operators paid Connecticut $108,265,925 in 2025. Operators can still deduct some promotional credits before tax. That deduction has been capped at 15% of win since October 2023.

October’s figures show the higher rate did not coincide with any immediate drop in revenue. That matters for other states weighing online casino tax rates. It also matters for the two operators, which now share a larger tax bill in a market they split almost evenly.

October was only the second month in the past 12 in which DraftKings, rather than FanDuel, took the larger share of Connecticut online casino revenue, according to Poker Industry PRO. FanDuel has led the state in most months over that period.

It has been revealed that operators must send each month’s online casino payment to the state by the 15th of the following month. The first payment at the 20% rate was due by November 15, 2026. Payments on November revenue fall due by the 15th of December.
