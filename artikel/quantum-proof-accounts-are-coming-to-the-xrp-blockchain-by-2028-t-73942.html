Quantum-Proof Accounts Are Coming to the XRP Blockchain by 2028. That's Not Likely to Be a Game Changer for XRP.

Sources indicate that according to Ripple, the creator of XRP (XRP -1.60%), the XRP Ledger (XRPL) will have quantum-secure wallets by 2028. Ripple itself set that target date in April of this year, and the XRP Ledger Foundation officially placed the corresponding upgrade onto its roadmap on Nov. 4.

XRP could sure use a catalyst, with its price being down 73% from its all-time high set in July 2025. But making more secure wallets will probably not do what holders are hoping for regarding the coin's price, at least not outside a very narrow and improbable set of circumstances. Here's why this upgrade probably won't be a game changer.

First, let's get some clarity on what is even at stake with the issue of security against quantum computers.

In technical terms, each XRP wallet, like nearly all other crypto wallets, has a secret private key and also a public key that anyone can see on a wallet-scanning website. The private key is what grants access to move funds from the account.

Deriving the private key from the public one is impossible in practical terms with today's hardware. But a powerful enough quantum computer could theoretically do exactly that in the future, meaning an attacker with such a computer could steal funds from individual wallets and likely spark a mass exodus of capital once the theft was discovered.

According to recent reports, in March of this year, Alphabet's Google researchers published a paper estimating that breaking the cryptography that secures most crypto wallets could be possible with a significantly weaker quantum computer than previously expected. In a survey published in March 2026, the Global Risk Institute found that experts put the odds of such a machine arriving within 10 years at 28% to 49%.

CRYPTO · As of 8:49 AM ET 11/21/26.

Therefore, the XRPL's target preparedness date of 2028 probably leaves it with a decent margin of error if quantum computers advance faster than anticipated due to quantum computing companies ramping up their efforts.

Recent findings suggest that plus, given that quantum computers are massive, complex systems, the first dangerous machines will probably belong to governments and the largest companies building them, not ordinary hackers. However, an owner of such a machine may not announce it.

The direct impact of XRPL wallets becoming quantum-secure on its price will almost certainly be minimal.

The upgrade will not increase demand for the coin on its own. And while financial institutions might find it appealing that the XRPL is quantum-secure in advance, it's unclear whether they're willing to pay anything for it.

However, adapting early could pay out major in one specific future scenario.

If a powerful enough quantum computer becomes widely available years before most cryptocurrencies have implemented security measures and it's successfully used to carry out a major crypto hack, capital might flee to XRP as a safe haven.

But don't hold your breath for that to happen; even if the XRPL is an early mover in this domain, there are many other quantum-secure blockchains already in existence, and likely more to come.
