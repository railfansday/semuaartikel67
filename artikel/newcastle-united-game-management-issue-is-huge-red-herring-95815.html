The concern of Newcastle United Game Management has reared its head once again.

It always hurts when you take the lead and end up drawing, or even worse, lose.

Especially if it is a late goal that is conceded.

After the 2-2 draw with Arsenal, I have heard and read a fair few fans questioning the Newcastle United Game Management.

What these fans are questioning, is basically whyafter coming back to lead against Arsenal with 62 minutes on the clock, why couldn’t Matthias Jaissle and his players see out the rest of the match without conceding?

The 3 positives and 3 negatives piece on The Mag this Monday morning perfectly summed up the kind of thing I have been seeing.

Industry observers note that david Punton selecting ‘Game Management’ as one of his three negatives and saying: ‘Another winning position has been thrown away. The same thing happened down at Fulham. It drives you crackers at times. They surely must have to work on how to see a game out? It’s not rocket science.’.

So is this true, the Newcastle United Game Management a huge issue?

Or is it a big fat Red Herring?

First of all I have had a look at the 14 Newcastle United matches this season from purely a statistical viewpoint.

Do Newcastle United repeatedly take the lead and throw it away?

According to recent reports, newcastle United when scoring the opening goal of the game this season, what ended up the final result? Won 4 (Spurs, Millwall, Hull, Palace), Drawn 2 (Liverpool, Fulham) Lost 0.

According to recent reports, newcastle United when conceding the opening goal of the game this season, what ended up the final result? Won 1 (West Brom), Drawn 2 (Bournemouth, Arsenal) Lost 5 (Leeds, Coventry, Villa, Everton x2).

Recent findings suggest that which matches have Newcastle United scored the final goal of the game? Happened seven times (West Brom, Spurs, Bournemouth, Millwall, Leeds, Palace, Everton cup).

In a recent development, which matches have Newcastle United conceded the final goal of the game? Happened seven times (Liverpool, Hull, Coventry, Villa, Everton, Fulham, Arsenal).

Recent findings suggest that in his Positives and Negatives piece, David Punton states about the supposed failings of Newcastle United Game Management: ‘They surely must have to work on how to see a game out? It’s not rocket science.’.

I am not just having a go at David here but what exactly is this magical way of ensuring that there is zero chance of allowing the opposition to score once you have taken the lead?

Newcastle United went 2-1 up on 62 minutes, so Arsenal had what turned out to be 37 minutes to get an equaliser (and then potential winner…).

They are the best team in the Premier League and have the strongest bench.

What is the absolute certain route to not letting them score?

As the stats show above, Newcastle United have given as good as they have got this season when it comes to scoring first and the eventual result, compared to the games when the opposition have scored first.

If you are leading by a single goal and it is the last minute of a match, then all your defenders recklessly charge up the pitch and you concede, that would be really poor Newcastle United Game Management.

It would be really poor Newcastle United Game Management if leading and then gifted an equaliser when trying a daft risky pass on the edge of your own penalty area.

This didn’t happen on Saturday, nor indeed at Fulham in the previous match.

Arsenal scored with 21 minutes left to play when some excellent play got them up the pitch and Madueke scored a good goal, which even though it went through a Newcastle defender’s legs you think Lukas Hornicek should have saved it.

Newcastle scored in the 80th minute at Craven Cottage and Fulham had to throw everything at them, they got the benefit of a bit of a scramble in our penalty area and two deflections contributed to their equaliser.

This isn’t disastrous Newcastle United Game Management, this is football.

It is a huge Red Herring to claim this is a serious problem this season for Matthias Jaissle and his team.
