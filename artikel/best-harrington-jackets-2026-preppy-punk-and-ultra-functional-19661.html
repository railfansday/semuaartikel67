Recent findings suggest that few jackets have managed to cross as many style tribes—and decades—as the Harrington. In fact, if you were to enroll in Classic Menswear 101, the Harrington jacket would be a prerequisite. The transitional season staple—a favorite of Elvis, James Dean, Steve McQueen, and every member of The Clash—is constantly found across lookbooks and style Instagram accounts, because it does so many things so well.

According to recent reports, for one, it's incredibly practical: Water-resistant, pocket-forward, lightly insulating, and ever-adaptable to the day's conditions thanks to its full-zip and collar. But the almost-cropped length—and popped collar, if you so dare—is also a style swerve that makes every wearer feel one dose cooler than they did before. No wonder it’s been embraced over the decades by everyone from golfers to preppies to A-Listers and punks.

It has been revealed that the original Harrington—a Baracuta G9—is still available almost a century on from its release, and still excellent. But if you’re looking to broaden your horizons, today’s Harrington jackets aren’t resting on their laurels. Below, the standout versions worth adding to your rotation before we’re into parka territory.

Why is it called a Harrington jacket?

The funniest thing about the Harrington? It wasn’t originally called a Harrington at all. The original—and, as you've read above, our favorite—was called the “G9.” It was cooked up by Baracuta in 1937 in rainy old Manchester, England, as a golf jacket—the “G” stands for “golf.” (The idea was to make a garment that could take a beating from British weather without cramping your backswing.) But the jacket earned its now-iconic name thanks to Rodney Harrington, a character from the 1960s TV show Peyton Place, who wore one so often that the name just stuck.

How should a Harrington jacket fit?

According to recent reports, the traditional Harrington jacket fits close to the shoulders and chest, but still has enough room for a full range of arm motion—it is a golfing style, after all. The hem then typically sits either at the waist, or for a more stylized look, just above the belt line. It should be easy to spot from product shots whether the designer in question has stuck to this traditional shape, or elongated the style a touch. From that point, the only thing left to consider is whether you plan to wear it over thick sweaters, or lighter T-shirts and shirts. If it's the former, you may want to consider sizing up.
