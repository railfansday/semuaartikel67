Sam Altman on Nvidia without Jensen Huang: the industry will be ‘fine’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman notes the AI industry will be “fine” without Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang at the helm, even as he acknowledged the special role Huang has played in the AI boom.

Huang resigned last week from Nvidia after admitting to having an affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

Altman made his first public comments about Huang’s departure on Monday at Semafor’s Silicon Valley and the World event, where he also discussed topics ranging from data center pushback to recursive self-improvement.

Sources indicate that numerous executives and members of the AI industry have argued for Nvidia to reinstate Huang, including Elon Musk and Epic Games chief executive Tim Sweeney, who were among the signatories in a public letter. Many fear that Huang’s departure may cause Nvidia to rethink the enormous financial risks it has taken that have fueled the AI boom.

Huang is also reportedly setting up a “war room” of Silicon Valley executives to lobby for his return.

When asked whether he thought Huang should return to the tech giant, Altman stated: “I don’t think I know enough to answer that question.”.

He expressed empathy for Huang’s situation, citing his own ousting from OpenAI in 2023 after board members accused him of withholding information.

“It’s hard to imagine what the AI revolution without Jensen and the company he has built,” Altman said, adding that he sees Huang as a hero, and that “heroes have flaws.”.

OpenAI and Nvidia have had a close relationship for the last decade, one that has evolved from a simple chip supplier-customer relationship into a potentially circular financing arrangement, in which Nvidia’s money helps fund purchases of its own chips. Most recently, Nvidia agreed to provide a guarantee of up to $105 billion backing OpenAI’s Ohio data center project. That’s just one of many such circular deals Nvidia has made to bankroll the AI boom while fueling demand for its own chips.

Altman said he’s not worried that the Bank of Nvidia will dry up.

“Nvidia is, like, a behemoth of a tech giant,” he noted. “I think people still trust their guarantees very much.”.

Altman acknowledged the “ambition” Huang brought to the industry, the scale of which “has been quite crucial.” But he didn’t go so far as to agree with the many who worry about ripple effects from Huang’s departure.

“I think there’s so much demand, and it’s so clear that AI is real, and people want to use it, and the revenues and demand are growing so strongly that I think the industry will be fine,” Altman said. “But again, I think Jensen has played this extremely special and valuable role as someone to really drive things forward with great ambition.”.
