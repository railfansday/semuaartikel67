Google is bringing Seattle-based Howie, maker of an AI scheduling assistant that works from your inbox, into its Google Labs division.

Industry observers note that terms of the deal were not disclosed this week. Google Labs, the firm’s experimental arm, declared the acquisition in a post on X, saying it “can’t wait to learn from them as we build the next generation of intuitive AI tools together for small businesses.”.

In a recent development, howie CEO and co-founder Austin Petersmith stated on LinkedIn that the team will continue building Howie at Google. “Howie has always been built on top of Gmail and Google Calendar, so Google Labs is the perfect place for us to take this product to the next level,” he wrote.

Sources indicate that petersmith co-founded Howie around 2023 with CTO Dave Newman. The two first worked together in 2013 at Assembly, which Newman co-founded, and prototyped an AI assistant in 2015 before deciding the market wasn’t ready.

We’re excited to welcome Howie to Google Labs! The @Howie_ai team has spent years figuring out scheduling and calendar management. We can’t wait to learn from them as we build the next generation of intuitive AI tools together for small businesses 🤝 https://t.co/8bzjebeGdD.

People cc Howie on an email thread, and it coordinates meeting times, adds events to calendars, follows up as needed and flags scheduling conflicts. Customers set their own rules in an open-ended preferences document, such as “if it’s a pitch call, set it to 25 minutes and use Zoom.”.

Behind the scenes, Howie ran tasks through fine-tuned language models and looped in human scheduling experts when the AI wasn’t confident.

In a recent development, howie raised a $6 million seed round led by True Ventures in September 2025, when it rolled out publicly out of private beta. At the time, it had more than 1,000 paying customers, including startup founders, venture capitalists, recruiters, consultants and journalists, and was scheduling more than 5,000 meetings a week.

The deal comes as Google and rivals including OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta race to build AI agents that complete tasks on behalf of people. Google Labs acquired music generation platform ProducerAI earlier this year.
