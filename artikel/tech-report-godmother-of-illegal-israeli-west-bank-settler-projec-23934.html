Australia has announced another tranche of sanctions on individuals and organisations involved in “illegal settlement expansion and human rights abuses against Palestinians” in the West Bank.

In a statement, the foreign affairs minister, Penny Wong, remarked it was the first time Australian sanctions had targeted a construction tech giant involved in the building of settlements.

The sanctions prohibit Australians and Australian businesses from trading with or providing financial or other support to three individuals and four organisations.

“We are taking these actions because the rapid expansion of settlements and settler violence are extinguishing the possibility of a two-state solution,” Wong’s statement mentioned.

“This includes the E1 settlement where demolitions and the displacement of Palestinians have occurred in recent weeks. Israeli settlements in the West Bank are illegal under international law.”.

Among those sanctioned is Daniella Weiss and her Israeli settler organisation, the Nachala movement, which the government stated has provided support for outposts and settlements that have perpetrated serious human rights abuses.

Weiss is known as the “godmother” of the settler movement and was sanctioned by the UK in May 2025, shortly after she appeared on Louis Theroux’s documentary The Settlers.

She and Nachala have also advocated for the settlement of Israelis in Gaza and the expulsion of Palestinians from the area.

The 80-year-old was previously the mayor of Kedumim, an Israeli settlement in the West Bank where far-right Israeli minister Bezalel Smotrich, who has also been sanctioned by the Australian government, also lives.

Prominent West Bank settler Eliav Libi has also been sanctioned, as well as the Eyal Hari Yehuda construction company which has helped develop settlements and outposts and demolished Palestinian structures in the West Bank.

Wong stated the sanctioned individuals and organisations had “actively participated in the planning, coordination and establishment of newly introduced settlements, including raising funds, recruitment of volunteers and providing support to violent settlers”.

The government said the sanctions were coordinated with the UK, who unveiled sanctions on Israeli companies planning to build illegal settlements.

The UK foreign secretary, Ed Miliband, said the “status quo in the West Bank and Gaza is unacceptable”.

“That is why my message on E1 is clear: do not bid, do not build, and do not profit from the destruction of the two-state solution. And we will not stand by while those blocking desperately needed aid deepen the intolerable suffering of civilians in Gaza.”.

More settlements had been approved under the four years of Benjamin Netanyahu’s government than in the previous 20 years.

Israel’s possible newly introduced prime minister, Gadi Eisenkot, has said that he would take a tougher approach than Netanyahu on settler violence.

Wong stated she had spoken to her Israeli counterpart, Gideon Sa’ar, on Tuesday night, and outlined Australia’s position.

Industry observers note that “We’ve made clear we will continue to take action in respect of those involved in the settlement enterprise. The settlement enterprise is violent.”.

Wong had signalled further sanctions in September, after the government ruled out an import ban on trade from illegal settlements in the West Bank.

Recent analysis by human rights organisation Global Echo showed 85 of 132 food shipments over eight years had contained more than US$5m of organic dates grown in settlements.

On Wednesday, the foreign affairs minister stated the government was still considering further action.

“We’ll continue to look at targeted measures we can take to push back against what the current Israeli government is doing,” Wong told journalists.

When asked whether Wong was concerned Israel would respond to Australian sanctions with countermeasures, the minister remarked it had done in the past.

It has been revealed that “We’ve seen the Israeli government take action against Australia, including asking our diplomats to leave Ramallah. We’ve also seen the [now] caretaker prime minister, Netanyahu, be very public in his criticism of Australia.

“So obviously, there is a reaction to Australia acting, but we think this is the right thing to do.”.

The Greens leader, David Shoebridge, said the sanctions did not go far enough to stop illegal settlements or take action against the Israeli government.

The minor party has pushed the government to join other nations including the UK and Canada to ban imports of products made in illegal settlements.

“This announcement won’t touch most of the hundreds of businesses that have a role in this brazen breach of international law, or the Israeli government that backs them,” Shoebridge mentioned.

The government warned Australian businesses they must not bid for construction tenders for illegal settlement developments.

Individuals breaching sanctions can attract a penalty of up to 10 years’ imprisonment and a fine of up to $910,000 or three times the value of the relevant transaction.

Businesses can be fined up to $3.64m or three times the value of the relevant transaction.

The Australia Palestine Advocacy Network president, Nasser Mashni, pushed for further sanctions against individuals, companies and the Israeli government.

“Australia has sanctioned a company that builds illegal settlements. Now sanction the Israeli government that orders Palestinian homes demolished,” he remarked.
