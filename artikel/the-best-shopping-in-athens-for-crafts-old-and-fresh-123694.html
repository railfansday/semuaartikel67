The Best Shopping in Athens for Crafts, Old and Fresh

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Resplendent, timeless Athens is no stranger to feats of invention and imagination. From the hand-carved marble stelae and temple friezes of the stately Acropolis to painted terra-cotta vessels unearthed throughout Attica, this is a city with millennia's worth of artistic output. You'll see this legacy on full display today in the museums and galleries that protect its artifacts. But, crucially, a upcoming wave of makers are sculpting ceramics, weaving baskets, and vigorously curating heirlooms for the contemporary eye. There is plenty to discover in the historic center—at Monastiraki's sprawling outdoor market and ceramics studios, and Syntagma's perfumeries—though that's only the start. Athens may be among the world's oldest cities, but in bustling flea markets and quiet showrooms, its many creatives are paving the path forward.

The Acropolis rises over Monastiraki Square and Tzistarakis Mosque.

It has been revealed that following a stint in Paris designing for Jean Paul Gaultier and Kenzo over 25 years ago, Lito Karakostanoglou set up her jewelry brand, Lito, in Kolonaki. Treat the boutique and its nature-inspired offerings as a cabinet of curiosities. The showroom upstairs is your go-to for one-off pieces. Meanwhile, renowned family jeweler Lalaounis, on Voukourestiou Street, contains glittering collections that take cues from Nubian and Hellenistic art. Harder to find, but easier on the purse, is Neso Studio, in a prewar residential building on Praxitelous. Gold, gold-plated, and silver pieces are handmade there using wax casting techniques. It's best to check ahead for opening times.

Former managing director of label Yiorgos Eleftheriades, Yiorgos Mo turned his knack for collecting into Mo Vintage Athens in 2020. At his tiny shop in arty Koukaki, nab designer pieces from the 1970s to '90s—keep an eye out for Mugler rarities. A 20-minute walk north will take you to L'On, focusing on '80s and '90s garb; think Armani bodysuits and denim sprinkled amid garments from lesser-known Greek labels. Among Psirri's graffiti-sprayed shutters a few blocks over, shop custom-designed Mantility silk scarves and Homatino ceramics on display at Monument Hotel Athens. Nearby, hat workshop Savapile, led by Lisa Sarigiannidou, is the only remaining straw-hat maker in Greece still sewing Italian braid with traditional millinery techniques.

Sheep’s wool kilim and handloomed carpets are draped on a wall of Anthologist.

In eclectic Monastiraki, Mon Coin Studio—which features works from some 300 Greek ceramists—is equally varied. Its collaborative efforts culminate in three annual group demonstrates, one of which is always dedicated to a region rich with a ceramics past such as Sifnos, Lesbos, or Crete. Head north to Vathi for blown-glass amphorae and handloomed throws at Anthologist; it's likely founder Andria Mitsakos will offer you tea and dates as you peruse. South of the Acropolis, shop at Ere Athens, where you can tuck into a cappuccino and banana bread (a perk of going to a store that's run by a pastry chef) while browsing locally made pottery neatly laid atop wood cabinets. Popular picks include paint-splattered cake stands by potter Nicola Fasano and painterly vases from Naked Summers.

Founded by brothers Giorgos and Apostolis Korres, The Naxos Apothecary near Syntagma Square creates tinctures and creams inspired by the Med. Scientific vessels and cloaked candles decorate the space, populated by staff wearing navy lab coats reminiscent of an old-school pharmacy. Leave no display case unexplored, then head upstairs for the herbal tea bar and an outpost of label Ancient Greek Sandals. Just as curated is perfume boutique and florist Phāon in Kolonaki, owned by art director and set designer duo Alexandros Kalogiros and Dimitra Louana Marlanti. Here, find soaps and scents from Astier de Villatte and Ormaie Paris, as well as golden candlesticks by Vasiliki and ceramic lotus flowers courtesy of Despina Charitonidi. Gnocchi the Jack Russell gives everyone a warm welcome.

Hyper Hypo’s selection of books.

Noting a lack of art and design bookshops in the city, graphic designer Stathis Mitropoulos and former fashion editor Andreas Kokkino opened their own. Hyper Hypo, meaning “above and below,” offers literature on photography, interior design, local culture, and more. Dimitris Papaioannou's Sketches From Life, drawn over three summers in Anafi, and Eleanor Lines's illustrated Doors of Kypseli stand out against the Aegean blue walls, while a pair of neon-lit eyes glow above the counter. Plus, Hyper Hypo's seasonal event programming and rare book pop-ups make a visit all the more memorable.
