What happens now for Bayern Munich and Musiala?

Jamal Musiala came off the bench with 14 minutes remaining in Bayern's defeat by Lille on Wednesday.

Bayern Munich - and representatives of one of their most prominent players - have collided over recent weeks.

During the summer, the public became aware that their star 23-year-old midfielder Jamal Musiala suffered from absence seizures.

This neurological condition causes a brief, sudden lapse in awareness and responsiveness. Musiala had two of these lapses during pre-season games, but episodes already happened during the previous year.

At first, the shock among fans and observers was huge. Bayern kept Musiala's condition a secret for some time, and only a few insiders knew about it. In the days after Musiala's lapse during a friendly against Heidenheim, speculations arose over whether the player's medical matter had been known before he signed a highly lucrative contract extension in February 2025.

In a recent development, but it has been confirmed by sources close to the team - and Bayern themselves - that the condition had not been detected before April 2025. One source described Musiala, at the time of signing his new contract, as being "in great health".

In April 2025, days after suffering a muscle tear, Musiala caused a high-speed car crash which cost him his driver's licence. Munich's public prosecutor's office responded to a recent enquiry saying there is no evidence that the accident was caused by a lapse, nor that it was the cause of Musiala's condition.

Sources indicate that nevertheless, checks a couple of days after the crash revealed he was suffering from absence seizures. Musiala's camp consulted numerous experts and hired doctors to treat the player, with the knowledge of Jochen Hahne, Bayern's team doctor. How close the consultations between the external doctors and Bayern's medical staff are remains a point of discussion, among many other things in this complex matter.

Sources indicate that soon after, Musiala's football-related injuries became an matter. He broke his fibula and injured his ankle during the Fifa Club World Cup after colliding with then PSG goalkeeper Gianluigi Donnarumma. He needed months to recover and suffered a subsequent ankle injury, likely related to the leg injury.

According to recent reports, after the 2026 World Cup he had a metal plate removed from his left foot, which affected his pre-season preparation. Musiala just recently recovered from a weeks-long absence due to a hamstring tear.

However, his comeback lasted just two games - he came off the bench in Bayern's 1-1 Bundesliga draw against Cologne, and in the Champions League defeat by Lille, but the club confirmed on Friday that he has torn his right hamstring and will be out until further notice.

On top of that, new incidents related to his neurological condition have come to light. Days before his recent return to training, Musiala had another lapse, just like he did on the morning of a Champions League game against Real Madrid in April.

All of this is the tragic story of a highly talented footballer who might never return to his previous level and must deal with a medical condition for the rest of his life.

It has been revealed that absence seizures require daily medication. There are other high-level athletes, such as England rugby union player Tommy Freeman, who have had to deal with the same condition.

Sources indicate that A controversy erupted last Friday, as Bayern faced Cologne in what was supposed to be an ordinary league game. Karl-Heinz Rummenigge, once chairman of the board and now a member of Bayern's supervisory board, asked a TV crew to come to his section in the stands.

The unscheduled post-game interview unfolded in a way that no-one at Bayern, not even other members of the club's hierarchy, expected.

"He has a health concern, which is known, and it is known that he has a contract which includes hefty sums of money," Rummenigge said. "You need to talk to the family, to the player and most importantly to the agency, because these sums are - with all due respect - not justifiable any longer.".

A day later, chairman of the board Jan-Christian Dreesen and club president Herbert Hainer tried to defuse the situation at the yearly membership assembly, but were also attacked by club member Jonathan Hoffmann.

Hoffmann, a neurologist, stated it wouldn't help to go after Musiala publicly, for which he received applause from many of the present members.

One might wonder why an experienced deal-maker like Rummenigge would willingly put down one of his players who could still be valuable to the club, even if it's just for a sale.

Industry observers note that he might have been agitated by the way the home game against Cologne went. Harry Kane suffered a ligament tear in his ankle early in the game and star winger Michael Olise had to leave the stadium on crutches.

But, even more importantly, it has become clear that Rummenigge was unhappy with how contract negotiations with Musiala's camp - between October 2024 and February 2025 - unfolded.

Bayern and particularly Max Eberl, the club's director of sport, were running out of time, as Musiala's contract would have ended with the conclusion of the 2025-26 season. If they hadn't reached an agreement before April or May 2025, Bayern would have felt obliged to sell Musiala during the summer transfer window.

Recent findings suggest that musiala played an impressive Euro 2024 for Germany and was one of Bayern's best players during the period of negotiations. Among others, Real Madrid were interested, but only for a free transfer in 2026.

Keep in mind, Musiala has quite a unique profile as a No 10 that can also come from the wing and break down low-block defences with his dribbles and passing.

In a recent development, under pressure and confronted by a shrewd negotiator in Christian Schmid, Musiala's agent and a trained lawyer, Bayern agreed to a salary package that could earn Musiala, in a best-case scenario, Â£21m a year, with Â£15m as a base salary. Bayern also agreed to grant a signing fee of close to Â£25m, paid over the stretch of the contract. Musiala even got his wish granted of receiving his own box at Allianz Arena.

Some reports indicated that the fact Musiala's deal includes sick pay for six months, in the case of injury or illness, could be interpreted as circumstantial evidence that the player's representatives may have known about his condition beforehand. A standard Bundesliga contract only offers sick pay for six weeks before insurance would shoulder the payments.

But even six months is a short period in comparison. Premier League players receive their full basic wage for the first 18 months. And, when Bayern negotiate with top players, they ultimately compete against Europe's elite.

Rummenigge, and to a lesser extent his long-time boardroom compatriot Uli Hoeness, made Eberl responsible for the unfavourable terms - arguing that Schmid essentially played Eberl.

Eberl has been the target of criticism from the two former powerbrokers for quite some time. At a recent supervisory board meeting, Hoeness is mentioned to have attacked Eberl for his inactivity over the Musiala issue in the past six months.

That being said, Bayern bought into the Musiala hype for a couple of years, hoping that they would have a superstar of their own who could be marketed across the globe.

The relationship between the club and agent Schmid has been strained to say the least, with Rummenigge particularly disliking him. Some sources believe this was one of the root causes for Rummenigge's recent attack on live TV.

Industry observers note that when a 16-year-old Kennet Eichhorn came on the market during the summer, as he wanted to leave second-division side Hertha Berlin, Bayern were interested but ultimately dropped out of the race for the highly touted midfielder. Eichhorn is represented by Schmid and ended up joining Bayer Leverkusen.

Bayern boss Vincent Kompany (left) has himself recently been diagnosed with meningeal irritation - an inflammation of the protective membrane surrounding the brain and spinal cord.

Hoeness met with Schmid during the summer to discuss how to proceed with Musiala, but the player's agent did not indicate that they would agree to a wage reduction. Hoeness called the matter a "dilemma" for Bayern during a TV show earlier this month, but did not go as far as Rummenigge.

Industry observers note that as long as Musiala is not medically forced to retire, he will receive the wage that was agreed upon in February 2025. Even if Bayern would intend to part ways with the player, they would need to do everything in their power to get the player as close to his previous form as possible.

Industry observers note that the release clause of Â£125m, which could be triggered next year, seems unrealistic. Any comments by Bayern bosses against Musiala, or further leaks to the media, may indeed lower his market value down to zero - unless the player can simply shake things off and perform again.

Eberl and head coach Vincent Kompany have come out to back Musiala. Joshua Kimmich, one of Bayern's veteran players, also criticised the treatment of Musiala after a 2-1 loss to Lille in the Champions League on Wednesday. "Of course, the club is above all and bigger than the player, but not bigger than the human. This is an absolute no-go and also not Bayern-like," Kimmich said.

Even football players' union Fifpro condemned Rummenigge's comments. On Wednesday, Bayern put out a statement saying that a meeting between the club and Musiala's representatives took place on Tuesday, in which it was agreed that the player's health should be paramount.

It has been revealed that whether the damage can be repaired is up in the air. As Bayern have a number of attacking players sidelined, they need a healthy and mentally prepared Musiala in the coming weeks and months.
