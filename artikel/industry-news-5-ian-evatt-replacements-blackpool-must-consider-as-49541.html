Blackpool have confirmed the sacking of Ian Evatt following a year in charge at Bloomfield Road, with the Tangerines now intent on reviving their fortunes under fresh management and avoiding a battle against relegation from League One.

Evatt had led the side to a respectable 13th-placed finish during the previous campaign after taking over from Steve Bruce, though the former Bolton Wanderers boss would struggle to get going once again at Bloomfield Road this term.

Last Saturday's 1-0 defeat away to fellow strugglers AFC Wimbledon marked the sixth in just ten games for Blackpool, who now find themselves only positioned above Doncaster Rovers in 21st by virtue of goal difference at this moment in time.

Blackpool's key decision-makers eventually listened to intensified sack calls from supporters by relieving Evatt of his duties on Thursday afternoon, and whilst the relegation-threatened third-tier outfit have confirmed that Steve Thompson will be in charge for this weekend's clash with Wigan Athletic, the search for a replacement has already begun.

With that in mind, FLW has taken a close look at FIVE managers who Blackpool should be seriously considering as potential successors to Evatt.

One manager who should surely feature within Blackpool's thinking is Andy Woodman who, remarkably enough, currently finds himself out of work following a controversial EFL sacking for the ages from League One rivals Bromley.

Woodman had, of course, inspired the plucky Ravens' unprecedented rise into the EFL pyramid for the first time in their history before winning the League Two title against all odds last season, although matters both on and off the field in that particular corner of South London have not been plain-sailing as of late.

Nonetheless, it was a huge surprise when the 52-year-old's historic 289-match reign at Bromley came to an abrupt end last month, and one suspects he won't be sat around unattached for too long.

Woodman's resourceful ability to get the very best out of what was at his disposal with the Ravens certainly reads well, and he frequently elevated and identified talent with great success.

His strong body of work, albeit at a lower level, means that he should be entering the thinking of the Tangerines hierarchy in the coming days.

A previous specialist of the firefighting genre, Gary Rowett's knack in that field has faded in recent times and he may need to re-attempt his hand in a lower division.

As such, Rowett could represent the most outstanding realistic candidate available to Blackpool right now, with the former Derby County, Millwall and Stoke City boss having been linked with the vacancy at promotion hopefuls Bradford City prior to Michael Duff's appointment.

The last few years haven't reflected too kindly on Rowett, who led both Birmingham City and Leicester to relegations while setting Oxford United up for the same fate in the same season that he was in charge of the Foxes' demotion last time out.

Therefore, the wily 52-year-old may not score favourably in the consideration of Championship decision-makers and could instead drop to League One for the first time in his managerial career.

Previously, of course, Rowett has excelled in firing teams towards safety and, though much of his fresher work has been conducted on a short-term basis, there's no reason why he couldn't provide a stable, steady and longer-term presence with the Seasiders.

Matt Bloomfield, meanwhile, having reportedly held talks with Blackpool following the aforementioned Bruce's departure in October of last year, is only likely to re-emerge as a contender for the Tangerines' top job in the near future.

Bloomfield was ultimately unable to prevent Oxford's relegation to League One after succeeding Rowett, yet many believed his subsequent sacking from the Kassam Stadium to represent a surprise and even harsh call - albeit Aaron Ramsey has since vindicated the Yellows' change in direction.

Still aged just 42, Bloomfield's stock has taken a few hits and he's perhaps yet to recover from an ill-fated spell in charge of Luton Town, in which he fell narrowly short of ensuring survival on the 2024/25 second-tier term's final-day before failing to inspire a tangible promotion push in League One.

He has, however, previously excelled at this level, with Wycombe Wanderers having been situated just two points behind eventual-runaway champions Birmingham at the time of his departure to Luton in January 2025.

Bloomfield may now require a sustained spell back in League One leading a side with more modest expectations such as the Chairboys, where he was able to work his magic - and Blackpool can provide just that.

In contrast to Rowett and Bloomfield, Stuart Maynard lacks pedigree above League Two level, but that shouldn't rule the ex-Notts County and York City boss out of contention.

Indeed, at the age of 46 and with an obvious appetite to prove his managerial mettle higher up the pyramid, Maynard could be an ideal candidate for Blackpool in their efforts to not only survive, but begin laying the foundational blueprint for longer-term stability and success.

Much like Woodman at Bromley, Maynard's dismissal from York in the wake of promotion back to League Two following a decade-long exile as National League champions with no fewer than 108 points was scarcely believable. The difference, of course, is that Maynard did not even receive the opportunity to lead the Ministermen into the current campaign, and their loss could turn out to be Blackpool's gain.

Maynard has a solid body of work to boast of from his time at Notts County, where he failed to deliver promotion but did take the Magpies into the fourth-tier play-offs during their second season back at the level and ultimately put the foundations in place for Martin Paterson to go one better with the Meadow Lane outfit.

Blackpool fans, having sat through an overly-patient and slow-paced possession-based style under Evatt, would likely warm to a more dynamic, attack-oriented approach through Maynard, who would tick many boxes for the League One strugglers.

A more left-field option with greater risk but no shortage of potential upside, Ryan Mason remains available following his departure from West Bromwich Albion at the start of the year and represents an interesting candidates for lower-ranked EFL outfits to consider.

Mason was highly-thought-of during his time coaching Tottenham Hotspur and even taking occasional interim charge of the Lilywhites, which meant that many were surprised when the one-cap ex-England international failed to get going at West Brom.

He would take just nine wins from 27 matches across all competitions with the Baggies before being dismissed ahead of a relegation battle in the deeper stages of the previous campaign, with James Morrison going on to prove just how much his two predecessors in Mason and Eric Ramsey had underperformed at the Hawthorns.

It was, however, Mason's first senior, full-time gig, and it would be harsh to judge him entirely from that alone.

Mason highlighted plenty of signs at West Brom that suggest he'd be a more-than-capable candidate somewhat lower down the ladder, with his progressive style, experience with young talent and connections within the game all representing healthy selling points for a club in Blackpool's position.

It's essentially a case of risk versus reward, and only Blackpool's key boardroom figures can decide whether it's a gamble worth taking amid the threat of relegation to League Two.

They do have a history of appointing older managers with their best years very much in the past, though, and there are worse options than going for a change of tact and rolling the dice on Mason.
