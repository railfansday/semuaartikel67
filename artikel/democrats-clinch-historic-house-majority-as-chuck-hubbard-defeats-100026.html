Democrats have stormed back into power in the House, securing the largest majority of any party since 1992 in a sweeping midterm victory that will reshape the balance of power in Washington and leave Republicans with significantly less influence over the chamber.

In a recent development, the victory was cemented after Democrat Chuck Hubbard was elected to the House from North Carolina, according to The Associated Press (AP). He defeated longtime Republican incumbent Rep. Virginia Foxx, who chaired the powerful House Rules Committee, which determines which bills reach the House floor for a vote.

With several general election results still being called, Democrats have picked up 44 seats so far, giving them at least 258 seats in the House, their largest majority in more than three decades, according to The AP.

Meanwhile, Republicans have been left with at least 173 seats.

Four House races remain undecided in Minnesota, Tennessee and two December runoffs in Louisiana, according to The AP.

Recent findings suggest that the party's projected majority will surpass its post-election totals in every election since Bill Clinton first won the presidency in 1992, when Democrats secured 258 seats, the outlet noted. The party's next-largest showing reportedly came in 2008, when Barack Obama was elected president and Democrats won 257 House seats.

The shift in the 435-member House will set the stage for a dramatic power struggle in Washington.

Democrats are also poised to take control of the Senate, where they secured a 56-seat majority in the 100-member chamber, their largest advantage in more than a decade.

Democrats scored some of their biggest victories in traditionally competitive states and Republican strongholds alike, including Texas, Georgia, Kansas, Iowa, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina.
