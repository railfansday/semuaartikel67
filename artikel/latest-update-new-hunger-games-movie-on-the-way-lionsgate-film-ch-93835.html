The Hunger Games franchise has whetted appetites for more films, given the success of its latest title.

Lionsgate released The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping over the weekend, with the movie grossing a commanding $119.3 million from 4,018 North American locations to open in first place at the box office. The feature adaptation of author Suzanne Collins‘ 2025 prequel novel notched a better opening frame than the two most recent entries in the six-film series that kicked off with 2012’s Jennifer Lawrence-led The Hunger Games. During a conversation with The Hollywood Reporter to celebrate the success of Sunrise on the Reaping, Lionsgate Motion Picture Group chair Adam Fogelson emphasizes that the studio would love to collaborate again with Collins on another project in the franchise. Director Francis Lawrence‘s latest movie stars Joseph Zada as Haymitch Abernathy, portrayed in the first four films by Woody Harrelson. The $150 million-budgeted feature is set 24 years before the events of Collins’ original 2008 book.

According to recent reports, “We have complete respect for Suzanne as the architect,” Fogelson says. “I have told her that, if and when she is ready for me to read something else, I will drop everything and go anywhere in the world to get to read it. And I will sit patiently and wait for that phone call.”.

Sources indicate that the Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping had a far bigger impact at the box office than the 2023 prequel film The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, which starred Tom Blyth, Rachel Zegler and Hunter Schafer. Centering on the backstory for villainous President Snow — portrayed in the latest movie by Ralph Fiennes — Songbirds and Snakes opened to $44 million domestically before ending its run with $166.4 million domestically and a global sum of $361.8 million.

“All of us understood that the story that Suzanne wanted to tell on Songbirds was a challenging one and that she was inspired to tell it,” Fogelson explains. “She did it in an extraordinary way. Clearly, the movie worked really well for the audience that saw it, but the origin story of the villain — with no grand Hunger Games spectacle at the center of the story — is a challenging ask.”.

It has been revealed that he continues, “But we are in very regular, very authentic dialogue with this giant fan base, and we had plenty of indications that a story like this one could relight the fire in an exceptional way. It’s why we chose to invest the way that we did, both in the production blueprint and in the casting. And so, we were confident that there were plenty of signals that, if you gave the audience a story like this one, they were ready to return in big numbers.”.

Sources indicate that as far as what a potential sequel movie could entail, Fogelson notes that the franchise’s supporters have offered suggestions about potentially fertile storytelling opportunities within the dystopian world. The Sunrise on the Reaping book sold 1.5 million copies in its first week, making it the biggest launch among Collins’ titles in the series.

According to recent reports, “The fan base have definitely let us all know that there are other stories they would be thrilled to both read and watch,” claims Fogelson. “We’ll see if any of them spark Suzanne to go ahead and write them.”.

The exec pointed out that each major character reveal during the film’s Newly introduced York premiere was met with feverish cheers from the audience. The new movie’s expansive cast includes Jesse Plemons, Glenn Close, Kieran Culkin, Elle Fanning, Mckenna Grace, Maya Hawke and Whitney Peak.

“It starts with the fact that Suzanne decided to tell a story that the audience had clearly been desperate for — anchored by a character that the audience both loved and was fascinated to learn more about — and then the filmmaking team and the production group at the studio partnered to cast and make an exceptional version of that story,” Fogelson indicates. “This is one of the most exceptionally put-together casts I’ve ever been a part of.”.
