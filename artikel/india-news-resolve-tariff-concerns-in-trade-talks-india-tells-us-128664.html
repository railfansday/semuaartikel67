Game 3 of the classical chess world championship is up and running in Geneva now.

Javokhir Sindarov offered Gukesh the exact same opening as in the first game, starting with his king's pawn and inviting a quick queen exchange and the Berlin defense from black.

But the defending champion opted for a different approach, not initiating the same early endgame as he did on Tuesday.

That means both players have rather more firepower and complexity still on the board after the opening phase of play than the last time the Uzbek challenger played with white.

As the initial flurry of moves starts slowing down and the players start making use of their thinking time, Sindarov's queen has adopted a powerful but potentially vulnerable position in the center of the board, while Gukesh appears to be barricading his king in a defensive fortress on the king's side of the board.

Normal service resumes at this point: A resolution to the game could take several hours, depending on how it progresses, and a draw is considered the most likely outcome.

Game 3 will start at around 1400 GMT, and could last deep into India's evening or night if it goes the distance.

Wednesday's game, however, finished at a surprisingly sociable hour even for those watching on the subcontinent.

That's because Gukesh Dommaraju, playing with the white pieces for the first time in the match, offered a draw an an unexpectedly early juncture in the game.

Both players still had almost all their pieces and had played just 20 moves each when they fell into a repeating series of moves and alerted the arbiter, who declared the early draw.

Challenger Javokhir Sindarov, facing a somewhat unorthodox London opening from Gukesh, had appeared to neutralize any early advantage for white and it wasn't clear how the defending champion could try to launch an attack.

The match score stands at 1-1 after two drawn games, and after Thursday's game the players will have a day off, before returning on Saturday, Sunday and Monday for games 4, 5 and 6.

India's top court on Thursday tightened government proposals for labeling food packaging to warn about high contents of salt, sugar and fat.

It stated that rather than having to glitch a single warning for high content as the government had proposed, products with high salt, sugar and fat contents should carry individual warnings about all three.

The court also urged multinational companies to adopt globalized standards, rather than adapting their products to meet regional or national regulations, noting concerns about product composition being different in India than other parts of the world.

Industry observers note that "If true, such double standards are deeply troubling," the court remarked in its judgment. "A consumer in India is entitled to no less protection, and no less information, than a consumer in the United States or the United Kingdom.".

India's government recently watered down its own food labeling prepares after campaigns from companies like Coca-Cola and groups backing Nestle and PepsiCo, which argued that warning labels were ineffective.

However, several companies have implemented similar measures voluntarily in Europe. And varying health and safety standards by region can famously lead to discrepancies in the contents of products with the same name in different markets.

Most sugary soft drinks, for example, have to be sold in Europe with considerable lower sugar contents and fewer additives than is necessary to meet regulatory approval in the US, among other markets.

As the world's most populous country and with rapidly rising living standards, India is already a massive market that is likely to increase yet further in importance in the coming years for global food and drinks giants.

The Supreme Court said that in future, warning labels should carry a "standardized pictorial representation" of the nutrient-of-concern, together with with written warnings saying "High Fat," "High Sugar," "High Salt," or as many of the trio as are required.

Thursday marks 18 years since the Indian city of Mumbai was rocked by a 60-hour terror siege that left over 160 people dead and hundreds more injured.

Devendra Fadnavis, the Chief Minister of Maharashtra state where Mumbai is located, on Thursday attended a ceremony commemorating the anniversary of the attacks and paid tribute to the victims, which included civilians and security personnel.

"Today, on the anniversary of that attack, we all have to make a resolution that we will never let such an attack happen in our country while we pay our deepest gratitude to all the people who sacrificed their lives for this country," the minister noted.

He also commended the city's resilience.

"Terrorists cannot demean the spirit of Mumbai," Fadnavis said.

On November 26, 2008 — 10 Islamist militants belonging to the Pakistan-based militant Lashkar-e-Taiba (LeT) —  carried out attacks across the Indian financial hub, including the iconic Taj Mahal Palace Hotel, the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus railway station, a popular restaurant and the Nariman House Jewish community center.

Nine of the attackers were killed by the Indian security forces, while one was captured alive and executed later in 2012.

India on Thursday marked 77 years since the formal adoption of its Constitution on November 26, 1949.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi, who officially declared November 26 as Constitution Day or Samvidhan Divas in 2015, hailed the Constitution as "the priceless heritage of Indian democracy.".

Before 2015, the date was observed as Law Day.

"Greetings on Constitution Day, a day to pay homage to the makers of our Constitution and an occasion to reaffirm our commitment to strengthening the values enshrined in it," he wrote on X.

Modi also used the observance to attack the opposition.

In an article published on his website, the prime minister wrote that the constitutional democracy had a "clearly defined role for the opposition.".

He deemed the opposition as becoming "more of an obstructionist.".

Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi called India's Constitution "the foundation" of Indian democracy and the "greatest weapon against injustice.".

"It is the shield of every citizen's dignity, the strength of every voice - the guardian of justice, liberty, equality and fraternity." Gandhi mentioned — echoing the four core values enshrined in the Preamble to the Constitution of India.

India's Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution two years after the country gained independence from British colonial rule.

It came into force two months later on January 26, 1950, establishing India as a Republic.

The most prominent leader of India's Dalit Community, Dr B R Ambedkar —  also referred to as Babasaheb —  was the chief architect of the Indian Constitution.

In its statement on planned upcoming US tariffs, India's commerce ministry urged the US to resolve trade disputes and complete negotiations over a new trade deal.

The ministry said that it was "committed to working closely" with the US towards an "early conclusion" of the trade deal.

The US proposed a tariff rate of 25% on goods from India, reduced to 17% if a trade deal is reached.

US President Donald Trump and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi first announced talks for the US-India Bilateral Trade Agreement (BTA) last year.

But negotiations were delayed over strained ties and global conflict, reestablishing headway only this year.

The two nations released the framework for an interim trade agreement in February. As part of that deal, India would "eliminate or reduce tariffs" on all US industrial products and a range of US food and agricultural goods.

However, the negotiations for a final deal were once again stalled after the US Supreme Court invalidated much of Trump's sweeping global tariffs.

In October, India's Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman described the talks as having reached "a plateau, beyond which giving or taking might be very, very difficult.".

But US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said a few days later that Newly introduced Delhi and Washington were "about as close as you can be without having an executed agreement.".

Facing a new United States tariff threat, India has stressed that any trade concerns be addressed within the framework of the ongoing trade deal talks between the two countries.

Upcoming Delhi's statement comes after Washington on Wednesday unveiled a new wave of tariffs on 14 of its major trade partners, including India, claiming that the economies were promoting excess industrial capacity.

"India and the United States are engaged in negotiations for a Bilateral Trade Agreement. It has been India's position that any trade-related concerns should be addressed within the framework of these negotiations," a statement from Newly introduced Delhi's Ministry of Commerce and Industry said late Wednesday.

The Office of the US Trade Representative (USTR) confirmed latest import duties of 20% to 40%  to counter the alleged excessive manufacturing abroad as it completed a monthslong probe into alleged unfair trade practices involving 16 trading partners.

The fresh tariffs will be subject to a period of public comment before taking effect.

"Today, after lengthy investigations, Ambassador Greer has determined that the acts, policies, and practices of various economies designed to artificially support manufacturing exports are unreasonable and burden or restrict US commerce and has proposed responsive action," the USTR said on Wednesday.

The USTR remarked that it will reduce the tariff rate for Indian imports to 17% if a trade deal is sealed between Newly introduced Delhi and Washington in the long-drawn-out talks trade negotiations between the two sides.

India stated that it had "engaged constructively" throughout USTR's investigation and said that it did not have "structural excess capacity in any sector.".
