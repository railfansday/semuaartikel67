📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that after years of pure torture in Earth's Hell, trapped in a cocoon surrounded by singing fairies and dancing angels, Frieza is finally back in Dragon Ball Super: Beerus. The enhanced remake spent its first six episodes tightening up the God of Destruction Beerus saga, but episode #7, “Resurrection 'F',” brought the Emperor of the Universe back into the picture. Sorbet and Tagoma used Earth's Dragon Balls to revive him, and now episode #8 is ready to kick the Golden Frieza saga into full gear.

Recent findings suggest that that makes this a significant test for Toei Animation. The original Dragon Ball Super TV retelling of Resurrection 'F' became infamous in 2015 for its rushed schedule, something that even the creator, Akira Toriyama, was unhappy about. Frieza's return deserves a lot better, especially after the remake has spent weeks showing just how much the studio can improve the old material.

Taking over One Piece's Sunday slot on Fuji TV, this 19-episode remake has been on a roll since its October premiere. Toei trimmed the original 14-episode Beerus arc down to six tight installments, cutting out dragged-out fights and overblown gags while giving the visuals a major facelift. If episode #8 keeps that same energy going through Frieza's comeback, Dragon Ball Super: Beerus could set the stage perfectly for the upcoming Dragon Ball Super: The Galactic Patrol anime, which will adapt the Moro arc.

It has been revealed that dragon Ball Super: Beerus episode #8, titled “Revenge 'F',” officially premieres on Sunday, November 29, 2026, at 8:00 AM PST / 11:00 AM EST (4:00 PM GMT) on Crunchyroll and Hulu. It will also be accessible through Disney+. Meanwhile, Netflix viewers in the US will have to wait a little longer, as episode #8 won’t arrive on the platform until Saturday, December 5, making it a six-day delay.

Australian Eastern Daylight Time (AEDT).

The English dub is also underway on Crunchyroll, with Sean Schemmel and Christopher R. Sabat back as Goku and Vegeta. Dubbed episodes aren’t released simultaneously with the Japanese version, though. Newly introduced episodes drop on Thursdays at 8:00 AM PT, and the dub is currently running roughly five episodes behind Japan after launching on November 12.

In a recent development, now that Frieza has been pieced back together aboard Sorbet’s ship, it’s time for the tyrant to get back to work. Episode #8 moves into his infamous four-month training run, the one that leads to his Golden form. Meanwhile, Goku and Vegeta are training under Whis on Beerus’s planet, working toward Super Saiyan Blue. With Toei Animation keeping this 19-episode remake moving at a much snappier pace, there’s little reason to expect the studio to spend too long getting Frieza and his 1,000-soldier army to Earth.

It has been revealed that the episode is also expected to close on a hype note, with Gohan, Piccolo, and the rest of the Z Fighters holding the line while they wait for Goku and Vegeta. That’s a familiar setup, but Resurrection 'F' has a decade of baggage to deal with. The original story drew plenty of criticism for its messy power scaling, and tracksuit Gohan remains one of the saga’s most infamous choices. Cleaner animation is a welcome start, but Toei needs tighter storytelling to make this return land.

After wrapping up the God of Destruction Beerus saga in just six episodes, Dragon Ball Super: Beerus isn’t slowing down for Frieza’s comeback. With the remake reportedly set for 19 episodes covering the Beerus, Golden Frieza, and Universe 6 sagas, the Resurrection 'F' retelling could be done in just six or seven more. That would leave room for Champa’s tournament, while cutting a lot of fat from the 2015 anime, which spent 13 episodes on Frieza’s return alone and more than 40 on all three arcs.

Industry observers note that the story may be familiar from the movie and the original TV anime, but this remake is giving it a fresh shot. Toei’s tighter pacing and reworked visuals are already making a difference. Now episode #8 has to keep that momentum going, because Frieza’s return deserves more than a prettier rerun. If the remake can stick the landing here, Dragon Ball Super: Beerus might finally deliver the sharp opening run this series should have had from the start.
