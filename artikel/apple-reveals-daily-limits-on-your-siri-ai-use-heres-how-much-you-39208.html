In a recent development, have you been using the latest Siri AI? If so, how much? I’ve been turning to it a lot since the release of iOS 27. Well, how we use it — and how often — will soon depend on how much we pay for an iCloud+ plan.

Also: 5 apps I need Siri AI to work with now that it’s open to third-party developers.

Sources indicate that apple hasn’t been shy about stating that the use of Siri AI and Apple Intelligence for specific tasks would face daily access limits. Increased limits would be granted based on your iCloud+ subscription. Now the company has revealed how much it will cost you and when the limits will begin.

Sources indicate that “Starting with the 2027 software releases, Siri AI and Apple Intelligence features are available for free for owners with a supported device set to a supported language,” Apple said on a support page updated November 16. “Some features rely on powerful server models and have limits where increased access is available with eligible iCloud+ looks, starting with the 27.3 release.”.

iOS 27.3 is expected to launch in early December, so that’s when the daily usage limits will kick in.

At that point, people with no paid iCloud+ strategy and even those with the $0.99-per-month 50 GB plan will be the most limited, with Apple saying only that they’ll face a “Standard Device Limit.”.

What is a standard limit? That depends not just on how many times you tap into Siri per day but on what tasks you assign it. Basic Siri commands, such as setting timers and alarms or sending standard text messages, will remain free and unlimited.

One positive here is that the limits are per device not per account. That means you can use Siri AI and Apple Intelligence with the same account across your iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Apple TV, and Vision Pro without fear of chewing up too much of your quota.

In a recent development, this all sounds like a way for Apple to steer people to more expensive iCloud+ accounts. But the limits are in place for a key reason. Basic commands are processed on your device, so they don’t require the use of Apple servers. More advanced tasks are processed using server-side AI models, which can feel the strain if too many people access them all at once.

Also: What you’ll pay for AI agents will be wildly variable and unpredictable.

Sources indicate that how will you know if you use up your daily allotment and what happens then? You’ll receive a notification if you hit your limit for a particular feature category. At that point, you can still give Siri basic tasks to perform. But advanced ones will be paused until your limit resets the next day. Otherwise, Apple is undoubtedly hoping that people who continually run into limits will upgrade to a pricier iCloud+ blueprint.
