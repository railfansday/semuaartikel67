She built a miniature wheelchair using cardboard, tape, zip ties, wooden rulers, and Hot Wheels toy cars. What really matters is understanding the individual cat's needs and making reasonable adjustments to help them live comfortably.

For a cat with a spinal injury, this may include providing easy access to food, water, resting areas, and litter boxes. Non-slip flooring, ramps, and thoughtfully arranged furniture can also help some cats move around more safely. Depending on the injury, a cat may need help with bladder or bowel function, regular skin checks, or a rehabilitation proposal.

It has been revealed that these are not small details. Some cats with spinal injuries cannot empty their bladders normally, which can lead to serious complications without very devoted care. A veterinarian can assess bladder function, mobility, sensation, and other concerns, then explain what support the cat will need at home. The real challenge is finding someone with the time and patience to take over these responsibilities on a daily basis.
