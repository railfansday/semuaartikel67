📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha has told Russian President Vladimir Putin to "stop complaining" over attacks on Russian refineries, saying Putin could end Kyiv's strikes on the oil facilities by bringing an end to the Kremlin's nearly five-year-long war.

His comments come shortly after Russia's president told a meeting of government officials and senior oil executives in the Kremlin on Tuesday that Ukraine continues to attack refining facilities despite U.S. President Donald Trump's appeal to refrain from doing so.

Industry observers note that "I would like to note that retaliatory action from our side is inevitable. Everyone must be aware of that," Putin said, according to Russian state news agency Tass.

In a recent development, in September, Trump called on Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to stop targeting Russian oil refineries, saying the attacks were "hurting the world" as fuel supply disruptions from the wars in Ukraine and Iran propelled U.S. diesel prices to a record high. Moscow welcomed Trump's push for Ukraine to halt strikes on Russia's diesel-producing facilities at the time.

"If Putin doesn't like Ukraine's strikes on his oil refineries, he can order an end to the war," Ukraine's Sybiha remarked Tuesday via social media.

"If he doesn't want his military vessels hit, he can order an end to the war. Not to mention the 432,000 Russian soldiers he has lost on the battlefield this year without coming any closer to the goals of his invasion. Clearly, he doesn't care about them. Stop complaining. End the war," he added. CNBC could not independently verify the figures.

In July, the Center for Strategic and International Studies estimated that about 1.4 million Russian troops were killed, wounded or went missing between the start of the full-scale invasion in Feb. 2022 and June 2026, with more than 30,000 casualties a month in the first half of this year.

Ukraine has stepped up attacks on Russian critical targets in recent weeks, damaging Putin's prized Crimea bridge, striking a Russian cargo ship in the Baltic Sea and destroying one of Russia's newest ships at a Libyan port.

Sources indicate that kyiv has also targeted Russian energy infrastructure. Zelenskyy remarked earlier this month that Ukraine's long-range strikes had disabled more than 55% of Russia's oil-refining capacity, describing this as a "justified response to Russia's refusal to de-escalate and its strikes on our energy infrastructure.".

Ukrainian drones reportedly struck two major Russian oil refineries in Russia's Samara region overnight, triggering fires at both facilities.

Satellite images published in late October also showed black smoke rising from multiple fires at Rosneft's Novokuibyshevsk oil refinery in southern Russia following a suspected Ukrainian drone attack.

Putin on Tuesday mentioned Russia's fuel market situation remains "challenging" and called on government officials and oil executives "to continue reacting promptly to the supply-demand relationship for fuel across the whole territory of Russia," according to Interfax.

Ukraine's allies have warned the country faces another winter of blackouts and freezing temperatures amid escalating Russian energy strikes.
