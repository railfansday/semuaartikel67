According to recent reports, I hate to credit Sony's aggressive (and presumably very expensive) marketing campaign for GTA 6—you know, the one which appeared to have also plucked the ability for Xbox to even mention the game by name. That's something I'm still shocked was even a thing anyone was allowed to do.

According to recent reports, so I won't credit it… mostly. We all already know that PC is going to get the best version of GTA 6 when it finally hits our desktops, whenever that might be. But I am going to agree with one part of the whole "Grand Theft Auto 6 plays best on PS5" shtick: the DualSense controller. It's so good that I fear those who reach for a mouse and keyboard will be missing out on some incredible immersion.

It has been revealed that I never expected a controller to actively add to my experience, but I also forgot just how many goodies Sony stuffed into this thing. Seriously, Rockstar has taken full advantage of everything the peripheral has to offer.

I've been weaving through traffic way more in GTA 6 thanks to a huge increase in general vehicle density and some criminally narrow roads that can cause some major bottlenecks. Flooring it all the way from A to B isn't quite as easy as it used to be, which is why I kind of love how you can use the adaptive triggers to comfortably maintain a steady cruising speed before clicking it all the way in as a way to floor it. It makes my speed feel just that little more deliberate, with that tactile feel for when I really want to amp up how fast I'm going.

But driving might actually be the least interesting way Rockstar has made full use of the controller. The parts that have gotten me more than anything are the small ways haptics have been implemented to strengthen story moments or make parts of Leonida feel even more alive.

According to recent reports, the first time I really noticed the controller was back in the beginning of the game, when I'm instructed to go and pick Lucia up from prison. As I'm standing outside as Jason, watching her emerge from the gate, the controller starts thumping just a little bit. A heartbeat. I'm literally feeling Jason's heart racing as Lucia approaches. No dialogue, no internal monologue letting me know how nervous he is. It's such a small detail, but one that massively hammed up the moment for me.

My absolute favourite way it's been implemented, though? The clubs. The goddamn clubs. Leonida's sprawling nightlife is already impressive in isolation—Hitman's Berlin level was the last time I felt this enamoured with a videogame nightclub—but this is where Rockstar went all-in on the controller's haptic feedback. Step into a club and you'll immediately feel the bass pulsating through the controller.

Recent findings suggest that it's not just the same, monorhythmic beat for every song though. The bass perfectly matches the song; those long, teeth-shaking booms cut with shorter, less intense thumps. I feel like I'm back in my first year of university trying to sleep while the nearby student nightclub blasts the same songs until 3am every damn Wednesday, the bass somehow reverberating across the campus and straight through my bedframe.

In a recent development, when I first set foot inside the Jack of Hearts club at the beginning of Chapter 3, I was practically stunlocked by how much more immersive being able to feel the bass through the controller was making the entire experience. Seriously, I cannot overstate just how much it sucks you into what's happening around you.

According to recent reports, these are all things that I genuinely feel folks are going to miss out on with a simple mouse and keyboard. Sure, your aiming will be better. Yes, none of the things I've described above actually change anything about the gameplay. It'll still be the same GTA 6 no matter what you play with. But for all the ways Rockstar has managed to elevate the experience and the immersion I've been feeling just through something as small as the controller I'm holding? It at least deserves consideration for your go-to playthrough peripheral.
