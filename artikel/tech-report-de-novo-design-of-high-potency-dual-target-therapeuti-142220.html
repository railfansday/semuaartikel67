Tech Report: De novo design of high-potency dual-target therapeutic peptides with ORIDTP

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, peptides capable of modulating two therapeutic targets offer opportunities for treating complex diseases1,2. However, the development of such peptides at both targets is challenging, limited primarily by low design success rates and weak bioactivity. Here we present ORIDTP, an artificial intelligence platform that integrates single-target knowledge transfer and dual-target reinforcement to enable the rational design of high-potency peptides. ORIDTP successfully designed dual-target co-agonists for the GLP-1R and glucagon receptor (GCGR, also known as GLR), six of which surpassed the strongest natural binders of each receptor in bioactivity. Notably, the lead peptide GA-Dual.R5.S3 induced substantial weight loss and glycaemic improvement in diet-induced obese mice, and exhibited pronounced antidiabetic effects in db/db mice, without inducing nausea or aversion. Furthermore, ORIDTP demonstrated generalizability through successful zero-shot design of dual-target co-inhibitors for structurally and mechanistically distinct immune checkpoints, PD-1 and CTLA-4. Out of 15 designed peptides, 8 blocked both checkpoint interactions and 3 further exhibited in vivo antitumour efficacy in human checkpoint knock-in tumour-bearing mice. The lead peptide IC-Dual.P3 achieved the strongest tumour growth suppression, reducing tumour volume by 94.2% relative to controls. Together, these findings establish ORIDTP as a powerful and generalizable platform for de novo dual-target peptide discovery.
