York City boss Scott Lindsey has insisted midfielder Hiram Boateng is “in a good place” following his court appearance earlier this week.

At Manchester Magistrates’ Court, the 30-year-old pleaded not guilty to using threatening, abusive or insulting behaviour with intent to cause fear of, or provoke, unlawful violence.

He is alleged to have punched a Rochdale fan during a pitch invasion that took place when the two sides met in the National League on April 25.

York have offered their support to Boateng, who has been training ahead of Walsall’s visit tomorrow (November 21).

Recent findings suggest that “I haven’t had to [put an arm around Hiram],” remarked Lindsey, as reported by York Press. “I probably have subconsciously.

“I have spoken to him and he’s in a good place, he’s playing some really good football at the moment and is getting back up to speed playing some football after being out for a while.

“What was pleasing was when we were looking to bring him off around the 60 minute mark [against Rochdale], he was okay to stay on.

“He did get a little bit of a knock just before he came off, but he’s okay.".

Boateng is charged with a section four public order offence that carries a maximum sentence of six months in prison.

A spokesperson for York noted Boateng “maintains the full support of everyone at York City and is a much-valued member of the squad, both on and off the pitch”.

The ex-Crystal Palace man missed the early stages of the season through injury but has appeared in four of York’s last five League Two games.

“He’s playing some good football at the moment,” said Lindsey. “I think that his focus is on the football, which it needs to be.

“For all of the time that things are going on in the background, you’ve got to focus on something that’s good, and that’s good in his life at the moment.

“We need to continue doing that.”.

Fourth-placed York could break into the automatic promotion places in League Two with a win over sixth-placed Walsall, who trail the Minstermen by just one point.
