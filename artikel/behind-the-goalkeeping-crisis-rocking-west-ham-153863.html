Behind the Goalkeeping Crisis Rocking West Ham

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, while Mads Hermansen’s recent performances have come under scrutiny and Finlay Herrick has found the transition challenging, culminating in his role in Portsmouth’s winning goal on Tuesday night, I still believe Herrick has the attributes to succeed. His potential is clear, but it requires time, guidance and development.

Meanwhile, supporters continue to ask why Alphonse Areola has not been available for first-team selection.

Let’s start with the facts. Alphonse Areola is no messiah. Last season, the French goalkeeper conceded 37 goals in 20 Premier League appearances, made 77 saves and failed to keep a single clean sheet. At the start of this season, Areola was informed that he was no longer Nuno’s first-choice goalkeeper. Despite having just one year remaining on his contract and reportedly taking a 50 per cent reduction in salary, now believed to be around £60,000 per week, no offers materialised. That still makes him one of the highest-paid goalkeepers in the Championship.

This season, he has been named on the bench nine times in 17 matches. However, questions remain over his fitness. Earlier in the campaign, he was sidelined with an unspecified injury despite being photographed attending a tennis tournament. He then reportedly suffered another setback during the warm-up ahead of the Blackburn Rovers match on 7 November, although the club has yet to officially disclose the nature of the injury.

Scott Parker has offered little clarity, saying only that “the club was struggling a bit with Alphonse” and describing his availability as “touch and go”. Inevitably, conspiracy theories have emerged, with some suggesting the goalkeeper has chosen not to make himself available for selection. However, there is no evidence to substantiate that suggestion, and he attended the Preston game with his family and watched from the stands.

The most likely explanation, as Martin previously outlined, is simply fitness, or a lack of it. For a club with only two recognised senior goalkeepers and one established academy option, the current situation is far from ideal.

While the team’s defensive issues cannot be blamed solely on the goalkeeping department, the number of goals conceded this season has undoubtedly affected results. It is an issue that requires an urgent resolution and one Parker will be acutely aware of.

Another area worth examining is the goalkeeping coaching set-up. Since Xavi Valero’s departure in May 2025, the club has never truly found a settled replacement. Casper Ankergren arrived alongside Linus Kandolin, Rui Barbosa subsequently replaced them and Billy Lepine later stepped into the role. In the space of just 18 months, the club has had four different goalkeeping coaches.

Continuity matters, particularly in such a specialised position, and that level of turnover is far from ideal.

According to recent reports, for me, the club already has a ready-made solution in Łukasz Fabiański. His experience, leadership and understanding of the position make him more than qualified to take on such a responsibility.

Industry observers note that whatever the answer, Parker needs to resolve the situation rapidly. The clock is ticking.
