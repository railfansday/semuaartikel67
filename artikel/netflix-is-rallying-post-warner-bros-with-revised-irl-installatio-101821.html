In a recent development, OK, so Netflix failed to buy Warner Bros. Discovery.

What if it bought NBCUniversal, tho?

That potential acquisition is apparently top of wishlist for Netflix (and Variety already deemed it “Hollywood’s Next Big Deal”), but Comcast co-CEOs Mike Cavanagh and Brian Roberts say they’re not selling.

Recent findings suggest that well, according to Bloomberg, the current initiative is for Netflix to revamp its approach to permanent interactive IRL installations, aka Netflix Houses. The streamer first unveiled these in 2023, with a demo setup that looked like someone stocked the gift shop in Jurassic Park with merch of the laser murder doll from Squid Game.

At the time, Josh Simon, Netflix’s then-VP of Consumer Products (now CEO of collectibles manufacturer Funko) noted the concept for Netflix Houses came from knowing “fans love to immerse themselves in the world of our movies and TV reveals,” and “thinking a lot about how we take that to the next level.”.

Netflix had flirted with pop-ups before that; those were mainly focused on selling stuff. Make no mistake, Netflix Houses are also focused on selling stuff, but in a Disney World kind of way, where fans pay $39 to enter the House and head through an art gallery-esque space filled with themed exhibits like exclusive theater indicates, movie/TV set recreations, VR experiences, and arcade games. There are cafeterias and merch shops, but by and large the things to buy share billing with things to do.

The Houses are permanent installations (for now just in Dallas and Philly, with one planned to open in Vegas in 2027) but the exhibits within rotate regularly to keep up with whatever’s trending on Netflix.

According to recent reports, this year, for example, the Houses were renovated to feature animated smash megahit KPop Demon Hunters. They’ve also spotlighted Stranger Things, One Piece, Wednesday, and the aforementioned Squid Game.

Industry observers note that per Bloomberg, Netflix initially planned to open dozens of Houses across the U.S., focusing on quieter suburban areas away from downtowns. It wanted to snap up spaces in malls, which are not doing very well and might be willing to rent for cheap.

But apparently the results from its first two Houses haven’t been quite what Neflix expected, so it’s pausing openings beyond Vegas until it can reassess.

Sources indicate that people familiar with the matter told Bloomberg the Philadelphia location has daytime draws like mini golf and a movie theater, but doesn’t see much traffic at night. Dallas, meanwhile, is breaking even financially, and has done well with larger groups and corporate events.

Sources indicate that netflix is also planning to see what comes from its Las Vegas location. Vegas, as you may know, is not in fact a quieter suburban locale–so if the House there proves to be profitable, with more traffic than Dallas and Philly, Netflix could reconsider where it opens in the future.

People familiar with the matter stated Netflix is also looking at maybe taking Houses overseas. But wherever the next one ends up, it probably won’t open for at least another year, they said, until significant data from all three flagship U.S. locations is in.

Now, with all this in mind, it’s worth remembering Netflix just shock-fired its longtime Chief Content Officer Bela Bajaria and replaced her with Peter Rice, Disney‘s former General Entertainment Chairman. Film chief Dan Lin is out too. Clearly, Netflix hopes to revamp more than its IRL installations…and perhaps it’s hoping Rice’s Disney EXP comes in handy with the Houses?
