Industry News: Bank of England to test how UK finance would recover from an AI disruption

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Bank of England will use a frontier AI scenario in its 2027 operational stress test to see how a disruption involving advanced AI could affect UK finance. One question is whether firms’ recovery intends would work together if several institutions faced disruption at once. The voluntary, exploratory exercise will examine both individual firms and the wider sector.

The Bank set out the proposal for its Cyber and Operational Resilience Stress Test in its Nov. 26 Financial Stability Report. It is discussing possible scenarios with firms, industry groups and technical experts. The Bank intends to invite suitable participants for testing in summer 2027 and publish findings on common themes in the fourth quarter.

The Bank’s Financial Policy Committee record warned that a weakness at a common supplier could disrupt several firms and financial market infrastructure at once. A firm can test how quickly it would restore its own operations. The committee stated risks can remain across the system even when individual firms have credible fallbacks. A Solana routing outage showed how a shared provider can disrupt independently run infrastructure. Validators representing about 29% of stake stopped voting when one provider’s routing failed.

A problem at a shared provider can hit several firms at once. Their separate recovery aims have to work together to keep services running.

Credible fallbacks at individual firms can still leave gaps in the services the sector needs to restore.

Sources indicate that in its wider resilience work, the Bank is examining whether firms’ workarounds can collectively sustain a minimum level of activity in retail and wholesale payments and sterling interest-rate markets. In those markets, a priority is keeping settlement of UK government bond trades running. That work illustrates the stakes of joint recovery while the Bank weighs scenarios for the AI exercise.

Industry observers note that the Bank indicates a prolonged interruption to retail payments could leave households and businesses unable to make or receive essential payments. In wholesale payments, disruption could tie up cash and collateral and leave firms unsure when incoming payments will arrive, putting pressure on other obligations.

According to recent reports, an October survey by the Bank and the Financial Conduct Authority shows where firms already rely on the same providers. The three largest cloud and computing providers accounted for 79% of reported relationships in that category. The three largest providers of model access and hosting accounted for 54% of reported relationships in theirs.

The survey also found that adoption of AI agents was widespread among respondents and broad autonomy was rare. Among firms that answered the respective questions in the full survey, 80% noted they had deployed AI agents and 2% said they had deployed agents with broad autonomy.

The Bank’s stress-test program includes a separate 2026 exercise on disruption involving a cloud provider and third parties, with findings planned for summer 2027.
