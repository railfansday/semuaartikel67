By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER.

Published: 17:18 EST, 25 November 2026 | Updated: 05:01 EST, 26 November 2026.

A week into the job and Mauricio Pochettino must be getting used to the madness of Manchester United by now.

If it's not drones flying over the training ground, red card controversies and random divots of grass, it's his own players shooting themselves in the foot.

At least, Pochettino emerged from the craziness in Lisbon with a draw on a night when Bruno Fernandes celebrated his return to the Jose Alvalade Stadium with a couple of goals.

Sources indicate that but these are the kind of nights that must drive coaches like Pochettino potty. After the carnage of Michael Carrick's final two games in charge against Roma and Aston Villa, and the rancour of the Argentine's introduction to the Old Trafford circus at Anfield on Sunday, this was another mad, mad spectacle.

In a recent development, where do we start? The two acts of self-sabotage by goalkeeper Senne Lammens? United's two disallowed goals? The first was harsh on the scorer, Amad Diallo, but also Youri Tielemans who received a booking that led to his sending-off in injury-time. Or maybe that divot, which Fernandes claimed he created and then hit to put United ahead with a bizarre deflected goal?

At the end of all the chaos, United had conceded five penalties in their last five games and picked up four red cards.

Senne Lammens (left) two errors cost Man United a deserving win at Sporting as they drew.

Upcoming United head coach Mauricio Pochettino comforts Lammens at full-time after his mistakes.

That is not something that will sit well with Pochettino. But after venting his frustration with the officiating over Leny Yoro's sending-off at Anfield, which dominated the build-up to this game after it was rescinded, here was another sequence of events that seemed to conspire against him.

Sources indicate that it's not as if the Jose Alvalade Stadium was a cauldron of emotion to scramble the senses of the two sets of players and match officials either. Fernandes got a polite round of applause from the fans and a framed memento from his old club before kick-off.

When the teams came out, he was one of three former United players alongside Cristiano Ronaldo and Luis Nani honoured on a tifo of the Sporting Lisbon greats.

It has been revealed that it was all very flat until the half-hour mark when United self-imploded again. Geny Catamo picked up the ball on the edge of the United box and teased Lisandro Martinez, playing at left back.

Martinez took the bait and stuck his leg out. He barely caught Sporting's No 10 and it took Catamo a full second to realise he had, before going down theatrically and earning a very soft penalty from Polish referee Szymon Marciniak.

According to recent reports, sporting's top scorer Luis Suarez stepped up and made it easy for Lammens to make the save. It was a let-off for United and potentially a substantial moment in a difficult season for Lammens.

But he undid his good work 75 seconds later when Maxi Araujo chased a long ball over the top pursued by Noussair Mazraoui. For some reason, Lammens thought it would be a good idea to race 25 yards out of his goal.

Araujo stepped inside the Belgium goalkeeper and went down under his clumsy challenge. Marciniak pointed to the spot again, and this time Suarez made no mistake.

Lammens started positively saving this Luis Suarez penalty before conceding a spot-kick.

Yet, minutes later Suarez scored after being given a second penalty following Lammens error.

United thought they had eqaulised before half-time but Amad Diallo's header was ruled out for a percieved foul in the build-up by Youri Tielemans that earned him a booking.

After all the upset over the officiating at Anfield, United were on the wrong end of another massive call just before half-time here when Tielemans stretched to poke the ball through to Diallo and inadvertently caught Goncalo Inacio who went down claiming serious injury.

Sources indicate that goalkeeper Kaique Pereira saved Diallo's first effort and Marciniak blew his whistle for a foul before the Ivorian headed in at the second attempt so there was no opportunity for VAR to review it. The referee booked Tielemans, but there was a strong argument that it wasn't even a foul.

United re-emerged for the second half undeterred and hit back to score twice through Fernandes within five minutes of the restart.

The United captain played the ball through for Martinez and then converted from the spot after his team-mate went down in a tangle of legs with Eduardo Quaresma.

Bruno Fernandes levelled for United just after half-time when they were awarded a penalty.

He then put them ahead with this left-footed shot that hit a divot on the pitch before flying in.

United's lead was undone late on when Lammens' second major error allowed Suarez to score.

United's evening was compounded when Tielemans was sent off late on for a second yellow.

Sources indicate that two minutes later, and having made the divot turning in the penalty box 17 seconds earlier, Fernandes somehow managed to hit it with a hopeful effort from 25 yards. Pereira had the ball covered but it took an outrageous deflection just outside the six-yard box and flew through his arms.

But this is United and nothing is straightforward. In the 80th minute, with victory within their grasp, Lammens had all the time in the world to catch a high ball from Ivan Fresneda. Perhaps too long. Unforgivably, he dropped it in front of Suarez who hooked into the net.

There was still time for more drama when United had another goal from a Fernandes' corner chalked off for Patrick Dorgu's foul on Pereira, after VAR sent Marciniak to the monitor, and he then showed Tielemans a second yellow card.

Pochettino looked on frustrated and probably as confused as the rest of us.
