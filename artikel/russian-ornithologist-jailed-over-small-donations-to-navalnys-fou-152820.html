In a recent development, boris Rosenfeld shows DW a photo of him with his daughter Sofia and can't hold back tears. "This was the last time we saw her. Then she left for Moscow, and this whole case began," he says.

It has been revealed that helives in Israel, while Sofia is in Russia. For more than a month now, she has been behind bars on charges of financing extremism. The case goes back to two donations totaling 2,000 rubles (about $30) to the late opposition politician Alexei Navalny'sAnti-Corruption Foundation (FBK), which the Russian authorities have since designated as extremist.

On October 7, a court in Moscow sentenced Rosenfeld to three and a half years in a penal colony.

Recent findings suggest that rosenfeld is a prominent ornithologist who has devoted her life to studying and protecting Arcticbirds. "Sofia was an avid young naturalist from childhood. She loved birds, horses and dogs," her father remarked as he leafed through a photo album.

Sofia was a teenager when the family moved to Israel but she returned to Russia a few years later and now holds both Russian and Israeli citizenship.

According to recent reports, she studied biology at Moscow State University and developed a significant scientific career in Russia. Her colleague, Alexandra Panyutina, who now also lives in Israel, explained that Rosenfeld was devoted to her work from an early age.

"Birds and the Arctic are the defining passions of her life," she said.

Recent findings suggest that rosenfeld was among the first Russian researchers to develop a systematic approach to monitoring migratory bird populations. To support her research, she even developed a special aircraft.

Industry observers note that "They could fly at almost the same speed as the birds and observe them from a low altitude," says Dmitry (name changed), a colleague of Rosenfeld's. "It quickly became clear that this was a unique method.".

Rosenfeld is considered to be one of the leading figures in Russian ornithology. Her work has extended well beyond Russia and she has been deeply involved in international research projects. In the field of migratory bird conservation, cross-border cooperation is essential.

Helmut Kruckenberg, the head of the German Institute for Wetlands and Waterbird Research (IWWR), said Rosenfeld was highly respected within the international scientific community.

"She has done an enormous amount for nature conservation and for the protection of Siberian and Arctic species," he remarked. "What is happening to her is simply beyond comprehension for us.".

Sources indicate that in the summer of 2021, when Russian authorities designated Alexei Navalny's Anti-Corruption Foundation, also known as FBK, as an extremist organization, Rosenfeld was on a remote Arctic expedition without a phone or access to the internet. Therefore, she did not cancel her regular donations to the FBK immediately, doing so only after her return to the Russian capital Moscow.

Her friend Olga (name changed) described Sofia as a person of absolute honesty. According to her, Rosenfeld's donations to Navalny's foundation reflected a genuine desire to fight corruption. Through her work in Russia's regions, she had seen plenty of it firsthand.

It has been revealed that "She was never involved in political activism. Her donations were solely intended to support anti-corruption work," mentioned her father Boris Rosenfeld. "It's unthinkable".

Sources indicate that for him and others close to her, the sentence came as a shock. Although prosecutors had requested a five-year prison term, friends and colleagues expected that she would receive a fine at most.

Sources indicate that there were reasons for that expectation. According to calculations by independent media outlet Mediazona, about 85% of convictions in the 225 cases involving donations to Navalny's foundation have so far resulted in non-custodial sentences. Yet Rosenfeld got three and a half years in a penal colony.

In a recent development, just a few months after Rosenfeld defended her doctoral dissertation in October 2025, her home was searched. She was also subject to travel restrictions. She continued to work but she had to cancel her annual summer expedition to the Arctic.

It has been revealed that even on the day of the sentencing, her conversations with  her colleagues focused largely on science:  "We discussed how the work could continue in Sofia's absence," Olga mentioned. "But no one really believed she would end up in prison.".

"The only thing we think about now is how to bring her here," Boris Rosenfeld states as he looks at the pictures from Sofia's last visit.

Sources indicate that friends and colleagues who spoke to DW say that his daughter never considered leaving Russia, even as the prospect of a prison sentence loomed. "Sofia would never have left. She simply can't imagine life without geese," indicates Dmitry.

Sources indicate that now, her family, friends and colleagues are campaigning to have her sentence overturned. More than 1,000 people have signed an appeal to Russia's Presidential Council for Human Rights.

Boris Rosenfeld is also hoping for support from Israel but so far Israeli consular officials have not been granted access to his daughter in detention.

Recent findings suggest that the two continue to write to each other and he is trying to keep his spirits high: "She writes that she's trying to adjust to life in prison, and that it's hard," he claims. "But she's a strong person.".
