📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

With the reveal of its latest financial results partly on a pro forma basis after closing Disney’s Hulu merger, Fubo ended the fourth quarter with a net loss of $51.8 million, compared to a net loss of $38.8 million in the same period of 2025, and on overall revenue at $1.5 billion, up from a year-earlier $1.48 billion.

Adjusted EBITDA was $12.3 million, against a year-earlier $4.1 million on a pro forma basis, driven by an increase in pro forma advertising revenue. During the three months to Sept. 30, Fubo TV saw subscription revenue come to $327 million. That compares to a year-earlier $406.8 million in reported subscription revenue, before Fubo gets set to align with Disney’s fiscal calendar in the first quarter of 2026.

Bowen on the analyst call teased other new initiatives to drive growth as Fubo gets deeper into Disney’s broad digital ecosystem: “Beyond pay TV, we see an opportunity to build on Fubo as a fan engagement platform with new content and new experiences, especially through mobile, that will open new vectors of growth, including transactional, ad-supported and subscription revenue models for the future.”.

Bowen also pointed to Fubo continuing to add to its offering of regional sports networks, or pay TV channels built around live local sports, to differentiate itself in an increasingly crowded and disrupted TV market.

“We’re very committed to prioritizing our resources to support the U.S. business. Our international review is ongoing and we’ll update when we have more to conclude on that front,” Bowen told an analyst when asked about the future of its international operations under pressure from declines in the traditional linear TV space.
