Emily Carr and E.J. Hughes lead the way at Heffel's $10m autumn auctions

Sources indicate that the Heffel Fine Art Auction House held its marquee autumn evening sales on Wednesday (18 November) in Toronto, attracting a packed house of bidders and spectators to the city’s swank Yorkville district and generating C$14.4m ($10m) in sales, works by Canadian masters taking top dollar. As things got underway, a Heffel representative noted that any US tariffs were “the responsibility of the buyer”, a reflection of these often-testy times.

“At a time of strong national pride, it is especially meaningful to see collectors celebrating Canada’s artistic achievements and our distinct national culture,” David Heffel, the auction house’s president, said at the end of the evening.

Heffel's evening auctions in Toronto on 18 November Courtesy Heffel Fine Art Auction House.

Industry observers note that the back-to-back sales (41 lots of post-war and contemporary art followed by 47 works of Canadian, Impressionist and modern art) got off to a flying start when a watercolour by the Vancouver-born artist E.J. Hughes hammered for C$475,000 ($331,700)—nearly 12 times its high estimate. Titled Wharves at Chemainus (2000) and depicting the titular logging community on Vancouver Island, the painting is modest in size at 20in by 24in; with fees, the price came to C$571,250 ($398,900).

Industry observers note that hughes had made waves at Heffel’s auctions last spring, when his Coastal Boats Near Sidney, BC realised a whoppingC$5.7m ($4.1m), including fees. Several more Hughes works were up for sale Wednesday night including two that significantly outperformer their estimates: The Hope River at Chilliwack, BC (1961), which took in C$253,250 ($176,800) with fees; and The Freighter “World Ares” at Crofton (1980), which hauled in C$229,250 ($160,000) including fees.

Emily Carr, Alert Bay (with Welcome Figure), around 1912 Courtesy Heffel Fine Art Auction House.

It has been revealed that the second sale’s cover lot, Emily Carr’s luminous canvas Alert Bay (with Welcome Figure) (around 1912), brought C$1.3m ($922,000) including fees, comfortably within its estimate. It was the only seven-figure result of the night. Some of the proceeds from its sale will benefit the Audain Art Museum in Whistler, British Columbia, where it was shown in the context of a major traveling exhibition of Carr’s work in 2019-20.

It has been revealed that A work on paper by the Carr, Music in the Trees (around 1935), topped its high estimate to achieve an impressive C$421,250 ($294,100) with fees. In all, six works by the Victoria-born artist were sold at Heffel on Wednesday night, fetching a combined, fee-inclusive total of C$2.5m ($1.7m).

Jean Paul Riopelle, Herbeuse, 1967 Courtesy Heffel Fine Art Auction House.

A handful of works by Jean Paul Riopelle were on offer during the post-war and contemporary portion of the night, generating around C$1.3m ($907,800) in sales. His large-scale, wintry abstraction Herbeuse (1967),proved particularly appealing. Swift bidding pushed it to around double its high estimate. With fees, the price came to C$571,250 ($398,900). It was shown in the Carnegie International the year Riopelle painted it, and later in Paris, where the Montréal-born artist, a master of the palette knife, spent decades. The work with the highest estimate in the post-war and contemporary sale, Riopelle’s Brumaire (1953), was expected to bring between C$1.5m and C$2m, but failed to sell.

Recent findings suggest that among the night’s other big-ticket items was Paul-Émile Borduas’s oil painting Empâtement (1957), from the artist’s black-and-white period—and punctuated with splashes of red. It realised C$841,250 with fees ($587,400), easily topping its high estimate of C$500,000. It had been acquired directly from the artist by London’s Arthur Tooth & Sons gallery in 1957 and, more recently, was in the collection of Québecois lyricist and songwriter Luc Plamondon.

Paul-Émile Borduas, Empâtement, 1957 Courtesy Heffel Fine Art Auction House.

According to recent reports, J.E.H. MacDonald’s oil sketch Magnetawan River Rapids (1912), a preparatory work for a major canvas that is in the collection of the Art Gallery of Hamilton, achieved C$451,250 with fees ($315,000), tripling its high estimate of C$150,000.

Sources indicate that arguably the surprise of the night was Ivan Eyre (1935-2022), who was long a professor at the University of Manitoba and is best known for his prairie landscapes. His intensely autumnal acrylic-on-canvas landscape Golden Rod (1981) realised C$391,250 with fees ($273,200), more than tripling its high estimate of C$120,000 while setting a newly introduced auction record for the artist. That record was smashed in turn just one lot later, when Eyre’s Yellow Wood (around 1995) realised C$481,250 with fees ($336,000), five times its high estimate of C$90,000.

Ivan Eyre, Golden Rod, 1981 Courtesy Heffel Fine Art Auction House.

The beloved William Kurelek, who was raised on a farm near Winnipeg, also made a splash.His snowy landscape painting I Knew How the Clouds Arise (1969), which was featured on the cover of the catalogue for the artist’s first retrospective in 1970, realised C$313,250 including fees ($218,700), just over its high estimate, while the evocative campfire scene Wiener Roast on My Father’s Farm (1974), exceeded expectations to sell for C$265,250 with fees ($185,200).
