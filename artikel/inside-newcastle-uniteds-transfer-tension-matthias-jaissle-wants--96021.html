By CRAIG HOPE, CHIEF FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 12:49 EST, 19 November 2026 | Updated: 05:54 EST, 20 November 2026.

Industry observers note that this time, Matthias Jaissle wants his fingerprints on the bank notes. But what the head coach wishes for in January, and what Newcastle United are willing and able to give, is likely to be very different. When that happens, it rarely ends well - on the pitch or off it.

The need for reinforcement is clear, even on the back of £270million invested in the summer. Jaissle knows as much and is not afraid to say so. The rookie German has to protect himself as much as he does his club.

Recent findings suggest that because, as Newcastle prepare for what threatens to be a long and testing winter, Jaissle is revisiting the brochure presented to him in the summer. And that is the 38-year-old's concern and frustration - the prospectus for the season ahead was not of his making.

According to recent reports, he arrived three weeks before the start of the campaign and with four of eight signings already through the door. At a cost of £110m, that quartet have started just three Premier League games between them, and each of those by teenager Sean Steur. Baz Toure and Aladji Bamba are virtual spectators, while reserve keeper Ewen Jaouen is injured.

For Jaissle, you suspect, that money was not spent as he would have advised, had he been in situ. Now, in the latest year, he wants greater control, as well as a No 10 and No 9. For any Newcastle follower averse to tension, you best watch this transfer window through your fingertips.

Industry observers note that jaissle, sporting director Ross Wilson and chief executive David Hopkinson are the protagonists who will navigate January. At least, that is, if Jaissle makes it into 2027. He deserves that time - not least because of the turbulent and transitional situation he inherited - but the next four fixtures will potentially make or break him and the season.

Head coach Matthias Jaissle is seeking greater control of Newcastle United's transfer strategy.

Aladji Bamba has been a virtual spectator and joined in the summer before Jaissle's arrival.

After the visit of league leaders Arsenal on Saturday, there is a trip to second-placed Brighton, followed by a seismic home double header in the space of five days against Mauricio Pochettino's Manchester United and bitter rivals Sunderland.

It is why, despite rumblings behind the scenes in recent days of potential transfer discord, Jaissle was on message - the collective, club message - when I asked him about the profile of player he might want in January.

'It's not the time to talk about the transfer window,' he said. 'We have players capable to deliver higher performances on the pitch. We have major quality, not only up front, but throughout the whole squad. We believe we can do better and this is what we now want to influence.'.

However, there was a softening when I sympathised with the head coach over him being judged by a window for which he did not have ownership. Did he, then, want more influence this time?

'Now, it's a different story (compared to the summer), if you're already in and also if you have clarified your clear idea, your clear needs, it's going to be easier,' he said.

According to recent reports, 'But the transfer market is still a long time ahead, at least it feels like that right now with our fixtures. Our task, especially from my stance, is to focus on the next match and the upcoming challenges.'.

In a recent development, jaissle knows that his case will only be strengthened by a steadying of results, even though, conversely, more defeats would heighten the need for signings. They have lost four from six in all competitions.

The problem is that the hierarchy are on the record as stating the summer business went pretty much exactly as they wished and believed they had a squad for the entire season. There are also Squad Cost Ratio limitations. It is understood there is headroom for one player, but not the two the manager would prefer and not at a fee approaching the £51m they paid for Matias Fernandez-Pardo on deadline day.

Jaissle wants a upcoming No 9 to compete with the likes of Yoane Wisse who has one league goal.

Matias Fernandez-Pardo (left) has failed to impress so far at Newcastle after his £51m arrival.

The 21-year-old forward is another problem. He is still to score from seven appearances and there is a fear he is not yet strong enough for the Premier League. Interestingly, Jaissle revealed on Thursday his dismay that he was booked during the 1-1 draw at Fulham last time out for screaming at Fernandez-Pardo to stand up.

That is not to say Jaissle does not believe in the talent of those brought in. He does. But for a coach in his first season in a major European league, he needs players who can impact today, not tomorrow. He needs, and wants, more experience. One of his first recommendations in August was to sign 31-year-old midfielder Pierre-Emile Hojbjerg. Nico Gonzalez eventually arrived in that position, but the 24-year-old looks like a player who has spent two seasons in reserve at Manchester City.

Sources indicate that so, it is easy to see why Jaissle needs and wants more in January. It is also easy to see why he might not get it. There could be trouble ahead, and that is not just the upcoming matches.
