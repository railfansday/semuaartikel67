‘I Tried Not Looking at the Rack.’ Indiana Hunter Tags 199-Inch Buck on State Park Land

According to recent reports, avery Fisher of Delphi, Indiana, and his best friend since high school, Nathan Van Dokkenburg, put in for an Indiana parks draw hunt on the recommendation of a buddy. Both drew the two-day Nov. 16-17 firearms season.

Indiana has special limited-draw, short-term deer hunts on about 25 public parks around the state. The hunts help manage the burgeoning deer herds, and most allow sportsmen to take three deer per hunt, with a limit of one buck. The number of hunters drawn for each park varies, but about 150 hunters are typically allowed on a park for each two-day hunt.

Industry observers note that neither Fisher nor Van Dokkenburg had ever walked the park they drew to hunt, which they declined to name. But they spent a lot of time figuring out where they should be on the property well before the hunt.

Industry observers note that “We studied a lot of Google Maps and onX to get an idea where we should hunt far back in the park well away from other hunters,” Fisher tells Outdoor Life. “It’s rolling hills with massive creek drains and draws … I’ve hunted that kind of land previously, and I had a good idea where to go early that Nov. 16 opening morning.”.

The hunters checked in when the park opened at 5 a.m. and were prepared for an all day sit with packs, gear, and food.

It has been revealed that “We got far back into the park, about one and a half miles from the road,” says Fisher, 25, who works as a financial advisor. “We stayed on ridgetops, and about one mile in, Nathan and I split up.

Fisher went about another half mile, hiking by the light of his headlamp.

Recent findings suggest that “In the dark I spotted a tree line that had a lot of buck rubs. Then I saw a doe, and I figured it was a pretty good place.”.

He climbed about 30 feet off the ground and settled into his saddle with his coffee and snacks his favorite .44-cal Ruger, a gift from his father years ago.

It was cold early that morning, and he heard something in a nearby ravine around 7:45 a.m.

“I thought I saw a buck and put my binoculars on it, and it was one with a good spread and nice tines about 100 yards away.”.

He raised his rifle and rested it against the tree for support while looking for the buck in his scope.

In a recent development, “But my breath kept fogging the scope,” he says. “I kept cleaning it with my gloves, and the deer kept getting closer … I tried not looking at his rack as he turned uphill and then I saw his sticker points as he was rubbing trees.”.

Industry observers note that fisher found the shoulder in his scope and squeezed the trigger. The buck ran uphill past Fisher, wobbling as it went. When the buck stopped at 60 yards, Fisher shot again. The buck dropped.

Fisher texted Van Dokkenburg and told him he’d shot a nice buck, but to keep hunting because they were allowed to shoot up to three deer apiece.

It has been revealed that “A group of does came in at about 9 a.m., and I shot one and it fell,” Fisher indicates. “I decided not to shoot any more deer that morning.

Industry observers note that “We had to bone and pack out two deer like we were on a Western hunt. I figured that was enough for us to lug out of there that day.”.

Van Dokkenburg saw nothing to shoot and met up with Fisher about 11 a.m., filming Fisher as he first walked up to his buck and realized how huge it was.

Recent findings suggest that “I was speechless at the size of his rack,” states Fisher. “I knew he was big, but not a rack like that. I’m grateful and thankful to the Lord.”.

Using a wheeled cart and backpacks, the two hunters hauled both deer back down to their vehicle.

“I think my pack with deer and gear weighed over 100 pounds, and I had to stop and offload more from my pack to the wheeled cart,” Fisher says.

They then drove to check in their deer with park personnel.

Fisher’s 13-point buck has been measured with an unofficial green score of 199 5/8 inches. It turned out Fisher’s second shot had been unnecessary, since his first had struck the heart.

The two buddies returned to the park to hunt the next day, though Fisher states they were exhausted from the previous day’s hunt.

In a recent development, “We saw some deer, including another far-off buck, but we didn’t shoot another deer that second day,” he says. “Nathan is drawn for another two-day park hunt later this year, so he still has a good chance of taking deer, maybe even a huge buck.”.
