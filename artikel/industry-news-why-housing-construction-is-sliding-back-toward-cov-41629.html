Industry News: Why housing construction is sliding back toward COVID-era lows

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The great housing construction boom that people have been cheering about for four years now looks to be on the cusp of falling to COVID-era lows. How is this possible when people have been saying for the past five years that we need to build more homes and Congress just passed the giant ROAD to Housing Act this summer — which was designed to help spur more construction.

It has been revealed that the old laws of economics still play out here; there simply isn’t enough housing demand to build more homes. This is something I wrote about back in June 2021: when mortgage rates eventually rise this whole thing will reverse. Now, in November 2026, we are not that far away from the COVID-19 recession lows, as housing permits are already at COVID-19 recession levels and mortgage rates are above 7%.

According to recent reports, from Census: Building Permits: Privately-owned housing units authorized by building permits in October were at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1,295,000. This is 7.4 percent below the revised September rate of 1,398,000 and is 8.7 percent below the October 2025 rate of 1,418,000. Single-family authorizations in October were at a rate of 772,000; this is 8.7 percent below the revised September figure of 846,000. Authorizations of units in buildings with five units or more were at a rate of 475,000 in October.As we can see in the chart below, housing permits have been slowly falling for years and we are not only at levels last seen during the COVID-19 recession, but we’re on pace to hit the lows of that time period if this trend continues. And all this is happening when mortgage rates are close to 7.5% and the Fed is starting a newly introduced rate hike cycle.

Industry observers note that single-family permits are not only close to the lows of 2022, but not that far from the COVID-19 recession lows either. Builders have done an admirable job keeping new home sales elevated enough to keep housing starts from falling further, but that has limits as profit margins fall. As you can see here, the single-family permits have taken a noticeable dive.

One question I got recently, which has merit: Why is builder confidence at multiyear lows, but upcoming home sales have grown over the last few months? Just remember that the builder confidence index is tilted toward smaller homebuilders, not the major publicly traded homebuilders that are still buying down rates to move product.

Latest home sales are still at 2019 levels, and so are housing starts and permits. If the substantial builders lose their ability to buy down rates, this sales data should eventually break below 2019 levels, but for now sales have operated within a small range for the past 10 years.

Since mortgage rates shot toward 7.60%, we have seen housing data turn very negative. Compare that to last year at this time, when mortgage rates were heading toward 6% and existing home sales hit a nine-month high. The massive builders can only do so much to keep starts from falling more. For now, it doesn’t look good for housing construction in 2027, given where mortgage rates are at and the builders profit margins falling more and more.
