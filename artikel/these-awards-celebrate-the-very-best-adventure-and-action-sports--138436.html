The category winners for the Illume Awards have been revealed, an extreme sports photography competition built on the legacy of Red Bull Illume.

For 20 years, Red Bull turned the Illume Awards into the most recognizable competition for adventure and action sports photography.

In a recent development, this year, it is just the Illume Awards. But the breathtaking images keep coming; check out this year’s category winners below.

French photographer Louis Garnier has won the Snow Category of The Illume Awards 2026 with his photo of skier Kevin Rolland captured in front of a fire fountain in Tignes, France.

“The energy around the whole project was incredible. We only had three attempts with the flames, and it is still my favorite photo I’ve ever made with Kevin”, he recounts about the day the winning photo was taken.

Austrian photographer Tom Klocker takes the title in the Climb Category with his photograph of climber Mario Andexlinger in Iceland, selected as the winning image from 20 Climb Category Finalists.

“This shot was very challenging, and you had to have extremely good ice climbing experience. The mystical atmosphere that day went perfectly with the colors,” notes Klocker about the making of the photograph.

French photographer Tim Rochas has won the Aerial Category of The Illume Awards 2026 with his photograph of paraglider Pierre Bottero flying alongside Factory Butte in Utah, United States.

Captured from another glider in flight, the image places the pilot against the vast, rugged desert landscape, creating a striking contrast between the scale of nature and the small human presence in the air.

According to recent reports, the story behind the winning photograph began with a flight from the top of Factory Butte. As the pilots took to the air above the extraordinary desert landscape, Rochas saw an opportunity to capture Bottero alongside the immense rock formation.

“For me, this image is about the contrast between the fragility of a human in flight and the raw power of nature,” he says.

Recent findings suggest that portuguese photographer Beatriz Ryder is the winner of the Surf Category of The Illume Awards 2026. Her photograph of surfer Shaley Mercado in the Solomon Islands was selected as the winning image from 20 Surf Category Finalists.

Industry observers note that “We were in one of the most remote parts of the world, the Solomon Islands. Right in front of where we were staying was this little wave – shallow, fast, and with short, fun runners,” states Ryder about the location behind her winning photograph.

Spanish photographer Ágata Dominguez has won the Trail Category of The Illume Awards 2026 with her captivating photo of late athlete Ida Amelie Robsahm captured in Sancti Spiritu, Spain, during the Zegama-Aizkorri Race 2026, a legendary trail running race.

Recent findings suggest that “What I love most about photography is being there for the moments that are difficult to explain afterwards, capturing that look, that movement, and that energy in a split second: the tension, the concentration, and the feeling of being completely immersed in what’s happening. I’m not just interested in getting the perfect shot; I would like my images to make you feel something and, ideally, make you feel like you were there,” she explains her approach to photography.

German photographer Robert Christ takes the title in the Skate Category with his photograph of skater André Gerlich in Germany, selected as the winning image from 20 Skate Category Finalists.

Robert Christ’s photo captures André Gerlich performing a backside air trick on a sculpture by Andreas Kiessling in southern Germany.

“André and I found out about a sculpture bike path and immediately made intends to go there. André chose to skate this rough piece of concrete, did a stylish backside air, and we got a photo we were both really stoked about,” Christ states.

American photographer Lear Miller has won the Bike Category of The Illume Awards 2026 with his photograph of Hannah Bergemann, captured as part of Chris Benchetler’s Mountains of the Moon project in Pacific City, Oregon, United States.

Getting the photo was not easy, as Miller explains, “We shot the bike segment of Chris Benchetler’s Mountains of the Moon over four nights, from about 10 pm to 4 am, in some pretty brutal rain. The riders were riding in the dark while we constantly battled water on the lenses and gear.”.

Chilean photographer Gonzalo Robert Parraguez has been named the winner of the Adventure Category of The Illume Awards 2026 with his photograph of kayaker Aniol Serrasolses in Svalbard, Norway.

The photo captures Aniol Serrasolses descending a waterfall in the Norwegian archipelago of Svalbard.

“We always talk about the photo, but we rarely talk about the athletes, and I dedicate this 100% to Aniol; he was the one who pushed for this whole project to happen, so that we could be taking these incredible photos in the middle of the Arctic”, indicates Parraguez.

For more on the contest, head to the Illume Awards website.
