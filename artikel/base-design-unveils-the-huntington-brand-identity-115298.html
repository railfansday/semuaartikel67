Base Design rebuilds The Huntington brand identity around a sculpted H monogram, a hidden gem idea and green drawn from pigment and plant life.

Recent findings suggest that the Huntington is a library, an art museum and botanical gardens on 200 acres in Southern California, founded in 1919, and most visitors knew only the gardens. Base Design starts from the mark itself. Two flanking pillars in lime green hold a diamond shaped gem at the center, set against a deep green field. The studio calls the idea a hidden gem, treasured, layered, quietly extraordinary, and the mark behaves that way. It never shouts. It sits still and lets the green do the talking, while accents shift like light on a leaf or oil on canvas.

The wordmark is set in a serif with flared terminals, THE HUNTINGTON debossed into a green textured surface so the letters read as much by shadow as by ink. The same voice carries the exhibition posters, where single words stack large and flat. ROSE breaks across four stacked lines in pink, cut-out rose photographs tucked between the letters, on a poster lying in the flower beds. The orchid show poster sets its title in pink and purple over a full-bleed orchid photograph.

In a recent development, the H doubles as a pattern. On the What's On board it repeats in four colourways, orange, green, blue and yellow, each stem holding its gem. A close crop of the mark fills a whole frame with one lime stem and crossbar against dark green, the curve of the pillar edge doing all the work.

It has been revealed that print carries the system. Brochures rest on giant green leaves, their covers carrying the H and the names of the collections. A flat lay of the print suite shows the orchid show poster, the bonsai collection guide, a portrait exhibition guide and a yellow Stories from the Library sheet, each piece holding the same green and the same serif. The Library Quarterly sits on a leather chair, its cream cover and red masthead quiet against the dark wood around it.

Outside, the system scales up without changing its voice. A dark green wayfinding totem stands among the planting, its directory set in small capitals above the H at the base.

Two exhibition billboards pair portraits with green type, one for Mineo Mizuno, one for Don Bachardy, and the same green runs through the noticeboards.
