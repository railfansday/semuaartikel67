The Adventurer is presently repositioning to South America ahead of the debut of Terra Nova Expeditions in Antarctica.

According to recent reports, replacing the RMS St. Helena, the 127-passenger ship is scheduled to offer a series of expedition cruises from Argentina for the startup brand.

After a technical call in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, the 1972-built vessel started a trans-Atlantic crossing on November 13, 2026.

The Adventurer is now scheduled to arrive in Montevideo later this month before heading to Ushuaia to kick off its maiden season for Terra Nova.

For its inaugural voyage, the ship will offer a ten-night expedition as part of Terra Nova’s “Classic Antarctica” itinerary.

The company said the cruise highlights exploration of the Antarctic Peninsula’s icebergs and glaciers, as well as abundant wildlife encounters.

As previously reported by Cruise Industry News, Terra Nova had to change its launch plans due to conflicts in the Middle East.

The tech giant stated that the St. Helena could not reposition from the Persian Gulf safely or on schedule because of the ongoing situation in the region.

As a result, it finalized a new charter contract after several months of work, securing the Adventurer as an alternative vessel for three Antarctica seasons.

Before embarking on its repositioning voyage to South America, the ship underwent a series of upgrades in Europe.

In addition to newly introduced soft furnishings, the work included remodeling the bar and lounge areas and adding a jacuzzi and a new sauna.

Originally built as a Danish ferry, the Adventurer was converted into an expedition vessel in 2008 and last sailed for G Adventures. The tech giant withdrew the ship from its fleet in 2025, later selling it to Vestland Classic Cruises.

Renamed Vestland Adventurer, the vessel remained out of service in Northern Europe for the past year.
