Dollar Tree’s stock is skyrocketing. One number in its earnings report helps explain why

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The broader markets are seeing red today, primarily led lower by yet another steep selloff in technology and artificial intelligence stocks. But one manufacturer nearly as far from those sectors as you can get is seeing its share price surge by over 10% today: the discount retailer Dollar Tree. Here’s what you need to know. What’s happened? This morning, the discount retail chain Dollar Tree, Inc. (Nasdaq: DLTR) reported its third-quarter fiscal 2026 results, which cover the three-month period ending October 31. The most salient metrics from those results are as follows: Net sales: $5.1 billion Adjusted earnings per share (EPS): $1.16 Comparable store net sales: up 4.6% Foot traffic increase: 3.8% New stores: 84 Total stores: 9,511 To put those financials in perspective, LSEG analysts estimated $5.04 billion in net sales and earnings per share of 91 cents, notes Reuters. Announcing the company’s Q3 results, Dollar Tree CEO Mike Creedon said, “Our third quarter results show the strength of Dollar Tree’s value proposition and the progress we’ve made to improve the customer experience. Comp traffic accelerated to 3.8% and drove comparable sales above the high end of our outlook. Our broader assortment, better stores and stronger customer engagement give shoppers more reasons to choose Dollar Tree and come back more often.” Why are Dollar Tree’s shares surging? After announcing its Q3, DLTR stock surged—one of the few to do so today on Wall Street. Dollar Tree’s price jump comes down to a few factors, both of which seem to have pleased investors, based on the company’s share price. The first was the net sales and earnings per share beat. Net sales increased by 6.9% versus the same quarter a year earlier. This was helped by a 4.6% increase in comparable net store sales. Those comps were driven by a 3.8% increase in foot traffic and a 0.8% increase in the amount people spend on an average ticket. In other words, most of the comp increase was not driven by higher tickets, but by increased foot traffic—which is exactly what you want as you enter the final quarter of the year: the all-key holiday period. In addition to favorable quarterly results, another factor that buoyed investors’ view of the company was its full-year fiscal 2026 outlook. Dollar Tree increased its full-year earnings per share range to between $8.05 and $8.25. That was up from its prior range of $7.70 to $8.05. DLTR’s up more than 60% in six months As of the time of this writing, DLTR shares are right now trading up 11% to $154.41 on the back of its Q3 results. That’s an exceptional one-day jump for a discount retailer, but it goes to show the faith investors currently have in the company, which may be one of the few to benefit from an inflation-wary consumer. As prices rise, people tend to cut back on discretionary spending, which can cause downward pressure on retailer shares. But as everyday costs get higher, consumers also tend to shop around for the best price for essentials and everyday items. Dollar Tree has a low-cost pricing structure (many items cost around $1.25, with higher-priced items selling for between $3 and $7) that appeals to cash-strapped consumers operating in an economy where sticker prices at higher-end retailers seem increasingly out of reach. While the company’s roughly 11% share price jump today (as of the time of this writing) is impressive, looking back a little further reveals even better stock price growth. In the past month alone, DLTR shares are up 22%, and over the past six months, DLTR shares are up 61%.
