📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that hi Auto announced on November 18, 2026 that Checkers & Rally’s had extended its AI drive-thru ordering partnership through 2030. The restaurant manufacturer intends to expand the deployment from 267 drive-thrus to more than 360 by the end of the first quarter of 2027.

In a recent development, the plan represents a 38% increase in Hi Auto’s footprint at the brands and continues a relationship that began in 2023. The longer contract and larger rollout make voice ordering an ongoing operating layer across the franchise system, with deployment performance now tied to sales and staffing measures as well as order volume.

Industry observers note that hi Auto reports that the system has processed more than 11 million orders at Checkers & Rally’s during 2026 and is handling more than 1.1 million orders a month. Across the deployment, average ticket size has risen 1.1%, while the number of orders needing a crew member to step in has fallen by half since January 2026.

Those measures track different parts of the business case: ticket size reflects sales per order, while crew interventions show where automation still requires human assistance. Evaluating both provides a fuller picture than counting automated transactions alone.

“Checkers & Rally’s challenged us to prove that AI in the drive-thru drives growth, not just efficiency,” remarked Hi Auto CEO and co-founder Roy Baharav.

Sources indicate that the deployment takes orders in English and Spanish and can follow a guest who switches between them within the same order. Checkers & Rally’s states that capability fits the diverse communities its restaurants serve, making language support part of the ordering workflow across different local markets.

The AI Order Taker greets guests, takes and upsells orders, and sends them directly to the restaurant’s point-of-sale (POS) system. Human assistance remains available when a guest needs a team member.

It has been revealed that hi Auto’s product documentation says the platform combines large language models for natural-language processing with in-house audio filtering and automatic speech recognition tailored to drive-thru environments. An audio box connects the system to existing headsets and POS platforms, with HME, PAR and Xenial listed as supported integrations.

The documentation adds that a trained human supervisor steps in when the AI has low confidence. This design separates routine order taking from exception handling, reducing the need for restaurant employees to correct every uncertain exchange while retaining human oversight for difficult conversations.

Across approximately 1,000 stores, Hi Auto reports order completion above 93% and accuracy of 96%. Completion is the percentage of orders sent to the POS without staff intervention, while accuracy is the percentage sent with every item and customization exactly right. These figures cover the company’s broader deployment base rather than Checkers & Rally’s alone.

Industry observers note that for franchise operations, the platform provides centralized control of voice persona, scripts, upsells and regional configurations. Franchisees can adjust menus, limited-time offers and regional offers within brand controls, allowing local variation while maintaining system-wide standards.

According to recent reports, the next phase of the rollout will test whether the reported ticket gains and reduced crew intervention hold across a larger franchise footprint. That makes consistency across restaurants, languages and supervised exceptions the central operational question behind the longer commitment.
