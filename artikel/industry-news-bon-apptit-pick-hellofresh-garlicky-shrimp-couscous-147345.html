According to recent reports, most nights I try to hit up an exercise class after work, which means when I finally get home in the evening I’m tired, hungry, and craving something full of nutrients. With 26 grams of protein and just 25 minutes of prep-and-cook time, HelloFresh’s Garlicky Shrimp Couscous Bowl is a great solution for dinner.

Recent findings suggest that this flavorful bowl reminds me of Dig Inn’s glory days—only I can enjoy it without elbowing my way through midtown lunchtime mobs. Roasted broccoli and shrimp sautéed with lemon and plenty of garlic are spooned on top of large couscous pearls. Chili flakes add an extra kick.

Industry observers note that you’ll need to grab olive oil, butter, salt, and pepper from your own pantry; everything else is included in your delivery. The shrimp arrives fully cleaned and deveined, thank god. I love that a small pouch of veggie stock is included for cooking your couscous, a detail that many meal kits overlook.

True to the recipe card’s promise, the meal cooks in less than half an hour. Prep your mise (chop the broccoli, garlic, and parsley; slice your lemon; rinse the shrimp), then get your broccoli in the oven and couscous on the stove. While they do their thing, you cook the shrimp so that everything finishes around the same time.

Recent findings suggest that I found the instructions incredibly clear to follow, including when to add water and butter to your shrimp, and when to reduce the pan’s heat. I don’t often cook shrimp at home, and I was pleasantly surprised how easy it was and how quickly they pinked up.

In a word: delish! Garlic can cover a multitude of sins, and there’s a generous amount used when cooking both the couscous and shrimp, the latter also includes garlic powder, for good measure. Lemon and parsley give the dish a nice freshness; the flavors remind me of my favorite clam pie from a local Brooklyn pizzeria.

It has been revealed that this is a great dish for pescatarians, those looking to cut back on red meat, and health-conscious eaters. The small ingredient list is full of nutritional value: carbs, fats, and lean protein to help me refuel after a long day, plus five grams of fiber and vitamin K, calcium, and potassium from the broccoli (cruciferous veggies are super good for you).

In a recent development, would make again. Sometimes you want to use your time in the kitchen to discover new flavors and learn new techniques, and sometimes your main goal is getting dinner on the table swiftly. This recipe hits on the weeknight trifecta: It’s tasty, easy to make, and nutritious.
