THE HOLIDAYS AND GLP-1 can feel like a cruel joke. Mashed potatoes and gravy? Come on. Pumpkin pie? Dude. Your grandma’s urge to Eat! Eat! What’s wrong with you? It can be a lot.Luckily, we’re far enough along in this whole GLP-1 experience that researchers and experts now know how people who take the weight-loss medications can not only survive the holidays—but actually enjoy them, gravy and all. We talked to some of these smart people to come up with your GLP-1 game plan—both at the table, and for around the table when those relatives you only see once a year get sassy.

A POPULAR STRATEGY is to skip a dose for the holiday week. But doctors tend to recommend against this strategy.

Vijay Kasi, MD, PhD, cardiologist at Orlando Health says that you might even experience short-term changes in blood pressure and blood sugar spikes, which is really only a concern if you are diabetic. Now would missing one GLP-1 dose severely impact the heart? Not really.

Dr. Kasi says increased risk for cardiac events is more likely to happen when people quit the medication for good and slide back to unhealthy eating habits. A 2026 study found that any heart benefit from taking a GLP-1 disappears after two years. Those same people were linked to a 22 percent increased risk of stroke, heart attacks, and early death.

But again, we’re talking about a week—not two years. So what’s wrong with resuming a day or two after the holiday? If you are still adamant about skipping a dose, resuming it immediately after is recommended. “If they ate a really large meal and then immediately took the medication, they may feel very uncomfortable,” notes Mir Ali, MD, medical director of MemorialCare Surgical Weight Loss Center at Orange Coast Medical Center. “But if they wait 24 hours and the meal has moved through their system, then it probably won't be as problematic.”.

The real concern doctors have is if people continue to delay their GLP-1 schedule for multiple weeks. Dr. Ali claims people stop feeling the effects a week after they stop the medication because that’s how long it stays in your system. Appetite returns and so does food noise—the persistent and intrusive thoughts related to cravings or obsessing over how much food you ate. Ashley Koff, RD, author of Your Best Shot, says other issues that may promote overeating are that you no longer have a delay in gastric emptying and an alteration or sheer lack of fullness signaling. “When you stop this medication abruptly you go from [constant] sustained hormone back to your own which works at best for 2 to 5 minutes when triggered by food,” she says.

Recent findings suggest that while one day of eating more than usual won’t make a significant change to your health, Dr. Kasi indicates that some people do struggle to get back on track after the holidays end. “For a few people, it may be easy, but a vast majority trying to break away from their established regimen might have problems.”.

Industry observers note that DOCTORS ARE GENERALLY against taking a smaller dose than usual because it is not included in FDA guidelines for how to safely and effectively use a GLP-1. “If a lower dose wasn't effective for you beforehand, it's not going to be effective for you right before a holiday either,” notes Dr. Ali. “It’s similar to skipping a dose.”.

Sources indicate that there is one exception. Dr. Ali claims people who have attained a healthy weight or reached their goal weight may be able to lower their dose or take it less frequently as a maintenance strategy.

According to recent reports, “For some people who have been on it for a long time, they titrate down to the most effective dose,” indicates Heidi Skolnik, MS, CDN, nutritionist and author of Your GLP-1 Game Strategy. “You're trying to find what's the lowest amount that still is effective for you.”.

Industry observers note that lowering your GLP-1 dose is a conversation you’ll want to have with your doctor ahead of the holiday season. Experts say to avoid self-prescribing.

Sources indicate that IF YOU’RE ON a GLP-1, your plate will likely look different from your relatives who are scooping up substantial portions of carb-heavy sides. With a lower tolerance for small portions and a change in appetite, your choices will involve a delicate balance of trying to meet your nutritional needs while also not making your stomach hurt from overindulging. You can, of course, still enjoy as many courses as you like.

If you are going to someone’s house, Skolnik advises calling ahead and asking what’s on the menu so you can plan in advance. For appetizers, you’ll want to avoid any high-fat items. “It's okay if you don't hit your protein and fiber goals. But what you don't want to do is eat a lot of high fat,” she indicates. “This is because your gastric system is slowing down. With high-fat foods, you may not digest all of that.”.

Not sure what’s high-fat or not? Play it safe and bring your own appetizer. Not only are you being a hospitable guest, but you’ll have something you can eat. Skolnik recommends:.

Don’t think that you’re banned from eating any certain foods, but with small portions, you have to know if it’s worth indulging. Koff claims to use the "Delicious to Me" rule. Stop after the first bite to assess how much you enjoy the food. “I give them what that means on a scale they devise from 1 to 10, where a 7 or greater means it’s delicious and a go.”.

Recent findings suggest that so include those carb-heavy sides like mac and cheese and creamy mashed potatoes—just keep it to around a quarter of your plate. The majority of your plate should include a mix of vegetables and protein, which Skolnik says is usually manageable with turkey.

In a recent development, common side effects of GLP-1 use include nausea from drinking and a reduced craving for alcohol. If you can tolerate one or two, both dietitians say to go for it. A drinking variation of the “Delicious to Me” strategy is the “9 Sips” rule. “You still need to factor in what sugar, caffeine, and alcohol will do but a smaller amount is the win while still enjoying,” states Koff. You’ll also want to drink water at regular intervals while you’re drinking.

Recent findings suggest that if you want to avoid being pressured into drinking, Skolnik states to also have a drink on hand with a lime slice to give the appearance of an alcoholic drink. “Nobody has to know what's in your glass.”.

No need to ruin a favorite dessert by trying to choose only high-protein items. If you want to save your appetite for an extra slice of pie, Koff indicates to go for it. Just make sure to meet your nutritional needs in different ways as with a protein drink before dinner or supplements.

IT’S NOT JUST the food you need to navigate; the interactions matter too. Depending on your family situation, you may need to set some firm boundaries on or redirect comments related to your weight or appetite. That doesn’t mean the holidays are the time to announce to everyone you’re on a GLP-1 (unless you want to). Some phrases you can use include:.

Skolnik claims having an ally is extremely crucial if you’re expecting other people to comment on your eating habits or your looks. They can help to redirect any uncomfortable conversation, provide support for your choices so you don’t feel isolated, or just provide emotional support. “Have someone else in the room with a code word or a look that signals them to intervene and change the conversation or divert attention away,” she says.

According to recent reports, even if you don’t have an ally physically next to you, have someone available on the phone that you can text or call. Skolnik states to escape to the bathroom if you need and use it as a place to recenter.

To avoid offending people who probably spent hours cooking, it can also be helpful to practice polite ways to turn down second helpings or pass on specific dishes.

According to recent reports, “When the giving of food is looked at as love, the receiving of it is love,” indicates Skolnik. “But you can still receive the love without the food.”.
