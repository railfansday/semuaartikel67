iRestore Elite Laser Hair Growth System Review: Husband-Approved

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that the Aging Process is hard. It’s jarring, for example, when the luscious, flowing locks of your youth start to disappear before your very eyes. But there's nothing to do about this natural hair loss—or is there? While still the subject of ongoing research, red-light therapy has been used to treat inflammation, pain relief, and wrinkles, and—as it turns out—it may also help with hair regrowth. It’s also a much more accessible option than flying to Istanbul for a full-on hair transplant.

I learned about iRestore’s Elite Laser Growth Hair System after it came across my TikTok FYP. My algorithm likely served it to me because of my own hair woes (scalp irritation and eczema). Upon learning more about the system, I hoped it could help me with cell regeneration and kill off the bacteria causing flares on my scalp.

Industry observers note that however, an even better test subject stood before me: my sweet husband, who is dealing with a rapidly receding hairline. So I roped him into testing this laser helmet—for science, of course. And to better understand noted science, I spoke with Zakia Rahman, MD, FAAD, a clinical professor of dermatology at Stanford University School of Medicine.

In a recent development, red-light therapy didn’t start as a treatment for antiaging and recovery. It actually came about as an accidental solution for hair loss in the 1960s, when Hungarian scientist Endre Mester was researching whether exposure to red light could treat cancer in hairless mice. Imagine his surprise when the mice started growing hair.

In a recent development, red-light therapy has come a long way since then. According to Rahman, red-light wavelengths are thought to activate the mitochondria in skin cells, which may encourage an uptick in the production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) to promote latest growth. Red-light therapy may also improve blood flow to hair follicles.

Importantly, iRestore's devices are FDA-cleared—a safety designation from the US Food and Drug Administration indicating that a product is “substantially equivalent” to another legally marketed device. There are three hair-growth devices in iRestore's lineup: the entry-level Essential ($425), the mid-tier Professional ($799), and the Elite ($1,699). Each contains iRestore’s proprietary Lumitech technology, which combines LED lights and medical-grade lasers. These laser diodes, called Vixo lasers, operate in the range of 655 to 680 nanometers (nm). (Preliminary studies with mice have shown that using red light in the 600- to 660-nm range, and infrared wavelengths in the 800- to 850-nm range, can promote cell regeneration, pain relief, and more.).

The idea is that this dynamic duo of lights and lasers can penetrate deeper into the skin than ordinary red light with lasers, while the LEDs ensure light is distributed across the scalp for maximum efficacy. Together, they reach a maximum of 2,500 milliwatts (mW) in the Elite, compared to the Professional's 1,410 mW and Essential's 600 mW.

According to recent reports, the Elite is iRestore’s top-of-the-line offering and has the most LEDs of the three laser hair-growth systems, with 500 diodes in total—that's 300 lasers and 200 LEDs. The Essential has 51 lasers and 69 LEDs for a total of 120 diodes, while the iRestore Professional has 82 lasers and 200 LEDs for a total of 282 diodes. The Elite was also designed to cover more of the scalp than the other two models and requires 12 minutes of daily use, while the Professional and Essential require 25 minutes every other day.

If price is not a factor, the Elite is the no-brainer choice due to its higher-concentrated light therapy, shorter use time, and potentially faster results.

Industry observers note that the helmet arrived in hefty red packaging containing the helmet, a portable battery pack (annoyingly, this is a $99 add-on purchase), a charging cord, and a red-and-black zippered travel case. It feels very fragile, and I wouldn't throw it around—then again, you probably wouldn't proposal to shove such an expensive device in the back of your bathroom vanity.

Sources indicate that during the daily 12-minute sessions with the Elite helmet, you'll feel as if you're sitting under bright, hot sunlight. The remote displays a countdown of how many minutes you have left, and it beeps once the cycle is complete. Because I was testing the helmet to treat my dry scalp and eczema, I grew increasingly concerned with every passing minute of feeling warmer and warmer. (Sweating is a big no-no for eczema, so I wasn’t thrilled to experience this side effect.) With time, my husband was able to get used to the heat-lamp effect, as it’s not painful, just initially uncomfortable.

Within the first two weeks of continuously using the helmet, I was thrilled to see baby hairs begin to pop up around my husband's hairline. The growth continued through two months of regular use. The top of his crown had more volume and overall hair density, while new growth appeared in the more drastically affected areas near his forehead. While the additional hair didn’t completely cover his scalp, the change was stark enough for old college friends and family members to remark on how good his hair looked.

In a recent development, the effects aren't permanent, though. Unfortunately, my husband stopped his iRestore routine after a few months due to travel. While the iRestore Elite comes with a case that makes it super portable, my husband was nervous about misplacing it while we were on the go. (Also, the thing takes up a decent amount of space.) Unfortunately, stopping the treatments reversed the progress in a matter of weeks. Just like that, he was back to square one.

Sources indicate that rahman likens this phenomenon to the importance of a commitment to a healthy daily diet and regular exercise; one instance of either won't make a difference in your health. Rather, consistency is required to drive results. When I told her that my husband's hair growth had fallen off, she strongly encouraged us to pick it back up.

In a recent development, in addition to using the red-light therapy helmet as recommended, iRestore suggests adding thickening haircare products (shampoo, conditioner, supplements, and serum) to your regimen to get the best results. The star ingredient in the serum and shampoo is plant-based Redensyl, which is meant to fortify hair follicles during their regrowth. Additional elements—like the shampoo's castor oil and biotin, and the conditioner's vegan keratin and argan oil—act as natural strengtheners.

The supplements contain natural dihydrotestosterone (DHT) blockers derived from saw palmetto. These blockers work to prevent the enzyme (5-alpha reductase) from transforming testosterone into DHT. In essence, this hormone is a driver of pattern hair loss and can lead to general inactivity in hair growth.

The supplements, strengthening serum, shampoo, and conditioner come included with the purchase of the iRestore Elite. The extras didn't seem to boost my results but may have supported my husband's regular use of the helmet. (Full disclosure: He was also using his usual Rogaine throughout this entire process.).

According to recent reports, ultimately, these included products are a nice bonus, but you don't need to go out of your way to buy them from iRestore or to replenish your stash. Plenty of anti-hair-loss treatments are available, including oral and topical medications and hair-transplant procedures. All have their merits in slowing hair thinning and promoting fuller hair. Even so, “combination therapy will work better than a stand-alone," states Rahman, in response to my question about whether red-light therapy could work as an isolated treatment or if it's best to include it as part of a multicomponent regimen. Essentially, the potential benefits of red-light therapy only stand to increase when you supplement it with minoxidil and other highly researched hair-loss measures.

It has been revealed that as for my eczema, the iRestore Elite did quell my scalp flare-ups, but I don't think I'd throw down so much money for just that. For hair regrowth, that's a totally different story. My husband was already using Rogaine before the iRestore Elite showed up. But combined with the helmet and iRestore's hair-growth products and supplements, he experienced greater results. For those pursuing thicker hair at home, the iRestore Elite is surprisingly effective.
