In a recent development, I first got into the driver’s seat of the Ioniq 5 a couple of years back and came away impressed by its futuristic aesthetics and loaded suite of tech features that really added value to its bottom-line cost. At the time, it stood out as one of the easiest electric crossovers to recommend, easily one of the best electric cars for the money.

In a recent development, fast-forward to today, and I feel a bit differently after driving more than 80 EVs this year alone. While there's more competition in the compact crossover SUV segment now than ever, car shoppers can’t overlook the $50,815 as-tested price attached to the 2026 Hyundai Ioniq 5 AWD Limited I tested.

Sure, I’ve driven compelling rivals like the Ford Mustang Mach-E, Kia EV6, and the newer Subaru Trailseeker, but you can’t count out the Ioniq 5 just yet. Here’s why.

According to recent reports, I drove the 2026 Hyundai Ioniq 5 for a total of 142.3 miles, reaching an impressive 3.9 mi/kWh throughout that time. Not only is that a marked improvement over the 3.3 mi/kWh average I got when I tested the 2024 model, but it also easily outpaces rivals like the 2026 Subaru Trailseeker and the 2027 Rivian R2.

Recent findings suggest that the Ioniq 5 really shines around town, hitting mostly local roads with stop-and-go traffic and reaching a peak short-distance efficiency of 5 mi/kWh. On longer highway drives, it’s still pretty efficient with a best output of 3.8 mi/kWh. This improvement is important because while Hyundai rates it for 259 miles on a full charge, my 3.9 mi/kWh average translates to roughly 328 miles of real-world range from its 84 kWh battery.

According to recent reports, unfortunately, I couldn’t charge it at home because it didn’t come with a charger, nor did I have an adapter to connect my older J1772 charger. Despite this, adopting Tesla’s NACS charging port makes public charging far more accessible — and Hyundai claims it can add 178 miles of range in just 15 minutes on a 350kW DC fast charger.

In a recent development, one of the biggest surprises during my week with the 2026 Hyundai Ioniq 5 wasn't just how smoothly it drove — it was how little energy it used doing it. Again, I can’t stress enough how impressive the 3.9 mi/kWh overall average is, which puts it squarely among the most efficient electric crossovers in its class, especially for a dual-motor AWD model.

Usually, hitting numbers near the 4 mi/kWh mark is reserved for more aerodynamic models like the Lucid Air — or smaller, lighter crossovers like the Subaru Solterra and Toyota bZ. Seeing a boxy SUV deliver this kind of real-world mileage proves how much Hyundai has refined the Ioniq 5's powertrain efficiency.

Despite its compact crossover footprint, the 2026 Hyundai Ioniq 5 feels remarkably open and spacious on the inside thanks to its flat floor and long wheelbase. Both front and rear riders get generous legroom, with back-seat passengers easily stretching out thanks to 39.4 inches of space.

Recent findings suggest that I’m also a fan of Hyundai’s H-Tex seating upholstery, mainly due to how it strikes a great balance between plush cushioning and everyday durability — the seats feel soft and supportive right away, yet the material comes across as rugged enough to handle accidental spills. Around back, that spacious theme carries over into an expansive trunk with a wide opening and plenty of room for luggage, groceries, or bulky gear, offering 26.3 cubic feet of accessible cargo space.

In a recent development, yes, the design of the 2026 Hyundai Ioniq 5 hasn’t changed much from previous years, but I can’t help falling in love all over again with the headlights. Hyundai’s signature Parametric Pixel design language remains one of its biggest visual cues, anchored up front by its distinctive stacked LED headlights.

Recent findings suggest that unlike conventional round headlights, the slit-like light bars around the hood and squared-off pixel clusters give the front fascia a charming, retro-futuristic edge that still turns heads. Beyond looking great, the crisp LED projector beams cast a bright light pattern on dark roads.

It has been revealed that for a crossover with generous interior space, the 2026 Hyundai Ioniq 5 XRT feels surprisingly agile. Thanks to its low-mounted battery pack and dual-motor HTRAC AWD setup, it delivers instant acceleration off the line and stays remarkably planted through tighter corners and highway lane changes.

The steering is well-weighted without feeling heavy, and I personally prefer setting Hyundai’s regenerative braking paddles to their most aggressive level, which likely contributes to its superb efficiency.

Hyundai continues to set the bar for camera visibility in mainstream EVs thanks to how generous it is with premium features. While most EVs come with backup cameras at the very least, the 2026 Ioniq 5 is equipped with a Blind-Spot View Monitor that pops the camera feed directly into the 12.3-inch digital instrument cluster, giving me an unobstructed look at adjacent lanes before I merge.

Sources indicate that that thoughtfulness carries over to the 360-degree Surround View Monitor, which stitches together a sharp overhead view of the vehicle on the center display. Combined with multiple angled curb views and parking distance sensors, maneuvering the Ioniq 5 into tight parking spots is a breeze.

In a recent development, I’ve been in so many different vehicles this year that there are probably fewer than a dozen that stand out for their interior aesthetics. Unfortunately, the Ioniq 5 won’t make the cut this time around. While the exterior still turns heads, the cabin is starting to show its age and feels overdue for a proper refresh.

The dual 12.3-inch display setup and physical climate controls remain intuitive, but the minimalist gray-heavy trim comes off as boring by today's standards. Put it up against newer rivals like the 2027 Rivian R2 — with its warmer materials, cleaner architectural lines, and far more modern cabin styling — and the Ioniq 5's interior starts to look noticeably bland.

Sources indicate that as much impressed as I am by the 2026 Hyundai Ioniq 5’s efficiency and sub-$50K as-tested cost, there are quite simply more compelling options out there. If you're willing to stretch your budget just a bit higher, the 2027 Rivian R2 Performance immediately stands out with fresher styling, a far cooler and more modern interior, a spacious frunk, and drop-down rear glass.

On the flip side, if you want to save money without giving up capability, the 2026 Subaru Trailseeker undercuts the Ioniq 5 on price — while offering way more cargo room, higher towing and roof-rack utility, and the kind of brand recognition that makes it an easier everyday sell.

It has been revealed that even with tougher competition, the 2026 Hyundai Ioniq 5 is still well worth buying. Sure, its cabin is starting to look a bit dated, but its standout 3.9 mi/kWh efficiency, generous rear legroom, and sharp camera tech continue to make it one of the most well-rounded electric crossovers on the road.

In fact, it remains the EV I’m most inclined to recommend in Hyundai’s entire lineup, striking a sweet spot that neither the smaller Kona Electric nor the pricier three-row Ioniq 9 quite matches for everyday versatility.
