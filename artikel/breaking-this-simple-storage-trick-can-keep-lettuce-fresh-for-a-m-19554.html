The trick to keeping lettuce fresh for a month is already in your kitchen.

It has been revealed that there are few things more irritating than throwing away food you had every intention of eating. Fresh produce is especially frustrating because it seems to operate on its own schedule. You buy a beautiful head of lettuce, imagining all the fresh salads and sandwiches you’ll make that week, only to find a head of wilted leaves waiting for you when you finally get around to it.

Recent findings suggest that enter Chef Charles Kim, better known as Chef Fatty on social media. He has an impressive knack for making me rethink my cooking. He’s already changed how I caramelize onions and make the juiciest chicken breasts, and now he’s apparently coming for my crisper drawer. His trick promises to keep lettuce fresh for an entire month using nothing more than aluminum foil—a claim I couldn’t resist investigating.

Sources indicate that the idea is simple: Instead of leaving your head of lettuce in the plastic produce bag, loosely wrap it in aluminum foil before putting it in the refrigerator. That’s it. No special containers, paper towels or elaborate produce-preserving rituals required.

Sources indicate that chef Fatty recommends removing any yellowing or deteriorating leaves before wrapping the lettuce. If it’s dirty, you can wash it first, but make sure it’s completely dry. Then loosely fold the foil around the head and place it in the refrigerator’s crisper drawer.

The reasoning has to do with moisture and airflow. Lettuce loses its crispness as water escapes from its leaves, but trapping too much moisture can also encourage decay. A loose foil wrapping strikes just the right balance, protecting the leaves from drying out without sealing them inside an airtight container.

Lindsay Parrill For Taste Of Home.

I wanted to see the difference for myself, so I bought two heads of romaine lettuce at the same time. I left one in the plastic produce bag from the grocery store and loosely wrapped the other in aluminum foil. Both went into the same crisper drawer in my refrigerator, where I left them undisturbed for a month. When I finally pulled them out, the difference was unmistakable.

After 30 days in the refrigerator, the foil-wrapped romaine was in dramatically better shape than its plastic-bag counterpart. The lettuce stored in its original bag had deteriorated considerably, while the foil-wrapped head had retained much more of its original appearance and crispness.

It has been revealed that I had to peel away several of the outer leaves of the plastic-bag lettuce before I found anything resembling a crisp texture. The foil-wrapped head wasn’t perfect, but it was still pretty crisp, with leaves so fresh that I happily turned them into a Caesar salad for lunch. This was an entire month after I’d bought it. I’d call that a successful experiment.

In a recent development, while I tested the method with romaine, it’s also worth trying on other lettuce varieties. How long each stays fresh will depend on its condition when purchased, the refrigerator it’s stored in, and the variety itself. And lettuce isn’t the only vegetable that might benefit. Taste of Home senior editor Lindsay Mattison told me she uses the same aluminum foil trick to store celery, which makes sense given its tendency to lose moisture and go limp in the refrigerator.

I wouldn’t necessarily recommend buying lettuce with the intention of ignoring it for a month, and it’s still notable to check for slime, mold or other signs of spoilage before eating it. But knowing I can give my produce a much longer window before it ends up in the compost is reason enough to keep an extra roll of foil handy.
