Sources indicate that minnesota’s fresh paid leave law, championed and signed by outgoing Democratic Gov. Tim Walz, is already causing major headaches as public employees take advantage of the program at levels that are straining government services.

Eleven months after the law took effect — allowing Minnesota workers up to 12 weeks a year off with partial pay to care for a newborn or a sick family member, and up to 12 weeks to recover from their own serious illness — almost one in four Minneapolis city employees has used the benefit, the Minnesota Star Tribune reported.

The outlet reported that the absences are putting a "squeeze" on some departments, including the agency that takes emergency calls in the city.

According to recent reports, on Tuesday, a clip posted by Alpha News from an October budget presentation to the Minneapolis City Council went viral on social media. In the video, Emergency Communications Center Assistant Director Leticia Cardenas tells the council that 41% of her 911 staff were out on Minnesota’s new Paid Family and Medical Leave program.
