📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, you put off tasks that feel too substantial, too vague, or likely to go badly, and waiting to feel motivated won’t change that. Beat procrastination by making the start easy. Pick a first step you can finish in five minutes, tell someone your deadline, and aim for a rough version, not a perfect one.

Recent findings suggest that putting those tasks off isn’t laziness, but it still costs you. A bill paid late can bring a late fee, and work handed in late can cost you your boss’s trust.

You might clean the whole kitchen to avoid writing one report. That’s real effort, spent on anything except the task that makes you uneasy.

The fix is to make starting the task easier than avoiding it.

Take a freelancer who is weeks late on her quarterly estimated tax payment, the tax she pays the IRS four times a year because no employer withholds it. The IRS figures its underpayment penalty on how much is unpaid and for how long, so every week she waits adds to it.

She commits to five minutes of gathering last quarter’s invoices. With them in one pile, the rest goes swiftly, and she pays that afternoon.

Choose the task you’ve put off longest, write down its five-minute first step, and start a five-minute timer.
