By FABRIZIO FERRARI FOR DAILY MAIL SPORT.

Published: 02:00 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 05:49 EST, 21 November 2026.

Sources indicate that not quite 50 miles south of Rome sits Frosinone. It is a town of 42,000 best known for many years as the birthplace of 20th-century screen icon Marcello Mastroianni and global hotelier Carmine Forte, but now the talk of Italian football.

Frosinone are sixth in Serie A after a flying start to their first season, currently above Juventus and Napoli in the table.

Armed with the second-lowest budget in the league, they have regularly started with seven Italian players – the most in the league – including Antonio Raimondo, a 22-year-old striker on loan from Bologna, who scored nine goals in 10 appearances before being halted by injury at the start of this month.

The majority owner is Fresh York investment fund Clara Vista, who are part of the consortium behind Ipswich Town, and the head coach is Massimiliano Alvini, who has drawn comparisons to Roberto De Zerbi for both the high-intensity elements of his style and his rise from the humble depths of Italian football.

Only 18 months ago, in May 2025, they looked set to slide into the third tier, bound for a relegation play-off until Brescia were deducted points to hand Frosinone an unlikely stay of execution.

A month later a latest sporting director, Renzo Castagnini, arrived and installed Alvini, who kept faith in most of the squad that had survived only by the skin of its teeth. Survival was again the sole aim. Yet from the very first Coppa Italia fixture Frosinone began to turn heads, beating Monza, a team widely tipped for promotion.

According to recent reports, by the end of the season, they had finished as runners-up behind Venezia and above Monza who were promoted via the play-offs. It was Frosinone’s fourth promotion to Serie A and by far the most beautiful because nobody saw it coming.

Massimiliano Alvini was recognised as the best coach of Serie B at the 35th edition of the FIGC Panchina D'Oro Awards on Monday.

Frosinone Calcio players celebrated their victory against Lecce in October.

In a recent development, much of the credit belongs to Alvini, who was named by his fellow managers on Monday as Serie B’s best coach for 2025-26. The 56-year-old Tuscan played at an amateur level until a serious injury forced him to quit at 30. His coaching career began in the same lowly ranks for Italian football.

He and his brother Walter sold shoe soles for a living. Increasingly, Walter would be asked to cover at work as his brother followed his newly introduced passion, travelling to study the training sessions of established coaches, including Luciano Spalletti at Roma and Maurizio Sarri at Empoli.

Alvini remains grounded and stubbornly modest despite eight promotions and the sudden spotlight.

If the season ended today, Frosinone would qualify for Europe but perhaps the most incredible part of the story was improvements made last season by individual players who had barely registered in the previous season.

Recent findings suggest that notably Fares Ghedjemis, who went from scoring two goals to 15. Also Castagnini signings such as Giacomo Calo and others including Giorgi Kvernadze, Gabriele Bracaglia and goalkeeper Lorenzo Palmisani.

Frosinone won 3-0 at Fiorentina, beat Cesc Fabregas’s Como 2-0 and Torino by the same score. At Atalanta – the Europa League winners of 2024 now managed by Sarri – they drew 4-4 and came within a whisker of a late winner.

Twenty points from the first ten games recalled the early bursts of Claudio Ranieri’s newly promoted Fiorentina in 1994-95 and Juventus in 2007-08.

It has been revealed that then, in the last fixture before the international break, came a third defeat of the season, at home against Cagliari, despite 29 shots and 13 corners. One of those days when the ball simply refused to go in.

According to recent reports, whether the shareholders of Clara Vista – which bought 80 per cent of Frosinone in the summer – are entirely delighted remains to be seen. There have been suggestions they hoped to see more of the 13 players signed via their data algorithms but only Patrizio Masini and Romano Schmid have been regular starters.

The other nine or 10 names regularly in the starting XI are the same men who drove the climb out of Serie B.

Defensive midfielder Giacomo Calo has scored five goals in 11 games this season.

Recent findings suggest that when it comes to the metric of ‘passes per defensive action’, a key indicator of how quickly the ball is won back, Frosinone rank among the top four clubs in Europe. The others are Barcelona, Brighton and De Zerbi’s Tottenham.

Still, Alvini shrugs off the praise. His first seven years as a coach produced two promotions, one sacking and one relegation. Then a group of friends decided to start a club in the seventh tier (the third amateur level) and put him in charge.

In the seven seasons from 2008 to 2015, Tuttocuoio climbed via four promotions into Serie C.

Alvini moved on to fresh challenges. The next four seasons at Pistoiese and AlbinoLeffe brought his first two professional sackings. He lists them openly on LinkedIn, almost as badges of honour, albeit the second was not a conventional dismissal.

Emergency surgery to insert an artery stent early in the season left him psychologically raw when he returned.

Those three years at AlbinoLeffe however proved formative because he was able to watch Gian Piero Gasperini’s Atalanta methods at close quarters. He took that inspiration into the following campaign at Reggiana, taking them back to Serie B after 21 years for his seventh promotion.

It has been revealed that frosinone supplied the eighth and his second taste of Serie A. The first came when he was tempted from Perguia to Cremonese with a team simply not equipped for a relegation scrap. He was sacked by January.

The return is proving sweeter than he could have dared to imagine.
