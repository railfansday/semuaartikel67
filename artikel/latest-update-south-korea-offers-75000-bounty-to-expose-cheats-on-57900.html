South Korea is offering a $75,000 bounty for anyone who exposes people cheating on a national exam, following a viral investigation into a man cheating on his certified tax accountancy exam.

In a recent development, over the years, students have found different ways to cheat on exams so that they can get better grades, ultimately opening the door to bigger opportunities. Well, that is if they don’t get caught in the process.

In mid-November, a 34-year-old man from Seoul, South Korea, sparked a massive investigation after he managed to get 159 correct answers on a test with 160 questions. He later apologized and admitted to using both his phone and generative AI to cheat, noting he wanted to make his parents proud.

Now, in the wake of that, Korea is clamping down on other possible cheating, offering up substantial money for whistleblowers.

President Lee Jae-myung announced that rewards of 100 million won ($75,000) are being offered to help catch cheaters on Korea’s Suneung college entrance exam.

Recent findings suggest that “Cheating on exams using advanced equipment is no different from stealing the scores of hundreds of thousands of examinees who worked hard. To ensure that honest examinees are not harmed, we will mobilize the nation’s full capabilities with the resolve of a wartime effort to eradicate this practice, so that no one even dreams of stealing scores,” the Korean president remarked on X (formerly Twitter).

It has been revealed that “If anyone is considering cheating on the exam, please do not ruin your life by hoping for a lucky break. Give it up for your own sake and everyone else’s.”.
