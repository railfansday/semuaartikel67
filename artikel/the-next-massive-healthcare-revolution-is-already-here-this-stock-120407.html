Industry observers note that blood tests used to happen because a doctor ordered one. Increasingly, people are ordering them for themselves. Function Health sells a membership that includes more than 160 lab tests a year for $365, and Apple is preparing to let iPhone owners order a panel of more than 50 biomarkers from its Health app for $119.

In a recent development, every one of those tests needs a lab, and for many of the biggest names in consumer health, that lab is Quest Diagnostics (DGX +0.10%). I think that makes it one of the better ways to invest in the shift toward proactive health.

According to recent reports, this isn't a someday story. Quest generated nearly $250 million in consumer revenue in 2025, including roughly $100 million from its own questhealth.com site, with the rest coming through partners such as Function Health and the wearables makers Oura and Whoop. Meanwhile, its Apple partnership rolls out later this year. Quest doesn't need to build the next hot wellness brand. It runs the labs and collection network behind several of them, so it gets paid whichever app wins the customer.

Quest's consumer revenue is growing briskly. Management said on its third-quarter call that consumer growth should top 30% this year. Growing 30% from $250 million implies roughly $325 million this year, which is about 2.7% of the $12.1 billion in revenue Quest expects at the midpoint of its guidance. That's small today, which is exactly why the runway matters.

NYSE · As of 11:13 AM ET 11/25/26.

Industry observers note that the consumer business isn't the only growth engine. Third-quarter revenue rose 10% to $3.1 billion, and adjusted earnings per share (EPS) climbed 15.4% to $3.00, driven by the strength of its physician, hospital, and consumer channels and productivity gains from automation and AI. Quest raised its 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to $11.25 to $11.35, a nearly 15% year-over-year increase. That's an acceleration from last year's 10% adjusted EPS growth rate.

In a recent development, the total U.S. and Canadian lab market is right now approaching $90 billion and is growing at a low-single-digit compound annual rate. Quest is growing faster, driven by acquisitions and other expansion initiatives, including new high-growth areas like consumer-initiated testing. It sees more than 20% compound annual growth potential from those new growth areas, much faster than the mid-single-digit growth rates of its legacy businesses.

If consumer-initiated testing became more mainstream, it could be a long-term growth accelerator for Quest and drive strong returns for investors. For example, if Quest delivered 12% annual EPS growth over the next decade, its EPS would reach $35 per share by 2036. Assuming Quest maintains its current valuation multiple of around 23x earnings, its stock would be worth over $800 per share a decade from now, a roughly 200% gain. Add in dividends (Quest yields around 1.3%), and the total return would be even higher.

Recent findings suggest that the bull case is below Quest's current outlook. The diagnostics firm expects to deliver 4% to 5% compound annual revenue growth and 7% to 9% compound annual EPS growth over the long term. If Quest's EPS grows 9% a year and the multiple eases to 20 times as growth matures, the stock reaches about $535 by 2036. That's a respectable but modest 7.5% a year. If growth slows to 6% and the multiple falls to 17 times, the stock reaches about $344, implying just 3% annual growth.

Industry observers note that several things could push Quest toward those outcomes. While consumer-initiated testing is a potentially high-growth area, there's no guarantee it will become more mainstream. Meanwhile, the continued upward pressure on healthcare costs could cause governments and insurers to push back against price increases, pressuring Quest's margins.

Sources indicate that quest doesn't have to invent the next breakthrough longevity platform. It can supply the testing behind many of them. For the bull case to play out, consumer-initiated testing needs to continue growing briskly, driven by strong uptake in partnerships such as its Apple Health panel. The early signs are positive, which makes Quest an exciting stock to hold for the next decade or more.
