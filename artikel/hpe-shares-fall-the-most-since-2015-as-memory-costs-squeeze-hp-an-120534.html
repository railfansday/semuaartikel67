HPE shares fall the most since 2015 as memory costs squeeze HP and HPE

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that HP and Hewlett Packard Enterprise split from Hewlett-Packard in 2015. This week both told investors that memory costs are squeezing their profits. HPE’s shares had their worst day since the split. HP gave a cautious outlook for its fresh fiscal year.

HP’s revenue rose 9.3% to $16.0bn in its fiscal fourth quarter, HP said on Tuesday. AI PCs made up nearly 60% of shipments, but total PC units fell 29%. Adjusted earnings per share fell 10.8% to $0.83.

“In the fourth quarter, we continued to grow our higher-value businesses, with AI PCs reaching nearly 60% of shipments, while taking steps to address memory and storage cost pressures,” said Bruce Broussard, HP’s interim CEO.

In a recent development, for fiscal 2027, HP expects adjusted earnings of $3.15 to $3.45 a share, against $3.33 this year. It also raised its quarterly dividend to $0.31.

“While our fiscal 2027 outlook reflects a prudent view of continued cost and demand pressures, we have a strong track record of addressing challenges,” mentioned Karen Parkhill, HP’s chief financial officer.

PC makers have been dealing with memory costs for months. HP, Asus and Acer were moving production back to China in October to cut them.

HPE’s revenue jumped about 50% to $14.5bn in the quarter, driven by AI servers, it reported on Monday. Its Cloud and AI division grew 68.6% to $11.3bn. It raised its sales outlook for the latest fiscal year to $59.4bn to $63.1bn.

But HPE expects the division’s operating margin to shrink to 14% from 18.4%, Dina Bass reported for Bloomberg. The shares fell 16% to $40.19 on Tuesday. It was their biggest one-day drop since HPE began trading in November 2015. The stock had doubled this year.

“We expect supply of memory, NAND, and many other critical components to remain constrained through at least the second half of calendar 2028,” Antonio Neri, HPE’s chief executive, said on the earnings call.

According to recent reports, neri told Bloomberg Television that the turmoil at Nvidia had not changed customers’ behaviour. Nvidia brought Jensen Huang back as CEO on Monday. Rising component costs have already pushed server makers to raise their prices.
