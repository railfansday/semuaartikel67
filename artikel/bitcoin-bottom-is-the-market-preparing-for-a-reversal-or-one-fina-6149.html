Bitcoin found its bottom? Bitcoin is fighting to hold $65,000 after a brutal weekend selloff pushed BTC USD below $62,000. The rebound offers bulls some relief, but the market is showing signs of a much deeper liquidity problem.

The world’s largest crypto has lost 13% since the 8th of November, with Ethereum and XRP also hitting multi-month lows. Billions in leveraged positions have disappeared as traders rush to reduce exposure.

According to recent reports, yet the selloff has created an interesting setup. Extreme fear, forced liquidations, and heavily discounted prices could signal that much of the speculative excess has finally been removed.

The problem is that capital continues leaving the crypto market. Stablecoin supply has contracted for five consecutive weeks, while US spot Bitcoin ETFs have recorded seven consecutive sessions of outflows.

Bitcoin now faces a defining question: has the market finally found its bottom, or is another crash waiting around the corner?

Recent findings suggest that the latest liquidation wave has given the Bitcoin bottom argument some credibility. When leveraged traders are forced out, selling pressure can eventually exhaust itself, leaving the market with fewer speculative positions vulnerable to another cascade. What comes down must go up, and vice versa.

Industry observers note that the weekend recovery also reveals that buyers are willing to defend the $62,000 region. BTC rebounded to $64,000 – $65,000 after briefly touching $62,006 this morning.

It has been revealed that market sentiment has also deteriorated sharply. Extreme fear is returning as investors abandon riskier positions and traders become increasingly defensive. This is often where market reversals begin. Sellers who wanted to exit have already sold, while longer-term investors start looking for attractive entry points.

Sources indicate that galaxy Research has also presented a bullish argument. In an October 26 report, the firm said it believed Bitcoin’s bottom was already in, pointing to diminished selling pressure, reduced speculative positioning, and stronger long-term fundamentals.

Sources indicate that however, a convincing recovery needs more than a short-lived bounce. Bitcoin must attract fresh spot demand and reclaim important resistance levels before bulls can claim victory.

The biggest obstacle to a Bitcoin bottom is the lack of fresh money entering the market. Stablecoin supply has contracted for five consecutive weeks. Glassnode puts the 30-day decline at $17.1 billion, marking the fastest contraction since June 2022.

Stablecoins provide much of the dollar liquidity used to purchase cryptocurrencies, so when investors redeem stablecoins for traditional dollars, the market loses potential buying power.

It has been revealed that bitcoin ETFs are sending another warning. US spot Bitcoin ETFs have lost $2.35 billion over two weeks, extending a difficult period for institutional demand. Corporate buying has weakened too. Public companies collectively sold 11,647 BTC worth approximately $869 million. Strategy remained a buyer, adding 1,037 BTC, but the wider corporate treasury trend has turned negative.

Macroeconomic conditions are making the situation worse. Hotter-than-expected US inflation and employment figures have pushed interest-rate expectations higher. Futures markets now fully price a 25-basis-point Federal Reserve rate hike in December.

Higher rates strengthen the dollar and make non-yielding assets like Bitcoin less attractive. Meanwhile, the dollar index has climbed to 102.9, adding another headwind for crypto markets.

According to recent reports, coinbase Institutional also warned that Bitcoin could struggle to recover without renewed crypto-native demand. Its analysis places $62,000 among the crucial support levels, with $58,000–$59,000 emerging as another potential downside target.

The technical picture remains fragile. Bitcoin needs to reclaim the $66,000–$68,000 region before bulls can establish a convincing recovery. A sustained move above $73,000–$75,000 would provide a much stronger reversal signal.

Bitcoin has already experienced a substantial correction, but the rebound hasn’t settled the argument. The bullish case rests on exhausted sellers, reduced speculative exposure, extreme fear, and buyers defending the $62,000 region. The bearish case rests on shrinking stablecoin supply, ETF withdrawals, weaker corporate accumulation, and rising borrowing costs.

For now, liquidity remains the deciding factor. A genuine Bitcoin bottom needs more than liquidation-driven relief rallies. It needs fresh capital entering the market, institutional demand returning, and spot buyers absorbing available supply.

Industry observers note that if stablecoin supply stabilizes and ETF flows turn positive, Bitcoin could build a foundation for its next recovery. But if capital continues leaving the ecosystem, the $62,000 floor could give way to another painful correction.

Bitcoin may be approaching its bottom, but the market hasn’t yet shown that buyers are ready to take control.
