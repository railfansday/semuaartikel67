It has been revealed that bitcoin's plunge toward $62,000 on Nov. 15 coincided with a sharp increase in coins concentrated around the selloff low, potentially strengthening a key support zone.

It has been revealed that in its Nov. 17 report, Glassnode estimated that approximately 66,000 BTC entered the $62,000 to $66,000 short-term-holder cost-basis band during the two heaviest days of selling.

The price range, which initially formed around Bitcoin's August low, now accounts for roughly 20% of short-term holders' coins, making it an important level to watch if selling pressure returns.

Exchange trading data suggests buyers have begun absorbing some selling pressure after the crash.

Sources indicate that glassnode reported that sellers dominated trading during the selloff before buyer-initiated volume overtook seller-initiated activity in the 24 hours preceding its Nov. 17 snapshot.

According to recent reports, standing buy orders within 5% of Bitcoin's market price also outweighed sell orders by approximately 2.3 to 1 across Binance and Coinbase. The largest single Binance buy level since August was near $60,000.

According to recent reports, bitcoin fell to about $62,000 on Nov. 15, the lower edge of the cost-basis band. Two days later, Glassnode put Binance’s largest bid level since August near $60,000.

In a recent development, the price band and the open orders measure different things. A retest would show how much selling buyers actually absorb.

The 66,000 BTC figure estimates change in the short-term-holder cost-basis band. The $60,000 marker identifies a Binance order-book level. Orders can be canceled, and the depth ratio measures open orders rather than executed volume.

However, the recovery in buying demand has been uneven.

Sources indicate that bitcoin briefly traded at a premium on Coinbase during the Nov. 15 crash, but that advantage disappeared by Nov. 17. A separate Coinbase Premium Index from Axel Adler Jr. revealed Bitcoin trading at a 0.110% discount on Coinbase relative to Binance that day.

More recent trading activity nevertheless suggests renewed buying interest.

With Bitcoin trading as high as $65,365 in the last 24 hours, Blockmetric data revealed aggressive buying on Binance's spot market exceeded selling by about $5.5 million during the six hours ending at 07:54 UTC.

The platform also recorded $12.7 million in standing Binance buy orders between $62,500 and $62,600, reinforcing the evidence of demand around the lower end of Glassnode's support zone.

The combination of growing holder concentration and stronger exchange buying suggests the $62,000 to $66,000 region could play an important role in Bitcoin's next price move.

If Bitcoin retreats toward $62,000, the critical test will be whether buyers can absorb renewed selling pressure. Sustained buyer-led trading and a recovery in Coinbase's premium would strengthen the case for the support zone holding.
