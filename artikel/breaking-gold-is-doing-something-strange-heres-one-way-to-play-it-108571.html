Breaking: Gold is doing something strange. Here's one way to play it

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (914 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways

  • It has been revealed that over long timeframes, gold's returns tend to move independently of the S&P 500's.
  • According to recent reports, the options market is also behaving unusually.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that gold has stopped trading like a haven and started trading more like a market index this year. The past week has underscored it. Fears around AI, a hawkish Fed, and a stronger dollar sent many investors rushing to raise cash, and gold was sold along with stocks. GLD (SPDR Gold Shares) and the S&P 500 both slid around 9%. Gold then shared in the market's Monday rebound, with GLD up about 3% as the S&P 500 jumped nearly 5%.

It has been revealed that over long timeframes, gold's returns tend to move independently of the S&P 500's. From 2004 to 2025, the correlation between their daily returns has been just 0.05 (+1 means the two move in lockstep and -1 means they move in opposite directions). That relationship fluctuates, and 2026 has seen an unusually strong link. The correlation has been about 0.41 so far in 2026. Its rolling three-month reading peaked near 0.80 in June and now sits at 0.54, still higher than about 95% of rolling three-month readings since 2005. Since the end of August, GLD has fallen on 77% of the S&P 500's down days, compared with roughly half historically.

According to recent reports, the options market is also behaving unusually. Gold options typically price calls richer than puts (call skew). Like other commodities, gold is prone to sharp rallies, and investors use calls to bet on them or hedge against macroeconomic risks. However, gold options have mostly shown the reverse this year, with puts at the moment trading richer than calls (put skew). It's a sign investors are paying a premium for protection against further declines, which is unusual for gold and more typical of stock options.

It has been revealed that those richer puts come as uncertainty around gold runs high heading into December. The PCE inflation report arrives Wednesday, followed by the jobs report next week and the Fed's decision and CPI the week after. GLD's implied volatility, a measure of how much the options market expects the fund to move, is now higher than on about 80% of days over the past year. Options are pricing in an expected move of about 6%, or roughly $20, by Dec. 18. With gold options pricier than usual and puts unusually rich, that presents an interesting setup for traders willing to sell premium.

Industry observers note that based on Monday's close, I'm selling the Dec. 18 310/300 put spread and 360/370 call spread in GLD for a credit of roughly $2.24.

This is a directionally neutral trade. Gold has been swinging with the market and is down about 33% from its January high. With the Fed and inflation data ahead, it's hard to say whether gold has bottomed or has further to fall into mid-December. A neutral position can collect the elevated premium without picking a direction, and the richer put side adds more.

The short strikes sit just outside the range the options market is pricing for Dec. 18, roughly 7% to 8% from Monday's close. That leaves room for a continued selloff or an extended rebound. The trade reaches its maximum profit of $224 if GLD closes between $310 and $360 on Dec. 18. It carries a 62% theoretical probability of profit and can show a gain before expiration if GLD remains relatively rangebound.

Recent findings suggest that the maximum loss is capped by the long options and reaches $776 if GLD closes below $300 or above $370, 10% to 11% from Monday's close. Defining risk is especially crucial with the upcoming catalysts and recent price volatility in gold. GLD has had 30 daily moves of more than 3% this year, including a 10.3% drop on Jan. 30. The trade breaks even at $307.76 on the downside and $362.24 on the upside.

Industry observers note that A close between $360 and $370 can assign the short call and leave a short position in the fund, so investors looking to avoid that should consider managing the trade before expiration. If GLD finishes between $300 and $310, the short put can be assigned, creating an obligation to buy 100 shares per spread at $310. After the credit, that's a cost basis of about $307.76, roughly 8% below Monday's close.
