A fresh view of Amazonian leaf seasonality

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Amazon rainforest plays a key part in the cycling of carbon and water on regional and global scales, and is widely viewed as a perennially evergreen ecosystem. However, writing in Nature, Song et al.1 report that there are extended periods when trees in most of the rainforest remain leafless. Known as deciduousness, this state occurs between the shedding of old leaves and the emergence and early development (flushing) of fresh ones. The authors also found that hydroclimatic conditions such as annual rainfall have a more crucial role in determining this pattern of leaf behaviour than do soil properties and light availability. The seasonal variation in tropical leaf cover and photosynthetic activity in the Amazon observed by the authors is less pronounced than that seen for broadleaf trees in temperate and boreal climate zones.
