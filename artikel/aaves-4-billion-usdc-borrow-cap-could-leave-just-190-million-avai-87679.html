Aave’s $4 billion USDC borrow cap could leave just $190 million available for withdrawals

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Aave raised its USDC borrow cap in the main Ethereum V3 market to $4 billion on Nov. 20, a day after one wallet deposited more than a billion USDC. Lenders had about 1.14 billion USDC available to withdraw late on Nov. 20. If borrowing hits the new cap and deposits stay at 4.19 billion USDC, about 190 million USDC would remain for withdrawals.

Aave's Ethereum Core market had 4.19 billion USDC supplied and 3.05 billion borrowed at 22:11 UTC on Nov. 20, according to an Aave API snapshot published by Aave Tracker. With 72.8% of the reserve lent out, subtracting the loans leaves about 1.14 billion USDC unborrowed. The same figures put borrowers about 950 million USDC below the upcoming cap. That gap measures room under the borrowing limit.

The address, 0x176F…a132, supplied 400 million USDC and 350 million USDC in two transactions about eight minutes apart on Nov. 19. The same wallet received Aave's receipt tokens from both deposits.

Later that day, Aave raised the market's USDC deposit limit from 3.5 billion to 5 billion. LlamaRisk had reported that the old limit was 99.6% used. Four minutes and 24 seconds after the change, the same wallet supplied another 738 million USDC, bringing its deposits to 1,488 million USDC over about 75 minutes.

Coindaro reported that at 17:43 UTC on Nov. 19 the wallet held about 1.495 billion in aEthUSDC, the token that represents its deposits and accrued interest. At that same reading, the market had 4.228 billion USDC supplied and 2.604 billion borrowed, leaving roughly 1.62 billion unborrowed. At Ethereum block 26,459,205, just before 22:11 UTC on Nov. 20, on-chain balances showed the address holding about 1.495 billion aEthUSDC, or 35.7% of the receipt token's supply.

Industry observers note that llamaRisk recommended the higher borrowing limit on Nov. 20 after loans reached 98.5% of the previous $2.7 billion cap. Aave put the change into effect at 11:15 UTC. The amount of cash in the pool changes when users actually borrow, deposit, repay or withdraw.

At the late Nov. 20 supply of 4.19 billion USDC, the pool would have about 690 million left with 3.5 billion borrowed and 190 million left with 4 billion borrowed. The latter would put utilization at about 95.5%, above the 94% threshold where the variable borrowing rate starts to rise more steeply.

According to recent reports, llamaRisk's earlier numbers put the full-cap buffer slightly higher. The firm estimated that $4 billion of debt would use 94.8% of the supply at the time of its recommendation, implying roughly 220 million USDC unborrowed. If deposits eventually reached the $5 billion supply cap, the same $4 billion of debt would leave 1 billion USDC in the pool.

Industry observers note that aave pays withdrawals from the USDC still available in the pool. At the late Nov. 20 reading, the wallet's roughly 1.495 billion USDC position exceeded the pool's estimated 1.14 billion of unborrowed USDC. A hypothetical 500 million USDC withdrawal at that reading, with debt held at 3.05 billion, would leave about 640 million for other suppliers. The same request would exceed the pool's liquidity in the $4 billion debt scenario. As the cash balance falls, so does the share of that wallet's position it could redeem at once.

Industry observers note that transferring aEthUSDC to a newly introduced holder would move the claim on the deposit. Redeeming it would take USDC out of the pool. How much other lenders can withdraw depends on the size of those redemptions and whether repayments or fresh deposits replenish the pool as loans grow.
