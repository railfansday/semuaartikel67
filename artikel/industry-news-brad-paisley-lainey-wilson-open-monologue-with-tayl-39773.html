Grab your bell bottoms and cowboy hats — because Lainey Wilson kicked up some dust and brought the country charm in her 2026 CMA Awards opening monologue with some assistance from none other than legend and bonafide #Swiftie, Brad Paisley.

As Wilson, 34, kicked off the Wednesday, November 18, ceremony at the Bridgestone Arena in Nashville, Paisley, 54, crashed the party to rewrite several of the biggest country hits of all time with updated lyrics.

“This is bigger than me: We have to talk about the biggest story of the year. Somebody got married,” Paisley exclaimed, revealing the bride was a “blonde singer” who married a “football player.” (Taylor Swift, of course, married Travis Kelce in July just months after Wilson tied the know with Devlin “Duck” Hodges.).

“It was such a big deal,” Paisley added before Wilson thanked her fellow singer for mentioning her big day.

“You got married? What arena did you get married in?” Paisley added, referring to Swift’s Madison Square Garden wedding venue. Wilson and Hodges, however, noted “I do” inside a cave.

“I will never forget what my husband remarked to me when we went looking for wedding venues,” Wilson said, setting up another original song. “I beg your pardon/ I never promised Madison Square Garden.”.

The cameras cut to Swift, 36, who laughed alongside BFF Brittany Mahomes, who is married to Kelce’s Kansas City Chiefs teammate Patrick Mahomes.

According to recent reports, “I can’t believe I wasn’t invited to your wedding,” Paisley added. “I did go to Taylor’s. Hi, Taylor! Remember me? I ate all the shrimp.”.

Wednesday’s ceremony is Wilson’s third time emceeing Country’s Biggest Night, made even more special considering her three nominations. Wilson is up for Entertainer of the Year, Single of the Year and Female Vocalist of the Year.

It has been revealed that “I am gathering ideas and I’ll tell you one of my ideas because I told Ella [Langley] this,” she teased on an episode of Country Heat Weekly last month, referring to Langley’s nine nods. “I stated, ‘Girl, I’m going to bring you a bedazzled broom,’ so she can sweep it up. I’m going to be there with a bedazzled dust pan.”.

Jokes aside, Wilson knows that attending — and earning CMA trophies — is a huge honor in itself.

In a recent development, “I think it just stands for representing country as a genre, whatever that looks like,” she added on the podcast. “Each year that I have been nominated for Entertainer of the Year, I feel like it looks like something different for me. I think it just means representing the genre in the best light that you possibly can. And there’s a lot of people doing that.”.

Wilson’s 2023 Entertainer of the Year accolade represented the first time a woman won the category in a decade. (She took home the trophy for the second time during the 2025 awards show.).

In a recent development, “I’ve known since I moved to Nashville in 2011 that there are going to be some barn doors I was going to have to go kick down myself, that nobody was going to do it [for me],” Wilson exclusively told Us in November 2025 of breaking through the industry’s glass ceiling. “It was truly a bitter pill that I swallowed a long time ago. So in the times when I felt like maybe it’s a little bit harder just because I’m a girl, that’s when I dug in deeper and worked harder.”.

She continued at the time, “The only reason I’m even here in this position is because of what the women before me have done — those other women who have continually knocked barn doors down for each other. And we’re gonna have to keep doing it generations on. We have no choice.”.

Since she found success, Wilson has tried to help mentor other rising artists.

“Being a listening ear, sharing the wisdom that was passed along to me from people like Reba [McEntire] and Dolly [Parton], who were more than willing to give me that wisdom from the goodness of their heart,” she recalled. “Everything they say, I bottle it up. I’m like, ‘OK, these women have done something right, and they are where they are because of the things they’ve learned along the way.’ I try to pass that along to other girls and help pull them over that wall, mention their names in interviews.”.
