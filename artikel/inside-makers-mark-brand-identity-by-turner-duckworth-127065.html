Turner Duckworth rebuilt the Maker's Mark brand identity around its red wax, casting Star Hill Farm materials into marks that tell the story.

The bottle does the talking. Turner Duckworth kept the hand-dipped red top exactly where it belongs, at the center of the system, then built a whole language out of it. Wheat stalks, oak rings and native flora from Star Hill Farm were dipped and cast in the same red wax, so the marks that carry the brand are literal pieces of the place. Against a Wheat Cream ground, the red reads as one loud note in a quiet room, with Printmaker's Black holding the type steady.

The type comes from a person, not a library. Co-founder Margie Samuels shaped the original bottle, and the studio recreated her handwriting as a custom typeface with Nick Cooke at G-Type. Hundreds of characters, contextual alternates and ligatures keep it feeling handwritten rather than set, and her voice runs through the labels, menus and posters in a flowing, wax-like hand.

It has been revealed that layouts stay plain on purpose. A letterhead puts the Samuels family's SIV mark at the center and the distillery address in the corner, so place sits at the heart of every sheet. Type stays large and calm, with room around it, and the wordmark does its work without decoration.

The palette is the bottle, remembered in three tones. Wheat Cream is the ground, true to the label. Printmaker's Black carries the type. Wax Red does the rest, in finish and in form: powder-coated metal, glazing and varnish applied to objects with smooth, flowing shapes. The same red runs across mugs, folders, books and table tents, so every object in the world feels dipped from the same pot.

According to recent reports, scott Grummett photographed the world at Star Hill Farm in warm cream tones and natural light, with touches of glossy wax red. The pictures tell the slower story behind the liquid: hand-rotated barrels, hand-cut labels, hand-dipped bottles, and the land that gives it flavour.
