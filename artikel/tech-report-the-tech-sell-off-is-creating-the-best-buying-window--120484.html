Recent findings suggest that if you've checked your portfolio lately, I'm sorry. I looked at mine, too.

Industry observers note that the Nasdaq had its worst day since April 2025 on Nov. 5, and things got worse after Nvidia (NVDA -8.80%) stated on Nov. 12 that Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO. Not even Nvidia's better-than-expected earnings could stop the slide. By Nov. 20, all three major averages were in correction, the Nasdaq was negative for 2026, and the Nasdaq and Dow had just logged their worst week in more than six years.

Recent findings suggest that then the scenery changed. Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Huang, who remarked, "I can't wait to get back to work." Nvidia shares jumped 31% for their best day since 2003, and the Nasdaq posted its best day since April 2025. The relief faded fast, as Nvidia gave back about 7% in the next session and pulled other chip stocks down with it.

Sources indicate that huang's return answered one question, not the bigger one: Is AI spending slowing? Democratic midterm wins and signs that big tech may rein in spending have raised doubts about the AI build-out, and traders have been bracing for the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates in December. Nvidia's board also says its investigation will proceed to conclusion.

Sources indicate that I can't call the bottom, because market timing rarely works. But in the latest sell-off, the market sold the great businesses right along with the shaky ones. Here are the two I'm buying.

According to recent reports, ASML (ASML -7.13%) is the only company in the world that makes extreme ultraviolet lithography machines, the tools Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing uses to print the most advanced chips. That's about as wide an economic moat as you'll find in tech.

ASML got whipsawed with the rest of the chip group, once dropping 5.8% in a single session as chip stocks sold off across Asia and Europe. Even after the bounce, shares closed Nov. 23 about 31% below their 52-week high.

The selling assumes chipmakers will pull back. ASML's customers say otherwise. "Our customers continue to increase their short- and medium-term equipment demand forecasts with us," CEO Christophe Fouquet said last month, adding that "some customers are expressing an interest in placing orders with us for 2029 and even 2030." He added, "our supply as presently planned for both 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet the demand of our customers.".

Recent findings suggest that china is the risk I'm watching. In mid-November, the Commerce Department moved to close a loophole that let Chinese firms rent Nvidia computing power abroad, and ASML still expects China to be about 20% of sales.

NASDAQ · As of 11:16 AM ET 11/25/26.

Shopify (SHOP -5.72%) had nothing to do with Nvidia's drama, yet it fell 4% in a single session during the sell-off. Even after the rebound, it closed Nov. 23 about 26% below its 52-week high. With a P/E ratio near 80, it's exactly the kind of stock that gets hit when rate-hike fears grow.

The business looks nothing like the stock. Revenue grew 32% last quarter, and CFO Jeff Hoffmeister remarked, "We extended our track record of consistent execution, with 32% revenue growth and a 22% free cash flow margin." Its guidance calls for revenue growth in the "low thirties" this quarter, and President Harley Finkelstein said, "I do think this is the first holiday season where AI-assisted shopping capabilities will become mainstream.".

NASDAQ · As of 11:16 AM ET 11/25/26.

Recent findings suggest that recent shopper news has been encouraging. Walmart (WMT -0.22%) raised its full-year outlook. Target (TGT -2.23%) posted its best sales growth in more than five years. The test comes soon: Shopify usually reports its merchants' Black Friday-Cyber Monday sales the Tuesday after Cyber Monday. A weak number could push the stock lower. Still, as Finkelstein put it after Shopify's earnings-day drop, "we're going to keep building for the long term and ignore the noise day to day.".

In a recent development, that's my initiative, too. In recent weeks, the market panicked over one set of headlines and cheered another. ASML and Shopify kept growing through both, and I'll take their shares at a discount.
