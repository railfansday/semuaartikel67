“From trusted national figure to registered sex offender,” Huw Edwards is to be interviewed and profiled in a Channel 4 documentary next week.

Huw Edwards – Guilty will see the disgraced newsreader face questions publicly for the first time on TV about his criminal actions and astonishing fall from grace.

Airing Tuesday at 9 p.m. GMT (1 p.m. PT), the documentary follows the ex-BBC News anchor at his home in rural Wales as he navigates his existence in the wake of grooming allegations and his conviction of making indecent images of children. A short trailer sees Edwards say “I’d lost the plot” after being questioned over why he did what he did. The interviewer can then be heard saying exasperatedly, “Oh, come on.” Watch on Deadline.

Channel 4 described the doc as “an uncomfortable portrayal of what happens when a previously beloved public figure betrays the trust of the people and asks what accountability really means in the wake of a serious criminal conviction?”.

Sources indicate that we understand Edwards was not paid to take part in the documentary and had no editorial control. He has addressed his actions on his Substack but this will be the first time he speaks about them on TV with an interviewer.

It has been revealed that edwards rose to become one of the UK’s best-known news anchors before workplace misconduct allegations and a child pornography conviction ruined his reputation. In 2024, he pled guilty to making indecent images of children and was given a suspended sentence.

A Martin Clunes-starring 5 drama about him, Power: The Downfall of Huw Edwards, is currently being probed by regulator Ofcom over “fairness and privacy.”.

In a recent development, sacha Mirzoeff, Senior Commissioning Editor at Channel 4, mentioned: “Channel 4 has an established reputation for tackling controversial subjects through exemplary filmmaking. Huw Edwards – Guilty is no exception in its quest for answers from a man who is trying to come to terms with his abuse of power.”.

Director Patrick Forbes added: “This was an important film to make. It’s rare to find a subject that touches on so many significant contemporary challenges – whether it’s celebrity culture or the power of the media – as well as enduring questions about crime and punishment.”.

Huw Edwards – Guilty is commissioned by Mirzoeff and produced by Oxford Films. The producer is Stephanie Collins. The executive producers are Vanessa Tuson and Nick Kent.
