AC Milan are five points behind the somewhat surprise league-leaders Lazio, but belief is growing regarding a Scudetto push.

As La Gazzetta dello Sport write, Milan re-emerged from the two-week international break and put in arguably their best performance of the season against Frosinone. They scored five goals and it could have been more, racking up a third league win in a row to move back into the top four.

The table remains quite tight at the top, with Inter the only team to slip up over the weekend, and the Rossoneri are firmly in the running. The paper highlights give reasons that a title fight is very possible.

Sources indicate that gerry Cardinale and Ruben Amorim are inextricably linked, and that might seem obvious, given that one is the owner and the other the coach. In reality, the bond is deeper than usual, for one simple reason.

Recent findings suggest that amorim has been handed the keys to create the ‘new Milan’, one that Cardinale has decided to manage completely differently since the end of May. The Portuguese is the coach to whom RedBird have given the longest contract so far, and Cardinale believes he’s the right man at the right time.

According to recent reports, he’s demonstrating this concretely not only with his constant presence and his reassuring words, but also with his intention to improve the squad quickly and with the arrival of a key management figure like Markus Krösche. Now, Amorim will receive even greater support.

Gonçalo Inacio, Nicolo Tresoldi, Endrick. These are just some of the names circulating – and these aren’t rumours, but actual targets – ahead of a potentially crucial January transfer window.

The question is obvious: with a couple of top-notch signings for the second half of the season, how far could this team go? Staying up there for starters remains the minimum objective, with Champions League football a must.

Industry observers note that then, Amorim and co. hope that the rest will follow, if their rivals begin to lose pace. The coach is starting to make his team look like they are clicking, but Cardinale has realised the need to accelerate to fill the remaining gaps in the squad.

Recent findings suggest that amorim (fortunately) rapidly realised that the average tactical level of his colleagues in Italy is too high to allow himself to present his Milan team in the same way every single way. So, the 3-4-2-1 that was previously seemingly set in stone has become plasticine in his hands.

Industry observers note that the manager moulds and shapes the playing system based on the opponents, rather than his own needs. Since Ruben the fundamentalist became Ruben the quick-change artist, we’ve seen a bit of everything: 4-2-3-1, 4-3-3, 4-4-1-1, 3-5-2.

The former Sporting CP coach coach locks himself in the lab throughout the week and tailors the formation he deems best. Sometimes he gets it wrong, but most of the time he gets it right.

Milan have won five of their last six league games and, game by game, they are increasingly able to camouflage themselves effectively tactically. Opponents never know what formation they will face, while Amorim has several players capable of playing well in multiple roles, which helps.

The data is remarkable and reads as follows: only Rocco (29) and Czeizler (31) coached Milan sides that have scored more goals than Amorim’s (28) in their first 12 Serie A games in charge.

In short, despite all the problems involved (Goncalo Ramos in particular, more on him to come) the coach’s attacking vocation is clearly visible. Furthermore, it is a Milan with a high conversion rate in proportion to the shots on target.

It is also a Diavolo side that have somehow found a way to make up for Ramos’ poor contribution in terms of goals. Alphadjo Cisse, Diego Moreira, Christian Pulisic and Adrien Rabiot take turns to set each other up and score, a sign of a growing offensive physiognomy.

Industry observers note that more often than not, a striker simply needs to get a goal in order to build confidence and then begin a virtuous cycle. Milan hope that this is the case for Ramos, who ended his month-long goal drought on Sunday.

However, the reflection goes further: while the Rossoneri having the luxury of being able to score without goals from their No.9 (who is currently on three in the league), surely things can only get better from here?

In short, if the Portuguese international actually does start firing more regularly, then Milan might unlock another dimension to their attack that could really see them surge towards that elusive Scudetto.
