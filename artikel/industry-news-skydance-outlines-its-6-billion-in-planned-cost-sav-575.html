Industry News: Skydance outlines its $6 billion in planned cost savings. Here's the breakdown

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Skydance is pulling back the curtain on how it aims to achieve at least $6 billion in cost savings in the next three years.

"By combining two global streaming services, two film and TV studio groups, two television portfolios, and overlapping corporate functions, we can reduce duplicative spend across an expense base of more than $50 billion," the tech giant wrote in a letter to shareholders Monday as part of Paramount Skydance's third-quarter earnings release.

According to recent reports, skydance, the organization formed by the combination of Paramount Skydance and Warner Bros. Discovery, said it projects at least $2.2 billion in cost reductions from eliminating "redundant back-office and corporate functions," including eliminating management layers and ramping the use of artificial intelligence. Those savings also account for "rationalizing" its office footprint.

In a recent development, skydance expects at least $1 billion in savings from streamlining marketing and promotional spending. And around $1.4 billion in savings will come from unifying its streaming businesses and broadcast architecture and integrating back-end technology.

A final $1.4 billion in savings will be tied to "optimizing third-party spend," eliminating overlapping expenses, and consolidating purchasing decisions to one centralized group, Skydance noted.

According to recent reports, "We are confident in achieving these savings, drawing on our playbook from Paramount," the organization wrote. "That program is on track to achieve over $2.7 billion in run-rate efficiencies by the end of 2026.".

Some of that cost-cutting has already begun.

In a recent development, last month, Skydance began offering voluntary buyouts to employees ahead of official layoffs. At the same time, the manufacturer adopted a five-day-per-week in-office policy. Additionally, last week saw the departure of eight executives — four from Warner Bros. and four from Paramount.

The organization has previously said it plans to eventually merge the Paramount+ and HBO Max streaming services into a single platform.

On Monday, Skydance noted it would take the first step toward that goal early next year, offering a bundle of the streaming services "at an even greater value.".

Co-CEO Ynon Kreiz told investors during the firm's earnings call that Skydance is also looking for ways to optimize its content budget. The company spends more than $30 billion annually on shows and films.

"As one tech giant, we can make smarter choices about how we distribute and monetize both new content and library content," Kreiz mentioned.

Sources indicate that skydance noted that it's expecting a $200 million headwind in the fourth quarter from theatrical underperformances at Warner Bros. studio related to already released titles and upcoming releases.

The tech giant will also utilize AI to speed up production and decrease costs to make films, co-CEO and chairman David Ellison said on the call.

"We think these tools will help unlock more original storytelling by being able to make the budget more digestible to be able to take that amount of risk," Ellison said.

The updates came alongside third-quarter results for the legacy Paramount Skydance tech giant that included revenue of $7.03 billion, which was roughly in line with StreetAccount estimates of $7.04 billion, and adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization of $1 billion, topping StreetAccount estimates of $913.5 million.

However, adjusted earnings per share of 45 cents were not immediately comparable to StreetAccount EPS estimates.

Sources indicate that the results don't include the impact of the Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition, which closed after the end of the third quarter.

Skydance detailed business units of the combined entity, including studios, direct-to-consumer and TV, and noted it will provide full-year guidance during its fourth-quarter earnings report early next year.

"Going forward, we will provide guidance on an annual basis, as we prioritize long-term value creation, which we believe will be more consistent with our strategic approach," the tech giant told shareholders. "We will continue to provide quarterly updates, including certain forward-looking commentary where applicable, which will give investors visibility into our near-term progress of the business.".
