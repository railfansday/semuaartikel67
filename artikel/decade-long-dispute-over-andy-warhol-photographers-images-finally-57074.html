A trial over the long-disputed estate of Andy Warhol’s photographer Billy Name, including the rights to his extensive photo archive, finally begins this week.

Name became Warhol’s close collaborator and photographer in the 1960s. He lived in Warhol’s Fresh York studio, the Factory, for seven years and became the artist’s in-house photographer. Using a Pentax Camera, Name captured thousands of iconic photos of Warhol, his art, and the stars who visited the Factory.

His Factory-era photographs are regarded as some of the most important visual records of Warhol and the Pop Art movement.

However, when Name — who was born William George Linich — died in 2016, a legal dispute broke out over his estate, which included the rights to his valuable collection of photographs. More than a decade after Name’s death, the rights and fate of thousands of his photographs still remain legally contested.

Sources indicate that according to a report by Times Union, a latest jury trial at Ulster County Surrogate’s Court in Ulster County, New York, will finally decide who has the right to license the black-and-white photographs Name took of Warhol and who will control the future of his extensive photo archive. The case will be the first jury trial over an estate that Ulster County Surrogate’s Court has ever presided over.

It has been revealed that the dispute is between Name’s longtime friend and agent Dagon James and his niece Suzette Linich both of whom claim to be the photographer’s rightful heir. At the center of the legal battle are two different wills left by Name. Linich presented a will dated 2011 that named her as the executor and sole beneficiary of his estate. Although the court accepted the document, James subsequently presented a more recent will, signed in 2015, that left the entire estate to him.

It has been revealed that A key question in the trial is whether Name had the mental capacity to make the 2015 will. By 2014, the photographer, who is in his early 70s, was reportedly suffering from arthritis, diabetes, and depression and was frequently hospitalized. Name’s family argues that he was often confused and vulnerable to influence, alleging that James persuaded the photographer to exclude his relatives from his will.

Industry observers note that james disputes those claims, maintaining that Name was mentally competent, trusted him, and made a deliberate decision to leave him in charge of his photographic legacy. The attorney who prepared the 2015 will testified that Name had clearly expressed his intention to exclude his relatives from his estate and leave everything to James.
