📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that hundreds of millions of people around the world are affected by diabetes mellitus1. It develops when the activity of insulin, a glucose-lowering hormone secreted by pancreatic β-cells, becomes insufficient to maintain healthy glucose levels in the blood. The condition can be managed with oral medication or by injecting insulin and other metabolic hormones, but the constant need for people with diabetes to manage their lifestyle and blood-glucose levels can impair their quality of life. Therapeutic approaches that monitor and control blood glucose automatically are therefore highly desirable. Writing in Nature, Xiu et al.2 report proof of principle of a bioelectronic device called NeuroAxis that does exactly that in rat models of diabetes.
