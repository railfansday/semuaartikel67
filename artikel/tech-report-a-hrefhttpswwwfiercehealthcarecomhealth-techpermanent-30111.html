The Permanente Medical Group revealed on Monday the launch of a physician-led innovation company it mentioned will strike technology partnerships to co-develop, incubate and launch upcoming enterprise healthcare delivery technologies.

Permanentech—a wholly owned subsidiary of the 10,000-physician medical group, which is itself part of the massive integrated nonprofit Kaiser Permanente—looks to rapidly advance potential new healthcare offerings informed by its frontline physicians and the broader organization’s focus on value-based care.

It marked the launch by announcing its first portfolio company: Wrapt Health, which offers an automated tool that predicts likely missed medical appointments for early outreach and rescheduling. In line with Permanentech’s broader thesis, it was born from a TPMG physician’s experiences, validated within the medical group and so far scaled to 22 sites in Kaiser Permanente’s Northern California region.

“Our clinicians are continuously innovating and refining how we deliver care,” Kenneth Trauner, M.D., CEO of Permanentech and chief innovation officer at TPMG, said in a release. “Permanentech gives them a dedicated resource to rapidly turn their insights and creativity into scalable solutions that benefit both our patients and the wider health care community.”.

Permanentech will be focused on technologies and AI-enabled tools addressing patient service, access and quality, TPMG mentioned, with successful products also having the potential to improve “the economics of care delivery.” The business is structured “to move at the pace of the technology industry,” but has “a clear advantage” via its access to TPMG and Kaiser Permanente’s substantial footprint for deployment and validation, it said.

Those efforts will involve both home-grown tools and those developed by established or early-stage partners.

According to recent reports, the former will champion physician-developed tools created inside TPMG’s ecosystem as an independent company rolls out. Starting from the concept phase, that support would include financing as well as clinical and operational validation, as was the case with Wrapt Health.

“Our physicians and care teams see every day where technology can make care better,” Kristine Lee, M.D., an associate executive director and chief transformation officer at TPMG, stated. “Permanentech gives them a real hand in building tools that lift administrative burden and give time back to patients. When clinicians help build the tools, those tools fit the way they actually work.”.

Strategic partnerships and joint ventures, meanwhile, will focus on co-development, clinical valuation and scaled deployment. Permanentech noted the latter will begin with local deployments that are expanded to other TPMG sites when solutions “prove their value,” and could include additional strategic support as a partner tech giant looks to advance their offering beyond TPMG.

Permanentech aims to add more portfolio companies and partnerships “in the coming months,” according to its announcement.

According to recent reports, “For decades, our physicians have delivered high-quality, preventive care inside an integrated system where clinical expertise, data and incentives all point toward keeping people healthy,” Maria Ansari, M.D., CEO and executive director of TPMG, said. “Permanentech is our commitment to putting that ecosystem to work alongside the best technology from inside and outside our walls.”.

Pleasanton, California-based TPMG is the country’s largest medical group, caring for 4.7 million Northern California members with about 10,000 physicians and nearly 47,000 nurses and support staff. It operates at 203 medical offices and facilities, as well as 21 hospitals.

Its parent organization, Kaiser Permanente, is the country’s largest nonprofit health system by operating revenue ($127.7 billion in 2025). Topline financial results shared earlier this month outlined a 9% year-over-year increase in Q3 operating revenue, while the system’s Risant Health value-based care platform subsidiary in October announced intends to acquire Integris Health, Oklahoma’s largest nonprofit provider.
