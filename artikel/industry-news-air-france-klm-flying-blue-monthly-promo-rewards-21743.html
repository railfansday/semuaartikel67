If you have 40,000 transferable points sitting in a Chase or Amex account, this is the difference between a $400 economy ticket and a $3,000 business class experience — for the same points. The pricing inefficiency exists because Flying Blue uses these promotional awards as a marketing tool to fill premium cabin seats on specific dates that might otherwise go out with empty seats. You, as someone who's built a transferable points balance, are perfectly positioned to take advantage of the moment they make that trade-off. This isn't a loophole that's about to close. Flying Blue has run Promo Rewards consistently for years. It's a **structural feature of the program** — one the ChooseFI community can build an annual travel strategy around. The challenge isn't finding the deal. It's being ready when it drops.

The mechanism behind Flying Blue Promo Rewards is elegantly simple once you understand it, and it separates this program from virtually every other airline loyalty play in your toolkit.

Flying Blue Has a Dynamic Award Chart — With a Monthly Override.

Flying Blue moved to dynamic pricing, which typically means the program is less predictable and often less valuable than the old fixed-chart days. But here's where it gets interesting: Promo Rewards operate as a fixed-price overlay on top of that dynamic system. On the 1st of each month (or within the first few days), Flying Blue publishes a curated list of routes — often 20 to 40 city pairs — with fixed, deeply discounted mile prices. Business class seats on transatlantic routes regularly appear at 40,000 to 50,000 miles one-way. These are not dynamic prices that will creep up as demand increases. They're locked promotional rates on specific routes.

This means the program essentially gives you two separate games to play: the chaotic dynamic pricing game (usually skip it), and the monthly Promo Rewards game (always pay attention).

The Fuel Surcharge Nuance You Must Know.

Flying Blue does pass on carrier-imposed surcharges for standard awards on Air France and KLM flights — this can add $200 to $500 to what should be a points redemption. But here's the critical detail: Promo Reward bookings eliminate those surcharges. You pay the miles, a small booking fee, and that's it. This is a significant hidden value that casual points people miss entirely. The published cash price comparison becomes even more favorable once you factor in that you're not paying the surcharge on top.
