📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Airline ticket prices have surged this year, especially for travel over the holidays. Don't expect many bargains if you're booking flights with miles, either.

Tickets bought with frequent flyer miles for flights between large U.S. airports over the Thanksgiving travel period will cost 32% more than last year's holiday, according to data from Seats.aero, a miles and points redemption search site. While fares have skyrocketed this year, airline executives last month remarked demand remains strong.

Industry observers note that airlines have largely gotten rid of static mileage redemption charts in favor of dynamic pricing, making budgeting for an award ticket more difficult. Dynamic pricing means redemption costs change depending on demand, routes and other factors.

Many of the most expensive routes this Thanksgiving are understandably longer, including from Hawaii, but the list also includes a nonstop from New York to Houston.

It has been revealed that jet fuel prices have surged since the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran on Feb. 28, which has eaten into airline profits. Carriers have sought to pass costs along to consumers as much as possible.

Even some of the cheapest routes for award tickets are more expensive than last year, including for short routes like Tulsa, Oklahoma, to Dallas Fort Worth International Airport.
