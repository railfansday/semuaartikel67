Guide: United Airlines Has Starlink On More Than Half Its Flights, But These Aircraft Offer The Best Odds

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

United Airlines has reached an impressive milestone: Starlink is now available on more than half of its daily flights. I increasingly choose United when I know Starlink will be onboard, but customers generally cannot determine that when booking. Here is what United should change and which aircraft right now offer your best chance of getting Starlink.

According to recent reports, united announced last week that Starlink had crossed the 50% threshold across its daily schedule. The airline expects that number to approach 64% by the end of 2026 and looks to complete fleetwide installations by the end of 2027.

That is an extraordinarily fast rollout for an airline with more than 1,000 mainline aircraft plus a large regional fleet.

It has been revealed that the announcement was packaged around Thanksgiving travel, with United expecting to carry more than 6.6 million passengers between November 19 and December 1. But the more consequential development is that Starlink is no longer a novelty you might encounter on a regional jet. Instead, it is becoming a defining part of the United experience.

I have used just about every major inflight internet system, and Starlink is clearly the best.

The connection is fast, latency is low, and it works well enough that I can complete almost everything onboard that I would ordinarily do from my office. Large files load quickly, video calls are possible (but thankfully not allowed on United), and the service supports multiple devices. It is also free for MileagePlus members.

That changes how I value a flight.

If two departures are similarly priced and one has Starlink, I will choose the Starlink flight. I may even accept a slightly less convenient departure time because several productive hours in the air are worth far more to me than leaving an hour earlier.

It has been revealed that that is precisely the sort of customer behavior United wants. Scott Kirby has repeatedly suggested that Starlink will persuade customers loyal to other airlines to try United, after which the rest of the experience will convince them to stay.

I understand his confidence. Once you experience internet that works almost like it does on the ground, returning to a slow or unreliable connection is painful.

Here is the problem: United generally does not confirm Starlink availability until three days before departure.

The airline offers a useful Starlink tracker, but it is designed to check an upcoming flight rather than help someone choose among flights several weeks or months in advance. By the time the tracker confirms the aircraft, most passengers have already purchased their tickets.

Aircraft substitutions will always occur, and no airline can guarantee that a particular tail number will operate a flight. But that does not prevent United from displaying the expected connectivity during booking.

Recent findings suggest that united already identifies many onboard features in the reservation process. It could add a Starlink indicator based on the scheduled aircraft, accompanied by a clear disclaimer that equipment may change. Even an imperfect forecast would be useful.

It has been revealed that united carefully notes Starlink is available on more than half of its daily flights, which is not the same as saying more than half of its aircraft or passenger seats have Starlink. Regional jets and narrowbody aircraft generally operate more flights per day than widebody aircraft, allowing each installation to influence a larger number of departures.

According to recent reports, united’s entire two-cabin regional fleet now has Starlink. If you want the best chance of getting it, book a flight operated by a Bombardier CRJ-550 or Embraer E175.

If you are traveling on a mainline aircraft, the Airbus A321neo is most likely to have Starlink, with 88% of that fleet equipped. The Boeing 737-800 follows at 77%, while 67% of the Boeing 737-900 fleet has it.

Avoid the Airbus A319, Airbus A320, and Boeing 757-300 if connectivity is vital to you. None right now have Starlink, and the aging Panasonic systems installed on some of those aircraft are most likely not to work at all.

Personally, I avoid 757-300s and go out of my way to fly on A321neos. You should too if onboard connectivity is crucial to you.

Progress on the widebody fleet remains slow. Only 2% of United’s Boeing 787 fleet and 5% of its Boeing 777 fleet at the moment have Starlink, while none of its Boeing 767s are equipped.

Delta Air Lines has selected Amazon Leo for its next-generation internet, with installations scheduled to begin in 2028. As I discussed yesterday, a rocket failure that destroyed 40 Amazon satellites illustrates the risk of selecting a network that is still being constructed.

Recent findings suggest that delta already offers free Wi-Fi on most of its fleet, and I have found it perfectly usable on many flights. This is not a choice between internet and no internet. But in terms of functionality, Starlink is better. It is not even a contest.

It has been revealed that united may complete its fleetwide rollout before Delta installs Amazon Leo on its first aircraft. That gives United time to turn a technical advantage into a behavioral one: customers try Starlink, discover they can be more productive in the air, and begin considering connectivity when selecting future flights.

It has been revealed that that is why displaying Starlink during booking would be valuable. If the product is strong enough to influence purchasing decisions, United should make it easier for customers to find before purchasing.

Industry observers note that it would also help reduce disappointments. I was recently on a Boeing 777-200 with old-school Panasonic Wi-Fi, and a business class passenger across the aisle from me was fuming that there was no Starlink. Better to underpromise and overdeliver than the other way around.

Starlink is now available on more than half of United Airlines flights and should reach nearly 64% by the end of the year. That is impressive progress and gives United a genuine advantage over its domestic competitors.

It has been revealed that I increasingly choose flights based on whether Starlink will be onboard. The frustration is that United generally cannot confirm it until three days before departure, long after the booking decision has been made.

A Starlink indicator during booking would not need to be a guarantee. It could simply tell customers what United reasonably expects to offer. In the meantime, regional jets, Airbus A321neos, and Boeing 737-800s give you the best shot at Starlink on United.
