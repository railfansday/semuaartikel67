Sources indicate that it's been yet another year of price hikes in the world of movie and TV streaming. Lots of our favorite streamers raised subscription costs in 2026, including Peacock, Apple TV, Disney+, Hulu, Netflix, ESPN, and Paramount+. Viewers everywhere are looking for the chance to save some money. Thankfully, a solution has arrived with this year's selection of Black Friday streaming deals.

Most of the massive streamers have dropped Black Friday offers, but we expect even more streaming deals to roll in during Cyber Week, which is the week after Black Friday. I'll be keeping a watchful eye out and will revision this list as any other noteworthy streaming deals pop up. In the meantime, here are all the best Black Friday streaming deals live. Most of these deals expire on Nov. 30 (Cyber Monday), unless otherwise noted.

The HBO Max Black Friday deal is top dog once again in the streaming world. This year, you can secure an entire year of streaming for half the usual cost. That drops it to just $5.49 per month, or $65.88 total for the year. Better yet, the offer is available as a standalone deal, a Prime Video add-on, or a Roku subscription.

In a recent development, yes, you'll have to watch with ads, but for the price, it's easy to look past that. With a Basic subscription, you also won't get access to live sports. Everything else is yours to enjoy — including recent box office gems like Backrooms, Wuthering Heights, and The Drama, as well as HBO Max's many excellent original series like The Pitt, The Comeback, A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms, and Industry.

In a recent development, this year's Paramount Plus Black Friday deal is exactly the same as last year's. That's not necessarily a bad thing, though, as it's one of the only streamers to offer a deal on its ad-free tier. You can get two months of either Paramount+ Essential or Premium for just $2.99 per month (available as both a standalone deal and an add-on). In other words, there's really no reason to not go with Paramount+ Premium. You save almost 80% compared to just 67% by going with Premium, and you get to enjoy your content ad-free. Not to mention, with a Premium subscription, you'll also unlock access to Showtime in its entirety, as well as your local CBS channel live. It's a no-brainer.

It has been revealed that apple TV's outstanding library is well worth the subscription, even if you cancel after the six-month promotional period. It's stacked with Emmy-winning TV series like Widow's Bay, Pluribus, and Severance, as well as Oscar-winning and nominated films, including Coda, F1: The Movie, and Killers of the Flower Moon.

Disney has shockingly revived the exact same Black Friday bundle deals for Disney and Hulu from last year, despite raising prices across the board earlier this fall. If you're looking for the most bang for your buck and prefer to watch without ads, you can get an entire year of both Disney+ and Hulu Premium for only $14.99 per month. Considering an ad-free standalone Disney+ Premium plan typically costs $21.49 per month, the typical bundle price of $21.99 on a normal day is already an excellent deal. But this knocks an extra $7 off, getting you both streamers for less than the cost of one.

You can also opt for the ad-supported initiative and get both Disney+ and Hulu Basic for only $4.99 per month for a year, which is an even steeper discount.

A Disney+ and Hulu bundle subscription is your ticket to the Super Bowl this season, as well as select live ESPN College Football games, the entire Skywalker Saga and most of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, fresh releases like Toy Story 5, Hulu and Disney+ Originals, hit ABC TV highlights, and so much more. Plus, you'll lock in a full year at a fixed cost, bypassing any potential increases once the upcoming merger goes live.

Recent findings suggest that normally I wouldn't recommend Peacock's Select tier, as it's extremely limited in scope and not worth it for the usual cost. However, with this Peacock Black Friday deal, it's definitely worth a second look. At $29.99 for one year, which breaks down to about $2.50 per month, you'll get access to all of your TV favorites from NBC, Bravo, and more. It doesn't include Peacock Originals, hit movies, or live sports — in order to unlock access to those, you'll need at least the Premium tier. Fortunately, that tier is also included in the Peacock Black Friday offer. You can get Premium for $89.99 for one year, which saves you $40.

I'm going to be completely upfront about this: most streaming deals aren't going to change your finances in any significant way. The truth is that Black Friday streaming deals have gotten steadily less impressive over the past few years. It's also worth noting that most deals apply only to the ad-supported tiers (with a few exceptions). Still, any and every discount is welcome in a time when prices keep rising. Every cent counts, and it's easy to lose track of just how much you spend across various streaming services.

Most streamers have announced their Black Friday deals at this point, but you may be wondering if there's more to come. Aside from some stragglers later this week, I would say the best streaming deals have already arrived.

Sources indicate that I'm shocked that Peacock's Black Friday deal is as good as it is for 2026. Last year, the streamer didn't offer a deal at all, so this is light-years better than I was expecting. If you've been holding off on subscribing, now is likely the only time you'll get a decent discount. The Paramount+, HBO Max, and Disney+/Hulu Black Friday offers are pretty much on par with their deals of years past. I would lock in HBO Max and the Disney+/Hulu bundle for a full year, as more price increases could pop up in 2027. Paramount+ tends to offer discounts throughout the year more than any other streamer, so if you miss out on this one, it's no biggie.

Recent findings suggest that it's not worth holding on for a Netflix Black Friday deal, unfortunately. Netflix has never offered a Black Friday discount, or any kind of discount for that matter. Netflix is the top name in streaming and obviously feels that it doesn't need to offer Black Friday deals. I don't expect this year to be any different, but I'm always willing to be proven wrong. It would be a nice and welcome surprise.
