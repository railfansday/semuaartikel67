Jensen Huang Is Back, and So Is Nvidia’s Stock

🌐 Source: WIRED • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (497 words, 📖 Standard)

  • Huang departed the organization he helped found on November 12 amid suspected asset-kissing and allegations of extramarital affairs with employees.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Jensen Huang is back, baby, and Wall Street is thrilled.

It has been revealed that the chipmaker’s shares have surged more than 30 percent following the board’s announcement that it was reinstating Huang as president, CEO, and board member. The manufacturer’s valuation has soared by more than $1 trillion, which puts it on track to break Microsoft’s record for the greatest single-day gain in market value by hundreds of billions of dollars.

Huang departed the organization he helped found on November 12 amid suspected asset-kissing and allegations of extramarital affairs with employees. Each report chipped away at the company’s value until nearly $2.2 trillion was erased over the past month.

Last week, board chair Harvey Jones stated during the tech giant’s earnings call that the board did “not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” WIRED also reported that Nvidia’s board had been alarmed by Huang’s “lack of candor” and “lying.”.

Sources indicate that and yet, despite all this, the board announced on Monday that it had brought Huang back—with some help from the US government. Semafor reported that, hours before Huang was reinstated, President Donald Trump dialed into the publicly traded organization’s board meeting to argue for the ousted CEO’s return.

Many questions remain unanswered. What happens at the conclusion of the board-led investigation into Huang’s behavior? Multiple government agencies and lawmakers are reportedly investigating Huang; given the Trump administration’s aggressive support of the executive, what sort of accountability can anyone reasonably expect?

Sources indicate that the turmoil has also raised red flags about what happens when so much of the market revolves around one person at one company. Nvidia has massive sway over the wider artificial intelligence ecosystem, due to its deals with and investments in other companies. Last week, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 10 percent, in part due to Nvidia’s struggles—the index’s biggest decline since March 2020.

On Monday morning, CNBC asked Trump if Nvidia’s influence on the stock market influenced his decision to push for Huang’s return. “Probably so, but the bigger impact was the long term,” Trump responded.

It has been revealed that trump, who has worked closely with Huang during his second administration, established himself as a vocal supporter of the CEO amid the recent chaos. Trump told The Newly introduced York Times that he believed the board responded positively to his argument for Huang’s return because of the stock market dip, with the president suggesting that investors didn’t have much confidence in interim CEO Colette Kress.

It has been revealed that “I just thought he should be running the company, and I didn’t know it was going to have this kind of an impact,” Trump told The New York Times. “I’ve never seen anything quite like it, actually. The stock is up.”.

According to Trump, however, his decision wasn’t guided solely by the market.

“The bigger impact was, you know, he’s got other chips in his brain that nobody else knows about,” the president told CNBC. Sure!
