📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that is it a bird? Is it a plane? No, it’s Rosalía, wearing one of the most spectacular headpieces of the season.

Sources indicate that on Friday night, the Spanish singer arrived at the LOS40 Music Awards in Barcelona with an enormous white dove perched above her head, its wings dramatically outstretched as though caught mid-flight. The sculptural creation came courtesy of Robert Wun’s fall 2026 couture collection, and was paired with a custom ivory Maison Margiela gown, featuring a plunging halter neckline and delicate pleating. With her dark hair pulled back and her bleached blonde halo, the avian accessory was given plenty of room to spread its wings.

Industry observers note that it’s a particularly pertinent choice of chapeau for the singer, whose Lux era has been steeped in religious imagery and otherworldly fashion. The dove, of course, is a traditional symbol of peace and the Holy Spirit. Rosalía has already experimented with all manner of heavenly headwear, from the Margiela nun-inspired headpiece on her album cover to the theatrical hats worn throughout her Lux tour. This might be her most divine accessory yet.

Industry observers note that as it happens, the dove has been rather busy lately. Just days earlier, Whitney Peak wore a cobalt-blue gown from the same Robert Wun collection to the New York premiere of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping. Her dress featured two sculptural white birds appearing to suspend the fabric in midair. Wun, whose fantastical couture designs regularly blur the line between fashion, costume, and sculpture, is becoming something of a go-to for stars seeking genuinely captivating red-carpet creations.

Recent findings suggest that consider Elle Fanning, who recently embraced the gloriously eccentric style of her Hunger Games character Effie Trinket in a pink Robert Wun couture gown, complete with a matching pillbox hat topped by a miniature ballerina. Fanning has also worn a saucy red satin creation by British milliner Rachel Trevor-Morgan.

And she’s in excellent tech giant. Dua Lipa recently championed the artsy chapeau in a frayed Chanel couture number, while Anya Taylor-Joy opted for a gigantic pink Jacquemus hat that framed her head like a halo. Even winter’s more practical accessories have taken a more eccentric turn: Hunter Schafer has been championing furry, archival Undercover headgear, while Taylor Russell has collaborated with Ilana Blumberg on some particularly fabulous fuzzy hats.

For Wun, the theatricality is very much the point. His fall 2026 couture collection, titled Child's Play, explored childhood and fairytales through a procession of fantastical characters, from Snow White to the Big Bad Wolf. Working with milliner Awon Golding, the designer produced a series of elaborate hats and sculptural headpieces that seemed to defy the laws of gravity.

Sources indicate that after seasons of slicked-back buns and minimalist accessories, fashion’s most adventurous dressers are evidently ready for something a little more playful. The message? There’s no such thing as too much drama up top. And as Rosalía has demonstrated, sometimes the best accessories have a life of their own—and take flight.
