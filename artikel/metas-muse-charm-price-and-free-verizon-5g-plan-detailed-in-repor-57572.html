📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, meta plans to price its Muse Charm AI gadget at $300 and include a free 12-month Verizon 5G data proposal, The Information reported in a November 19, 2026 account carried by The Verge. Meta has not officially declared the price.

The report also said Meta’s separate smartwatch launch has moved to 2027 as the manufacturer works to bring the Charm to market. That schedule puts the Charm ahead of the smartwatch in Meta’s hardware aims, while the reported bundle pairs the dedicated AI device with cellular service for its first year.

Meta spokesperson Tyler Yee supplied the same statement to both publications: “We’re charmed by the interest in our AI devices, and we’ll have more to share soon.” The statement did not confirm the reported price.

Recent findings suggest that at the earlier Muse Charm reveal, CEO Mark Zuckerberg said Meta expected to ship the device “in time for the holidays in December” and that it would include the broader Muse experience, including real-time voice and avatar technology, but he gave no price. The screen-equipped Charm has a fingerprint sensor, and a tap lets people talk to a Muse AI agent without unlocking a phone or opening an app.

It has been revealed that meta’s November 5, 2026 announcement distinguishes that broader product vision from the features already rolling out. Voice and expressive avatars are arriving initially in the US Muse app on mobile, web, and Mac, while voice conversations are coming soon to AI Glasses and Muse Charm.

Sources indicate that in the Muse app, real-time voice is designed for immediate responses and interruptions. The avatar uses expressions and gestures that match the conversation; consumers can keep the experience voice-only, place the avatar beside the conversation, or make it full-screen. Meta states hundreds of voices are available and can be changed whenever the user wants.

Recent findings suggest that the planned Charm sits alongside a separate open-source hardware effort. Meta’s Muse Gadgets documentation provides SDKs for ESP32 boards and Raspberry Pi or other Linux systems, supporting connections to displays, buttons, sensors, actuators, microphones, and speakers. The device SDKs and firmware use the Apache 2.0 license and are provided as-is without warranty.

One official example pairs a Waveshare board with a round 1.75-inch AMOLED touchscreen, push-to-talk control, a speaker, a microphone, and a battery. Other projects add color e-ink or small status displays. Meta says the third-party devices shown are neither endorsed nor warranted by the company.

The Charm and the gadget program represent two distinct approaches to accessing Muse: a dedicated device with reported cellular service, or hardware assembled by users themselves. The reported price makes that choice concrete ahead of the Charm’s expected holiday shipping window.
