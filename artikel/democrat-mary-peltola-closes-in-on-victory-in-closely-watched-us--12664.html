Hello and welcome to the US politics live blog.

The final Senate race of this year’s midterm elections could be resolved tomorrow if Alaska election officials run the state’s ranked choice voting system to determine the result.

Industry observers note that with 98% of ballots counted, Democratic challenger Mary Peltola is out in front with 49.98% of the vote. By contrast, incumbent Republican senator Dan S Sullivan has a 45.93% share, according to the latest Associated Press figures.

Industry observers note that in terms of actual voting numbers, Peltola is ahead by just over 11,000, with 141,637 compared to Sullivan’s 130,162. All eyes will be on Peltola to see if she can keep the pace to achieve 50% of the ballot, two weeks on from the election.

If not, then voters’ second choice votes will be counted. The outcome will determine the final balance of power in the chamber for the next two years, with Democrats already holding a 55-44 majority.

It comes in a turbulent Alaska race, which has seen president Trump demand that Peltola be removed from the ballot and prosecuted. Piling on, Alaska’s Republican governor has since ordered an investigation into Peltola’s campaign.

In a recent development, A Newly introduced York Times investigation reported that operatives working with Peltola’s campaign helped recruit another Dan Sullivan – Dan J Sullivan – a retired teacher from Petersburg, to split the incumbent’s vote. Peltola and her campaign have denied this.

The ‘other’ Sullivan, who stood as an independent candidate, has only received 4,684 votes – a 1.65% share – and is looking unlikely to be the cause of the Republican senator’s failure should he not retain his seat.

Elsewhere, president Trump has arrived in Manila, the capital of the Philippines, for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) summit, where he is due to meet south east Asian leaders and take part in the first leaders’ meeting of the Quad partnership – consisting of the US, Japan, India and Australia – since he returned to the White House in January 2025.

In a social media post, the president was particularly critical of a report on the more than 800 American service members wounded and 23 killed in the war in Iran that 60 Minutes aired on Sunday.
