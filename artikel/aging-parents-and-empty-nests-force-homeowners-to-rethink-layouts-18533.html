Aging Parents and Empty Nests Force Homeowners To Rethink Layouts Years Before the Move

Most homeowners know their homes may need updates to accommodate their changing needs as they age.

Some, however, are thinking even further ahead.

Instead of waiting until those changes are necessary, they’re investing in renovations years in advance to prepare for aging parents who might eventually move in after their children leave the nest.

It has been revealed that angi’s 2026 State of Home Spending Pulse study found that 66% of homeowners blueprint to make a major home investment within the next five years. And the 2027 Home Trends To Watch report predicts that 2027 will be the year they take a proactive approach and focus on creating a home that grows with them.

If you own your home, experts agree that you should anticipate how your needs could change in the next five to 10 years and use that information to guide your long-term home improvement goals today.

By doing so, you can make the most out of your renovation budget, avoid costly last-minute adjustments, and ensure your home comfortably adapts to the different stages of life.

Matt Plaskoff, CEO and founder of One Week Bath in Los Angeles, is seeing this proactive, multigenerational "future-proofing” trend among his clients.

“It comes down to people having heard stories of it going the other way. Mom falls or receives a diagnosis, and suddenly the family is trying to put in a walk-in shower and grab bars on short notice while suddenly incurring more costs with doctors and insurance,” states Plaskoff.

Recent findings suggest that multigenerational living is also at an all-time high, and the recent National Association of Realtors® Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers report reflects this shift. It found that 17% of homes purchased last year were intended for multigenerational households.

And when adult kids leave, there’s suddenly more space that makes it easier to create a home that works well for both aging in place and aging parents.

We can’t forget the housing affordability crisis forcing families to rethink the homes they already own.

“The house may no longer fit the household, but buying a better-fitting home can mean a higher price, a higher mortgage rate, and higher monthly expenses,” notes Jon Brooks, co-founder of Momentum Realty in Jacksonville, FL.

Plus, it's more cost-effective to future-proof your home when you blueprint properly and actually have the funds to support your projects.

According to recent reports, “The worst time to remodel is when you don’t have the budget for it. You make decisions that should take time and careful planning under pressure and may just accept whatever contractor can show up ASAP, even if it means you have to pay more,” states Plaskoff.

There is also a practical side.

If you're already opening up walls for a bath remodel, for example, adding blocking for future grab bars, widening a doorway, or adding a curbless shower will be faster and cheaper than if you were to come back later and redo finished work.

Experts agree that a strong universal design features a functional yet beautiful bathroom with a wider doorway, good lighting, lever handles for knobs, a walk-in shower, and slip-resistant flooring.

“If done right, it feels more like a spa than a hospital, and that’s what matters most to those who are using it every day,” Plaskoff explains.

A home that supports privacy and independence is just as important.

“An example can be a primary suite on the ground floor with its own bathroom, or a room with a separate entrance. Parents want to keep their routines and privacy, without having to sacrifice accessibility,” claims Plaskoff.

You might also consider adding a second structure to your backyard like an accessory dwelling unit or tiny home.

“For families with the space, an ADU is one of the best solutions we see. It gives an aging parent true independence, with their own kitchen, bath, and front door, while keeping them close enough for daily connection and support,” explains Michelle Squire, designer and principal at Studio SQUARE in San Francisco.

If long-term resale is important to you, be sure to consider what buyers value in your neighborhood. This way you can determine where to make permanent changes and where to keep things flexible.

“If you’re overimproving for the area, just understand you may have a harder time recouping that money if you choose to sell,” says Christina Rordam, real estate agent at Florida Realty Investments in Orlando, FL.

At the end of the day, prioritize flexibility, try not to over improve for the neighborhood you’re in, and most importantly, consider what works best for you and your family.
