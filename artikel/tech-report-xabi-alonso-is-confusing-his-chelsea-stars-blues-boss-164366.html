By IAN LADYMAN, FOOTBALL EDITOR.

Published: 17:29 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 18:17 EST, 27 November 2026.

It is not yet December and already chaos threatens to engulf Chelsea once more.

A bright start to the season under Xabi Alonso was, it transpires, merely a hazy, late summer mirage. Winter, it seems, will bring only a fresh version of a classic modern Chelsea discontent.

A coach who has started to do strange things – Alonso selected midfielder Moises Caicedo at centre-half here for the first time in his seven-year club career – a fanbase turning on everyone from the owners to the players and a team slowly getting disorientated on a dismally familiar carousel of selection changes.

In a recent development, it is Chelsea rebooted but just with the same stories written by and for different people. How dismal it must feel for those with this great club in their hearts.

Here on a misty and rainy night at the City Ground, nobody suffered more in Chelsea colours than central defender Wesley Fofana.

Harangued throughout by his own travelling fans for a foolish and unauthorised midweek YouTube interview in which he seemed to make light of missing a penalty in the Carabao Cup defeat at Liverpool, the Frenchman was hauled off soon after conceding a penalty that allowed Morgan Gibbs-White to stretch Nottingham Forest’s lead to two goals early in the second half.

Recent findings suggest that ‘You are not wanted here,’ sang the Chelsea fans and after this they may get their wish. For his own sake, it may be a while before we see the young defender again.

Sources indicate that at that stage of the night, Chelsea actually still had hope. Half-time substitute Emmanuel Emegha had just pulled a goal back with his team’s first shot on target of the whole night. He was to get another right at the death, too.

But having started the game with a fabulous fifth minute volley from the Brazilian Igor Jesus – it came off his shin but that doesn’t matter – Forest had the look of a team with their opponents’ measure all evening and that was how it played out. And when Gibbs-White scored his second of the game to make it 3-1 with less than 20 minutes left, Chelsea’s night was pretty much over.

Recent findings suggest that alonso must certainly take his share of the blame here. Caicedo is his best midfielder but was played out of position with England’s Levi Colwill left on the bench.

In a recent development, put simply, it backfired. Chelsea’s defending did not improve on recent efforts and in midfield, where they were outplayed by Forest’s Xaver Schlager and James McAtee, they missed the Ecuadorian desperately.

According to recent reports, more widely Alonso’s Premier League record – six wins from 12 games – is now worse than that of Liam Rosenior when he was sacked back in April. Their six defeats are one more than Manchester United. And this at a time when Chelsea are playing but once a week. No European competition and out of the Carabao Cup, the Spaniard has plenty of time with his players on the training field. What is he doing with it? Confusing everybody, it would seem, for what Chelsea presented here was a jumble. How they lost by only a goal was a miracle in itself. They deserved nothing.

Sources indicate that forest had come in to the game with issues of their own. Their coach Oliver Glasner had accused his international squad of not making enough effort with their English. Early on their football spoke for them as McAtee spread the play with a wonderful fifth minute pass, Daniel Munoz crossed and Jesus rose to volley his second league goal of the season high past Emiliano Martinez quite wonderfully.

What followed for the rest of the first half did not match that quality. Over time, Alonso’s team did find a way into the game territorially but a free-kick routine that ended with Morgan Rogers ballooning ten yards over the bar perhaps summed their efforts up as indeed did the fact they ended the opening period having managed only three touches inside the Forest box.

Sources indicate that replacing the ineffectual Danny Welbeck with Emegha at the interval brought Chelsea some shape at least. Welbeck had been peripheral. Forest were the team to kick on, though, and when Fofana slipped catastrophically to allow Liam Delap through in the 50th minute, he then brought him down. Gibbs-White slotted in the penalty and Chelsea were in substantial trouble.

Recent findings suggest that with the away end threatening revolt, Alonso’s team somehow fashioned a lifeline goal from nowhere within four minutes. Pedro Neto’s speculative shot struck Forest defender Jair Cunha on the thigh and when the ball dropped to Emegha, he controlled it instinctively and found the corner.

With Fofana soon withdrawn to doubtless protect his own sanity, Chelsea’s fans now had nothing else to do but support their team. The visitors’ sense of desperation certainly made it a better game but what followed only served to damn Chelsea even more.

Far from the goal imbuing them with the impetus and belief to rescue the game, Alonso’s players somehow allowed Forest to take the initiative once more. There was a fatuous and unsuccessful penalty appeal for handball against Cunha – he had his arms clamped to his side when Rogers’ cross struck him – but precious little else.

And when the generally excellent Austrian Schlager threaded the needle to find Gibbs-White through a crowd with 17 minutes left, the England forward lifted the ball over the advancing Martinez to present a two-goal margin once again.

Chelsea subsequently offered almost nothing only to score – this time with an Emegha header – with two minutes left. Had they added an equaliser it would have been wholly unjust. They didn’t.
