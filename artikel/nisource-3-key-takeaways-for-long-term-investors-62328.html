Industry observers note that on its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 28, 2026, NiSource (NI -0.63%) raised its long-term financial guidance while expanding its capital investment commitments. The utility is blending standard regulated energy distribution with power contracts for giant technology facilities.

Three things management noted stand out.

NiSource signed a 20-year agreement with Microsoft, marking its fifth data center power contract across three technology giants. The projects run through Genco, the company's power generation segment, and lower electricity bills for retail customers of NIPSCO, its electric utility subsidiary.

Executive Vice President Shawn Anderson detailed a fresh seven-year spending forecast that dedicates $32 billion to core regulated utilities and $8 billion to data center generation. Management expects this capital deployment to support compound annual adjusted earnings per share, or net income divided by outstanding stock, growth of 10% to 11%.

Industry observers note that utilities usually wait years to collect revenues on newly introduced construction, creating regulatory lag that drags on cash flows. NiSource uses forward-looking rate structures and tracker mechanisms, which are regulatory processes that let utilities recover specific investment costs quickly, to streamline cash generation.

According to recent reports, large technology companies are expanding heavily across Northern Indiana, where available land, water access, and fiber networks give NiSource an operational edge over surrounding power grids. Management is watching regulatory approval schedules and construction milestones to ensure projects open on time.

The utility couples steady 6% to 8% earnings growth from its base gas and electric utilities with higher-margin growth from data center power contracts. That combination creates two complementary income streams that insulate shareholders from regulatory shifts in any single state.
