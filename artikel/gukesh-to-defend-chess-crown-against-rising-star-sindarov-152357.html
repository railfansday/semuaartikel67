When India's world chess champion, Dommaraju Gukesh, faced reporters at the pre-tournament press conference in Geneva on Sunday, there was no sign of the youthful, carefree energy one might expect from a 20-year-old.

"I don't think I'm here to defend anything—I'm here to win it all over again," he noted.

Starting November 24, Gukesh will be facing challenger Jakhovir Sindarov of Uzbekistan for a purse of $2.5 million (€2.3m) in the World Chess Championship.

Sources indicate that gukesh captured the title in 2024, making him the youngest-ever world champion at just 18. Since then, however, things have not as gone well for the player known for his exceptional calculation skills and a penchant for risky moves. He currently languishes in 26th place in the FIDE world rankings.

Sindarov, who has climbed to number four in the world, told reporters that he only began training "seriously" last year and has learned a lot from observing the reigning world champion.

"He has a very good strength in typical positions, his calculation is very good, and Gukesh can fight until the end," Sindarov stated.

Former world champion Viswanathan Anand, who was also one of the world's best players as a teenager, is not completely surprised by Gukesh's lack of form over the past two years.

"He became the world champion (very young). So suddenly the responsibility is shifted onto you and even expectations," Anand told DW.

"Clearly he has deficiencies in his chess as is normal for someone who's 20, he's not had time to experience a lot.".

By the time he became India's first world champion, Anand was 37 – ancient by the age-standards of today's top players.

"With powerful chess software and global connectivity, it is easier for young players today to reach the top level," Jan Gustafsson, coach of Germany's national chess team, told DW.

"Online chess provides many more learning opportunities for talented players," added Gustafsson, who sees the recent movement at the top of the rankings as a "generational shift.".

While Norway's Magnus Carlsen, long considered the greatest player of all time, remains number one in the world rankings, he rarely plays classical chess anymore and gave up his world championship title after declining to defend it in 2023.

It has been revealed that now, young players—particularly from Asia—are pushing to the forefront. Alongside Gukesh and Sindarov, players such as Rameshbabu Praggnanandhaa from India and Nodirbek Abdusattorov of Uzbekistan—an emerging chess powerhouse—are considered contenders to win a world championship in the not-too-distant future.

"Broad social acceptance and state support ensure that children can focus on chess at a very early stage," Gustafsson mentioned.

In a recent development, another contender is Germany's Vincent Keymer, who at 22 is number nine in the world and well established among the world's elite. Most recently, Keymer and his teammates won a bronze medal at the World Team Championship, often referred to as the "Chess Olympiad.".
