Recent findings suggest that want an SUV with speed, luxury, and practicality in equal doses? The Audi SQ7 offers that trifecta, with a premium interior, three rows of seats, and a 591-hp twin-turbo V-8 engine. An adaptive air-spring suspension system is standard, which is meant to help the SQ7 deliver dual-purpose transit with a softer ride for commuting and a stiffer setup for weekend corner carving.

According to recent reports, tech lovers will find plenty to like inside the SQ7's cabin as well, as the dashboard is literally covered in screens. A host of driver-assistance features are also on hand. But the SQ7's main draw is its performance, which is expected to be truly bananas for a family SUV.

Expert Tip: If the SQ7 sounds like a bit too much, consider stepping down to the Q7, which is essentially the same SUV but without the V-8 or the six-figure price tag.

The SQ7 has been redesigned for 2027, entering its second generation as the performance variant of the standard Q7 SUV.

If this year's model is largely unchanged and you're comfortable with a lightly used car, you may want to consider shopping previous model years. A one-to-three-year-old model will likely have a warranty and it'll cost less than buying latest.

While the standard Q7 SUV comes with a healthy 429 horsepower from its turbocharged V-6, the SQ7 offers a bonkers 591 horsepower from its twin-turbocharged V-8. An eight-speed automatic transmission and all-wheel drive are standard.

Industry observers note that when we drove the new SQ7, we found it to be a driver's delight. It comes with an adaptive air-spring suspension system as standard, which can be upgraded to an optional sport adaptive air-spring system, which results in a 1.2-inch lower ride height. Other upgrades over the standard Q7 include bigger brakes, available summer tires, and optional 23-inch wheels. It all adds up to an athletic SUV, especially considering the SQ7’s size.

Industry observers note that in Comparison: While the SQ7 may not end up being as quick as rivals such as the Land Rover Range Rover Sport SV or the Porsche Cayenne GTS, it'll likely be a close race. And we are willing to bet a 3.6-second 60 mph time for a family SUV will be overkill for all but the most aggressive drivers.

Sources indicate that in Comparison: The SQ7 is rated to tow the same 7700 pounds as the standard Q7. That number is slightly more than you can pull with the Range Rover Sport SV and the Cayenne GTS.

Industry observers note that according to the EPA, the SQ7 is rated for 15 mpg city, 20 mpg highway, and 17 mpg combined. Those numbers are predictably low given the powerful V-8 engine under the hood. We haven’t yet run the SQ7 on our real-world 75-mph highway fuel economy test, but we’ll revision this section once we do. For more information about the SQ7's fuel economy, visit the EPA's website.

In a recent development, first Row: The SQ7's interior design mostly mirrors that of the standard Q7's, except it wears a sportier-looking wardrobe. The center console sports a pair of cupholders along with two smartphone charging pads. Leather upholstery is standard and offered in a range of colors, including a cool blue color that Audi calls Baikal Blue.

Second Row: While a three-seat bench is standard, the SQ7 can be had with a pair of second row captain's chairs instead. Passenger space is on par with other three-row SUV rivals, and adults should have no trouble finding a comfortable position.

Third Row: The two-seat third row, however, is a place only children would find accommodating.

Recent findings suggest that cargo Capacity: With 15 cubic feet of space behind the third row, the SQ7 offers average cargo room for the class. Drop the third row, and there's a lot more space, up to 44 cubic feet. If you need to haul more stuff and people, the larger Audi Q9 SUV might be worth a look.

Three different digital displays cover nearly every square inch of the SQ7's dashboard. A 14.5-inch touchscreen sits between an 11.9-inch gauge cluster and a 12.3-inch passenger display. The software is Audi's latest version of MMI, which digitizes most of the SUV's controls, including—annoyingly—the direction of airflow from the dashboard vents.

Wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto are standard, as is Bluetooth connectivity and an in-dash navigation system. The base SQ7 comes with a 16-speaker stereo tuned by Bang & Olufsen, but a 22-speaker Bang & Olufsen stereo is an option, too.

Driver-assistance features abound in the SQ7, ranging from expected basics—such as adaptive cruise control and lane-keeping assist—to convenient extras such as automatic high-beam headlamps and parking sensors.

For information about the SQ7's crash-test results, visit the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) websites.

The SQ7 has class-typical warranty coverage, matching rivals such as BMW and Volvo.
