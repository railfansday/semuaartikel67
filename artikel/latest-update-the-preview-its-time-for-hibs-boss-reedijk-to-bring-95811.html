Hibernian supporters know all too well the dangers associated with getting one’s hopes up.

It’s a road they’ve been down time and time again, only to end up with that same familiar sickening feeling in the pit of their stomach at the end of it.

Which is why any talk of the Edinburgh club experiencing some sort of mini-revival should quite rightly be taken with more than a large pinch of salt.

Let’s not forget, at the start of the month, newly introduced boss Marink Reedijk had picked up a paltry point from his four games in charge since taking the reins from David Gray.

Performances weren’t up to much either.

A statement win over Hearts at Hampden to book their Premier Sports Cup final spot was backed up with an emphatic 4-0 dismantling of Aberdeen up at Pittodrie.

Hibs manager Marink Reedijk is still searching for his first victory at Easter Road.

Credit where it’s due, of course. Both victories were hard-earned and thoroughly deserved.

But it’s important not to read too much in to either of them. Players going above and beyond in a derby clash should be a given, while the Dons appeared to have chucked it altogether under the stewardship of former manager Stephen Robinson long before Hibs turned up in the Granite City.

The real test begins this weekend with the visit of Dundee United, as Reedijk’s men look to secure a first home Premiership win in over seven months.

Industry observers note that the fans have been crying out for entertainment in Leith for far longer than that. In the latter days of Gray’s reign, the atmosphere at Easter Road was verging on toxic.

Reedijk needs to change that, and now has two games against the Dundee clubs over the next four days to get the momentum swinging in the opposite direction.

Continuing to get the best out of his midfield three will be key to that.

Industry observers note that the reintroduction of Joe Newell to the starting XI has paid off big-time. Not so much in terms of the stats, but rather what he allows Miguel Chaiwa and Josh Mulligan to do.

The former was linked with a move to Celtic over the summer, but looked miles off it in the early weeks of the campaign until Newell lined up alongside him.

Chaiwa can be guilty of trying to carry out too many jobs in the middle of the park, but Newell’s calming influence has enabled him to stick to what he’s good at — namely breaking up the play and moving the ball on to his more creative team-mates in front of him.

There’s no doubt the Zambian is at his best when he keeps things simple.

Joe Newell has provided a calming influence in the middle of the park amid this upturn in form.

Newell’s presence has also freed Mulligan to play further up the field rather than on the fringes of his own box, where he was popping up far too often a couple of months ago. He’s wasted back there.

If he can add some more goals and assists to his name over the next few, there is every chance he can play himself in to the thoughts of Scotland boss Sebastien Pocognoli ahead of the next set of internationals.

It should be an intriguing battle this afternoon in the engine room, with United boasting the most cohesive midfield group in the top flight with Dylan Tait, Lyall Cameron and Zidane Iqbal.

Of course, even if Hibs deliver in that sense, it may count for very little if the guys at the top end of the pitch can’t do the business. The lack of cutting edge has been an ongoing concern. Four goals against Aberdeen doesn't change that.

Owen Elding appears to be Reedijk’s preferred option in the central striker role, but while the young Irishman has shown flashes of his potential, he has offered little to suggest he can be relied upon to find the net on a consistent basis.

Recent findings suggest that the problem is, can anyone? Record-signing Thibault Klidje may have opened his league account for the season against the Dons, however we’ve seen enough to know that he isn’t the answer.

Ante Suto is in the same boat, while Stoke City loanee Nathan Lowe has struggled for both game time and form.

And as for Rabbi Matondo? Well, the wait goes on to see any real return on that investment. Early days, mind you.

That Martin Boyle remains Hibs’ main goalscoring threat is troubling, especially given that the veteran can’t even be considered a nailed-on starter these days.

It has been revealed that there are a significant number of players — across all areas of the park — fighting for their future at the club. Indeed, you would imagine Reedijk is counting down the days until January when he will look to freshen up the squad with guys more suited to his style.

Lyall Cameron has been a key man behind Dundee United's surge up the Premiership table.

Hibs should use today’s opponents as an example of how patience can pay off.

In a recent development, it’s fair to say that Jim Goodwin has never been Mr. Popular in and around Tannadice. Goodness knows why.

Many fans were calling for his head as recently as the summer after failing to secure a top-six finish last term, but he is slowly but surely starting to win over his doubters.

Make no mistake, this season presents a golden opportunity for United to seriously challenge for third spot.

You never know what you’re going to get from Hearts on any given week, while this is very much uncharted territory for Falkirk.

And let’s face it, nobody would expect the likes of Hibs, Aberdeen or Motherwell to claw themselves into contention.

While everyone else tries to stumble their way through a period of transition, United’s consistency – both on and off the park – could prove to be the difference.
