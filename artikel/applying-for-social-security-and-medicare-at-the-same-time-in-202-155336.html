Applying for Social Security and Medicare at the Same Time in 2027? 3 Things You Need to Know.

By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 27, 2026 at 5:28AM EST.

Industry observers note that if you're turning 65 in 2027, you'll soon be signing up for Medicare, and of course, you'll have the option to claim Social Security, too, assuming you're not already receiving checks. But these are major financial decisions, so it's worth thinking them through carefully before you decide.

Here are three things you should be aware of before you apply for Social Security and Medicare in 2027.

You are within your rights to claim Social Security at age 65, but doing so is technically considered early claiming and carries an early withdrawal penalty. To avoid this, you must delay your Social Security application until your full retirement age (FRA). This is 67 if you were born in 1960 or later. Older adults have slightly younger FRAs.

According to recent reports, this doesn't mean you shouldn't claim Social Security at 65. It can still be the right move for you if you aren't able to work, have little personal savings, or have a short life expectancy.

But if you don't need your checks right now, you might get a larger lifetime benefit by waiting a few more years before you apply. If you'd like, you can continue delaying Social Security until you qualify for your largest benefit at age 70.

Industry observers note that when you're on both Medicare and Social Security benefits, the government withholds your Medicare Part B premiums from your Social Security checks. This gives you one less bill to worry about each month.

In 2027, the Part B premium will be $209.40 for most seniors, though wealthy Americans will pay more. If you opt to claim Social Security later, you will have to pay these premiums out of your own pocket until you apply for Social Security.

There's no rule saying that you must apply for Social Security and Medicare at the same time. Choose your Social Security claiming age based on your estimated life expectancy, finances, and personal preferences. Consider a variety of claiming ages before you settle on one to make sure you're comfortable with the trade-offs of your decision.
