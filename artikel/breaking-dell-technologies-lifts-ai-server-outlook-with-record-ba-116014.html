Dell Technologies raised its fiscal 2027 AI-optimized server revenue outlook to $78 billion on November 24, 2026, alongside third-quarter results showing revenue of $54.9 billion, up 103% year over year. It ended the quarter with a record $132.3 billion AI-server backlog, or orders not yet converted into revenue, after booking $60.2 billion in orders.

AI-optimized servers produced $22.0 billion of revenue in the quarter ended October 30, 2026. The broader Infrastructure Solutions Group (ISG), which also includes traditional servers, networking and storage, generated $7.1 billion in operating income and a 17.0% operating margin, compared with 12.4% a year earlier.

In a recent development, management attributed the margin expansion primarily to scale, operating-expense efficiencies and demand for storage based on Dell’s own intellectual property. Traditional-server demand was supported by enterprises refreshing aging systems, modernizing data centers and adding CPU-based compute for AI inference. Together, those trends give the infrastructure business sources of growth and profit beyond AI systems themselves.

In prepared remarks, Dell said its AI Factory customer base had passed 7,500 across neocloud providers, sovereign customers and enterprises, while its opportunity pipeline remained multiples of backlog. Enterprises were the fastest-growing part of the pipeline’s sequential expansion, indicating broader demand beyond large cloud buyers.

Industry observers note that every AI order is reviewed for the customer’s financial capacity and underlying end-user contracts, trade and export compliance, and access to capital, land and power. Those checks run when Dell agrees to a purchase order and again before production begins. The process ties backlog quality to financing, export compliance and the physical resources required for an AI deployment.

The upcoming AI-server forecast replaces Dell’s previous $74 billion target. The company also raised full-year total revenue guidance to $202.0 billion from $192.0 billion and expects approximately $23.5 billion of AI-server revenue in the fourth quarter.

In a recent development, management nevertheless said infrastructure demand continues to outpace supply, with the gap between component supply and end-user demand expected to widen in fiscal 2028. That puts pressure on procurement and manufacturing even as Dell’s order vetting and rack-level integration address customer quality and installation speed.
