By New Year’s Day 2027, Anthropic’s Claude AI predicts Lighter crypto will deliver one of the sharpest post-incident rebounds the perp DEX sector has seen this cycle, the kind of violent recovery that turns a scary headline into pure rocket fuel.

The LIT price took a hit on yesterday’s exploit news, dropping more than 20% to $2.50. However, the team’s response and USDC fund recovery have seen the price recover to just under $3, surging +10% over the past 24 hours.

On November 16, 2026, Lighter, the high-performance decentralized perpetuals exchange that settles on Ethereum with zero-knowledge proofs, activated Guardian mode and paused all withdrawals and fresh pool creation.

On-chain data highlighted roughly $10.39M in USDC leaving the platform’s Ethereum settlement contract in a single withdrawal. Some researchers put the total impact closer to $15.5M across two large market-maker accounts. The attacker used a leaked API key tied to a trader’s account. The root cause was insecure custom signing code that reused cryptographic nonces, allowing the attacker to reconstruct the private key from public signatures.

In a recent development, with the key, the attacker created a public pool from the compromised account, acquired most of its shares, moved the victim’s USDC into the pool, and redeemed it. Importantly, Lighter’s core protocol and smart contracts were never compromised; this was an account-level key compromise, not a smart-contract exploit. Funds were rapidly swapped into ETH, with portions routed to Hyperliquid. LIT initially dropped by 20% on the news before buyers stepped in.

Lighter moved fast. Within hours, the team publicly confirmed the cause, named the attacker wallet, and made clear that the protocol itself remained intact. The Security Council recovered approximately $5M in USDC and returned it to the affected trader. Guardian mode was deactivated the same day and withdrawals resumed. Upcoming safeguards followed immediately: wallet signatures are now required for transfers into public pools, API key signatures are non-public, IP allowlists are planned, per-key permission limits are in place, and automated monitoring for nonce reuse is active.

People running custom or third-party signing software were strongly advised to rotate keys and switch to Lighter’s official SDKs. The message was clear: the platform treated the incident as a user-key hygiene issue and hardened the surrounding controls without ever having a core vulnerability.

That combination of transparent communication, partial recovery, rapid resumption of operations, and concrete security upgrades is exactly the kind of response that historically flips sentiment from fear to “they handled it cleanly.”.
