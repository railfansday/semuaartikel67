It feels like a scene out of a sci-fi movie: Joaquin Phoenix is standing across from me, instructing me to speak into his eyeglasses in a foreign language.

According to recent reports, “Let’s see what happens,” the Oscar winner notes. “It could be any language.”.

According to recent reports, the idea we’re testing here is whether the glasses Phoenix is wearing—a dark-rimmed pair from the South Korean luxury eyewear brand Gentle Monster’s new Intelligent Eyewear collection—could live-translate these words into English. This is one of the glasses’ capabilities the actor was most delighted to discover while he was shooting their campaign film directed by another Oscar winner, Jonathan Glazer. The glasses were crafted in partnership with Google and Samsung, and feature AI-powered technology.

Joaquin Phoenix in the Jonathan Glazer-directed campaign spot.

The last time Phoenix appeared in a fashion campaign, it was 1997 and he was modeling moody suits for Prada. This time around, he stars in the Glazer-directed spot that Phoenix claims was partially inspired by the 1969 documentary Salesman, in which vendors travel across Upcoming England selling Bibles. In the Gentle Monster ad, Phoenix’s character—who has an intense, quasi-manic energy—ventures into an unnamed hotel, where he falls into a deep sleep and meets colorful, large creatures at the end of a hallway. (One might even call them…Gentle Monsters.) Per a press release, the “intelligent eyewear becomes his way of understanding their language and communicating with them, opening up unexpected possibilities for connection.”.

Phoenix had wanted to work with Glazer for a long time. “I’ve known him for years, and when we all approached him, we came up with this really interesting and offbeat idea that just [had so] much mood. He just gets super excited and inspired,” the actor says. During the shoot, the Zone of Interest filmmaker kept the camera rolling for prolonged periods of time, encouraging Phoenix to harness different emotions. A moment in the ad might last for “30 seconds, but we shot for 15 minutes,” he explains. “A whole range of things are happening in those 15 minutes, things that we were trying.”.

Industry observers note that in an email, Glazer reiterated that he was glad to have had a reason to work with Phoenix: “The first image I had was him asleep, maybe dreaming, with the glasses all wonky across his face. The rest kind of grew from there,” the director said.

When I meet the actor last Wednesday at 23 Wall Street in Manhattan—where Gentle Monster has staged an enormous activation for the Intelligent Eyewear launch, complete with fake horses with wagging tails and huge surreal sculptures—it’s hard not to think about his turn in the 2013 film Her, one of the most prophetic AI movies of our time. In the Spike Jonze near-future drama, Phoenix’s character, Theodore Twombly, chats with and subsequently falls for an AI-powered operating system voiced by Scarlett Johansson. If 2011’s Contagion foreshadowed the COVID-19 pandemic, Her’s depiction of chatbots feels eerily similar to the world of ChatGPT dots, Meta Muses, Claude Duets, and Gemini Sparks that we’re beginning to grow accustomed to.

Phoenix and his partner, actress Rooney Mara, wearing Intelligent Eyewear at the Gentle Monster event in Manhattan.

When I ask Phoenix if filming the campaign made him think of Her, he shrugs. “I wasn’t. It seems like I should have,” he says. “It’s funny because I think that sometimes films have more of, maybe, an impact on the people that view them than for me, but I hadn’t thought about that. Somebody just made a joke about it actually, and I was like, ‘Oh yeah, you’re right.’”.

Phoenix, who most recently starred in 2025’s politically tinged neo-Western Eddington, leaves on his Intelligent Eyewear glasses—which are Gemini-embedded, sleek, and thick-rimmed—while we speak.

“The thing is,” he says, “I’m not even aware that I’m wearing them.”.
