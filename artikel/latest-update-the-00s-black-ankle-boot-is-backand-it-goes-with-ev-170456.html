Sources indicate that what to say about ankle boots? A style so synonymous with bloggers of the early 2010s that they even acquired their own caption-friendly nickname: “booties”. OK, technically the term dates back to the medieval era, when it was used to describe any boot that stopped at the ankle, but you get the point.

Industry observers note that booties were the footwear of choice for people who, when real life got too frightening, retreated into the comforting embrace of typewriters, vinyl records and Polaroid cameras. And while culture has more or less left all that behind–along with the Peter Pan collars, little A-line skirts and knitted tights with which the shoes were worn (Zooey Deschanel had a lot to answer for)–booties are inching their way back into fashion.

Here, five surprisingly “now” ways to wear the ankle boots of “then”.

The most popular ankle boots of the 2010s were those with a sole jacked far above the ground. See: anything Jeffrey Campbell produced, which usually came studded within an inch of their lives. I’m not sure anyone is quite ready for those to make a return, but, as seen here on Alexa Chung, the attitude of a platform offsets a demure minidress pretty well.

Sienna Miller con minifalda de jean.

You need only spend a millisecond looking at this image of Sienna Miller in a tank top and denim (your choice of skirt or jeans) to appreciate that miniature cowboy boots are every bit as timeless as the Balenciaga City bag tucked under her arm.

A longstanding fashion archetype, biker boots are rarely not “in”, but have enjoyed a resurgence in recent seasons thanks, in large part, to Miu Miu’s buckled suede iterations. Worn with skinny jeans and a shrunken leather jacket à la Gisele Bündchen, the look is as much a fixture of the AllSaints shop floor in 2026 as it was in 2016.

Recent findings suggest that if anyone could make a convincing case for the surprising chicness of a suede ankle boot, it’s Kate Moss–who, in fairness, could make a convincing case for the surprising chicness of just about anything. Here she is wearing Alaïa’s corset-laced booties on a trip to Boots, of all places, with a close-cut military tweed jacket that feels particularly of today’s moment.

Hear me out: high-shine patent booties worn not in an “I’m going to the club because I just got my A-level results in 2010” way, but in a Mary-Kate Olsen attending the Burberry Prorsum (RIP) autumn/winter 2010 show way. The addition of don’t-look-at-me sunglasses and a caped wool coat lends the look that evil fashion editor quality to which we all—or at least I—aspire.
