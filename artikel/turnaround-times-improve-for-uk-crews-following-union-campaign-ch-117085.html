Turnaround Times Improve for U.K. Crews Following Union Campaign: ‘Change Is Possible When Workers Speak Out’ (EXCLUSIVE)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Turnaround times are improving, crew union Bectu has revealed.

But figures show numerous productions are still breaking the agreement to give crews their legally-enshrined 11-hours rest between shifts.

Recent findings suggest that figures provided to Bectu by producers body Pact demonstrated a “significant reduction” in breaking turnaround agreements on U.K. scripted productions, the union noted. For Band 1 productions, which cost up to £1.25 million ($1.5 million) per broadcast hour, broken turnarounds have fallen from 107 hours in 2024 to 71 hours in 2025, before dropping to zero hours during the first four months of 2026.

On Band 2 productions, which cost between £1.25 million and £3 million per broadcast hour, broken turnarounds dropped from 158 hours in 2024 to 106 hours in 2025 and then to 48 hours during the first four months of 2026.

Figures are not recorded for productions in Band 3 (£3 million to £8 million per broadcast hour) or Band 4 (over £8 million).

In a recent development, earlier this year Bectu led protests outside the London headquarters of studios including Disney, Netflix and Amazon to highlight the problem. The union has also been engaging with members and raising awareness with employers to push for better scheduling practices.

Bectu is now calling for Pact to continue measuring broken turnaround figures and extend reporting to Bands 3 and 4, including low-budget features and major film productions. The union also wants figures for unscripted productions as well as for all film and TV productions in the U.K. to pledge they will adhere to turnaround times.

“These figures are extremely encouraging and show that change is possible when workers speak out and the industry listens,” noted Bectu national secretary Spencer Macdonald. “It is also positive simply that this practice is now being recorded – it indicates a recognition that there is a problem, and creates a means to measure progress.”.

Industry observers note that “Broken turnaround has long been a concern for many crew members, who have experienced the impact that insufficient rest can have on their health, wellbeing and safety. The sharp reduction across both Band 1 and Band 2 productions suggests that awareness of the concern is translating into real improvements on the ground.”.

“There is still a lot of work to do across the industry, particularly in ensuring good practice becomes embedded. But these results demonstrate the value of collective action and maintaining constant pressure about unsustainable working hours and fatigue.”.
