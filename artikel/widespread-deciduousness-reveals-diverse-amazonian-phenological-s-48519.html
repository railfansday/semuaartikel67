Widespread deciduousness reveals diverse Amazonian phenological strategies

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that the Amazon rainforest is a cornerstone of regional and global carbon cycling1 and is widely regarded and modelled as a perennially evergreen ecosystem2,3. This prevailing view, however, masks substantial fine-scale variation in leaf phenology, limiting our capacity to predict ecosystem responses to climate change. Here by combining 10-m Sentinel-2 observations with machine learning, we uncover pervasive, spatially structured seasonal deciduousness across the basin. In 70.0% of the Amazon, leaf shedding precedes leaf flushing by at least 3 months, creating pronounced asynchrony between deciduousness and greenness seasonality, whereas the remainder reveals near-synchronous phenology. Hydroclimate emerges as the dominant factor associated with this asynchrony across 51.6% of the area, followed by soil properties (33.2%) and light availability (15.2%). Motivated by this widespread decoupling between deciduousness and canopy greenness, we incorporated deciduousness-constrained leaf turnover and the resulting leaf-age demography into ecophysiological models, yielding spatially widespread improvements in the seasonal agreement between simulated gross primary productivity and satellite-derived photosynthetic seasonality across 79.9% of the basin. These findings challenge the conventional view of Amazonian phenology, demonstrating that previously overlooked deciduousness influences Amazonian carbon dynamics. Incorporating these insights into process-based models is essential for refining projections of climate–forest feedbacks in the Amazon and for understanding its ecological functions under accelerating global change.
