Standing committee will oversee export compliance following allegations its GPUs were diverted to China.

Nvidia's board has created a national security and export control committee amid intense scrutiny over co-founder and former CEO Jensen Huang's departure and allegations that Singapore-based customer Megaspeed diverted GPUs to China.

The National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, announced on Tuesday, will monitor Nvidia's national security policies, export compliance, and global trade risks. It will meet at least quarterly.

Industry observers note that the committee will be chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson and include directors Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri as members. Nvidia's existing National Security and Global Trade Compliance teams will report on day-to-day operations directly to this upcoming committee, as well as to interim CEO Colette Kress.

The panel will oversee efforts to strengthen Nvidia's national security and trade compliance teams and retain independent legal, national security, and trade experts.

The announcement follows the resignation of Huang last week after he admitted to an affair with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive, and was allegedly involved in a second relationship with an Nvidia employee.

US authorities reportedly suspected Alice Huang of working for Chinese intelligence, an allegation that has not been proven. Megaspeed has separately faced scrutiny over suspected export control violations.

According to recent reports, the US Department of Commerce and Department of Justice confirmed on Tuesday in a joint statement that they were investigating Megaspeed back in 2024. The agencies mentioned they would pursue any criminal conduct uncovered by the investigation.

However, Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs stated that US authorities had provided only "limited assistance" when they were investigating the activities of Alice Huang and Megaspeed, as The Register reported.

The Latest York Times also reported that US investigations into Megaspeed were halted last year after an Nvidia executive contacted Commerce Department officials.

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) has issued latest regulations tightening up restrictions on AI technology exports and cloud operators offering remote access to it.

The US also expanded its Pax Silica initiative, getting allied nations to sign an agreement that "guarding AI supply chains" is a shared responsibility.

Nvidia indicates it recognizes that advanced silicon and AI technologies present challenges for national security, and it has a responsibility to address them.

"We are committed to working constructively with government and industry partners to help establish and advance a robust framework that uses every tool at our disposal to safeguard national security while supporting continued technological innovation," remarked Harvey Jones, chair of Nvidia's board of directors. ®.
