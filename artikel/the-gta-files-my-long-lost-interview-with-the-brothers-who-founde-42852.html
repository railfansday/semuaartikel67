Sources indicate that “You can’t fuck with Keith Richards.” That’s what Sam Houser told me when I asked the inspiration for his firm name, Rockstar Games. “I’m a huge fan of the classic golden era of rock stars—both the music, but also the life,” he went on. “You can't argue with Keith Moon riding in a Rolls Royce with a blowup doll hanging out the back and a woman in there screaming, ‘I'm being raped.’ The guy is the original punk rocker. And if we can bring a fraction of that to games, then we’re doing something.”.

Industry observers note that it was October 2002, just a few weeks before the release of their latest blockbuster, Grand Theft Auto: Vice City. I’d come to their glossy loft in Soho, Manhattan to interview the elusive 30-year-old Houser and Rockstar co-founders, his younger brother Dan Houser and managing director Terry Donovan, for Rolling Stone. It would be one of their first, last and only in-depth sit-downs together before they decided that the world’s most successful and controversial video game required little explanation from them at all.

Recent findings suggest that I continued reporting on them and their games during the raucous and turbulent years to follow: hitting over $10 billion in sales, getting compared to Balzac and Dickens in Time magazine, being sued (unsuccessfully) for causing real-life violence. As I investigated in my book Jacked: The Outlaw Story of Grand Theft Auto, they also triggered what remains the most outrageous and cinematic scandal ever to hit the industry: the discovery of a hidden sex scene in 2005’s GTA San Andreas that would inspire attempts to legislate and ban the game. As Hilary Clinton noted at the time, “The disturbing material in Grand Theft Auto and other games like it is stealing the innocence of our children and it’s making the difficult job of being a parent even harder.”.

With the gamer generation now grown up and in power, the Reefer Madness panic around GTA has finally given way to something more vital: this little game out of Scotland has become the most successful and influential entertainment product of all time. Today, Rockstar returns to Vice City with the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI. More than a decade in the making, with a budget Rockstar has never disclosed but analysts put between $1 billion and $2 billion, the game follows Jason Duval, an ex-soldier, and Lucia Caminos, who phones him from prison, as they’re pulled into a criminal conspiracy across a send-up of Miami. Its first trailer has been viewed more than 300 million times on YouTube, and footage premiering on Netflix nearly crashed the streamer’s servers. Take-Two Interactive, Rockstar’s parent organization, expects record revenue of $8.1 billion this fiscal year.

For longtime fans, GTA 6 marks something more personal: the first game in the franchise since the original band behind it broke up. Following the departure of Donovan and other central figures, Dan Houser exited Rockstar in 2020, leaving his big brother Sam as the last founder still there. With Sam remaining notoriously quiet about the fresh game, I went digging through my old tapes and found my long-lost interview with gaming’s original punk rockers. During our two-hour talk, the young co-founders were brash, unrepentant, and championing a lifetime of obsessions, as Sam put it. “I wasn’t just obsessed about games,” he told me, “we were obsessed about everything—obsessive about music, obsessive about film, obsessive about fashion.”.
