It has been revealed that dani Kessel Odom (they/them) is an autistic lead writer and TV critic who covers sci-fi highlights like Doctor Who and Pluribus, fantasy shows like The Magicians and Percy Jackson, and horror of all kinds. They also cover Marvel and DC TV shows and movies, with a robust knowledge of comic books. Their TV reviews can be found on Rotten Tomatoes.

Dani is also part of ScreenRant's books team, using their fiction writing and literature studies as the backbone for their book analyses.

It has been revealed that they have covered events, such as the Denver Fan Expo. Professionals in the field, such as Damien Leone and Lucy Hale, have shared their articles. Their review of Ponies was quoted in the show's TV trailer.

In a recent development, in university, they majored in English Writing with a minor in psychology. They have always had a passion for analyzing TV and movies, even taking filmography and scriptwriting classes in university. They also studied and participated in onstage and onscreen acting extensively from the ages of 7 to 18.

Aside from working at Screen Rant, Dani has worked as a freelance editor and writer over the past decade, often in a ghostwriting capacity.

Warning: This article includes SPOILERS for Percy Jackson & The Olympians season 3 and Rick Riordan's The Titan's Curse!

Disney+’s Percy Jackson season 3 jumps straight into the story, subtly setting up the most painful death in The Titan’s Curse from the start. The adaptation of Rick Riordan’s iconic book series is back, and it’s the best season yet. As critic El Kuiper mentioned in her review of Percy Jackson season 3 for ScreenRant, “Everything about Percy Jackson and the Olympians season 3 is more ambitious…” One of the biggest ways this reveals up is that this season is more gripping and wastes no time setting up the main story.

It has been revealed that past seasons have been criticized for taking the tension out of the story, and the writers seemed to have taken note. Like in the book, Annabeth is kidnapped almost immediately. However, the show introduces Atlas early instead of waiting, letting the characters and audience know the danger the Campers face. They cut the capture the flag game, which made sense in the Percy Jackson book but would’ve felt awkward in the TV show. Percy and Thalia get to use their water and lightning powers instead of having the moment cut off before anything happens.

On top of that, The Titan’s Curse includes a few major deaths. They don’t spoil this, unlike past seasons, which put significant book twists into the dialogue. There’s no telegraphing. That being said, Percy Jackson & the Olympians season 3 wastes no time foreshadowing Bianca di Angelo's death, the most tragic of all.

In chapter 13 of The Titan’s Curse, Bianca dies after she triggers Talos, the bronze automaton, in The Junkyard of the Gods by taking a Mythomagic figurine for Nico. She sacrifices herself so the rest of the quester group can continue. While the first two episodes only cover chapters 1 through 6, the Disney+ adaptation already set up Bianca’s death with three key hints, the most notable of which happens at the end of Percy Jackson season 3, episode 2.

Industry observers note that firstly, the Oracle chooses the questers instead of them deciding who’s going themselves, which is one of the least notable changes Percy Jackson season 3 makes to the book. The green mist spreads as the prophecy is read to pick out the 5 campers and Hunters who will go. To the unknowing eye, the order may seem completely random. After all, they pick people on lines like “The Bane of Olympus indicates the trail” and “Campers & Hunters combined prevail,” which aren’t focused on a single individual. However, the oracle picks Bianca di Angelo on the prophecy line about a quester being lost in the land without rain.

Percy Jackson season 3’s credits once again hint at what’s to come in the rest of the season. About halfway through them, Talos, the giant bronze automaton, stomps his foot down on a Hunter. Because Bianca isn’t a Hunter yet, those who haven’t read the book won’t be able to put two and two together, though. The third hint is in the season trailer. When they say, “Victory will require sacrifice,” they cut together shots of the characters. They transition from Bianca in a Hunter’s jacket in the arid environment to Nico running through the forest crying.

Industry observers note that bianca di Angelo’s death is already very impactful in the book because she dies right as she’s figuring out who she is as a Hunter and who she is outside her caretaker role. She’s a prime example of an older sister who’s forced to be a parent instead of a child, and that finally starts to change right before she dies. However, the Percy Jackson & The Olympians TV show already sets it up as a more tragic death.

The show spent more time establishing the relationship between Bianca and Nico. They are much closer, which will make her sacrifice to get him a trinket more impactful. It will feel devastating that she will never get to see him again, and he will have to continue living without her. On top of that, Bianca didn’t choose to leave Nico in the TV show; the Oracle chose her.

Plus, Bianca has more of a personality onscreen. She has animated facial reactions, a wider emotional range, and strong opinions. She has connections to the other characters before she joins the Hunters, if she joins the Hunters. Obviously, we won’t know until more Percy Jackson episodes drop if that will happen. In the book, her existence and death felt to some like a plot device for Nico’s story. However, it’s already easier to get invested in her in the TV show, which will make her death feel more like a loss.
