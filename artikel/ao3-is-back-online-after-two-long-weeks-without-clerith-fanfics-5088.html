Sources indicate that the internet has spent the past two and a half weeks deprived of many vital things, including easy access to stories in which Cloud Strife and Aerith Gainsborough get the ending their readers wanted. Fortunately for Clerith fans, Archive of Our Own (AO3) is finally back online.

Sources indicate that the volunteer-run fanfiction archive returned on Sunday, November 15, after going offline on October 29. Its operator, the Organization for Transformative Works (OTW), indicates a security incident prompted the extended shutdown. AO3 is usable again, although it hasn’t quite returned to normal.

For anyone who hasn’t spent much time on AO3, it’s a huge collection of fan-written stories covering games, movies, books, television, and just about any other fictional universe you can name.

Industry observers note that some stories fill gaps in the original work; others toss canon aside altogether. In Final Fantasy 7’s case, the apparently immortal debate about Cloud’s love life is practically built for it. Even Final Fantasy 7 Rebirth director Naoki Hamaguchi refuses to settle the Aerith-or-Tifa question.

OTW indicates it was able to reopen the archive before the far end of its four-week recovery estimate by switching off a handful of features. User and collection icons currently don’t appear, importing works from other websites is unavailable, and the only download option is HTML. No icons were deleted; they just aren’t being shown yet.

Traffic is another problem. AO3 has warned that some people will see slow loading times or even the maintenance page as it manages demand. Refreshing and trying again is the recommendation, although it may take a little patience to get in.

According to recent reports, initially, the October 29 outage looked like ordinary maintenance. The situation changed when OTW disclosed evidence of a security incident and warned that recovery could take between two and four weeks. It has brought in a security firm to review the incident, but isn’t detailing its fixes while that work is underway.

Recent findings suggest that the return also comes just after AO3’s seventeenth anniversary. Not a particularly fun way to celebrate, but at least readers can now get back to their bookmarks.

According to recent reports, that pause will remain in place until November 29. The catch is that dates shown on existing drafts may still reflect their original deadlines, not the extension. Writers are being asked to review unfinished work and save or publish it before automatic deletion resumes.

OTW also notes published works, bookmarks, kudos, and other archive data remain intact. That’s the good news. The security side is less settled, though. The organization says it has no reason to think its user database was compromised, but it can’t be ruled out.

It recommends an AO3 password change, as well as on other sites if you’ve reused your AO3 credentials there. You can follow further updates on the official AO3 status page.

According to recent reports, look, you don’t need to have read a single fanfic to understand the appeal. Games ask you to spend dozens, sometimes hundreds, of hours with fictional people. Once the credits roll, it’s hardly surprising that some players want to spend more time with them, or imagine things going differently.

The Final Fantasy 7 fandom is an obvious example. We have covered how central Tifa and Aerith are to Rebirth’s story, while a separate guide to Aerith’s relationship options shows how much attention players can pay to Cloud’s relationships. The official story gives players scenes and choices to dissect. Fanfiction lets them carry the argument somewhere else, without waiting for Square Enix to settle anything.

Recent findings suggest that beyond the romance, fans have been reimagining Final Fantasy 7’s cast in entirely different art styles for years. Writing an alternate ending or inventing a crossover isn’t so different in spirit. AO3 simply gives that kind of work a home, a tag system, and readers who actually go looking for it.

After 17 days of an error page, that home is accessible again. If you left a story half-read on October 28, there’s finally a chance to find your place. Just don’t be surprised if everyone else had the same idea.
