Kilmar Abrego Garcia lands in Madrid, Spain, after leaving the United States voluntarily amid a deportation fight.

Kilmar Ábrego García, the illegal immigrant from El Salvador whose case became a flashpoint for critics of President Donald Trump's immigration enforcement policies, landed in Spain on Wednesday after agreeing to leave the United States.

Ábrego García departed Tuesday for Madrid on a commercial flight and was swarmed with supporters and journalists after touching down and making his way through the airport.

His voluntary departure ends his battle with the Trump administration that began last year with his initial deportation over allegations that he was a member of the MS-13 gang. He ultimately aims to make his way to Mexico, The New York Times reported, citing his attorneys.

In a recent development, simon Sandoval-Moshenberg, one of Ábrego García's lawyers, said in a statement late Tuesday that this is a "temporary departure" for his client, who has left the U.S. as a "free man.".

TRUMP’S SPECIAL STRATEGY TO DEPORT ILLEGALS GIVES HIM ANOTHER TOOL FOR ENFORCEMENT.

Kilmar Ábrego García, the El Salvadoran national that the Trump administration has tried to deport for nearly two years, is swarmed by reporters after landing in Madrid, Spain, on Nov. 25, 2026. (Oscar Del Pozo / AFP via Getty Images).

Sources indicate that "Mr. Abrego Garcia has many options to return to the United States as the husband of a U.S. citizen and the father of a U.S. citizen child. We don't expect that to happen under this administration, which we know will never give him a fair shake, but we expect that the next administration will prioritize reunifying this family and bringing him back to Maryland," Sandoval-Moshenberg mentioned.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin celebrated Ábrego García leaving the U.S., maintaining that he is a gang member and a criminal.

Sources indicate that "We are delighted to announce that Kilmar Abrego Garcia has self-deported from the United States to Spain," Mullin said in a statement Tuesday night. "We promised the American people that this human trafficker, wife beater, gang member, and child predator would not remain in our country. Now, this El Salvadoran man cannot terrorize innocent Americans.".

Karoline Leavitt, formerly the White House press secretary, noted Wednesday that Ábrego García leaving the country has "vindicated" the Trump administration.

Recent findings suggest that "This was an individual who illegally entered our country, who has a very long and deep criminal record, by the way, and deserves and needs to be deported," Leavitt told Fox News host Rachel Campos-Duffy. "Kudos to the president, the Department of Homeland Security and Stephen Miller, who have been adamant about what's doing what's right.".

Industry observers note that A federal judge in Maryland ordered Ábrego García to leave the U.S. by Tuesday, according to two sources familiar with the matter. That order remains under seal, and the exact terms of his release were not immediately clear.

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) emphasized that Ábrego García did not receive any financial incentives through the Trump administration's voluntary self-deportation program, which offers migrants travel assistance and financial support to leave the country.

"To be clear: Kilmar Abrego Garcia departed the United States pursuant to a removal order. He paid for his own flight and received no incentives," DHS posted on X. "He will NEVER be allowed back inside the United States.".

DHS also rejected descriptions of Ábrego García as a Maryland resident.

Sources indicate that "Kilmar Abrego Garcia is not and will never be a Maryland Man—he is a criminal illegal alien from El Salvador and was a public safety threat to American citizens. He is not coming back, despite the efforts of sanctuary politicians who spent massive amounts of time and money defending this low-life," the department said.

According to recent reports, the move comes after sustained efforts by the Trump administration to remove Ábrego García, who entered the U.S. illegally more than a decade ago. At one point during the legal proceedings, federal authorities sought to deport him to several African nations, one of them being Liberia.

According to recent reports, ábrego García had been living in Maryland with his wife and children when immigration authorities detained him in March 2025. He was subsequently deported to El Salvador in error and held in the Central American nation's maximum-security CECOT prison despite a standing court order barring his removal there due to the risk of gang persecution.

Democrats rallied around him despite his suspected ties to the MS-13 gang.

It has been revealed that "A year and a half ago, the Trump Administration wanted to leave Kilmar Ábrego García to rot in a Salvadoran gulag. Today, Kilmar is traveling to a country of his choice, a free man — unharmed and with dignity," said Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who visited Ábrego García while detained in El Salvador.

"While the Trump Administration had been pursuing their vindictive prosecution against Kilmar and desperately trying to ship him against his will to countries known for human rights abuses, the Administration was forced to come to this agreement that respects Kilmar’s will and the due process rights guaranteed to him and to all in our nation," Van Hollen added. "I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free – including from this Administration’s constant persecution.".

Recent findings suggest that federal officials eventually returned him to the U.S. to face human smuggling charges from a 2022 traffic stop in Tennessee. He pleaded not guilty to the charges, which a judge later dismissed.
