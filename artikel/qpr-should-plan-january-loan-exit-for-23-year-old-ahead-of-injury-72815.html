QPR should plan January loan exit for 23-year-old ahead of injury comeback: Opinion

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that QPR didn’t touch their attacking ranks in the summer transfer window. Of their six signings, two were goalkeepers, three were full-backs, and one was a central midfielder.

Despite this, Julien Stephan has a deep group of forwards to choose from. Injuries have tested the Frenchman’s options at Loftus Road, but with as many as 11 players capable of operating in attacking roles, the Hoops have good depth and versatility to work with.

It can lead to reduced opportunities for some players though, and Alfie Lloyd has met that fate with Rayan Kolli reintegrated and Rumarn Burrell returning from injury. Lloyd is sidelined himself with a hamstring matter set to keep him out until 2027, but when January rolls around, a loan exit could be best for the 23-year-old.

Lloyd has played 40 times for the QPR first team since joining the club as a youngster in 2021. He progressed with the development squad and spent time out on loan at Eastleigh before becoming a more regular feature on the senior scene.

But, after signing the likes of Burrell and Richard Kone in the summer of 2025, Lloyd was loaned out. A stint at Leyton Orient didn’t have much fruit to bear, but he had a better spell at Lincoln City over the second half of the season.

At Sincil Bank, Lloyd offered a similar presence to the one he has with the Hoops. He’s a hard-working and powerful forward who is effective at running into channels, drifting across the front three and stretching the defence. It means he can really disrupt games off the bench, be it as a striker or winger.

But, with Burrell and Kone fit, Kolli back in the senior squad and Justin Obikwu lurking, opportunities at striker will be limited for Lloyd. The same goes for the left-hand side, as the fit-again Ilias Chair joins Koki Saito and Paul Smyth as options on that flank.

In a recent development, aged 23, Lloyd needs to be playing regularly, and not for the development squad. A loan back down to League One level would give the forward the game time he needs to grow.

Depth is key in a Championship play-off push, and there’s no reason why QPR can’t target such heights.

At striker and on both flanks, players returning from injuries mean that the R’s have no shortage of options. Cover is essential, of course, but it could be that the likes of Lloyd and Obikwu are left on the outskirts.

Once Lloyd returns, he will be eager to get back into the action. He has a better chance of doing so away from Loftus Road and will have no shortage of admirers eager to bring him in.
