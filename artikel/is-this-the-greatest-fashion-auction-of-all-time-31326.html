Is This the Greatest Fashion Auction of All Time?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that who owns four vintage Dior Maasai necklaces? Not very many people, except for, of course, one of Paris’s most mysterious women and deeply devoted collectors of fashion. The anonymous French clotheshorse is now the subject of an upcoming multipart, 3,000-piece sale at Maurice Auction. “It’s not someone who is known by the industry, it’s just someone who was very wealthy and that could afford such a collection as a true and passionate collector for more than 30 years,” Salomé Pirson, co-founder of Maurice Auction, tells ELLE.

Sources indicate that dubbed the Infinite Wardrobe, the multipart auction contains haute couture, ready-to-wear, handbags, and accessories from the likes of Chanel, Dior, Hermès, Valentino, Roberto Cavalli, Fendi, and Ungaro. The Infinite Wardrobe will kick off with a live auction on November 18 in Paris, followed by eight different online-only auctions sorted by brand or subject (Chanel, Italian fashion, Dior, bags, for instance). Even with the growing interest in auctions recently spurred by a younger demographic, it’s certainly one of the most extensive fashion auctions that has happened in recent years. Earlier this year, Maurice Auction broke records when it sold rare haute couture from the collection of couture queen Mouna Ayoub.

It has been revealed that much of the Infinite Wardrobe is unworn or only worn once, pristinely preserved like a time capsule for decades in one closet. Some of it even still has tags attached. “What’s unusual is the volume,” indicates Marie-Laurence Tixier, co-founder of Maurice Auction. “The client was really someone who collected, and when she liked a look or a pair of shoes, she would buy all the colors.” For the auction, that means Hermès Oran sandals in every shade of the rainbow and every iteration of vintage Chanel Gripoix necklaces. Some of the items will be sold in more than a dozen variations.

When the duo first encountered the collection, it was stored in a single Parisian apartment, all exhibited by color, type of accessory, and brand. “I’ve heard that people, when they went to have dinner at her place, they visited the closet, because it was a piece of art itself,” adds Tixier. It’s by far the biggest auction the company has ever done since it was founded in 2022, and also the biggest undertaking of their careers in terms of cataloging, researching, and organizing all of the pieces. “It’s like going into a fantasy closet where you realize it’s not gonna be a dream, it’s gonna be a real job for three months,” says Tixier. The rarest items were researched in collaboration with Kerry Taylor, fashion’s favorite London auctioneer.

If you love designer fashion, it’s a great opportunity to pick up a vintage luxury just before the holidays. But for the real fashion freaks, there are some ultra-rare gems that rarely come to market, too. Think: Jean Paul Gaultier-designed Hermès crocodile jackets, Emanuel Ungaro haute couture, and incredible, lightweight vintage Fendi furs. “She had exquisite taste, it’s very elegant, very, very refined,” says Tixier. “There are no logos.”.

Recent findings suggest that roberto Cavalli beaded trousers, Christian Dior by Gianfranco Ferré mini bustle dresses, and Gianni Versace embroidered jackets are just a few of the treasures available in the first part of the auction. In other words: the dream closet is real, and soon, it will be up for grabs.
