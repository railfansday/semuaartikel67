AI-powered trading tools are gradually becoming the default interface for Web3 trading, new research reveals.

A fresh report from Bitrue Research Institute tracks how trading bots have shifted from specialist software for technical people into everyday infrastructure across crypto markets.

The research paper combines public GitHub data on open-source projects, a survey of Bitrue users, and profiles of eight active bots to show where automation is heading.

Public GitHub activity reveals a clear change in focus. New repositories describing themselves as “trading agents” jumped from 110 in 2024 to 1,133 in 2025 and reached 6,121 through November 15, 2026, more than five times the prior year’s total.

Classic crypto trading bot projects also grew sharply, rising from 551 in 2024 to 2,399 in 2025 (a 4.4× increase) with another 2,948 created so far in 2026.

The longest-running open-source tools still dominate stars: Freqtrade (56,933), Hummingbot (21,231), Jesse (8,807), and OctoBot (6,951).

An industry survey of 3,708 crypto traders published by MEXC in October 2026 found that 84.8% had used AI for research or trading in the previous six months, with 53.1% doing so at least weekly.

Among AI users, 34.6% had used an exchange’s own strategies or bots and 17.1% had connected an external agent.

On one major multi-exchange platform, average daily bot launches rose 29% from July to September 2026, reaching 862 per day, with real-fund launches up 36% and DCA bots up 80%.

Recent findings suggest that in Bitrue’s survey of owners who had not yet tried an AI trading bot, concern about losses and risk control was the top reason for hesitation (45.5%). Other common reasons included not trading futures yet (27.3%) and simply not knowing the feature existed (22.7%).

Industry observers note that willingness to let AI trade rose from 55% when only notifications were offered to 71.8% when limits on funds, positions, leverage, and losses were in place. Only 6.5% of respondents said they would allow AI to control more than half their account.

In a recent development, among non-consumers, the most requested first steps were a clearer explanation of what the bot does (27.3%) and a trial reward for the first trade (22.7%). Half of those who had not tried an AI bot noted they were ready to do so; another 36.4% wanted to learn more first.

According to recent reports, users who had already tried Bitrue AI most often cited time saved from watching charts, automatic execution, and market analysis as the main benefits. Requests for improvement centered on more trading pairs, flexible parameters & alerts tied to indicators such as RSI.

According to recent reports, AI automation continues to expand, but adoption still hinges on control. Crypto traders want to see why a strategy was chosen, know the maximum loss, test it first, and understand connection security and costs.

Tools that explain their reasoning, enforce user-set boundaries, and allow small or reversible starts are the ones positioned to convert interest into regular use. The report does not forecast specific outcomes beyond these observed trends.

According to recent reports, the numbers show both rapid builder activity and cautious but growing trader demand. The gap between the two is being closed by guardrails, explanations, and simpler interfaces rather than by handing full control to AI.
