Breaking: Capcom dinosaur shooter Exoprimal's servers will go extinct next year, as shutdown meteor looms

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Exoprimal, Capcom's 2023 team shooter pitting exosuited fighters against T-rexes, is the latest game to be handed an expiration date. Its online services will cease as of late September 2027, rendering it unplayable, with the game set to be delisted from stores in March.

It has been revealed that capcom announced as much in a post running through all of the permutations of the shutdown, which is coming on September 29th 2027 at 8AM PDT. "We cannot thank you enough for the love and support you have shown over the years, and we sincerely apologize for the gravity of this announcement," they wrote.

The publishers have already stopped selling Exoprimal's seasonal survival passes, with plans to pull the game itself and all DLC from sale as of March 30th 2027. Those who already own Exoprimal will be able to re-download it in the time between it being pulled from stores and the server shutdown in September, meaning there's plenty of time to hop in for a final few rounds of dino shooting if you fancy.

It's long seemed like only a matter of time before the dino shooter was taken offline, with Capcom having stopped releasing new seasonal additions to it all the way back in July 2024. So, this seems more like a natiral petering out than an instance of an online game being cut down in its prime or not long after launch, as we've seen recently with extreme cases like Highguard. As such, one would hope there's a chance plenty of Exoprimal devs have already been redirected onto other Capcom games, minimising the amount who might be left in the often precarious position of having nothing immediate to work on following this shutdown.

In a recent development, it's fair to say Ed Thorn (RPS in peace) wasn't a fan of Exoprimal on reviewing it at launch. "I thought Exoprimal somehow managed to make dinosaurs versus mechs, aka the coolest concept ever, boring in my review-in-progress,"he wrote. "I held out hope it would improve, though, as lots of commenters assured me it's fun side would finally surface. "Just play several more hours and things really open up! Variety in the dinos, in the modes, in just about everything!". To some extent, they were right. There are fun moments to be had as you queue up for Dino Survival and suddenly, remarkably, things have changed a bit.

"But the game doesn't respect your time. It forces you to sit through hours of repetitive dino-slaying and then only offers you the chance, the chance to slay dinos in a way that isn't excruciatingly samey. Even as someone who finds the fun in mindless carnage, I'd much rather ditch my mech suit and let the dinosaurs run rampant. The dinosaurs don't deserve this.".

That's not exactly as positive a eulogy as the one offered by Capcom, but not all games are destined to stick the landing and be universally beloved mega-hits, especially those wading into the live service maelstrom.
