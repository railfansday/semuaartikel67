It Looks Like Canon Has a Hit on Its Hands With the EOS R8 Mark II

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Yodobashi Camera, one of Japan’s largest photo retailers, has shared its latest camera sales charts, and the results are great news for Canon.

Recent findings suggest that yodobashi’s new sales charts cover the second half of October — Yodobashi, unlike Map Camera, does biweekly sales recaps. While sales charts for an individual photo retail chain in Japan do not necessarily capture the behavior of the global market, Yodobashi caters primarily to enthusiast and professional customers, so the results are always interesting and, frankly, some of the only data that the industry has access to anyway.

In a recent development, on the camera side of things, the Canon EOS R8 Mark II made its debut in Japanese stores. Canon’s upcoming “entry-level” full-frame camera, which PetaPixel really likes, struck a chord with customers, too.

In a recent development, as CAPA Camera Web reports, the R8 II went on sale in Japan only on October 29, giving it just three days to surge up the sales charts to the No. 2 position, just behind the Sony a7 V. As PetaPixel has reported numerous times, the Sony a7 V has been an extremely successful and popular camera in its first year. Perhaps the radically redesigned, impressive R8 II could knock the a7 V off its perch with more time on store shelves.

It has been revealed that it is worth noting, though, that Yodobashi separates body-only sales and kit sales in its charts. The Canon EOS R8 II body finished second, but the kit with the RF 24-105mm f/4-7.1 IS STM lens was in fourth, and the kit with Canon's popular, budget-friendly RF 45mm f/1.2 lens was in the ninth spot. Perhaps if all R8 II configurations were counted together, the a7 V would have lost its top spot after all.

It has been revealed that both Sony and Canon scored other cameras on the list, including the a7C II for Sony and the EOS R10 and EOS R50 for Canon. The Fujifilm X-M5 kit with the XC 15-45mm f/3.5-5.6 finished third, and the X-T30 III with the XC 13-33mm f/3.5-6.3 landed in sixth.

Recent findings suggest that yodobashi also tracks compact camera sales separately. While overall digital camera sales are slumping a bit these days, the industry is buoyed by the growing popularity of compact cameras.

One of the most popular models overall is still the Canon PowerShot G7X Mark III. Despite launching in 2019, the G7X III continues to be a hot commodity. It is routinely sold out, but that didn't stop it from topping Yodobashi's compact camera sales charts.

According to recent reports, canon scored another big win with the PowerShot SX740 HS in second place. These are both trendy cameras that have found an audience among younger photographers. Canon had all but pulled out of the compact camera market but recently decided to cautiously reenter the space. The company's financial success is being driven in a big way by point-and-shoot cameras, so it's no surprise Canon wants to ramp up production again.

Recent findings suggest that the next camera on the list is very different: the Ricoh GR IV. While cameras like the G7X III and SX740 HS are popular with a nontraditional photography audience, the GR IV is a photographer's camera through and through. It targets a more premium price point and offers modern, high-end features.

The GR IV's upcoming 30th Anniversary Edition Kit landed in the sixth spot, by the way.

The rest of Yodobashi's compact camera top 10 is visible on CAPA Camera Web. And yes, the Fujifilm X100VI, which is still frequently sold out despite launching nearly three years ago, made the cut. Barely.
