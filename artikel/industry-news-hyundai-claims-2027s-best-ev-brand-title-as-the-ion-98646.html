Industry News: Hyundai claims 2027’s ‘Best EV Brand’ title as the IONIQ 5 and IONIQ 9 impress

According to recent reports, hyundai’s electric SUVs are getting some love. The IONIQ 5 and three-row IONIQ 9 helped the Korean automaker land its first-ever “Best Electric Vehicle Brand” award from US News & World Report for 2027.

“This win showcases the breadth and strength of Hyundai’s SUV and electric lineups,” Alex Kwanten, managing editor at Autos at US News, stated after naming it the Best EV Brand and Best SUV Brand for 2027.

This marks the first time Hyundai has earned the Best EV Brand award and the third consecutive win for Best SUV Brand.

Including hybrid and electric vehicles, Hyundai offers ten unique SUVs, “many of them at or near the top of our rankings in those classes for capability, comfort, performance and value,” Kwanten remarked.

Hyundai’s fully electric SUVs, the IONIQ 5 and IONIQ 9, “in particular, stand out for their combination of style and technology,” he explained.

Hyundai currently offers the IONIQ 5, including the sporty IONIQ 5 N and off-road-focused IONIQ 5 XRT, plus the three-row IONIQ 9.

Although Hyundai discontinued its electric sedan, the IONIQ 6, after the 2025 model year, the upcoming high-performance IONIQ 6 N joins the lineup in 2027.

It has been revealed that the IONIQ 5 continued to earn praise, taking its third consecutive Best Compact Electric SUV award. Its larger sibling, the IONIQ 9, took the top spot in the midsize electric SUV category.

Hyundai’s IONIQ 5 is the most popular EV in the US this year through September that isn’t a Tesla, ranking third behind the Model Y and Model 3.

Sources indicate that why does it continue winning over buyers? The IONIQ 5 checks all the boxes with long-range capabilities, fast charging, and a surprisingly spacious interior with modern tech.

According to recent reports, it’s also among the most affordable EVs in the US. Prices for the 2027 Hyundai IONIQ 5 start at just $35,250.

In a recent development, however, the base price is for the standard-range IONIQ 5 SE with an EPA-estimated range of up to 245 miles. Upgrading to the extended-range SE RWD model costs an extra $2,500, with prices starting at $37,750, but it offers up to 318 miles of range.

The three-row Hyundai IONIQ 9 electric SUV starts at $58,955 with an EPA-estimated range of up to 335 miles.

Sources indicate that hyundai’s IONIQ 5 is about the size of a Tesla Model Y, with two rows of seating and space for five passengers. Hyundai’s larger IONIQ 9 offers three rows of seating with six- or seven-seat options.

The larger IONIQ 9 offers up to 163.54 cubic feet of interior passenger volume, compared with up to 106.5 cubic feet in the IONIQ 5.

Both electric SUVs use an 800-volt electrical architecture for ultra-fast charging. The IONIQ 5 can charge from 10% to 80% in about 20 minutes, while the IONIQ 9 takes around 24 minutes using a 350 kW DC fast charger.

Hyundai assembles the IONIQ 5 and IONIQ 9 at the Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America outside of Savannah, Georgia.

When we toured the facility earlier this year, Hyundai told Electrek that its electric SUVs are winning over upcoming buyers at a higher rate than any of its gas or hybrid vehicles.

The IONIQ 5 had a 69.8% conquest rate last year, while the IONIQ 9 had 64.3%. Hyundai’s Elantra N ranked third (61.9%), followed by the Palisade (61.2%) and Kona Electric (60.9%).
