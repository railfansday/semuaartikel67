Despite being shipped off to Germany’s finest finishing school for young English talent, Ethan Nwaneri’s time at Borussia Dortmund is already at a crossroads.

The 19-year-old joined the Bundesliga side on a season-long loan in September after enduring a frustrating five-month spell at Marseille earlier in the year.

This was supposed to be the loan move to reinvigorate one of England’s best young forwards, at a club well-versed in doing exactly that.

Nwaneri became the fifth Englishman aged 21 or under to sign for Dortmund in the past decade, following in the footsteps of Jadon Sancho, Jude and Jobe Bellingham, and Jamie Gittens. The stars seemed aligned for Nwaneri to shine.

According to recent reports, but the opposite has happened. Having impressed and forced a Hoffenheim own goal off the bench on his Dortmund debut, Nwaneri started the team’s next four Bundesliga matches, but was hooked off around the hour mark in each one. The teenager has not made a starting XI since a goalless draw with Union Berlin on October 17.

Industry observers note that it is understood Arsenal – who host Dortmund in the Champions League tonight – are closely monitoring the situation around Nwaneri’s lack of minutes, and there is a January recall clause in his contract. The clause was initially inserted as insurance in case an attacking injury crisis arose at Arsenal. Nwaneri's lack of minutes wasn't expected.

Ethan Nwaneri is on loan at Borussia Dortmund from Arsenal but has struggled for minutes – he last started a match on October 17.

The 19-year-old is one of the most talented youngsters to emerge from Arsenal's prolific Hale End academy, but his career is in danger of stalling on loan in Germany.

So why isn’t his move at Dortmund, a club that made their name by developing young players, working out?

Well, Nwaneri's short career so far has been a complicated one, featuring enormous highs and challenging hurdles. To understand his trajectory, you have to look back at his time at Hale End.

Having risen through the academy rapidly upon joining aged eight, Nwaneri burst onto the first-team scene in September 2022 against Brentford, becoming the youngest player to appear in the Premier League, aged 15 years and 181 days.

Mikel Arteta still values the player but hinted on Monday that he needs to produce more than just ‘moments’.

‘You can have patches, but to find that consistency and to play a significant amount of minutes and have a huge impact in big football clubs like Arsenal or Dortmund, it's extremely difficult,’ he mentioned.

According to recent reports, ‘That's the reality. You can just shine in moments, but moments are not enough. Now you need to show the consistency. Ethan has the capacity and the mentality to do that, and it takes some time.’.

There's a feeling at Arsenal that Nwaneri has time on his side and shouldn’t be written off. He signed a five-year deal in August 2025 after all, with a plan to fully integrate the attacker into the first-team squad. His brother Emerson, 16, is thriving with the Under 18s.

However, if Nwaneri were to be sold in future, he would, as an academy graduate, generate pure profit on Arsenal's accounts.

‘His time at Borussia has been a disappointment so far because he was brought in to help immediately after Giannis Konstantelias tore his ACL,’ says Sebastian Wessling, who has covered Dortmund for 15 years at the Funke Media Group.

‘They tried to bring Nwaneri in, the summer before. So having tracked and spoken to him previously, they thought he would be ideal for short-term help. But he really hasn’t been that, it’s not worked out well so far.

‘He’s found it difficult to contribute with goals, assists, or just generally in Dortmund’s offensive game.

According to recent reports, ‘What the coach is looking for is a player to make a lot of runs in behind. Whereas Nwaneri looks to drop deep and have time on the ball, which the manager, Niko Kovac, isn’t looking for.’.

'He lacks confidence and I think that shows in his play,' says Dortmund expert Sebastian Wessling of Nwaneri.

There is still a serious player in Nwaneri, as seen in his two assists for England Under 21s in their 3-2 victory over France last week.

Nwaneri's lack of confidence and progress feel striking. This summer, he joined England's pre-World Cup training camp in Florida, and was an unused substitute in the warm-up match against New Zealand. Nwaneri was alongside Bournemouth's Alex Scott, Fulham's Josh King and Liverpool's Rio Ngumoha – both Scott and Ngumoha have since made their senior England debuts, while King made Thomas Tuchel's squad this month. All three are flourishing as Nwaneri struggles to even feature.

It has been revealed that to make matters worse for him, back at Arsenal 16-year-old Max Dowman is competing for a place in the starting XI. Nwaneri may have bundles of talent, but he is yet to fully kick on.

Wessling adds: ‘He lacks confidence and I think that demonstrates in his play. Niko wants him to be brave on the pitch, but he plays it very safe.

‘When he did feature, he often played short passes and didn’t advance Dortmund’s play. He’s not a total disappointment, but he hasn’t really contributed.’.

Recent findings suggest that there is still a serious player in Nwaneri, as seen in his two assists for England Under 21s in their 3-2 victory over France last week. He is eligible to feature at the Emirates tonight, and will be desperate to show Arsenal what they are missing.

Recent findings suggest that in any case, his next two months at Dortmund will reveal plenty about his future. It’s now about consistency – more than mere ‘moments’.
