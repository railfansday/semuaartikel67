SpaceX set a new record with its latest satellite launch out of California.

According to recent reports, A Falcon 9 rocket lifted off from Vandenberg Space Force Base's Launch Complex-4E on Sunday (Nov. 22) at 7:23 a.m. ET (1223 GMT; 4:23 a.m. local time in California), on a mission for the U.S. National Reconnaissance Office (NRO) called NROL-136.

It was the second launch from that pad in a little over 32 hours, after the USSF-317 mission for the U.S. Space Force. And that quick turnaround made history.

This smashed the previous record of 45 hrs from Pad 40. Team got there with several hours of margin. Next goal… the 24 hr turn! 🏎️🚀🏎️🚀November 22, 2026.

Industry observers note that "Massive high five to the @SpaceX west coast team for a world record launch to launch turn from Pad 4e last night! 32 hr, 3 min, 21 sec! This smashed the previous record of 45 hrs from Pad 40. Team got there with several hours of margin. Next goal… the 24 hr turn!" Kiko Dontchev, SpaceX's vice president of launch, said via X on Sunday. (Pad 40 is at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida.).

It has been revealed that NROL-136 was the sixth launch of 2026 so far for the NRO. All of them have been carried out by SpaceX's workhorse Falcon 9, and all have delivered classified satellites to low Earth orbit.

The launch marked another step in the NRO's effort to expand its intelligence-gathering capabilities through a proliferated network of spacecraft designed to provide more frequent and timely surveillance of locations around the globe.

Industry observers note that about two minutes after liftoff, the Falcon 9's first-stage booster separated from the rocket's upper stage, which continued carrying the classified payloads onward to orbit. The booster then reversed course and returned to Vandenberg, touching down at Landing Zone 4 approximately seven and a half minutes after launch.

Saturday's launch was the 14th flight for this particular Falcon 9 booster, dubbed B1097, which previously debuted NASA's Sentinel-6B ocean-monitoring satellite, NROL-172, USSF-259 for the Space Force, and eight Starlink missions.

NROL-136 was the 15th launch dedicated to building the NRO's "proliferated architecture," the satellites for which have been developed in partnership with SpaceX and Northrop Grumman. The network is designed to increase coverage and improve the "capability and resilience" of U.S. intelligence operations in space, according to the NRO's mission website.

Industry observers note that the NRO began deploying the constellation in May 2024 with the launch of NROL-146. The most recent proliferated architecture mission before Sunday was NROL-179, which lifted off on June 19, 2026.

As with any classified mission, the agency has not disclosed the exact number of satellites aboard NROL-136 or revealed details about their onboard technologies.
