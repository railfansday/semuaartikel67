Emiglia brand identity by Another Collective: a hand-cut red wordmark, script lettering and windowed packs for a Porto pasta maker and bar.

The rebrand unifies two sides of one business, the Pasta Fresca factory and the Pasta Bar, under a single voice. The studio kept the illustrations that were already part of the brand and built a stronger typographic layer around them, so tradition reads as confidence rather than nostalgia. Red does most of the work, a warm tomato red against cream, with deep burgundy as the second voice. The pasta itself demonstrates up everywhere, nests on steel trays, boxes with windows, trays stacked in the fridge, and the identity treats that craft as the subject, not the backdrop.

It has been revealed that the wordmark is the whole idea in one gesture. Chunky, rounded letters with soft terminals and slightly uneven edges, the kind of letterforms that look cut by hand rather than drawn on screen. It sits huge and flat, filling the width of posters and packs, with the round counter of the A and the arched legs of the M giving it a warmth no geometric sans could carry.

The order never changes. Wordmark first, script second, small caps last, and the same order repeats on boxes, menus, bags and tees.

Sources indicate that the packaging puts the pasta on display. Red boxes carry a clear window so the shapes inside do the talking, with a small illustrated label on each box. Foil trays stack in the fridge under bold red labels, and the same red runs across paper bags, sauce jars and the weekly menu posters.
