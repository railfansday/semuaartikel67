The audience score is 57%, and based on my Rotten Tomatoes math, that means there's a 43% chance the movie is good.

If you're going to skip one Thursday night game this year, this is probably the one you want to skip. The Colts are in a free fall, and at 3-7, there's a chance their head coach could be fired before the season is over. On the other hand, the Texans (3-6) have been one of the most disappointing teams in the NFL this year.

This is the second meeting of the year between these two teams, with the Colts pulling off a 19-17 upset back in Week 3.

Going into Thursday, the Texans will be missing several key players on defense, including Danielle Hunter (hamstring) and Azeez Al-Shaair, who was placed on injured reserve this week due to a groin injury. On the upside, the Texans should be getting David Montgomery back on the field. The running back hasn't played since suffering a groin injury in Week 4.

Every week during the NFL season, we rank all 32 starting quarterbacks, and heading into Week 10, we had a upcoming player on top: Joe Burrow. He remains there for Week 11 as well.

The QB ranking here is handled by Garrett Podell, who breaks down every possible QB stat to figure out who should be on top, and since Burrow leads the NFL in nearly every conceivable stat, it was an easy decision.

Sources indicate that here's a short explanation from Garrett of how his QB ranking works: "These rankings are fluid and designed to tell the story of each starting quarterback's respective rise and/or fall throughout the season." Basically, this ranking isn't based on what any QB has done in the past. It's only based on what they've done this year.

After 10 weeks of action, let's check out the top three names on the list, plus the QB who's ranked dead last:.

Sources indicate that 1. Joe Burrow, Bengals. Burrow is on a historic heater: He is the first player in NFL history with a 70-plus completion percentage and four-plus total touchdowns in five consecutive games. Burrow, Peyton Manning (2004, 2013) and Tom Brady (2007) are the only players in NFL history with 20-plus passing touchdowns in a five-game span. Burrow also leads the NFL in completion percentage (74.7%), passing yards per game (310.6), passing touchdowns (27) and passer rating (117.7). He is the undisputed No. 1 entering Week 11.2. Brock Purdy, 49ers. Purdy continues to produce no matter who is or isn't on the field for the San Francisco 49ers. Purdy leads the league in passing yards per attempt (9.0) and expected points added (EPA) per dropback (0.34) while ranking second in completion percentage (70.1%) and passer rating (115.2). His 0.34 EPA per dropback is the highest since Lamar Jackson's 2019 NFL MVP season, despite having the most missed games by wide receivers (44) in the NFL this season. Purdy cruised against the Dallas Cowboys on Sunday with 389 yards passing and three touchdowns on 22-of-28 passing while adding 21 more yards on the ground on two carries. He is operating Kyle Shanahan's offense with an efficiency that has him at the top of the league. 3. Trevor Lawrence, Jaguars. Lawrence is definitely in the NFL MVP mix. He dominated in Nashville (352 yards passing, three touchdowns and an interception on 25-of-35 passing with 27 rushing yards on four carries) in a Jacksonville Jaguars' 40-23 win. He is tied for third in the NFL in passing touchdowns (21) and No. 2 in yards per pass attempt (8.8). Lawrence also ranks third in the league in passer rating (112.8) and EPA per dropback (0.23) for the 7-2 Jaguars....32. Carson Beck, Cardinals. Beck looked like a rookie making his NFL debut against Aaron Donald and Myles Garrett in a 34-6 Arizona Cardinals defeat. Unfortunately for him, it doesn't get much easier against Steve Spagnuolo's aggressive Chiefs defense in Week 11.

With the Colts getting set to play on "Thursday Night Football," now seems like a good time to talk about who has the hottest seat in the NFL.

According to recent reports, carter Bahns went through and ranked the coaches who are sitting in the hottest seats heading into Week 11, and to no one's surprise, Shane Steichen is on the list. Here are the top three names:.

1. Jets coach Aaron Glenn. Defensive play-calling is Aaron Glenn's self-proclaimed superpower. He touted the New York Jets' potential on that side of the ball when he took over the duties at the start of the year. But after just half the season, his powers ran out. Glenn remarked on Monday that he ceded the play-calling role to defensive coordinator Brian Duker in last week's 31-12 loss to the Buffalo Bills. But if a coach with a so-called superpower cannot fix the defense, then why would a change in play-callers get the job done? It is a desperation move for a coach running out of answers and, frankly, running out of time.

Recent findings suggest that 2. Colts coach Shane Steichen. Steichen is a lame-duck coach now that his GM is gone, and he has fewer tools to work with than he did during the 2-7 start to the year. A difficult road ahead spells trouble. Do not forget, too, that the win they just pulled off came at home by a mere two points against a lowly Dolphins team that was without its starting quarterback. Two months from now, the Colts might look back at that win as their last of the season.

According to recent reports, 3. Cowboys coach Brian Schottenheimer. Offense will never be a problem for Brian Schottenheimer, but the defensive woes that plagued his first season and a half with the Cowboys just keep getting worse. At some point, Jerry Jones has to decide he cannot put up with the inexplicable lapses on that side of the ball, right? Some of that is his own doing and the result of his personnel moves, but a great deal of it falls on Schottenheimer and his assistants. It reached a new low in Week 10 — one that might expedite the process of moving on from Schottenheimer and staff.

For the second time this season, the 49ers are being sent out of the country to play a game, and once again, their travel schedule could be a key factor in the game's outcome.

Industry observers note that back in Week 1, the 49ers were sent to Australia to play the Rams, and the two teams took opposite approaches to travel. The 49ers headed to Melbourne a full week before the game while the Rams demonstrated up just 24 hours before kickoff. By the time the game kicked off, the 49ers were fully acclimated to the 17-hour time difference, and it showed: San Francisco won 27-7.

This week, the 49ers will face the Vikings in Mexico City, a location that creates a unique challenge for NFL teams because it sits at an elevation of about 7,350 feet. To put that in perspective, Denver has the highest altitude of any NFL city at 5,280 feet, over a quarter-mile of elevation below what the 49ers and Vikings will be dealing with in Mexico.

When it comes to getting prepared for the game, the two teams have taken two totally different approaches.
