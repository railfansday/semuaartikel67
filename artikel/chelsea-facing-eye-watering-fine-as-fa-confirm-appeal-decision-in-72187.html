Chelsea have been fined £150,000 as an FA punishment for offensive chanting during last season’s FA Cup game against Charlton was upheld on appeal.

The charges relate to chanting in the 56th minute of January’s 5-1 win at Charlton, which was Liam Rosenior’s first match in charge of the club.

Chelsea admitted the charge but then appealed against the punishment to an independent body, which upheld the initial punishment.

An FA statement on Friday afternoon read: “Chelsea FC has been sanctioned for crowd misconduct in relation to an FA Cup fixture against Charlton Athletic FC.

“It was alleged that around the 56th minute Chelsea FC failed to ensure its spectators and/or supporters (and anyone purporting to be supporters or followers) did not behave in an improper, offensive, abusive or insulting way with either an express or implied reference to religion or belief.

“Chelsea FC admitted the charge and an independent Regulatory Commission imposed an action blueprint, £150,000 fine and warning as to future conduct following a hearing.”.

Industry observers note that the statement continued: “The club subsequently appealed against these sanctions. The appeal was dismissed by an independent Appeal Board and the orders of the Regulatory Commission stand.”.

Chelsea confirmed the verdict in a statement of their own on Friday, adding: “We unequivocally condemn this behaviour. Sectarian abuse – chanting that creates division targeted towards a particular religious or political group – and discriminatory language of any kind has no place in our football club.

“Such behaviour only serves to cause hurt to others and does not represent the values we hold as a club. We want to create a safe and inclusive environment for all, and we have a shared responsibility in delivering this.

Recent findings suggest that “Chelsea takes a zero-tolerance approach towards discriminatory behaviour in whatever form it takes. We will always take steps to identify those responsible and take appropriate action where necessary.

“We understand that further incidents risk additional sanctions against the club and are doing everything we can to inform and educate supporters, as well as develop our existing initiatives to prevent, detect and respond to discrimination in future.

“We know Chelsea fans do not want to see any form of discrimination in our game, and we ask for their support in fostering a welcoming environment at Chelsea matches going forward.”.
