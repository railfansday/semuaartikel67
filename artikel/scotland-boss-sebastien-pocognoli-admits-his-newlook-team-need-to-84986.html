By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER.

Published: 17:30 EST, 15 November 2026 | Updated: 17:30 EST, 15 November 2026.

Sebastien Pocognoli has conceded that he doesn’t have 18 months to demonstrate that Scotland are on the right track under him.

Appointed Steve Clarke’s successor in the summer, the Belgian’s attempts to bring in fresh faces and a new style of play has produced mixed results in his opening five Nations League games.

Following Friday’s win over North Macedonia, John McGinn warned the Tartan Army that it could take as long as 18 months for everything to click into place.

But ahead of the final group match in St Gallen tomorrow evening, the manager acknowledged it’s incumbent on him to accelerate that time frame.

Sources indicate that asked if he felt he had to deliver sooner, he said: ‘Yeah, for sure, but we’re only talking about 18 months because John said that. He could have said 15 months. I don’t know. But, for sure, it needs to be quicker.

Recent findings suggest that ‘In 18 months, it will be the time of the Euros and that is what he meant. We have to go there and be prepared for this in 18 months’ time.

Sebastien Pocognoli  says his Scotland team need to show progress 'game after game'.

‘For sure, game after game, we need to see improvement.

‘Some fresh faces will come back in March. So, step by step we will have more ideas and in one month’s time a lot can change also.’.

McGinn also said that the players were fully behind the manager, reiterating the point previously made by Andy Robertson and Scott McTominay.

‘I can tell you that in the time we have had together the players have been doing well in terms of replicating what I ask them to do,’ the manager stressed.

‘They know that and that’s the most crucial part.

Industry observers note that ‘They buy in. They like it. I think they enjoy it too. You can see when players don’t enjoy it.

‘For sure, when you win it’s more enjoyable, but it would be very bad if they weren’t enjoying it and we were also not winning games.

‘So, right now I think we are working well. We give special attention to the collective.

‘In all the camps, I am very clear about what I expect on the pitch. The players wanted this when I arrived. They wanted clarity. And I think that’s what I have tried to bring.’.

To claim second place in the group, Scotland need to defeat the Swiss and hope that Slovenia don’t win in North Macedonia.

That outcome would ensure that Pocognoli’s men would be a pot two side in next month’s Euro 2028 draw.

Asked what it would take to pull off what would be a stunning win in St Gallen, the manager said: ‘Let’s be brave, but be intelligent.

‘It’s about mentality. They (Switzerland) are a very good team, so we need to be smart. Sometimes they will go through our lines with a low block or a high block as they have the quality.

‘We will try to be intense and strong in the duel because I think that’s the first piece to have a good game.’.

Pocognoli’s side were booed off at half-time on Friday with criticism of the performance persisting despite them going on to win the game.

According to recent reports, but the manager claims he’s oblivious to wider concerns about the performances to date, saying: ‘No, I’m not aware of this. That’s why I was surprised (at the question).

John McGinn suggested that it could take 18 months for Scotland to click under Pocognoli.

Recent findings suggest that ‘Yes, there were some boos at half-time. I think that’s part of it, it’s been like this for years.

‘I know we are colleagues, but I’m not reading the press.

Sources indicate that ‘So, I’m not aware of the criticism. I just want to make the supporters happy.

‘If they aren’t yet happy, we will try to do better.

It has been revealed that ‘There’s pressure in all the jobs with a club. It can be external, it can be internal. It is part of the job.

According to recent reports, ‘I’m very self-critical about myself. So, that’s the first thing. Try to be stable when we win, when we lose.

Sources indicate that ‘I’m always thinking what we can do better. If there’s some criticism, that’s fine.

‘But there there were also a lot of positives after the North Macedonia game.

‘I was the first one to say calm down as we need to see more.’.
