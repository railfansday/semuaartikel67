In a sport obsessed with the next major thing, there is more attention than ever being paid to teenage prodigies who appear destined for the very top.

Recent findings suggest that the latest player to have fans raving is former Manchester United academy star JJ Gabriel, 16, who joined the club aged nine but stunned those at Old Trafford by asking to leave. He is now closing in on a move to Real Madrid.

The acrimonious nature of the teenager's departure has left Gabriel vulnerable to a spectacular fall from grace, particularly after his team released montages showing him joining an agency and saying goodbye to United.

The spotlight is well and truly burning on Gabriel, and he would do well to learn the lessons of other youngsters who were tipped for greatness but ultimately crashed and burned.

Of course, injuries, personal complications and media pressure can all weigh heavily on a young player's prospects. There are countless pitfalls that mean tomorrow's stars often burn out before they have had the chance to shine.

Here, Daily Mail Sport runs the rule over some of the most famous wonderkids who never managed to live up to their potential.

Wonderkid JJ Gabriel, 16, confirmed his exit from Manchester United on Tuesday night.

It has been revealed that in 2004, Freddy Adu was viewed as the future of American football. Dubbed 'The Next Pele', the Ghanaian-born talent was the youngest professional athlete in major American sports when he signed his first contract at just 14.

In a recent development, the hype was extraordinary. He became MLS's highest-paid player, secured a lucrative Nike contract and appeared in an advert alongside Pele himself.

In a recent development, but things never quite worked out for the attacking midfielder. After three years at DC United and Real Salt Lake, he made the much-anticipated move to Europe, joining Benfica for £1million in 2007. It was largely downhill from there.

In a recent development, after making just 11 appearances in three years in Portugal, he moved to Monaco before embarking on a series of unsuccessful and increasingly disappointing loan spells in Portugal, Turkey and Greece. He eventually ended up at Brazilian club Bahia in 2013.

Recent findings suggest that from that point, Adu floated around clubs in Brazil, Serbia, Finland, the US and Sweden. He last played competitive fooball for Swedish third-tier side Osterlen in 2021, aged 32. He remains one of football's biggest 'what if' stories.

Freddy Adu was dubbed 'The Next Pele' but he never fulfilled anywhere near his potential.

Once described by Sir Alex Ferguson as 'the best player I have ever seen at his age', Ravel Morrison is a particularly unfortunate case of an English prospect failing to make the most of an extraordinarily promising career.

As a fresh-faced 17-year-old, he arrived at Carrington with the talent and ability to become one of the world's best players.

He had all the attributes of a world-class attacking midfielder and appeared mature beyond his years on the pitch. Off it, however, was a different story.

In early 2011, barely a year after signing his first professional contract with Manchester United, he was charged with witness intimidation in connection with a domestic violence case.

Since then, although he has never served time in prison, Morrison has faced several further charges, including assault, harassment and fraud. His repeated off-field troubles ultimately derailed a career that had once promised so much.

Morrison has gone on to represent 15 clubs during a career spanning 16 years, failing to fulfil the promise that once enchanted Ferguson. He is currently plying his trade in the second tier of UAE football.

Ravel Morrison was one of the most talented players to come through Man United's academy.

The first Brazilian on this list is former Manchester City forward Robinho. Having come through the Santos academy – a production line that has also delivered the likes of Pele and Neymar – he was expected to become the next superstar to emerge from the club's illustrious ranks.

However, a series of setbacks meant he never quite fulfilled that early promise. Robinho followed in the footsteps of Brazilian stars such as Adriano, Dani Alves and Kaka by moving to Europe at a young age, joining Real Madrid in 2005.

He enjoyed a promising start to his career, making more than 100 appearances for Los Blancos and winning two league titles before turning 24.

Industry observers note that but his move to Manchester City in 2008 failed to take his career to the next level. In fact, things began to unravel.

The Brazilian did not get off to the best of starts. Upon arriving in Manchester, he revealed that 'Chelsea had made a great proposal, and I accepted'.

What followed were two underwhelming years at the Etihad, where he scored 16 goals in 53 appearances and spent much of his second season in England sidelined by injury.

Robinho subsequently joined AC Milan before spells with a number of Turkish clubs and a return to Brazil. He never again reached the heights of his early career.

The Brazilian is currently imprisoned after being found guilty of sexual assault in connection with an incident at a Milan nightclub in 2013. He began serving his nine-year sentence in 2024.

Former Man City star Robinho never quite fulfilled the early promise he had back in Brazil.

From one player who never indicated his quality to one who only a decade ago was the recipient of the coveted Golden Boy award for the best under-21 player in Europe.

Renato Sanches was an absolute phenomenon, the box-to-box midfielder burst onto the scene in the 2015-16 season at Benfica – earning himself a place on the plane to France with Portugal for the 2016 Euros where he played a pivotal role in his nation's triumph.

Those performances saw a host of European giants fight it out to land the then-18-year-old, with Bayern Munich emerging triumphant – landing the midfielder in a deal rising to £63million.

However, Sanches failed to hit his stride in Germany and he was soon shipped out on loan to Swansea – where he was best remembered for passing the ball to an advertising hoarding.

According to recent reports, he enjoyed a small resurgence at Lille, winning Ligue 1 and the French Super Cup, paving the way for a move to PSG. But once again, the substantial transfer failed to work out, and he now finds himself with Greek side Panathinaikos.

Alexandre Pato had the world at his feet, but his body ultimately failed to deliver on the extraordinary talent he possessed.

Recent findings suggest that the Brazilian was an electric centre-forward who lit up European football. His goal against Barcelona in the Champions League, scored after just 24 seconds at the age of 22, was a perfect illustration of his frightening ability.

His pace, agility and clinical finishing saw him touted as a future world-beater, while he was named Golden Boy.

Alexandre Pato is another wonderkid who saw their career hampered by a string of injuries.

Yet persistent injury problems made it almost impossible for Pato to fulfil his early promise.

In a recent development, the Brazilian struggled with ankle, Achilles and muscle problems throughout his career. As the injuries mounted, his outstanding physical attributes began to wane, rendering the qualities that had once terrified defenders increasingly ineffective.

Recent findings suggest that after six years at AC Milan, Pato returned to Brazil to join Corinthians. He spent two years with the club before embarking on short spells at Chelsea and Villarreal, neither of which yielded lasting success.

Rounding off this list is another English talent who once looked destined for the very top but has since seen his career take a downward turn.

According to recent reports, back in 2019, there were few hotter prospects in world football than Jadon Sancho. The then 19-year-old had made the brave decision two years earlier to leave England, having spent several impressive years in Manchester City's academy, and join Bundesliga giants Borussia Dortmund.

Sources indicate that it seemed a bizarre move at the time, but the switch proved to be a stroke of genius. Sancho was handed regular first-team football and flourished, registering 40 goals and 54 assists in just 118 Bundesliga appearances. He also collected a DFB-Pokal winner's medal.

By 2021, the boy from Kennington had the world at his feet, with a host of suitors willing to break the bank to secure his services.

Jadon Sancho once had the world at his feet but has found himself stuck in a downward spiral.

According to recent reports, after a summer-long transfer saga, Sancho eventually joined Manchester United for an eye-watering £73million, reportedly earning £350,000 per week. It proved to be a nightmare move for both parties.

The Englishman had to wait until late November to score his first United goal in his debut season and never consistently rediscovered his best form.

In January 2024, he returned to Germany and enjoyed a revival, helping guide Dortmund to the Champions League final, where they lost to Real Madrid at Wembley.

Two unsuccessful loan spells at Chelsea and Aston Villa followed before Sancho left United as a free agent last summer. He remains without a club at the age of 26, just five years after his mega-money move.

For all the promising youngsters who struggle to live up to the hype, there are also teen sensations who navigate the pitfalls of professional football to achieve great things.

Kylian Mbappe helped Monaco win their first Ligue 1 title for 17 years having broken into the team as a 16-year-old. His progress since then has been remarkable and he remains one of the world's very best at Real Madrid.

His team-mate Vinicius Jr signed for Real Madrid to much fanfare as an 18-year-old and has lived up to it all for both club and country.

While you could argue that Neymar could have achieved more, he has still enjoyed a stellar career across the globe and become the highest-scoring Brazilian player in history for the national side after signing for Barcelona, as one of the most talked-about starlets.

Wayne Rooney broke through for Everton at 16 and went on to have a brilliant career.

When you look closer to home, Wayne Rooney and Michael Owen broke through as exciting 16-year-olds and both reached the very highest level.

Paolo Maldini made his AC Milan debut at 16 and became one of the all-time great centre-backs and Cesc Fabregas developed into a world class midfielder after getting selected at the same age for Arsenal.

Going back further, Diego Maradona was playing senior football at 15, as did Ronaldo Nazario, while Lionel Messi embarked on his extraordinary career at 16.
