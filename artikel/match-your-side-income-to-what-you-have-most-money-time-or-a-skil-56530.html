Match Your Side Income to What You Have Most – Money, Time, or a Skill

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, side income runs on money, time, or a skill, so build yours on the one you have the most of. Spare savings can earn passive income, free hours suit gig work, and a skill clients pay for usually brings the highest hourly pay. Then invest part of what your side income earns.

A side hustle picked because it’s popular can take the only two evenings you had free, or tie up savings you’ll need next year in a rental.

What you have to spare is the better guide, since money, time, and skill each pay in their own way. Money earns income on what you put in, but you can also lose what you put in. Time pays by the hour or the task, and the pay stops when you stop. Selling a skill also takes your hours, but you set the rate, so it pays more per hour than gig work when few people can do the job.

Say a software tester with a packed schedule has 10 spare hours a month. She’s short on time but has a skill clients pay for, so she skips gig driving and takes two five-hour freelance testing projects at $50 an hour, which bring in $500 a month.

It has been revealed that savings you won’t need for years can earn passive income from dividend stocks, bond funds, a rental property, or a stake in a small business. Stocks and bond funds can fall in value, a rental brings vacancies and repairs, and a business stake can be lost entirely.

A skill sells as freelance work, tutoring, or consulting, and a freelance marketplace can bring in your first clients while you build a client list of your own.

Over the years, what a hustle earns can grow into savings that pay you on their own. So whichever you start with, set aside the tax on each payment and move a fixed share of the rest into a low-cost index fund the day it arrives.
