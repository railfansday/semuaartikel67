Your guest count sets the size of the biggest wedding bills, since every name adds a meal, drinks, and a seat, and a longer list needs a bigger venue. Decide how many guests your budget can host before you book anything, then cut the list to that number with rules you apply to everyone.

Couples married in 2025 spent an average of $292 per guest, according to The Knot’s 2026 Real Weddings Study.

Industry observers note that not every dollar of that average goes away when you cut a name, since the photographer, the band, and your attire cost about the same at any size. But the venue, the biggest single bill at $12,900 on average, has to hold everyone, and catering averaged $80 a person.

In a recent development, so each table of eight you drop cuts $640 from the catering bill at that average, plus the table’s drinks, chairs, linens, and centerpiece. Cutting a first draft of 150 names to 110 removes five tables and $3,200 in catering alone, and can let you book a smaller, cheaper room.

Sources indicate that set your guest count before you tour venues or ask for catering quotes, since both depend on it. Take your wedding budget, subtract the costs that won’t change with the count, and divide the rest by what each guest adds in food, drinks, rentals, and invitations.

Then cut to that number with rules that apply to both sides of the family:.

Answer everyone who asks the same way, by saying you kept the wedding small to stay on budget. A small ceremony now and a bigger, casual party later let you celebrate with the people you cut for far less than a reception for everyone. An invitation that arrives weeks after everyone else’s highlights the guest they were a backup, so mail any second round soon after the first.

Cut the list to your number before any save-the-dates go out, since everyone who gets one expects an invitation.
