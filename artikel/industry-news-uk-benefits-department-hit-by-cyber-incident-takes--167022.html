Industry News: UK benefits department hit by cyber incident, takes services offline

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden called it a “serious cyber incident”, and Downing Street said there is at the moment no evidence of customer data loss. Some claimants told the BBC their payments had not arrived. TNW has updated this article.

The UK’s Department for Work and Pensions has taken its online services offline after detecting a cyber incident. The department, which runs Universal Credit, the state pension and most other British benefits, confirmed the incident on Friday morning.

“We have taken some DWP online services offline as a precaution after we identified a cyber incident affecting our systems,” the department said in its statement.

The department states the incident does not affect regular benefit and pension payments, which will arrive as normal. People who need help can call their usual benefit line.

The systems for pensions, Universal Credit and Personal Independence Payment applications are down, the BBC reported. So are Tell Us Once, which people use to report a death, and state pension applications.

McFadden said the department was aware of an glitch on Thursday and “did the right thing” by taking services down to protect people’s personal details. He said there was no evidence that the incident had compromised anyone’s data.

“When we get an incident like this, the most crucial thing to do is to protect the public,” McFadden said, according to the BBC.

The Prime Minister’s official spokesperson called it a “live and ongoing incident”. Officials could not rule out that a malicious actor was behind it.

Sources indicate that “At the moment there is no evidence of customer data loss. But the investigation is ongoing so I’m not going to get ahead of that process,” the spokesperson mentioned.

Recent findings suggest that the spokesperson added there was no evidence that a malicious actor had targeted other government departments. DWP is working with the National Cyber Security Centre, part of GCHQ, and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre.

Some Universal Credit claimants told the BBC they had not received their payments on Friday. DWP stated the incident had not affected regular payments. It asked anyone with problems to call its benefit phone line.

According to recent reports, debbie Abrahams, who chairs the Work and Pensions Committee, stated taking systems offline was “absolutely the right thing”. Chi Onwurah, chair of the Science and Technology Committee, said the disruption “should be a wake up call”.

“Quite often we are working on extremely old computer systems,” DWP’s permanent secretary, Sarah Healey, told MPs on 4 November.

In 2025-26, DWP spent £309.5bn on benefits and pensions for about 23 million people, its annual report says.

Attacks in 2025 disrupted Jaguar Land Rover, Marks & Spencer and other British firms. Last year, the UK’s privacy regulator also fined outsourcing firm Capita £14m over a 2023 breach that exposed pension scheme data, after initially planning a £45m penalty.

Industry observers note that DWP is the second major institution in two days to take systems offline over a cyber incident. News agency AFP took its content platform offline on Thursday. In the US, a FinCEN hack took Treasury systems offline this week, and Switzerland reported AI agent cyberattacks on critical infrastructure.
