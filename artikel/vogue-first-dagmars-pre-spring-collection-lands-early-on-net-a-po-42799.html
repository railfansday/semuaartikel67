Vogue First! Dagmar’s Pre-Spring Collection Lands Early on Net-a-Porter

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Opening a boutique is a delicate exercise in materials and mood to bring its world to life in a physical space. So it makes sense that for Karin Soderlind, Kristina Tjader, and Sofia Wallenstam, the sisters and founders behind Stockholm-based Dagmar, their Alyssa Kapito-designed flagship became a strong point of reference. When Wallenstam, the brand’s creative director, went looking for inspiration for pre-spring ‘27, the debut collection from new design director Rachael Proud, formerly creative director of Raey, the two didn’t need to look further than the walls of its new shop.

“When Rachel and I started to look at what the collection should be, we looked at our store,” Wallenstam shares with Vogue. In the flagship, a metal Jean Prouvé piece hangs on the wall alongside a handcrafted André Sornay sofa, and the tension between the two became the collection’s throughline. “The Prouvé piece on the wall is metal, quite hard, almost brutalist, and then we have this beautiful soft boucle sofa,” she claims. “That was the meeting point, the hard versus the soft.”.

Recent findings suggest that launching November 19 alongside a new brand identity, the 130-piece pre-spring collection spans relaxed denim (a favorite of Wallenstam’s), sharp-shouldered tailoring, a croc pencil skirt, open-cut tanks, suede outerwear, and boots, much of it drawing on the textures and tones of the store. “We wanted to reuse the bouclé, not in the same way obviously, but that feeling of it,” Wallenstam says. The colors, too—the blacks, whites, and browns—feel representative of the Dagmar woman, Wallenstam adds.

The collection also marks a latest chapter for the Dagmar woman, who Wallenstam describes as confident and living a busy, bustling life with a packed calendar. “She might go and have a school drop-off and go straight to a meeting, and then meet friends, go to a dinner,” she shares. “She wants to look smart and she wants to look cool at the same time.” With Proud on board, that approach is getting a little more edge, with roomier silhouettes and sharper shoulders at the forefront. It’s a bit more oversized than Dagmar has done in the past, with warmer tones integrated throughout. Wallenstam also pushes back on the notion of minimalism, so often pinned on Scandinavian fashion. “Of course, we have neutral tones, but it has heritage. It has a depth to it and a warmth to it.”.

Sources indicate that as for how she and Proud approached the collection together, “When Rachel and I first sat down to discuss what the collection should be and how it should feel, we started immediately with the Dagmar woman and what’s important to her,” Wallenstam states. And the pencil skirt is where Proud’s influence really demonstrates. “You can really see the meeting point between Rachel and Dagmar in the pencil skirt,” she says. “It’s a very feminine product, but it has an elastic waist to make it more relaxed, and it’s in croco, which is a little bit harder in the material.” It’s the piece in the collection that will feel as comfortable at your desk as it does at dinner—a full expression of the newly introduced Dagmar approach. It’s the Prouvé and the bouclé sofa all over again, translated into a single skirt. And of course, the same push and pull runs through the rest of the collection.

Prices range from $140 to $3,300, and Vogue readers can exclusively shop the collection on Dagmar for 24 hours, before its official launch on November 20; retailer drops on Net-a-Porter and Nordstrom to follow.
