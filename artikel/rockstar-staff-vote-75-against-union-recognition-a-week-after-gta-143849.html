Rockstar staff vote 75% against union recognition a week after GTA VI

Industry observers note that rockstar Games staff in the UK have voted against recognising a union, a week after the studio launched Grand Theft Auto VI. Three in four votes went against a recognition deal with the IWGB Rockstar Game Workers Union. The Independent Workers’ Union of Great Britain (IWGB) revealed the result on Thursday.

Industry observers note that about 1,600 staff below director level could vote. Of those, 1,544 took part, a turnout of 93.2%, Chris Kerr reported for Vertical Slice, which broke the result. The ballot ran from 12 to 26 November.

Industry observers note that the split was 75.3% against and 24.7% in favour across the Edinburgh, Leeds, Lincoln, Dundee, and London offices, GamesIndustry.biz reported. IGN put the count at 1,163 votes against and 381 for.

Sources indicate that A yes vote would have made the branch the largest recognised union in the UK games industry. It would have set up formal talks on pay, overtime, bonuses, leave, and pensions. Staff would also have had a say on HR policies and on how Rockstar uses generative AI, Vertical Slice reported.

It has been revealed that the union already exists, and staff can still join it. The result means management does not have to hold regular meetings with its representatives. Under UK law, the union cannot apply for recognition again for three years, according to GamesIndustry.biz.

“Hundreds of our coworkers voted in favour of our union at Rockstar, yet sadly we recognise we have more work to do to build understanding and win the trust of more of our colleagues,” the IWGB stated in a statement.

The union noted the result “isn’t a sign to retreat”. It will keep pushing for pay transparency, contractual overtime pay, and protections against AI and layoffs.

“This result reflects our team’s strong support for continuing the direct relationships, collaboration and open dialogue that have helped make Rockstar Games so innovative and strong,” Jennifer Kolbe, Rockstar’s head of publishing, mentioned in a statement.

In a recent development, the vote follows a long dispute. In October 2025, Rockstar fired more than 30 staff, all IWGB members. The studio notes they leaked confidential information in a Discord channel. The union says those talks were protected union activity and accuses Rockstar of union busting.

In a recent development, the fired workers took the case to an employment tribunal in Glasgow, which heard closing arguments on 19 November. Their lawyers argued the sackings came soon after an informant remarked union membership was nearing 10% of staff. That is roughly the level needed to apply for recognition. Rockstar’s counsel told the tribunal the workers were “caught red-handed”, Wccftech reported. Judge Lucy Wiseman has not yet ruled.

The ballot overlapped with the biggest launch in Rockstar’s history. GTA VI made $3.79 billion in a record first day, after it tied Metacritic’s record with a 99. Take-Two has stated the game was built without generative AI.
