Congress Just Voted to Redraw Yosemite National Park Boundaries. The Bill Is Heading to Trump’s Desk.

A upcoming law is heading to President Trump’s desk that would shift Yosemite’s boundaries by consolidating land between the National Park Service (NPS) and the National Forest System.

In a recent development, on November 18, Congress passed the Ackerson Meadow Land Interchange Act, a bill co-signed by Democratic U.S. Senator Alex Padilla and Republican U.S. Representative Tom McClintock, both from California.

If the president signs it into law, the Ackerson Meadow Land Interchange Act will transfer 160 acres of Stanislaus National Forest land to the NPS for management as part of Yosemite. Another 170 acres of Yosemite National Park land will be transferred to the U.S. Forest Service (USFS) for management as part of Stanislaus National Forest.

Sources indicate that the decision comes after a flurry of debate following announcements for public land swaps and transfers. An August report alleged that the NPS was attempting to execute a land exchange within Yosemite for a private road, which reportedly would have given property within park boundaries to a Nevada-based real estate company. Then, in September, Major League Baseball approached the Trump administration about hosting a game at Grand Teton National Park in northwest Wyoming.

Lawmakers say the Ackerson Meadow decision will address a problem in federal land management in the Sierra Nevada: the checkerboard of boundary lines. In 2016, the Trust for Public Land bought the 400-acre Ackerson Meadow parcel and gifted it to Yosemite. However, the chunk was almost completely surrounded by the Stanislaus National Forest with very little contiguous border to the park itself, lawmakers noted.

“Ackerson Meadow is one of the most critical wildlife corridors in our state, yet the current configuration of the region puts habitat conservation at risk,” Padilla remarked in a statement. “This land transfer is a simple and beneficial exchange that will help protect endangered wildlife, mitigate wildfire threats, and sustain our rural economy.”.

Jackie McGuinness, Padilla’s communications director, told Outside that the agreement will change Yosemite’s boundaries. A map McGuinness sent highlights the park boundaries shifting to the east, filling in a checkerboard pattern left blocked out by the previous perimeter lines.

USFS manages land ranchers use for livestock grazing on national forests and grasslands, while the NPS restricts most of these operations within park boundaries. The land exchange between the two agencies eliminates confusion caused by the jurisdictional split, McClintock said in a U.S. House floor statement on September 14.

Industry observers note that “The proposed land exchange would make that cooperative approach easier by eliminating the confusion caused by split jurisdiction,” he stated. “Ranchers need to know where their permits apply, where infrastructure can be maintained, and who is responsible for decisions affecting their operations.”.

It’s unclear whether or when Trump intends to sign the bill into law.
