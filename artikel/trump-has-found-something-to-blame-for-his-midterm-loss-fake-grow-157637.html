Trump has found something to blame for his midterm loss: ‘Fake’ growth numbers out of Howard Lutnick’s Commerce Department

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, the headaches just keep piling up for Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has been on his case about the Commerce Department’s handling of Nvidia, following the news of Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy. And now President Trump is blaming his team for publishing “fake” growth numbers before the midterms.“It was just revealed that the real numbers on the economy, published before the election, were fake, and a correction was issued yesterday by the people in the government agency that does these numbers,” the president posted on Truth Social.

“The American economy under the Trump administration is doing far better than the good numbers already released.”.

The agency Trump appears to be referring to is the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), which sits within the Commerce Department.

The BEA operates with statistical and methodological independence, a safeguard designed to protect the accuracy and impartiality of its statistics.

On Wednesday, the BEA issued revisions to its growth figures for the third quarter of 2026, saying GDP was 4.3%—not the 3.9% it had originally forecast. The upward revision of 0.9% on the initial estimate was “primarily reflecting upward revisions to investment and consumer spending.”.

Analysts are unlikely to be surprised. Revisions are a routine feature of U.S. economic statistics. Lower survey response rates have reduced the precision of some estimates. As UBS’s Pauk Donovan noted in a note to clients this morning: “Changes in U.S. economic growth are exaggerated by the fiction of annualization (a technique employed by very few advanced economies).”.

“Data revisions are more common as structural change and poor survey response rates reduce precision.”.

But President Trump suggested there were more nefarious reasons for the near-percentage-point difference in initial estimates to the published figures: “Why wasn’t the correct number issued before the election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation. This is not a small error, this is a massive, and purposeful, error.”.

Lutnick issued a bullish economic enhancement. Writing on X, the Commerce Secretary noted: “We’re bringing American manufacturing back at a scale nobody thought possible. America’s successes will become even more powerful in the coming years as these projects move from groundbreaking to full production.”.

In a recent development, trump’s latest clash with federal data agencies could further undermine confidence in U.S. economic statistics. In 2025, the president fired the director of the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), Erika McEntarfer, claiming she had overseen the publishing of “phony” job numbers for political reasons. (The president provided no evidence for his claim.).

Recent findings suggest that this, paired with Trump’s extraordinary attacks on the Federal Reserve under former chairman Jerome Powell, led many economists and investors to worry about the credibility of major U.S. institutions that either inform policy or make it.

At the time, Alberto Cavallo, Professor at Harvard Business School, told Goldman Sachs: “It’s difficult to avoid a comparison to the experience of my home country, Argentina, nearly two decades ago when the president, dismayed at rising inflation, removed statistical agency leadership.

“Some statistics stopped being reported, and official data no longer seemed to align with perceptions. That was a very chaotic period that destroyed trust in Argentina’s official statistics for over a decade.”.

While Cavallo said the U.S. is ahead of some of Argentina’s data bodies in terms of skills and institutional checks and balances, he said the situation was still “very concerning.”.
