📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that amazon's first satellite launch since a rocket failure doomed 40 of its spacecraft is set for Tuesday morning (Nov. 24), and you can watch the action live.

An Ariane 6 rocket carrying 36 satellites for the Amazon Leo constellation is scheduled to lift off from Europe's Spaceport in Kourou, French Guiana, on Tuesday (Nov. 23), during a 29-minute window that opens at 5:45 a.m. EST (1045 GMT; 7:45 a.m. local time in Kourou). You can watch it live here at Space.com courtesy of Arianespace, the French company that operates the Ariane 6. Coverage will begin at 5:15 a.m. EST (1015 GMT).

Sources indicate that the launch was originally supposed to take place on Monday (Nov. 23), but that attempt was scrubbed due to a technical issue affecting the rocket's ground support systems, according to Arianespace.

Amazon Leo, so named because it will operate in low Earth orbit, will compete with SpaceX's Starlink internet constellation, which right now consists of more than 11,000 operational spacecraft.

Industry observers note that amazon's network will eventually have more than 3,200 satellites, but just 400 or so have reached orbit to date. Another 40 were supposed to be delivered on Nov. 15, but they were lost after their rocket ride, a United Launch Alliance (ULA) Vulcan Centaur, suffered a problem shortly after launch.

In a recent development, ariane 6 and Vulcan Centaur aren't the only rockets Amazon is depending on to build its broadband constellation. Jeff Bezos' manufacturer has also booked rides on ULA's Atlas V rocket, Blue Origin's Fresh Glenn and SpaceX's Falcon 9.

Vulcan will be grounded for a while as ULA investigates the Nov. 15 failure and works to fix whatever caused it. And Newly introduced Glenn hasn't flown since April, when the rocket failed to deliver AST SpaceMobile's BlueBird 7 satellite to the proper orbit. New Glenn's return to flight was further complicated on May 28, when the rocket exploded during a routine prelaunch test.

In a recent development, the Ariane 6, the successor to Arianespace's Ariane 5 heavy lifter, debuted in July 2024 and has nine introduces under its belt to date. All of them have been successful except that maiden mission, which was a partial failure.

The upcoming mission will be the fourth Amazon Leo flight for the Ariane 6, following debuts in February, April and June of this year.

Editor's note: This article was updated at 5:30 a.m. EST on Nov. 23 to reflect the postponement of the Ariane 6 launch following a technical concern affecting the rocket's ground support systems. It was updated again at 10:30 a.m. EST on Nov. 23 with the latest target date of Nov. 24.
