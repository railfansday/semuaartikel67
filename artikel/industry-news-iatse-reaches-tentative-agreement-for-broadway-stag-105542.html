📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

IATSE Local One, which represents stagehands on Broadway, has reached a tentative agreement with the Broadway League, ahead of the lucrative holiday weeks.

According to recent reports, this comes after Local One members had voted to authorize a strike in October, after working on an expired contract since late July. If called, a work stoppage from Local One would have likely put a halt to most Broadway shows. The agreement still needs to be ratified by members of the union, with details of the tentative agreement planned to be available ahead of the union’s December meeting.

However, another local chapter of the union, IATSE Local 764, which represents Broadway dressers and others who work on costumes, still remains without a contract, and voted Sunday to authorize a strike if necessary.

Local 764 members have been bargaining with The Broadway League, a trade association for Broadway producers and general managers, since June. The members’ previous agreement had expired July 26. The League cancelled bargaining sessions scheduled for November 18 and 19, according to IATSE, and Local 764 has been pushing for newly introduced dates.

IATSE remarked the problems still in contention include wages and a gender pay gap that has existed for decades for Broadway wardrobe workers. The field is predominantly female, and has been paid “significantly lower rates than stagehand classifications historically held predominantly by men” according to the union, with Local 764 department heads needing to work 45 percent longer to earn pay comparable to workers in similar department-head roles.

Both Actors’ Equity and the American Federation of Musicians Local 802 also voted to authorize strikes last year, and came very close to doing so, before striking new contracts with the Broadway League.
