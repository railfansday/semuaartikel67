Barnsley and Doncaster Rovers - down among the dead men in League One - will be hoping that rings true as they seek precious shafts of light following arduous autumns.

They face back-to-back derbies in their next two fixtures, which will assume importance not just for the morale of each club, but also for Daniel Stendel and Grant McCann in their second spells at the Reds and Rovers.

Sheffield Wednesday, Bradford City and Huddersfield Town have their own motivations to contend with.

Industry observers note that in the second half of last autumn, Barnsley slipped up at home to Rotherham United, who won in the league at Oakwell for the first time in 15 visits since March 1969. But they did afford themselves the fillip of three points at Doncaster 14 days later.

The Reds, who welcome the Owls on Friday before visiting Bradford - managed by ex-boss Michael Duff - next Tuesday, would probably settle for a 50 per cent derby ratio this time around.

More especially given that their grim winless league run - nine matches and counting - has descended into a crisis.

They must make do without top-scorer and suspended captain Cameron McGeehan on Friday, while defender Jack Shepherd, a boyhood Wednesdayite, is also banned. The return of Patrick Kelly from international duty with Northern Ireland is a much more welcome development.

The sight of in-form Owls striker and Reds old boy Davis Keillor-Dunn compounding their autumnal angst certainly wouldn't be.

A match-winning display from DKD - who has struck in his last three Wednesday matches - would put the tinlid on things for Reds followers, whose expectation levels are exceedingly low heading into Friday.

If things turn sour, the mood in the home stands has the potential to turn mutinous towards the club hierarchy. Barnsley are without a league win since September 2 and are only kept off the bottom of the table by goal difference. A protest from fans group Tykes Union is planned beforehand.

Derbies brought out the best in McCann's Rovers in League One last season and their recent 2-0 win over Wednesday keeping to that theme.

Rovers secured a derby double against arch-rivals Rotherham for the first time since 1949-50 and made history by winning league games at Barnsley and Millers in the same season.

McCann's side also beat Town and Bradford at home and performed very well in both away games and were extremely unlucky to lose both.

They visit the Bantams on Saturday, seeking to get back onside with supporters following dreadful away performances in their last two matches in league and cup - an embarrassing 3-0 FA Cup loss at non-league Kidderminster and an equally atrocious 6-1 league beating at MK Dons.

It has been revealed that on both occasions, McCann issued an apology to travelling fans with some starting to openly question his future. The Rovers chief, whose side host high-flying Town next Thursday, prides himself as being a fighter.

Wednesday’s derby form this term has been in keeping with their wretched numbers on that count over the past couple of years.

They have won just once in their past 14 league fixtures against White Rose counterparts.

So far this season, they were bullied in the second half of their meeting with Bradford in their opening home league fixture of 2026-27 - a 1-0 defeat which was more emphatic than the scoreline sounded.

By common consent, they were somewhat fortunate to secure a late point at Huddersfield on October 10 - when Town were very much the architects of their own downfall.

A fortnight later, a disjointed, unsatisfactory performance at Doncaster proved the final straw for the Owls hierarchy regarding Henrik Pedersen.

It has been revealed that historically, Oakwell at least provides rather happier memories for the Owls, notwithstanding their last visit. They have lost just once on their last eight trips up the A61.

After dismal displays - not just results - in three derby losses at Valley Parade, the Club Doncaster Sports Village and Oakwell last season, redressing the balance would be a barometer of Town’s progress in 26-27.

It’s a case of so far, so good with the Terriers avenging last season’s anaemic loss at Bradford with a cathartic victory on Hallowe’en. Statistically speaking, the visitors’ 3-0 triumph was their biggest ever at BD8.

In a recent development, they have two other scores to settle. Town were fitful in their 3-1 defeat at Oakwell last August and it was a sign of things to come for ex-head coach Lee Grant. They visit Barnsley on December 19.

There were jeers and opprobrium following a tepid 1-0 reverse in Doncaster on a February night when Town’s top-six pitch was exposed.

In a recent development, the present-day worries belong to Rovers. And Barnsley.
