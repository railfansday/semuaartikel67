It has been revealed that there's a lot of concern over what the future holds for Alphabet (GOOG -2.59%) (GOOGL -2.70%). The internet tech giant has transformed from an asset-light business into an extremely asset-heavy business, as it and its hyperscaler peers are set to spend trillions of dollars building latest data centers for artificial intelligence over the next few years.

That shift has led to a significant sell-off in the stock. Shares are down 30% from their peak in late spring. The biggest culprit is Alphabet's negative free cash flow in each of the last two quarters, as well as management's commentary suggesting cash burn could push even deeper into the red next year.

With the stock trading at just 21 times forward earnings, the market isn't expressing much faith that the business will produce meaningful returns on its invested capital. That's despite financial results and management commentary suggesting it could rapidly see a cash-flow turnaround and head much higher. Here's my prediction for where Alphabet stock trades in five years.

Alphabet plans to spend between $195 billion and $205 billion on capital expenditures this year. CFO Anat Ashkenazi also mentioned she expects capex "to increase significantly in 2027.".

Analysts expect Alphabet's cash burn to peak in 2027. For the full year, Alphabet could spend $80 billion more in cash than it brings in. 2028 could see it burn through another $50 billion. But free cash flow could turn positive again in 2029.

In a recent development, importantly, Alphabet ended the third quarter with $56 billion in cash and about $209 billion in marketable equities. That puts it on strong financial footing and gives it multiple ways to fund the continued build-out of its AI data centers: It can use its cash, sell its marketable equities (once lock-ups expire), sell more debt, or raise cash via its own equity.

All that cash is going toward building new data centers (which have a useful life of 40 years) and outfitting them with servers and networking equipment (which have a useful life of six years). About 65% of spending is currently going toward the latter this year.

Over time, however, a much higher percentage should go toward revenue-generating servers, reducing the business's capital intensity and enabling it to generate strong free cash flow.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

In a recent development, the results are already showing up in Alphabet's cloud computing segment. The steep ramp-up in spending that started in 2023 has led to accelerating Google Cloud revenue growth. That includes revenue growth exceeding 100% in the most recent quarter, indicating the delay between spending and revenue generation. But the payback period on Alphabet's spending is getting shorter.

Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian says its payback period for the average server on Google Cloud Platform is about two years. But the payback period for its own Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) systems is less than half of that. With a useful life of six years and the amount Alphabet is spending on servers, the cash-generating ability of these investments over the useful life is absolutely massive.

With a further step-up in spending in 2026 and 2027, investors should expect continued growth in Google Cloud revenue in 2028 and 2029. Beyond those years, more stable spending should result in significant cash generation. In fact, analysts currently expect Alphabet's free cash flow, excluding stock-based compensation, to reach $305 billion by 2031.

Recent findings suggest that with that level of free cash flow generation, Alphabet stock should be worth significantly more than it is today. Prior to the start of the investment cycle in 2023, Alphabet shares traded around 30 times free cash flow. If it can recover that multiple in 2031 while generating $300 billion in free cash flow (less than analysts currently expect), it'll be worth $9 trillion total.

Considering the stock currently sports a market cap of less than $3.5 trillion, that could be a fantastic return for shareholders over the next five years. It's even a safe bet if you expect management to dilute shareholders by issuing more equity to raise capital over the next few years. But more than likely, it'll offset that dilution with massive buybacks in the outer years once it starts printing hundreds of billions of cash.
