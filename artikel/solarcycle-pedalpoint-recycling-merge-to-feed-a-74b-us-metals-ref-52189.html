📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that old solar panels have silver and copper worth recovering. A fresh US recycling company called Jupiter plans to send metals recovered from retired solar panels and data center hardware to Korea Zinc’s refineries, including a $7.4 billion plant planned for Clarksville, Tennessee.

Jupiter joins US solar panel recycler Solarcycle and PedalPoint Recycling, Korea Zinc’s US recycling and metals business, into a single, fully integrated organization. Jupiter says it’s launching with eight certified US facilities, nearly 1 million square feet of processing space, and about 250 employees. It’s also taking on customers from both businesses, including Microsoft, Prologis, and Brookfield.

For a solar farm or data center operator, that means a single company can take equipment out of service, resell parts with remaining useful life, and recycle the rest. Customers can track where the materials go and bring them back into their own supply chains.

According to recent reports, korea Zinc will hold a majority stake. Solarcycle’s Suvi Sharma will lead Jupiter as CEO, and PedalPoint’s Kevin Hahm will be CFO. Microsoft and Prologis Ventures are among Solarcycle’s existing investors.

Sources indicate that as Electrek reported in January, Solarcycle’s Cedartown, Georgia, plant was processing thousands of old solar panels a week when it opened. Solarcycle remarked the plant could handle up to 5 gigawatts’ worth of panels annually at full capacity, recovering materials such as glass, aluminum, copper, and silver.

The Tennessee plant adds a US refining step. Korea Zinc is developing the facility, called Project Crucible, with US government backing. It expects to start production in phases in 2029. The plant is designed to process about 1.1 million tons of raw materials per year and produce roughly 540,000 tons of finished products.
