Luka Modric may be 41 years of age, but he shows no signs of slowing down. In fact, he remains crucial or both AC Milan and Croatia.

La Gazzetta dello Sport begin a report by pointing out that Sunday’s game will be a first for Modric, even given the hundreds of games he has played. The midfielder will face Frosinone for the first time, the 227th opponent of his long career (155 at club level and 72 for the national team).

In ten days, Modric will have gone from from London’s Wembley Stadium where he faced England, to the Opus Arena in Osijek, Croatia, where he beat the Czech Republic and played 90 minutes as captain, back to San Siro for the Frosinone game.

In a recent development, yesterday, Luka returned to Milanello and trained with the Rossoneri, ready to take charge of the group again when the tournament resumes. He’s one of the eight players in the squad who have surpassed 800 minutes of total playing time across all competitions, including goalkeepers.

Industry observers note that the Ballon d’Or winner has featured 13 times this season, with 11 being in the league (eight starts), and twice as a substitute in the Europa League. For his national team, he’s appeared in six Nations League matches, four of them from the start.

According to recent reports, the match against Spain on October 6th was supposed to be his farewell to Croatia, but instead he was easily persuaded to stay and continue. He’s played 19 of the 21 matches since August, accumulating over a thousand minutes – 1,114 to be precise.

Modric scored for Milan at 40 years, five months and four days (the winner against Pisa) and is third in the ranking of the oldest goal scorers in the club’s history. Alessandro Costacurta is second (41 years, 25 days) and Zlatan Ibrahimovic is first (41 years, 5 months, and 15 days).

So, another record is in sight in the present, but what about the future? Modric is tied to Milan until the summer, with his contract expiring in June 2027. There are no further options for an extension but the relationship between player and club goes beyond written agreements.

According to recent reports, luka will decide his own future, as he did last summer, choosing to stay. He still wanted Milan despite being left behind without his family (who had since returned to Madrid) out of love for the Rossoneri. Next season, who knows?
