📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, it is critically important for an airline to know exactly what it is and isn't. The ultra-low-cost carrier, Allegiant Air, knows the answer immediately. It is and always will be unashamedly a leisure-focused operator, with the acquisition of Sun Country by the Allegiant Travel Company strengthening its position.

If Allegiant's network in Q1 2026 (January to March) is compared to what's available in the same three months in 2027, 26 routes have been removed. Inevitably, some of them involve airports to which the airline no longer flies. Mostly, however, the links have simply been removed from still-served airports.

Schedule analysis using OAG data indicates that all of the following markets saw the ULCC in Q1 2026 but will not do so in Q1 2027. Some may have had flights in January, February, and March this year, while others may have only had service in one or two of the months.

It has been revealed that 20 of them have been removed entirely from Allegiant's map due to what is normal route churn. No flights are planned beyond the stated month. The remaining six markets are very different. While they won't be served in the first three months of the newly introduced year, they'll simply return later in 2027. Some may say that including them is unfair, but they feature because two different time periods were compared.

Routes (All Served In Q1 2026 But Not In Q1 2027).

Bellingham to Los Angeles, Cincinnati to Los Angeles, Nashville to Peoria, Las Vegas to Boise.

Fort Lauderdale to Traverse City, Orlando Sanford to Columbia.

Bellingham to Palm Springs, Bellingham to Oakland, Fort Lauderdale to Columbia, Fort Lauderdale to Savannah, Fort Lauderdale to Tri-State/Huntington, Las Vegas to Chattanooga, Pittsburgh to Phoenix Sky Harbor, Punta Gorda to Fresh Orleans.

Bellingham to Burbank, Las Vegas to Lexington, Nashville to Akron/Canton, Nashville to Sarasota (returns later in 2027), Portland to Idaho Falls, Washington Dulles to Savannah.

Akron/Canton to Myrtle Beach (returns later in 2027), Boston to Des Moines (returns later in 2027), Denver to Knoxville (returns later in 2027), Denver to Peoria (returns later in 2027), Las Vegas to Asheville, Nashville to Gulf Shores (returns later in 2027).

As Allegiant has pulled out of Columbia, Los Angeles International, Oakland, and Palm Springs, the quartet's last remaining routes in the examined period inevitably featured. Los Angeles International joined the carrier's network in 2009. In the past ten years alone, over 13,000 departures were available. It was the carrier's 18th most-served airport in this period.

Recent findings suggest that while the following links were served by Allegiant in the first three months of 2026, they will not have nonstop operations in the same quarter next year. They are not cut, as they'll all return in spring 2027. Most have simply become seasonal, which is, of course, designed to better align capacity with demand to improve yield and load factor performance.

It has been revealed that located in Alabama, not far from Mobile and Pensacola, Gulf Shores welcomed scheduled services a year and a half ago. Allegiant is presently the only operator there, although Sun Country will debut next year. The pair initiative 14 routes in 2027, including Nashville.

The route from Tennessee had been on Allegiant's map since November 2025. Between then and August 2026, the load factor was a ridiculously low 64.7%. January and February were among the worst months, which is why flights do not resume until April. If things don't pick up, the route won't be coming back in 2028.

In a recent development, at 1,585 nautical miles (2,935 km) each way, Allegiant had service between Las Vegas and Asheville from March 2021 until November 2026. Not surprisingly, it had two weekly flights, with both the 156-seat A319 and 186-seat A320 deployed. As is often the case, the market did not have nonstop flights until Allegiant's arrival.

DOT data currently extends to August 2026. Between March 2021 and that summer month, Allegiant transported 162,157 round-trip passengers. It had 69% of the local market in this period, with the rest of the traffic flying via hubs with other operators. Allegiant filled 81.2% of the available seats, although the fares needed to achieve that result are unknown.
