The lame-duck session continues as lawmakers look ahead to Thanksgiving and the funding deadline that arrives in December. The primary legislative business this week is the Senate's bipartisan permitting overhaul, but a late-breaking aviation funding fight and the Senate Democratic leadership contest are also commanding attention.

Sources indicate that sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., last week formally debuted a leadership challenge to Senate Minority Leader Charles E. Schumer, D-N.Y., and both men are accumulating supporters ahead of leadership votes that are at the moment scheduled for early December — though there's been an effort to move that up to this week.

Van Hollen has expressed disagreement with Schumer's participation in primaries.

​"I don't think it's the role of the Senate majority leader to get involved in open, contested Democratic primaries," Van Hollen mentioned Sunday on CNN.

​Van Hollen has also said he wants to use all available tools to stop funding for the Iran war immediately, with a government funding deadline in December. That's going to be one of several flash points for December's funding discussions, as President Donald Trump and congressional Republicans try to use what leverage they have before Democrats take the gavels in the House and the Senate in January.

A newly introduced issue is the state of federal air traffic control after Friday's nationwide ground stop, with the Trump administration now seeking $50 billion to upgrade systems.

"I need additional money to do the additional work to keep the airspace safe," Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy mentioned on Fox News Sunday.

Sen. Brian Schatz, D-Hawaii, a senior appropriator who is in line to become majority whip, has suggested reallocating funds from the additional money the Trump administration and congressional Republicans allocated to Immigration and Customs Enforcement to address the challenges at the Federal Aviation Administration.

Sources indicate that "There is a pot of 60 billion extra dollars on top of the regularly appropriated amount for ICE and we should use at least a portion of that money for the FAA. We can solve this problem and not spend any additional taxpayer dollars," Schatz posted on X.

"We can do all things, we don't have to do one versus the other," Duffy noted in the Fox News Sunday interview. "I don't think that is the answer. The answer is let's do everything that's going to keep Americans safe, whether it's with immigration or it's with air travel.".

Meanwhile on the Senate floor, the energy permitting overhaul bill faces a key test Monday evening​, with a vote scheduled to limit debate on a substitute amendment that represents the most recent version of the bill. Votes on a number of President Donald Trump's judicial nominations are also lined up this week.

On the other side of the Capitol, the House floor schedule i​ncludes several labor-related bills. ​One of the measures, introduced by Sen.-elect Kevin Hern, R-Okla.,​ would set standards for the way labor law applies to franchise operators.

Fresh member orientation is also continuing, and committees in both the House and Senate will be holding hearings throughout the week as the outgoing Republican committee chairs aim to get through priority items on their agenda before the end of the Congress.

That includes a Tuesday morning Senate Armed Services Committee confirmation hearing for Hung Cao, the nominee to be Navy secretary, who is at the moment the acting secretary. The Senate Judiciary Committee will be busy as well, as Chairman Charles E. Grassley, R-Iowa, looks to tee up more of Trump's judicial nominations for floor consideration.
