STUDIO HERRSTRÃM rebuilds the Milkshake brand identity around car culture, a redrawn splash mark, high-vis color and road signs.

Sources indicate that milkshake is a Stockholm label, a division of Sony Music Entertainment founded in 2019, and its new identity starts in a car. The name was suggested on a car ride, milestones happened in cars, and new music was taken for a car test. The old splash is still there, redrawn as a rounded, blended symbol that reads like metal and liquid merging, and the wordmark runs through it with a zigzag cut that splits Milkshake into two stacked hits. On the posters the name fills the frame until the letters stop being text and start being the picture: acid yellow Milksh across the top, shake across the bottom.

In a recent development, the type system is Gates, a neo-grotesque from Contextype, shown in the specimen with arrows, asterisks, circled letters and roman numerals on a mauve field. The wordmark itself leans forward, its letters cut on a slant that matches the splash, and the supporting lockups borrow their logic from car window stickers and milk carton nutrition labels: small blocks of capitals that hold track names, release dates and the p-line symbol without breaking the grid.

The system proves itself on the objects it touches. A high-vis vest carries the full lockup on its back in black on acid yellow, with reflective bands borrowed from road markings. A neon green acrylic cube hangs outside the shopfront with the black wordmark on its face. A blender wears the whole icon set as stickers: the machine that makes the drink becomes the merch.

Color does the rest. Acid yellow is the signal color, while neon pink and soft lilac take the flavor cues and black keeps the contrast high. The same palette runs the subway poster for Tove Lo's single Me/You (always and forever), dated 18.09.26. Linn Wexell, founder and creative director of Milkshake, says the creative work was smart and on point and the visuals stunning.
