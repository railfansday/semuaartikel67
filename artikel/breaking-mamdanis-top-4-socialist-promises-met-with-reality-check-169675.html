Fox News Digital breaks down some of Mamdani's most controversial campaign promises and those he's made a priority in the first of his four years in office.

On the campaign trail, Mamdani pitched freezing monthly rent costs as a key strategy to lower the cost of housing for New Yorkers. Roughly 67.7% of those who live in the five boroughs of the city are renters, according to the most recent government data released in 2024.

In a recent development, newly introduced York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani listens to reporters' questions during a news conference at Athens Square Park Nov. 17, 2026, in New York City. (Michael M. Santiago/Getty Images).

"As mayor, I will freeze the rent every year that I’m in office. That’s a guarantee," Mamdani said in a campaign video posted near the beginning of his campaign in 2024.

True to his word, the New York City Rent Guidelines Board announced Mamdani’s promised freeze in June, detailing a 0% increase in one-year leases beginning between October 2026 and October 2027 and a 0% increase for two-year leases beginning in the same window.

Mamdani hailed the announcement as a significant victory for the city, highlighting the longer-lease lock.

Industry observers note that "This is a historic victory for Upcoming York City tenants. After reviewing the data and hearing from New Yorkers across the city, the independent RGB has delivered a freeze on one-year leases, and the first-ever freeze on two-year leases in our city’s history," Mamdani’s office remarked in a press release in June.

"This is the relief that working people across our city deserve.".

Sources indicate that notably, the freeze does not apply to all rental apartments in Upcoming York City, instead covering about 1 million rent-stabilized units. According to the Community Service Society, a nonprofit poverty advocacy group, rent-stabilized apartments make up 41% of the city’s rental units.

Although Mamdani’s city-run grocery stores haven’t yet debuted in earnest, a pilot test of what they could look like has raised questions about whether the idea will meaningfully address the food scarcity problems Mamdani highlighted on the campaign trail.

"This month we got polling that showed that two-thirds of New Yorkers support city-owned grocery stores, a policy we’ve championed for months and would guarantee cheaper groceries," Mamdani noted last year.

Even so, the first version of the city-run grocery store was met with complaints of long lines, shortages and disorganization when it debuted in February.

In a recent interview, Mamdani remarked he still has prepares to build on the idea.

"What we’re going to do is — in a city of more than a thousand supermarkets — we’re going to create five. … They’re cheaper supermarkets, supermarkets with a discount," the mayor remarked during a recent interview with The New York Times.

Mamdani has said he hopes to sell products at 30% below the market price.

The proposal has sparked skepticism from economists and commentators over how the city could deliver such steep discounts in an industry known for razor-thin profit margins. With grocery prices up about 33% nationwide since 2019, critics say the strategy must explain how prices could undercut private retailers by 30% without significant public subsidies.

Sources indicate that "This is completely insane. Also, not possible unless taxpayers are subsidizing all the groceries. Grocery store profits are tiny, a few percentage points a year, at best," Outkick founder Clay Travis wrote.

Like his grocery store initiative, Mamdani’s strategy to implement free universal childcare has struggled to keep pace with demand, falling short of the full promise.

"Since the beginning of this campaign, I’ve championed universal childcare, making a commitment to deliver it to New Yorkers as young as six weeks to five years of age," Mamdani said in a campaign video in 2025.

"My administration will address this crisis by providing no-cost childcare to families with children as young as six weeks up until they’re five years of age.".

Industry observers note that "I have said when we ran this campaign … I’m making buses fast and free. I have said that by the time I am done being mayor, those will have been delivered," Mamdani said in January.
