Could Nvidia Be Berkshire's Next Major Tech Purchase?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.08%)(BRKB -0.27%) has been looking a bit different of late. It's been investing more in tech, with the most notable change in its portfolio being that Alphabet is now one of its largest holdings. Not many investors would likely have predicted that to be the case even a few years ago, as its former CEO Warren Buffett was largely hesitant to invest in tech (aside from Apple) and in areas outside his expertise.

Under new CEO Greg Abel, however, there appears to be a greater appetite for tech, as is evident in Berkshire’s growing position in Alphabet and also its investment in Bending Spoons, a tech company that's been a serial acquirer that Buffett more than likely would have avoided in the past.

Industry observers note that the substantial question is whether Nvidia (NVDA -0.18%) will also find its way into Berkshire's portfolio. It certainly checks off a lot of boxes right now.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia is precisely the type of organization that Berkshire might invest in if it were comfortable with tech, and more specifically, artificial intelligence (AI). Nvidia has a strong moat, as tech companies rely on its cutting-edge chips for AI development. It dominates market share, and that enables it to charge high prices and post excellent profit margins.

Now, with the stock also falling sharply after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO and president, its lower valuation could make it a far more appealing buy. Trading at around just 10 times its expected future profits (based on analyst estimates), it looks like a bargain buy right now. If Berkshire's management is indeed taking a more favorable approach to tech, Nvidia might just be a slam-dunk buy.

NYSE · As of 9:56 AM ET 11/17/26.

Its recent moves suggest that Berkshire might not be the same business it was before, when, under Buffett, it might have shied away from the tech sector. While I didn't like its move to invest in Bending Spoons, adding Alphabet is an excellent idea. And if Berkshire also invests in Nvidia, that could signal to investors that tech will be a key part of its long-term strategy.

Industry observers note that for investors who have been craving more growth and aggressiveness from Berkshire's management, this could be a welcome change. The same may not be true, however, for investors who prefer a more cautious approach.

Investors may want to wait a few months to see whether Berkshire has indeed invested in Nvidia, as that will make it clear whether investing in tech will be the fresh norm moving forward.
