According to recent reports, apple’s first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, has been out in the wild for a month now, and more apps are being updated to natively support the dual-screened device all the time. Adobe Lightroom just got a substantial iPhone Duo compatibility update this week, and it is a strong example of how good the Duo can be.

As Adobe Photoshop and Lightroom Product Designer, Johannes Eckert, explains on Threads, the Adobe team has worked hard to fully optimize Lightroom for Duo.

Recent findings suggest that the iPhone Duo version of Lightroom works as normal on the device’s front screen, but when opened up, it is an entirely transformed experience, showing photos much larger than on other iPhone models and moving editing tools to the side. There’s also a “laptop pose,” as Eckert indicates, that puts editing tools on the bottom while the photo stays above the fold.

Sources indicate that in PetaPixel‘s testing, the updated app worked as promised and delivered a genuinely pleasant mobile photo editing experience. It’s nice to see my photos on the Duo’s large internal screen and to be able to access sliders, presets, masking tools, and more without obscuring my photo at all or needing to shrink it way down. You can still actually see your photo while utilizing Lightroom’s suite of editing tools, which is a big improvement over a standard iPhone photo editing experience.

According to recent reports, that remarked, there is still some room for improvement, and it seems like Adobe is already working on making tweaks. For example, when doing a Curves edit, the curves themselves appear on as an overlay on the photo, which is fine, except that it leaves much of the Duo’s inner screen totally empty.

“I think it’s better to have the curves to [the] right to get rid of the empty space,” Threads user itsrongarcia commented on Eckert’s original post.

“We agree. This will be in the next upgrade!” Eckert replied.

Other customers are looking forward to Adobe bringing some of its more powerful editing tools over from iPad, arguing that Apple’s latest A20 Pro chip is more than capable of running things like automatic dust removal and AI-powered noise reduction. There’s no word yet on whether Adobe is considering these things at this time.

Adobe will no doubt refine the iPhone Duo version of Lightroom further as more real-world owners get to test it out and offer their feedback, but so far, so good. Ultimately, the iPhone Duo’s lasting appeal will depend heavily on how apps utilize its distinct form factor. Lightroom is definitely taking full advantage of it, and Lightroom mobile has never been as enjoyable to use.
