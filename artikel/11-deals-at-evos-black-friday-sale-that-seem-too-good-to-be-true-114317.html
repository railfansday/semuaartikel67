Savvy shoppers know Black Friday is the Super Bowl of savings. These days, it’s more like Black Friday Week, which gives us even more time to browse the best deals.

Recent findings suggest that for skiers, there’s no better time to refresh your gear closet than November, when you start taking stock of what you own and what you need for the season. The good news is that Evo is a one-stop shop for everything from hard goods to travel bags, and the deals are plentiful. Keep reading to see what the SKI team is recommending from Evo’s big Black Friday sale.

Industry observers note that we’ll admit it’s not the sexiest gift on anyone’s list, but Evo’s Roller Ski Bag is on sale, and it’s a no-brainer for ski travelers who want to bring their own skis and ensure they arrive safely at their destination. The bag has wheels, which is essential for hauling skis through airports and parking lots. It also holds up to two pairs of skis, poles, boots, outerwear, and accessories, all in padded, durable compartments. Internal straps keep everything in place, and the bag folds up for compact storage between trips. —Samantha Berman.

I’ve long appreciated the Arc’teryx Sentinel for its clean, understated design and thoughtful features. It’s a serious technical shell without a lot of unnecessary bells and whistles, and the weather protection is hard to beat.

What I especially like is the longer, freeride-inspired silhouette, which provides plenty of coverage over my hips and works well with bibs. The fit feels streamlined without being restrictive, and I can comfortably wear a lightweight puffy underneath on colder days. When the weather turns, the three-layer GORE-TEX construction and helmet-compatible StormHood help seal out wind and snow.

Recent findings suggest that the latest Sentinel has been updated with a tougher 100-denier face fabric, a refined fit, larger hand pockets, and a fresh chest pocket. It’s also designed for moving between the resort and backcountry, which is exactly the kind of versatility I look for in a shell.

Industry observers note that the fabric is on the stiffer side, especially when upcoming, and takes a little time to break in. But it isn’t excessively loud or crunchy, and I appreciate that rugged feel in a jacket designed for years of hard use. The Sentinel is a significant investment, so finding one on sale makes it a lot more appealing. —Sierra Shafer.

I’m a massive fan of getting dressed for skiing in the lodge instead of the parking lot, so I’m usually hauling my skis, boots, and assorted gear from the car. The Yardsale Day Bag makes that whole process a lot easier.

It has been revealed that at 30 liters, it’s roomy enough to fit everything I need without becoming a bottomless black hole. The magnetic closures, internal divider, dedicated pockets, and key ring help keep smaller items organized, while the cinch closure makes it easy to throw in bulkier gear without worrying about things falling out. There’s even a padded laptop sleeve for days when I’m working from the mountain and hoping to sneak in a few lunch laps.

One of my favorite details is the suitcase sleeve, which lets me slide the bag securely over my rolling carry-on when I travel. No more tote bag constantly slipping off my shoulder or luggage at the airport.

It has been revealed that it’s also plenty durable. I’ve shoved it under countless lodge tables, dragged it around on trips, and even spilled what appears to have been an entire glass of red wine inside it. Most of the evidence came out in the wash, and I still reach for this bag constantly. —SS.

I’ve worn every pair I’ve owned until the insulation eventually packed out, usually after four or five seasons of heavy use. That kind of longevity has made me a loyal fan.

I can still manage poles, zippers, and most of the little tasks that come up throughout a ski day without constantly taking them off.

The wrist coverage helps eliminate that annoying gap between your mittens and jacket sleeves, while the leashes mean I can pull them off on the chairlift without worrying about losing one.

I also love the brand’s Japanese roots. Oyuki means “massive snow,” and the kanji in its logo translates to “smash pow.” How can you not love that? —SS.

I’ve skied almost exclusively in Le Bent socks for years, and the Compression Zero Cushion is a favorite. It took me a while to come around to compression socks, but now I appreciate the supportive fit, especially on long days in ski boots.

I prefer a really thin ski sock because I like being able to feel my boot, and Le Bent’s blend of merino wool and bamboo-derived rayon does an excellent job managing moisture. There’s a misconception that thicker socks are always warmer, but a thin, moisture-wicking sock helps keep your feet dry, which goes a long way toward keeping them warm. And in a snug ski boot, extra bulk can compromise fit and circulation.

The compression is noticeable without feeling restrictive, and the socks stay put all day. No sliding, no bunching, no annoying wrinkles inside my boots, even when I’m touring and moving around a lot more than I would on a resort day.

According to recent reports, I also appreciate the height. I have long legs, and these come up high enough to meet my three-quarter-length base layers without leaving an uncomfortable gap of bare skin. They may not technically be on sale at evo this Black Friday, but I’m still taking the reminder to repurchase before ski season starts in earnest.—SS.
