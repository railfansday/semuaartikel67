Alphadjo Cisse is one goal away from double figures for the season, and we have not even reached the end of November yet.

Industry observers note that as La Gazzetta dello Sport write, the numbers that Cisse is putting up are those of a centre-forward. He has six goals in 10 Serie A appearances, at an average of one every 74 minutes. In the Europa League he has three (a hat-trick against RB Salzburg), at one every 84 minutes.

Recent findings suggest that yet, his role remains a bit of a mystery. Cisse has his own idea of ​​his preferred position on the pitch, even if he’s a bit vague. Speaking after the match against Frosinone, he hinted that he’d like to move into the attacking midfielder position behind the striker.

In a recent development, “I’d have more fun and entertain the fans. It would also be easier to provide assists for my team-mates, something I enjoy and haven’t yet managed to do at AC Milan,” he confessed to Sky.

In Verona’s youth team, where Cisse first emerged, head coach Paolo Sammarco deployed him as a left-sided attacking midfielder. It was a position in which the then 17-year-old then went on to score 16 league goals in a breakout season.

At Catanzaro, in his first professional stint in Serie B under Alberto Aquilani, the Italian played more or less the same role in a 3-4-2-1 system, sometimes a bit wider. Before the serious injury that caused him to miss the final six months of last season, he has scored six goals in 22 games.

In a recent development, ruben Amorim initially deployed him as a left-sided winger, also due to Christian Pulisic’s temporary unavailability. He would later move him further forward and more centrally, to take up the spaces a striker would, a position he has played for Italy U21s under Silvio Baldini.

In this position, Cisse can utilise his extraordinary ability to position himself between the ball and the defender, before turning, aiming and often finishing first time. Despite not being physically big (at 180cm tall), he is strong and has a low centre of gravity that makes him tough to bump off the ball.

According to recent reports, this is where the dilemma arises. At Verona and Catanzaro, Alphadjo Cisse impressed because of his speed and agility. After the injury and under the watch of Amorim, he has become more of a positioning-based player, barely ever tearing down the flank.

So far this season, he’s attempted only 16 dribbles at just over three per game, and has only succeeded on five. With one successful dribble per game on average, his success rate is just over 31% (FotMob data).

These numbers perhaps help explain Cisse’s progression close to the penalty area, and also a concept that the player himself has repeated in recent months regarding where he is at in his personal development. On Sunday, he declared: “I’m not even halfway to my potential.”.

In a recent development, who knows if Alpha is referring to his overall growth, with horizons still unexplored, or perhaps his post-injury recovery. Amorim will continue to ponder things, but this is far from a headache, because the rule seems to be well established: if Cisse plays, he tends to score.
