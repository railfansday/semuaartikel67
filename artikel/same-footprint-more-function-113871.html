📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that the owners of this 1940s house wanted to add more space for a growing family, but site restrictions would only allow them to build upward, meaning the small home would tower over surrounding houses. To preserve the house’s place in the neighborhood and keep costs down, the solution was to remodel the existing space with a more functional layout, transforming the second floor from two bedrooms and one bath into three bedrooms and two baths while making the home feel more spacious, flexible, and connected.

In a recent development, outside, the remodeled house maintains the same proportions of the original but incorporates modern updates such as the ACRE composite-wood entry accents and the single entry-door sidelight. To improve flow inside, the main staircase was relocated to create a more functional, open interior. Latest sliding glass doors offer more light inside and a seamless connection to the outdoors, maximizing the feeling of space. The basement stairs are now accessed via a hidden floor hatch, preserving the open main living area.

Industry observers note that creating a more functional home within the same footprint means making the most of every inch of usable space. The area under the main staircase now houses deep storage drawers, and a new window seat offers extra space next to the picture window.

On the exterior, the window-seat bump-out lets the house live larger without increasing its footprint. Clad in the same wood as the front entry, the bump-out creates a cohesive and striking connection between the front and back of the house.
