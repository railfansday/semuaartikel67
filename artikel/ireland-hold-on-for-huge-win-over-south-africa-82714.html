Ireland vs South Africa Nations Championship 4:40pm Saturday 21st November.

Robert Baloucoune's brace of tries saw Ireland seal a huge 20-17 victory against South Africa in the Nations Championship.

The bonus point for losing within seven points of Ireland tied the Springboks with New Zealand at the top of the southern hemisphere standings, but a huge points differential separated them.

The world champion Springboks will face France in the final next Saturday at Twickenham but will go in with consecutive defeats for the first time in four years.

After losing to Six Nations champion France 21-19 last weekend, South Africa were expected to overpower Ireland as they did with ease a year ago in Dublin.

It has been revealed that but while the forwards set up both tries for South Africa, the backline attack was poor, as it was in Paris. The Springboks lost the aerial battle, struggled with Ireland's defensive line speed, and were flattered in the end to lose by only three points. The Boks led for only four minutes in the game.

They surged late into the match, with Malcolm Marx crossing on 58 minutes to set up a tense finish.

Despite a barrage of South Africa attacks, Ireland's defence was resolute, with Farrell's side moving above France to second in the northern hemisphere standings, with Les Bleus playing Argentina later on Saturday.

Farrell dubbed the victory "remarkable" and hailed his team for their performance against the world champions.

"These boys have done a lot of exceptional things over the years, but certainly in the last 12 months [this is the best]," the Ireland head coach said.

It has been revealed that "You always want to test yourself against the best. They are world champions, and not only that, they are the world No 1, and rightly so.

In a recent development, "This was always a challenge we were going to look forward to, and see if we could front up. These games matter.

"The game wasn't perfect, but it was never going to be. Most of the games at the top level aren't.".

Former Ireland captain Brian O'Driscoll noted to ITV:.

"We've been treated to a number of these major days in the last 10 years in Dublin, but this one is right up there.

"I mentioned before the game, South Africa are completely the best team in the world, with the consistency they've shown.

"Ireland today were outstanding. You have to credit Ireland rather than saying where it went wrong for South Africa.

"Ireland looked the better attacking team, and they won the battle in the air.
