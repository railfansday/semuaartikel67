As a Solo Traveler, I’ve Booked Tours Around the World—But This Fjord Excursion in Upcoming Zealand Took My Breath Away

Industry observers note that after taking the 17-hour Air Newly introduced Zealand flight from New York City to Auckland this May, I was determined to make the most of my first visit to New Zealand. I hopped around the North Island, relishing Auckland’s food scene, checking into the innovative Hotel Britomart, and visiting Wellington’s Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa. But when it came to the South Island, I realized the points of interest are far more spread out, with the massive Southern Alps to navigate through. The island has wide-open roads, but as an American conditioned to driving on the “wrong” side, I didn’t trust myself behind the wheel. So I turned to my favorite solution as a FOMO-fueled traveler who never wants to miss a highlight: scouring the GetYourGuide day-tour offerings to string together my ideal itinerary, a practice I’ve now participated in on all six continents.

Recent findings suggest that I booked several day trips, from an Arthur’s Pass train tour starting in Christchurch to a Walter Peak farm tour in Queenstown. And while New Zealand’s natural beauty and the genuine Kiwi kindness made every experience enjoyable, the highlight was my grand finale: the Milford Sound Business Class small-group tour starting in Queenstown from a local operator called RealNZ. The round-trip 12-hour excursion brings guests in a glass-roof Mercedes Sprinter for stops along the iconic Milford Sound Road, then aboard a Milford Sound cruise through the fjord known as Piopiotahi in the Indigenous Māori language, which some consider to be the eighth wonder of the world.

I was eager to see what “business class” would look like on a day trip, and the moment the doors to the Sprinter van opened, I was impressed. Each of the 14 black leather seats had a gray lambswool blanket, a plush green pillow, and a bottled shot of Manuka ginger Vitamin C boost juice. As a ginger shot addict who’s constantly searching for the best natural pick-me-up around the world, it was love at first sight.

Industry observers note that the long journey ahead—five hours each way—immediately shrank as our amicable guide provided context about where we were, explaining Milford Sound’s history, ecosystems, and Indigenous Māori connections. When all was quiet in the car, there was Starlink Wi-Fi to keep travelers entertained by their screens. After a quick refresher stop an hour in at the rural settlement of Garston, we reached the lakeside town of Te Anau, where one of its downtown shops served as our business class lounge.

Here, the coffee order I’d requested at the top of the day was already waiting in a covered reusable mug with my name on it, alongside a plate of pastries. It wasn’t long before we entered Fiordland National Park, part of the UNESCO World Heritage site Te Wahipounam. I looked up through the translucent roof as foliage from both sides of the road enveloped our van, winding through the gentle curves.

Emerging through the Homer Tunnel, the writer stopped for this first glance of Milford Sound.

Soon, the narrow road opened up, and we made our first stop at Eglinton Valley, an expansive glacier-carved flat where expansive fields of golden tussock grass are broken only by striking triangular peaks. Not long after were the aptly named Mirror Lakes, where the Earl Mountains are reflected near-perfectly in the glassy, still water; and Falls Creek, a craggy cascade that unfolds into a teal pond. During our stop at Monkey Creek, we spotted a couple of kea, the world’s only alpine parrots known for their playful behavior. Next, we drove through the Homer Tunnel, traveling less than a mile through the solid rock of Darran Mountain. Fittingly, our guide changed the soundtrack to the Jurassic Park theme as we dramatically emerged into Milford Sound at last.

Industry observers note that as we descended through the twisty road into the Cleddau Valley, there were gasp-worthy sights at every turn. One minute we were heading toward a daunting granite mountain wall and the next we were looking out to the waters of the sound in the distance. After a quick stop at a bridge to catch a glimpse of Fiordland's highest peak, Mount Tūtuko, in the distance, we arrived at Milford Sound Visitor Terminal to board the MV Sinbad.

A bridge across the Tūtoko River, which led to a view of Mount Tūtoko.

The Cleddau Valley, with Milford Sound in the distance.

Industry observers note that that's when the premium service was cranked up a notch. After I was welcomed with a glass of local Cloudy Bay wine, I was led to my own bean-shaped wooden table with two cushy swivel chairs on one side and a couch on the other, set against expansive windows that stretched overhead for views of the peaks and falls.

A trio of dishes containing local ingredients, curated by chef Tom Hishon.

Recent findings suggest that then came the five-course tasting menu. Already waiting was a seeded sourdough bun with whipped kelp butter, and it was followed by Fiordland wapiti (a local deer) bone broth soup. Next was a trio of artfully plated bite-sized dishes: paua (sea snail) with black garlic, a bite-sized fried ball made of titi (a local seabird), and roe from Mount Cook salmon. The penultimate dish was wapiti (elk) tartare with potato crisps plus smoked eel. It was all capped off with a “flat white”—an ice cream–topped dessert playing off the popular coffee drink.

Industry observers note that between dishes, the captain of the MV Sinbad talked us through the highlights of Milford Sound, steering us up close to its plentiful cascades, including Sutherland Falls (1,904 feet tall), Lady Bowen Falls (531 feet), and Stirling Falls (508 feet). When he noted that Niagara Falls is only about 180 feet tall, I thought I misheard and went over to ask. But despite the massiveness of Milford Sound, the falls exuded a calmness that overshadowed their height.

In a recent development, the area is home to bottlenose dolphins, Fiordland crested penguins, and humpback whales, and we caught a glimpse of a Newly introduced Zealand fur seal lounging at Seal Rock. Throughout the two-hour cruise, the peaks—some craggy and pointy, others smooth and sloping—were a reassuring constant.

Sources indicate that feeling rejuvenated thanks to both the power of Mother Nature’s beauty and the gourmet dining, we eventually returned to the van, where a generously sized bag of Marlborough Sea Salt Proper Crisps was waiting on my seat along with a Whittaker’s bar, Fresh Zealand’s most famous chocolate. Of course, since it is business class, I also received a refreshing hand towel as I boarded.

It has been revealed that as we began the journey back to Queenstown, I gazed upward, soaking in the view from the car’s glass roof, with a contented smile. A rest stop later, I was so relaxed that I drifted off into a deep sleep. The next thing I knew, we were back in the city. We had spent 10 hours out of the 12-hour trip inside the van, but I hardly realized it thanks to all the luxurious touches at every turn. In this case, the journey was absolutely part of the destination.

The Milford Sound Business Class tour launched in October 2025 as a joint operation between RealNZ and Altitude Tours. I originally saw the offering on GetYourGuide, my preferred tour booking platform for its flexible cancellation policies, but you can also book directly through the RealNZ website. The excursion starts at $383 per person for the business class tour, but a coach version is also available.

The tour I took transferred from Queenstown, but RealNZ also offers the trip from Te Anau, as well as a self-drive experience. There’s also the option to book travel back to Queensland via a 45-minute flight in a fixed-wing aircraft for an additional cost, though it’s weather-dependent. For those seeking deeper immersion, there’s also an overnight cruise through Milford Sound, with the choice of private cabins with en suite bathrooms or shared bunk-style compartments.
