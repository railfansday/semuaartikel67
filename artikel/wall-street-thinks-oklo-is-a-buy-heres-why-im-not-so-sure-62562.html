Wall Street Thinks Oklo Is a Buy. Here’s Why I’m Not So Sure.

Many stocks related to the AI boom trade at premium valuations. Some, however, have seen their rich valuations cut in half or more this year. Oklo (OKLO -4.75%) is one of them.

Oklo specializes in small modular reactors, or SMRs. These nuclear energy facilities can be built more promptly than conventional nuclear power plants. Their small size also allows them to be co-located next to AI data center infrastructure.

AI is energy-intensive. To avoid grid reliance, AI companies have invested aggressively to scale additional energy supply. Meta Platforms (META -5.93%), for example, is partnering with Oklo on a 1.2 gigawatt SMR system in Ohio.

Industry observers note that despite the 70% slide in 2026, Wall Street remains upbeat on Oklo stock. No analysts at the moment have a "sell" rating on the stock. Several have price targets that predict more than 100% upside potential.

Sources indicate that but here's the thing: I'm still not buying Oklo shares. And there's one reason why.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

I am a substantial fan of SMRs as a technology. Research from Bank of America suggests that SMRs will play a role in the world's long-term energy landscape.

In a recent development, the problem is that SMR scaling will likely take decades. That makes Oklo a suspect investment, even at today's prices.

Oklo's business model doesn't just include manufacturing SMR systems. The company also intends to own and operate the assets. That may create more long-term revenue and profit potential. But it also makes Oklo's business model more capital-intensive.

In a recent development, analysts don't expect Oklo to generate positive operating income until 2031 or 2032. That means the firm will need to raise latest capital for years to come. While shares are cheaper after the correction, it is now harder -- or at least more onerous for existing shareholders -- for Oklo to raise more money.

Recent findings suggest that oklo is in a strange position as an investment candidate. As shares get cheaper, it actually becomes harder for the company to finance itself -- something it will likely need to do for the rest of the decade. For that reason, I'm sticking to the sidelines for now.
