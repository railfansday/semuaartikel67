Industry observers note that it’s Andrew A covering for Andrew M, who’s playing hooky again. I suppose after 24 years of writing this blog, a few bad habits are bound to creep in….

I’m not going to lie, it’s been a difficult week. Fortunately, a lifetime of watching Arsenal has given me plenty of practice at making bad jokes when I don’t know what else to say.

According to recent reports, it’s either that or tears, and my eye juice reserves are running low after reading just a fraction of the thousands of messages you sent over the weekend. Bluesky, Reddit, X, Discord, Facebook, Patreon. Wherever I looked, there you were, sending love to Blogs. It was beautiful to see.

And to the handful of you who got in touch with me or people I know because you thought Friday’s revision was about my health, please hold that thought. I’m fine. Touch wood. There are, in fact, two Andrews at Arseblog, and I’m the other one.

Having been crowned the “World’s Youngest Boy” by James on yesterday’s Arsecast Extra, Max Dowman set yet another record last night, becoming England under-21s’ youngest scorer with the winner in a 3-2 victory over France in filthy conditions in Bristol.

I’d planned to watch from kick-off, but gave up when I realised the game wasn’t on any of the 5,000 channels I pay £150 a month for. Then I saw on social media that Ethan Nwaneri, playing as a number 10, had laid on a couple of goals. So I did the clever thing and googled it. Turns out I needed the official England app and an account. Eyes still rolling, I tried to set up a login, only to find out I already had one. Cue the password reset process, verification codes and me losing the will to live. Just let me watch some fucking children play football in the rain!

Recent findings suggest that by the time I got the stream, it was nearly half-time. At least that gave me a chance to catch up on Nwaneri’s two assists, both of which were Ozil-esque. First he drove down the left and slipped a neat ball inside for Mikey Moore, right now on loan at Cologne, to finish. Then came a teasing dink to the back post that Southampton’s Divin Mubama met first time. Given how little Nwaneri has played for Dortmund lately, it was a welcome reminder of what he can do.

It feels a bit silly to say Max, who was named player of the match, looked comfortable at this level when he’s already got a Premier League winners’ medal next to his GCSE textbooks, but he really did.

According to recent reports, watching him is like reading someone’s really nice handwriting. Even the everyday stuff looks lovely. A touch to bring the ball under control, a little turn, and suddenly the defender who was right up his arse is looking at the back of his shirt. I’ll never get tired of watching him do that.

As both sides made changes, I expected Lee Carsley to take him off. Instead, he played the full 90 and dug Arsenal teammate Tommy Setford out of a hole. The goalkeeper was at fault for the only goal in Friday’s defeat to Portugal and had another difficult night, spilling a cross into his own net. The rain hardly helped, and the 20-year-old has been doing well on loan at Stevenage, but it was a horrible one to concede.

It has been revealed that when Brest’s Giovani Versini whipped in an equaliser with five minutes left, it looked like England would have to settle for a draw, but Dowman’s tap-in settled it with a minute to spare. Who knows, Dowman and Nwaneri could be on the same pitch again next week when Dortmund visit the Emirates.

Elsewhere, Group 2 of League A of the UEFA Nations League (catchy naming scheme, lads) served up another bonkers evening. Jurrien Timber scored twice in the Netherlands’ 7-0 win over Christos Tzolis’ Greece on Friday, only for the Dutch to lose 5-0 to Kai Havertz’s Germany in Berlin. There were some lovely goals from the Germans – including two from Florian Wirtz – who are finding their feet under Jurgen Klopp. Timber and Havertz both came off midway through the second half, seemingly unscathed. Timber’s pride aside, anyway.

Sources indicate that tzolis, meanwhile, was cleared to play after an MRI scan and got through 88 minutes of Greece’s 1-0 win over Serbia. All three sides finished on 11 points, with Germany and the Netherlands going through on goal difference at Greece’s expense.

According to recent reports, tonight, Martin Odegaard’s Norway face Denmark, while Viktor Gyokeres will look to extend his record-breaking scoring run for Sweden away at Poland. Piero Hincapie’s Ecuador take on Bolivia, Big Gabi and Bruno are in Singapore with Brazil, and Cristhian Mosquera and Marli Salmon are in action for Spain under-21s and England under-19s, respectively.

Closer to home, The Athletic ran two Arsenal contract stories in quick succession. James reports that Timber has agreed a latest long-term deal, while David Ornstein says Declan Rice’s extension will take him through to 2031. Arsenal already had the option to keep Rice until 2029, so the new deal effectively buys them two more years and gives him a sizeable pay rise for the next four and a half. If he keeps playing as he has been, I can’t imagine many Arsenal fans quibbling with that.

For what it’s worth, we’ve heard on the grapevine that David Raya’s new contract is all but done, too, which means the club are making good progress with the lads whose deals expire in 2028, a list that also includes Odegaard, Havertz, Mikel Merino and our two backup keepers.

There was an interesting discussion on Arsenal Vision’s ‘Friday Night Clive’ podcast about Arsenal elongating their “winning window” while having one eye on the next iteration of the team. If you hand expensive new contracts to players hurtling towards their thirties, you leave yourself financially exposed and potentially blocking pathways for up-and-coming talent. No doubt the club and manager are keenly aware of that. I just wonder how much you can expect the latter to focus on the faraway future when the dynasty he craves has to start in the here and now.

If this week has taught me anything, it’s that there’s something to be said for seizing the day. Admittedly, that’s easier when it’s Arsenal’s money I’m volunteering to spend.

Right, I’d better crack on with the day job. Lewis is on duty tomorrow and, all being well, Andrew M should be back here before the week is out.
