In a recent development, arguments with your partner typically follow a general sequence of events: Something upsets you. You bring it up to your partner. You discuss (or argue). You go back and forth. You eventually work it out. You have wonderful makeup sex. And then—if the argument wasn’t super serious and you were able to hear each other out—you move on (give or take a few “I find it funny how...” monologues and a venting Facetime session with your best friend).

But what if after your conversation and orgasm, you find yourself still upset with your partner? What if, even after putting the argument in the “done” pile, you feel lingering emotions of pain and anger?

Recent findings suggest that while I’m a firm believer that makeup sex is the best stamp of completion for every argument, this isn’t always the case for every relationship. Sometimes you don’t realize that the argument hurt you deeper than you realized—and even a hot and heavy session can’t mask it.

“While some couples experience sex as a bonding and often reparative experience, it may not solve the underlying process of disconnection or conflict in a relationship,” licensed marriage and family therapist Molly Mindel tells Cosmopolitan.

She explains that while sex can be an incredibly powerful tool for connectedness and regulation, it can’t repair bigger challenges that may be at play—no matter how good the sex is.

According to recent reports, you typically only have makeup sex... when you think you’re making up with your partner. And for many of us, we genuinely believe that’s the case when it happens. But sometimes, we get so caught up in the emotions and connectedness of the sex that we realize we actually don’t feel fully moved on from the argument.

In a recent development, mandel explains that there are both physiological and psychological reasons sex can make us feel temporarily closer to our partner and less upset about whatever we were arguing about. .

It has been revealed that “For one, sex temporarily changes our neurochemical and emotional states,” she states. She explains that oxytocin and dopamine are released during sex, which can make us momentarily feel really good and satisfied. But these chemicals don’t change our foundational relationship beliefs, unmet needs, or feelings about an argument.

“Sex can also regulate ‘attachment distress,’ meaning we get very close and feel very secure temporarily to our partner (maybe even in ways we don't with them outside of a sexual interaction),” she explains. “But after sex is finished and we get back to our relationship, that sense of feeling understood or cared for or connected to can fade, which can cause emotional distress and alter our behavior in the relationship, bringing us right back to our regular cycle of conflict.”.

Think of post-nut clarity. While we get swept up in our horniness, makeup sex is simply a band-aid that will typically fall off once the sex is over. And even mind-blowing sex is not enough to keep the illusion going.

Sources indicate that it’s not just about the makeup sex. The makeup sex represents the finishing of an argument. And because you already talked about it (and f*cked about it) you should be completely over it, right?

It has been revealed that well, not necessarily. You shouldn’t feel guilty about having unresolved feelings after an argument, especially if you didn’t feel like all of your concerns were addressed.

“Lingering anger, resentment, and hurt days or weeks after an argument with your partner can be a normal part of conflict, and aren't always things we need to rush to ‘solve,’” Mandel states.

Recent findings suggest that mandel explains that it’s common for people to want to leave conflicts with a sense of resolve—“almost as if we tied a nice bow on the conversation.” But our arguments, like our relationships, are never perfect. It’s less vital that an argument is resolved in a certain time period and more that both you and your partner feel heard, respected, and valued—no matter how long it takes.

So if you feel mad even post-makeup sex, that’s totally normal.

“It's worth reflecting on whether the lingering hurt is discomfort from the argument and disconnection, or if there's genuine lack of resolve around a serious concern or emotion needing attention,” Mandel suggests.

Recent findings suggest that if the argument represents a larger pattern in the relationship, this may be the reason you still feel distress even after you “talked it out.” You may have discussed the specific instance, but still need to address the bigger issue at hand. Even if the argument was about something singular, sometimes you just feel like you haven’t fully noted your piece.

Recent findings suggest that ”In relationships, conflicts are messy, complicated, and cannot be solved in a matter of moments,” Mandel indicates. “Usually, there is something left unsaid, a feeling that was left unaddressed, or one that comes up later, which can be uncomfortable and sometimes painful.”.

Recent findings suggest that if your makeup sex didn’t seem to tie the argument together, you may be worried that nothing will. Don’t panic. Just because an argument leaves some lasting discomfort doesn’t mean that your relationship is doomed—it just means that you need to find a different method to approach the disagreement.

“Sometimes the powerful and healthy thing to do is to say, ‘We may not resolve this entirely’ and ‘Can we learn to be okay with that open ended-ness together for now?’”.

In a recent development, and from there, you have to work with your partner to figure out how to move forward. Mandel suggests revisiting the conversation when both partners are calm and emotionally available. This way, she says, both parties will be able to approach the issue with curiosity rather than blame.

According to recent reports, like any argument, it’s going to take a lot of communication and then checking in after the fact. Just keep reminding yourself that there actually is no linear path to resolving conflict. And if both people prioritize each other during a discussion, there’s almost always a way through—even if it does take a few more conversations than expected.

The good news, though? Once the argument really is resolved, and you leave feeling heard and attended to, there’s always more makeup sex to look forward to.
