Latest York bill would tax prediction markets at 10% as court lets Kalshi keep operating

Sources indicate that newly introduced York State Senator Jeremy Cooney has filed a bill that would bring prediction markets into the state's tax base instead of banning them. The proposal puts a state lawmaker at odds with New York's own lawsuit against Kalshi.

Revenue raised under Jeremy Cooney’s FUTURES Act would flow into a Futures Fund for Early Education to pay for universal pre-K.

It has been revealed that the timing gives the bill extra weight. Attorney General Letitia James had sought to stop Kalshi at a virtual hearing held the day the bill was filed. Governor Hochul and James announced on July 31, 2026, that Upcoming York had sued KalshiEX LLC for running an illegal gambling operation through its prediction market.

Cooney’s bill states that CFTC-registered markets “may operate within the state of Upcoming York lawfully.” That language sets it against the state’s executive branch. It also marks a shift from his earlier bill, S8889, introduced in January. That version would have required operators to get a license from the Department of Financial Services. “I’m placing a bet on the future of UPK in New York,” Cooney noted in his release.

It has been revealed that licensed mobile sportsbooks face a far heavier tax than the bill proposes. The gap could widen in practice. Operators would be allowed to deduct some operating expenses from taxable revenue, which sportsbooks cannot do.

Sources indicate that the scope is narrow. The bill covers sports contracts only, so election, economic and culture markets fall outside it. Its official summary also covers the use of prediction markets in election violations. Elected officials and some state employees would be barred from trading.

The consumer rules borrow from regulated betting. Operators would have to verify identity and age, run self-exclusion programs, and offer deposit limits and cooling-off periods. They would also file a yearly consumer-protection audit with the attorney general. The minimum trading age, however, would be left to federal authorities instead of Newly introduced York’s sports-betting minimum of 21.

In a recent development, age also came up in court. The state argued that Kalshi lets younger consumers bet. Justice Crane asked the company to consider safeguards for users aged 18 to 20. Kalshi’s lawyers noted the company could look at limiting access to its contracts for those users in Upcoming York.

Latest York is not alone in treating prediction markets as a taxable business. North Carolina’s new law recognizes the CFTC’s exclusive federal authority over prediction markets. It taxes platforms at 6% of net trading-fee revenue from state residents.

In a recent development, A judge denied Upcoming York's TRO against Kalshi. Hours later, New York introduced a bill to regulate and tax prediction markets.

That money would go toward wildfire costs. If more states follow, platforms such as Kalshi and Polymarket, along with their licensed sportsbook rivals, would face higher costs.
