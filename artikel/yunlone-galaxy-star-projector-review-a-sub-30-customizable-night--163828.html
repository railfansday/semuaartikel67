YunLone Galaxy star projector review — a sub-$30 customizable night light

The YunLone Galaxy Projector is an inexpensive but effective way to bring the marvel of space into your living room or bedroom. Costing around $28, this star projector can display beautiful dancing auroras across your walls and ceiling, and thanks to app support, a huge range of color options are available. In fact, the color combinations you can display here are almost infinite.

In a recent development, with built-in sounds, a Bluetooth speaker and endless customization options, this really is an all-singing, all-dancing star projector. We're amazed at just how much is loaded into this package for such a low price. It's surprisingly good quality, too, with the unit itself being sturdy and made out of hard-wearing plastic.

This isn't a scientific star projector, of course. For that, we'd recommend the Sega Toys Homestar Classic or the Hommkiety Galaxy Projector. But even though the YunLone Galaxy Projector won't give you an accurate view of the Northern Hemisphere's night sky or a close-up of the moon, it's still a projector we recommend. If you're looking to create a relaxing atmosphere, it's one of the best star projectors we've reviewed.

The YunLone Galaxy Projector doesn't have the most attractive design: It's clearly built for functionality rather than aesthetics. If an aesthetically pleasing projector unit is what you're looking for, perhaps consider the Jiawen Galaxy Projector Light. You won't find nearly as many functions as the YunLone Galaxy Projector packs in, however.

According to recent reports, that isn't to say that the YunLone Galaxy Projector is ugly, though. The design is basic, with a circular shape that houses a clear globe in the middle that acts as the projector. It sits on rubberized feet, so it doesn't easily slip or move around, and it's naturally tilted, so it points slightly diagonally rather than straight up. Given the wide reach of the projector, it helps spread the aurora lights across your entire room.

It has been revealed that the projector has a solid build quality: It's plastic but feels sturdy and well-made. If you're buying for a child's bedroom, you don't need to worry about little hands being too rough when using it. Those rubber feet help prevent it from being knocked off a desk or bedside table, too.

Sources indicate that as already mentioned, the YunLone Galaxy Projector isn't a scientific star projector. If you want to recreate actual constellations and close-ups of planets on your bedroom ceiling, this is not the projector for you. But if you're looking for beautiful, mesmerizing aurora-style patterns, it's a great choice.

Once you're connected to the app, you can control exactly what colors the projector displays. Not only can you choose from a number of preset arrangements — labeled as seasons, colors or holidays, so you can create a mood that matches the time of year or how you feel — but you can set your own entire color scheme by choosing from a color wheel. You can also set the brightness, whether the lights will have a "breathing" effect, and how fast they ripple across your ceiling.

Sources indicate that it's a very neat touch, and it goes beyond what we'd expect to find in a star projector in this price range. If you don't want to bother with app connection, you can still set a range of colors and options from the remote control, but the options here are more limited.

In a recent development, the YunLone Galaxy Projector comes equipped with a laser, so it can project green laser stars onto your walls and ceiling. We didn't love the look of these, though, so it's good that you can turn them off independently of the aurora lights. You can also change the laser's brightness if you'd like the stars to appear dimmer.

Both the laser stars and the aurora projections have a very wide reach, covering most of the walls and ceiling of the bedroom we tested the unit in. Obviously, this will vary depending on the size of your room and how far away from the ceiling/wall you place the unit, but we were pleasantly surprised by the coverage offered by this projector.

According to recent reports, the YunLone Galaxy Projector has a companion app that can be downloaded onto an Android or Apple smartphone. Once the app is set up and paired with your unit, you have full control over the colors, speed and brightness of your projections.

Recent findings suggest that the pairing process isn't entirely straightforward, however, with no simple way of forcing the projector to enter pairing mode. The instructions tell you to swiftly turn the unit on and off three times, but it took several attempts before we successfully put the projector into pairing mode.

Thankfully, once that's done, it should stay paired with your smartphone permanently so you won't have to repeat the process. And the app itself is very easy to use, working seamlessly to change the colors of the projection unit.

It has been revealed that the bundled remote control (which requires two AAA batteries, not included) is a little more complex than most, offering a range of buttons to control both the built-in speaker and the light itself. We'd highly recommend opting for the app option as it gives you much better control over the unit, but if your smartphone is unavailable, you can at least set basic color and speed with the remote.

You can connect a music player to the projector via Bluetooth to play your own audio through its built-in speaker. The quality is decent rather than outstanding: It obviously doesn't match the sound of a specialist speaker, but it's as good as we'd expect any $28 speaker to be. It lacks the bass that music requires to sound "complete" but will be ideal for listening to audiobooks and podcasts.

In a neat touch, the YunLone Galaxy Projector also has eight built-in sound effects, including white noise, waves, lullabies and a storm, which some users may find relaxing and good to fall asleep to.

We have been thoroughly impressed with what the YunLone Galaxy Projector offers. Its projections are beautiful auroras that dance across your ceiling, and the ability to fully customize the color and speed is something that we really didn't expect to find at a $28 price point.

If you're on a budget and looking for a non-scientific projector to use to relax and unwind with, we really don't think you can find a better unit. If, however, you want something that offers more scientific projections, you'll need to look elsewhere.

Sega Toys' range of projectors, including the Homestar Flux and the Homestar Classic, is excellent but comes with a much bigger price tag. The Hommkiety Galaxy Projector is worth considering too, offering realistic projections but with a price tag closer to $50.

Industry observers note that the good thing about these projectors is that they come with interchangeable discs, able to display various images of space. The Hommkiety Galaxy Projector has several discs in the box, but you'll need to buy extra discs for the Sega Toys range, so that’s an extra cost to factor in.
