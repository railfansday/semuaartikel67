An unprecedented off-pitch development has emerged ahead of the upcoming South Wales Derby between Swansea City and Cardiff, which has forced Sunday's hosts to initiate an investigation.

Both sides will want little distraction as preparations reach a head for Sunday's crunch South Wales Derby showdown, the first in close to two years after Cardiff's relegation to League One at the end of the 2024/25 Championship campaign.

The Bluebirds actually won the most recent derby, having romped to a shock 3-0 rout over their fierce rivals in January of last year, though the odds are somewhat against them this time around.

Neither side is heading into the derby in encouraging form, with Swansea remaining inside the play-offs despite failing to win any of their last four games amid Vitor Matos' frosty departure to Strasbourg during the international break.

Cardiff, meanwhile, have fallen to defeat in each of the last two against Norwich City and Preston North End, and have not won at Swansea since the 2020/21 term under Mick McCarthy.

Away from the pitch, however, a newly introduced situation has transpired just days before derby day, leaving Swansea to launch an investigation that will remain internal for the time being.

As revealed on Thursday evening by the Daily Mail, the Swans have started an internal probe after an inappropriate image of one of the club's players was shared across social media.

The image, which was first shared by a Cardiff supporter and began doing the rounds on X but seems to have been initially taken via Snapchat before being shared through a WhatsApp screenshot, appeared to show a Swansea player fully naked standing beside a bed.

It has stirred up a huge reaction online, prompting plenty of discourse between both Cardiff and Swansea fans ahead of Sunday's ill-tempered affair.

Ultimately, though, the image was in violation of X's rules, and has since disappeared online.

Swansea have confirmed that an internal investigation will be undertaken in order to gather the full story, which includes intentions to speak to the player in question.

It's unclear exactly how the situation will develop right now, and Swansea's immediate focus will be discussing it with the player and establishing the facts.

After that, however, there is every chance that a legal probe could arise.

That's because, in both England and Wales, sharing naked or intimate photographs without consent is a criminal offence under Section 66B of the Sexual Offences Act 2003.

Meanwhile, if an investigation were to reveal that the image has been tampered with ,including AI-generated 'deepfakes' - one of many allegations made surrounding the picture itself - then that is also classified as a criminal offence under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.

Swansea will be taking the investigation seriously, yet the immediate preference will be to go about proceedings away from the public eye and attempt to block out all the noise ahead of Cardiff's visit to the Swansea.com Stadium.

The Swans are beginning a new era following Matos' sudden departure, and remain under the temporary management of Ashley Williams alongside Joe Allen, Leon Britton and Kristian O'Leary.

All four are, of course, bonafide legends among the Jack Army faithful who all know exactly what it takes to compete in one of the country's most impassioned and high-stakes derby fixtures, and their presence in the dugout may only benefit the hosts.

Swansea do need to turn their fortunes around if they want to stay in and around the top-eight and there's no better time to start that than against Cardiff, who will be desperate to finally clinch their first win on the road all season while seizing a major scalp over their rivals.
