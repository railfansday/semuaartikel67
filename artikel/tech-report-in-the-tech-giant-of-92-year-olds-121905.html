Recent findings suggest that I recently visited a couple of 92-year-olds, one in Upcoming Jersey, the other in Florida. Both are spry, funny, and ready to die. But neither is in a big rush to do so.

Fresh Jersey was our first stop. Anne and I met 25-plus years ago at a workshop she was co-leading in California and have stayed in touch since. Over the years, we attended a few workshops together, the most memorable of which was in Seattle, where we ditched a talk and laughed ourselves sick in the tiny dorm room we shared.

Today Anne lives in a one-bedroom apartment in a town across the river from Latest York City. She never had kids, and her few relatives live far away. She’d been introduced to Dave, my new(ish) fella, by phone a while back, but this was their first face-to-face. I caught a whiff of flirtatiousness during their phone meeting, which was rapidly and delightfully confirmed in the flesh.

Sources indicate that I’ve traveled with Anne enough to have a decent sense of her natural flow. She’s more purposeful than she is slow, taking a leisurely pace with her morning ablutions. She also knows what she needs and when enough is enough.

Sources indicate that one day, for example, she insisted we take her car and toodle up the Hudson River to see the autumn colors. Sitting in a vehicle most of the day wasn’t her idea of fun, so she stayed behind. She offered detailed directions and drew us a map. Upon our return, she plied us with questions about what we saw and what we did on our adventure. We played Rummikub after dinner. She beat the pants off us both, then kicked us out.

Tears were shed as we said our goodbyes. Anne and Dave put their hands on each other’s shoulders, declaring themselves sister and brother of the heart, then folded me into their tight embrace.

Then we boarded the train to Florida, where my 92-year-old friend Bob and his daughter picked us up at the station. He and I met over 30 years ago, when my ex and I hung out regularly with a pack of folks in rural Oregon. After his much-adored wife died eight years ago, Bob relocated to be near family, and I hadn’t seen him since.

It has been revealed that we picked up where we left off, seamlessly folding Dave into civil discussions of the upcoming election and a long list of outings Bob proposed. Our number-one choice was the Kennedy Space Center.

Industry observers note that we three boarded a bus from the parking lot to the Apollo/Saturn V Center, located a few miles from the main Visitor Center. As we exited the bus, our driver suggested we step to the front of the line for our return trip so we could sit together and avoid having to walk to the back of the bus. Given how many miles we ended up covering on foot that day, it was a kind, but unnecessary accommodation.

Recent findings suggest that spry is Bob’s middle name. We traipsed through the entire campus, then he insisted we experience the Space Shuttle Vertical Motion Simulator, which recreates the experience of liftoff. We were shaken, squished, and elevated, each of us happy to have chosen less rigorous careers. A long day well spent.

During our time together, these two nonagenarians indicated us what it means to be warm, vital, and utterly companionable seniors. They’re both good drivers and mentally sharp. Neither complained about physical ailments nor expressed regret about how their lives had unfolded, including the tough stuff they’ve both endured. Rather, they celebrated our time together, our shared history, and the satisfaction of sharing their amazing lives.

I strategy on visiting Anne again next spring, so our goodbyes felt temporary. Leaving Bob, however, we acknowledged this was likely our last time together in person. We hugged tearfully and agreed to connect on the other side.
