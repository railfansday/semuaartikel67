These $90 Corduroys Are So Buttery You Could Baste a Turkey With Them

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, if there was one thing I could’ve told you about my winter wardrobe a month ago, it was that I was good on corduroys. In the past decade, I’ve acquired double-knee corduroys, patchwork corduroys, five-pocket corduroys, straight-leg corduroys, corduroys that are also painter’s pants, corduroy climbing pants and not one but two pairs of white corduroy shorts. I’m tapped out. I went through all the secret exits, my progress bar is a full 100%, and the only thing left for me to do this winter was to enjoy my cords. Just as I was settling into my Cordatorium, however, Banana Republic went ahead and released one of the best pairs of cords I’ve seen in years. So all of that planned restraint went out the window immediately.

It’s an unfortunate situation for a number of reasons, but each one of those is personal, and that’s what therapy is for. Otherwise, I couldn’t be happier because the Loose Pleated Corduroys are even better than I recklessly imagined.

Industry observers note that because they’re made from one of the most buttery, wide-ish wale corduroy fabrics I’ve seen a major brand use in ages, the first thing that you’ll notice about these is how soft they are, because, brother. They are soft. Like foam-pit-, down-pillow-, Jellycat-soft. But lots of things are soft, so let’s talk about the weight, which isn’t suffocating, but far heavier than most woven cotton fabrics— cozy as hell, sure, and weighted like a pair of vintage wool flannel trousers. As a result, the drape is fantastic and they hold their shape all day long.

And let’s talk about that shape. The straight-leg silhouette is inspired by vintage officer chinos—no year specified—which can be a pretty vague reference point. However, my favorite chinos on planet earth are a pair of OrSlow’s that are based on a model from the early-40s and these fit very close to those. They’re loose, with a high-ish waist and a straight leg that plays nice atop everything from boots to derbies to some daintier fare. And in regards to the pleat, it’s not overwhelming for those concerned about that kind of thing, but it also is very much part of the overall tapestry, which I appreciate; a deeper pleat (or multiples) can be a bit polarizing, but this one adds just the right amount of drama without becoming the focal point of the waistband.

According to recent reports, enough so, in fact, that these cords haven’t just cracked my rotation, they’ve been occupying a whole ass starter spot since they showed up. (On this team, we reward performance with minutes. Those are the rules.) And considering it’s only getting chillier, things probably won’t change. I’ll be wearing them to more formal functions with a sweater and some Thiers; I’ll throw them on with my shittiest V-neck or T-shirt to go shoot pool for hours without fear of wayward chalk or spilled Miller (they’re machine washable); I’ll be wearing them in place of jeans to run errands (again, they’re warm); and for doing virtually anything else for the foreseeable future, because they’re just a really good pair of pants.

So, if you’re feeding an irrepressible addiction like myself or just simply need a great pair of winter pants for less than $150, check these ones out. With that, my personal Cordatorium is booming again, and all I can hope for is that Banana stays quiet for the next few months.
