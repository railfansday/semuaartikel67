AppLovin’s reported interest in Criteo and Meta’s intends to explore CTV could put further pressure on independent ad tech, with some companies reporting challenging circumstances in the latest round of earnings calls.

Last week, Reuters reported unconfirmed acquisition discussions involving AppLovin and Criteo, the same day Next in Media reported that Meta was exploring partnerships with television manufacturers and free FAST services to extend its advertising platform into CTV.

If both sets of developments materialize, it would represent two of the largest established (mobile) ad platforms expanding into newly introduced channels using existing advertiser relationships, data, and buying technology.

It’s well understood that AppLovin wants to extend its performance advertising business beyond mobile applications, and Criteo — a company that is reportedly seeking an exit from the public markets — could provide retail relationships, commerce data, and access to advertisers across the open web.

Meanwhile, Meta could use its existing buying platform to help advertisers reach television audiences without relying on a separate demand-side platform.

In recent weeks, separate sources told Digiday this could prove a key advantage in helping the social networking outfit eat into Google’s lead in markets where its DSP, DV 360, dominates.

However, developments could reduce advertisers’ dependence on independent DSPs and supply-side platforms.

AppLovin’s potential shortcut into commerce.

Private equity firms Vista Equity Partners and Quinti Capital have also reportedly expressed interest in Criteo, which has engaged an investment bank to evaluate its options.

AppLovin reported third-quarter revenue of $2.19 billion, up 55% year over year, alongside $1.44 billion in net income.

Criteo reported a 12% revenue decline to $416 million, with net income falling to approximately $6 million.

AppLovin’s growth has been driven by mobile advertising, although the organization is increasingly targeting e-commerce advertisers outside of gaming.

Criteo has spent years expanding beyond retargeting into commerce media, developing relationships with retailers, access to shopper data and cross-channel advertising capabilities.

Per official figures, Criteo’s commerce intelligence draws on more than $1 trillion in annual transactions, 740 million daily active consumers and over 5 billion product SKUs, and acquiring it could accelerate AppLovin’s expansion into retail media, providing relationships and data it would otherwise need to develop independently.

However, integrating the businesses would be complicated given that AppLovin’s core technology is built around mobile apps, while Criteo operates across retailers, publishers, and other commerce environments.

The potential benefits would depend on whether their different data assets, buying systems, and commercial relationships could work effectively when integrated.

Earlier in the year, multiple sources told Digiday that Meta is ⁠exploring an option that would allow advertisers to extend campaigns into third-party television inventory through its existing advertising tools — a measurement tactic commonly known as audience extension.

It has been revealed that the latest reporting suggests Meta is considering how those relationships could support an advertiser-facing buying option, resembling Pinterest’s CTV audience-extension offering, which lets advertisers use Pinterest’s audience data to reach consumers beyond its owned properties. Meta could pursue a similar approach at considerably greater scale.

Its existing advertiser base could access television inventory without establishing separate relationships with specialist CTV buying platforms, an attractive option for smaller advertisers already using Meta’s automated campaign tools.

For DSPs and SSPs, the competitive implications would be significant, as Meta could bring more advertisers directly into CTV, potentially reducing the role of intermediaries that connect buyers with television inventory.

Recent findings suggest that but questions remain about how the proposed model would operate. For example, would Meta determine which inventory advertisers purchase? Plus, how would fees and media costs be disclosed? And what independent measurement would be available?

Such details are likely to determine whether Meta’s proposed offering creates a meaningful alternative to existing CTV buying arrangements or simply adds another layer between advertisers and publishers.

In a recent development, appLovin’s interest in Criteo and Meta’s reported CTV expansion reflect a broader shift in advertising technology, as larger companies with bigger advertiser bases and proprietary data extend their buying capabilities into additional channels. Amazon is pursuing a similar strategy through its DSP and retail media business, which is morphing into a unified agentic platform.

Recent findings suggest that the Trade Desk’s recent financial performance illustrates the difficulties facing some independent players. The company reported a 0.5% third-quarter revenue decline to $736 million, marking a rare contraction after years of sustained growth, and is on course to simplify and offer new measurement capabilities as it attempts to restore growth.

While The Trade Desk’s recent revenue decline cannot be attributed to Meta’s reported looks or AppLovin’s potential acquisition, the contrast provides context for broader competitive pressure on independent buying platforms.

The emergence of unified media platforms is another response, as ⁠last week’s explainer outlined; these systems aim to coordinate advertising across search, social, programmatic, and direct publisher relationships.

But coordinating buying across platforms is not the same as controlling the underlying inventory, data, or optimization technology. Independent companies will need to demonstrate value beyond simplifying workflows, particularly as AI makes campaign execution easier.

For independent ad tech, maintaining a meaningful role in that environment will become increasingly difficult.
