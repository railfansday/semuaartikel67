Freiburg in ecstasy as remarkable winning streak extends to six matches

SC Freiburg head coach Julian Schuster has praised his squad’s extraordinary team spirit following Thursday evening’s dramatic 2-1 Conference League victory over FC Twente. The Breisgauer secured their sixth consecutive competitive victory thanks to a stoppage-time winner from Niklas Beste, with Schuster emphasizing the importance of a united dressing room in navigating a demanding schedule.

The Freiburg trainer made six changes to the starting XI that defeated Elversberg 1-0 in the Bundesliga last weekend.Cyriaque Irié rewarded Schuster’s faith by scoring the opener in the 11th minute, but Twente remained the more threatening side for much of the first half and eventually equalized through a Younes Taha free kick in the 62nd. Freiburg nevertheless found another way to win. Substitute Derry Scherhant earned a penalty in the 89th minute, and fellow replacement Beste eventually converted the loose ball after Matthias Ginter’s spot kick struck the post.

Speaking to a group of assembled journalists after the post-match press conference, Schuster drew attention to the collective celebrations following Beste’s dramatic winner. The 41-year-old suggested that the team’s togetherness gives him the confidence to rotate extensively without compromising Freiburg’s competitiveness.

It has been revealed that “That scene in which the team celebrated together is emblematic of this group,” Schuster told the journalists. “That lends us the strength to rotate the squad with full confidence. One sees this with various clubs that have to deal with extreme pressure during this phase [of the season]. There’s also players who have international obligations. The pressure is extreme.

It has been revealed that “One really can’t leave anyone out,” Schuster continued. “We have this depth and there’s always little things that leave one needing to switch it up. Then it’s nice when players are ready to serve on a regular basis and not to get caught up in the competition of who makes the team sheet. That’s extremely crucial and I’m very pleased with the lads who started today and were able to make a contribution to the win.”.

Schuster’s observations build on his earlier comments concerning Freiburg’s exceptional dressing-room culture. In October, the SC trainer emphasized the importance of protecting the club’s special atmosphere and ensuring that difficult personnel decisions do not undermine the collective.

According to recent reports, the same philosophy has also been emphasized by Freiburg’s players. Veteran midfielder Maximilian Eggestein recently discussed the importance of sacrificing individual ambitions for the team, while Germany internationals Philipp Treu and Derry Scherhant credited Freiburg’s supportive club culture when reflecting on their own development.

Goalkeeper Mio Backhaus offered a vivid account of the hectic closing stages, during which Ginter’s missed penalty eventually led to Beste’s winning goal.

Sources indicate that “Plenty of chaos,” Backhaus responded when asked about the penalty scene by Baden FM in the mixed zone afterwards. “For [Matthias] Ginter it was also a difficult situation to [take a penalty] four minutes into injury time. And then there was all this turmoil. It was pure ecstasy that Nikki [Beste] pulled that off after all the commotion.”.

Recent findings suggest that “The fact that there were so many of us involved, so many boots on the ground, that helped us re-orient ourselves as a cohesive unit,” Backhaus continued. “The fact that Derry [Scherhant] came on to draw the penalty and Nikki converted [both of them off the bench] also wasn’t a coincidence.”.

Industry observers note that beste had already made an crucial contribution to Freiburg’s previous victory, setting up Igor Matanovic’s winner against Elversberg. The Croatian striker emphasized his instinctive understanding with his teammate after that match, highlighting the familiarity that has developed between Freiburg’s attacking players and the special chemistry emerging within Schuster’s squad.

Club captain Christian Günter similarly regarded the celebrations after Beste’s winning goal as a reflection of the team’s identity.

It has been revealed that “This scene describes our season up to this point effectively,” Günter answered when asked about the celebratory scenes by Kicker’s Carsten Schröter-Lorenz in the mixed zone afterwards. “From solidarity to the fact that everyone wishes everyone else all the best. That the team stands above everything else and that we never give up. And that’s something we’ll have to embody until the very last minute of the very last matchday.”.

“Irrespective of what the situation is, we must remain positive, supportive of each other and stick together,” Günter continued. “One can see that we have the quality and have proven that we can get through games that aren’t exactly pretty and require a lot of fight. We’ve got to preserve that and then we’ll get good things and a good season.”.

Günter’s assessment closely resembles Matanovic’s recent observations about Freiburg’s growing ability to grind out difficult victories. Speaking after the narrow triumph over Elversberg, the striker explained how Freiburg have developed the resilience necessary to prevail in matches that might previously have ended in frustrating draws.

Industry observers note that thursday’s encounter with Twente offered another example. Much like the Elversberg fixture, Freiburg struggled against an opponent that enjoyed lengthy spells of superiority but nevertheless managed to claim all three points.

Industry observers note that the victory extended Freiburg’s winning streak to six competitive matches and marked their 14th consecutive European home victory. The latter run began under former trainer Christian Streich in November 2023 and has continued under Schuster.

With Schuster able to trust both his starters and substitutes, Freiburg’s collective mentality continues to provide a foundation for their success across three competitions.
