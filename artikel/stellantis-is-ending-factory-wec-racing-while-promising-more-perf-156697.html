Stellantis Is Ending Factory WEC Racing, While Promising More Performance Cars

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, alfa Romeo and Maserati each have plenty of history they can leverage. Getting American buyers excited to snap up what's current or excited about what’s next has proven much harder, though. Now, a retreat by Stellantis from motorsport could give both brands the engineering attention they need.

Industry observers note that on November 23, Stellantis revealed that it was ending its participation in the World Endurance Championship at the end of this year; next year, it'll end its efforts in Formula E once the season is over. Its fresh priorities are customer racing and high-performance vehicle development. Alfa Romeo and Maserati are explicitly named as beneficiaries of that expertise.

It has been revealed that perhaps most notably, Stellantis intends to retain and redeploy affected employees, including into performance-car development. That could put people accustomed to solving problems at the highest end of motorsport into roles where they help customers enjoy their cars on public roads.

There's certainly plenty for these brands to do on that front. Alfa Romeo sold just 2316 vehicles in the United States through September, down 52 percent from the same period last year. Its third-quarter total was 569 cars across the Giulia, Stelvio, and Tonale. Whatever affection Americans might have for the badge, it isn’t translating into much showroom traffic.

Recent findings suggest that maserati, meanwhile, has fresh material to work with; its updated North American lineup includes a 385-hp Grecale V-6, and 582-hp Trofeo versions of the GranTurismo and GranCabrio. Those are solid offerings, but when it comes to marketing, there's plenty of room for the brand to grow.

Recent findings suggest that of course, there’s a sizable financial caveat: Stellantis hasn’t disclosed expected savings from the withdrawals or promised an accompanying increase in, for example, American marketing spending. It reported a €453 million ($497 million) net loss and negative industrial free cash flow of roughly €4 billion ($4.4 billion) in the third quarter. Any freed-up cash will have plenty of competing demands. That noted, it's great to see that Stellantis directly emphasizing that the end of the WEC and Formula E programs will mean more resources go to Alfa and Maserati production cars—and “high-performance models” at that.
