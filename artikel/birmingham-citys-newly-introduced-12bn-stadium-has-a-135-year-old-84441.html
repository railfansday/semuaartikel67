Birmingham City's 2026/27 season has got off to a remarkable start on the pitch, with supporters starting to dream about what might be possible under Chris Davies.

The 41-year-old previously split opinion amongst Blues fans, especially after the way in which the 2025/26 Championship season panned out following continued investment from Knighthead Capital Management.

The club's American investors have vowed to change plenty in such a short space of time, and that doesn't just concern the outlook of Davies' squad in the near future as they chase a Premier League return, but the city of Birmingham as a whole.

Tom Wagner has been heralded for the work he has already put in and what he is planning towards as Blues look to finally realise their potential.

The idea of looking at the past to move forward can often be frowned upon, but for Wagner and Co. doing exactly that means that Birmingham fans are never likely to lose sight of their identity as the club embarks on a upcoming era.

Last season may have been one of mixed fortunes for Birmingham and their supporters on the pitch, but that didn't mean it wasn't historic in other ways.

Recent findings suggest that indeed, just days before Blues celebrated their 150th anniversary game with a 4-1 rout over Norwich City, the club unveiled the design and name of its upcoming 62,500-seater stadium, which will stand tall less than a mile away from St. Andrew's @ Knighthead Park.

The Powerhouse will pay tribute to the Second City's rich industrial heritage, embodied by its name and design, and that comes across in more ways than one.

The Powerhouse will be the centrepiece of a multi-billion Sports Quarter project that will transform the Bordesley Green area with multiple commercial and retail hubs adjacent to the stadium.

Whilst the chimney-themed design has received major criticism from rival fanbases and beyond, those associated with the club will love how it will repurpose some of Birmingham's rich history.

Over 1,000 bricks which helped structure the City Hospital in Winson Green - built in 1912 as Birmingham Workhouse Infirmary - are being repurposed for the Powerhouse to stand tall on the former site of the Adderley Park Brickworks.

Blues fans, how excited are you to move into the Powerhouse? Comment below!

It has been revealed that city Hospital has been closed since 2024, with the former site making way for a 750-home development adjacent to the Birmingham and Wolverhampton Main Line Canal, approximately 4.1 miles away from St. Andrew's.

"Lovingly salvaged, cleaned, sorted and restored by local experts, the original bricks from Adderley Park Brickworks will be incorporated into the Powerhouse – Birmingham City's future home and a defining part of the city’s Sports Quarter," a section of a statement on Blues' website confirming the purchase of remarked bricks read.

In recent months, more work has gone into how the overall Sports Quarter and the Powerhouse stadium will look ahead of a planned opening in the summer of 2031.

Sources indicate that many Blues fans are excited by what is to come, as emotional as it will be when the club have to leave St. Andrew's, having called their current stadium home since the year 1906.

In May, mutiple 'CGI' images were generated and shared by Birmingham's official social media accounts, including how various hospitality packages will be incorporated into the chimney designs.

This includes a high-level view of the pitch which will be named the 'Chimney Seats', as well as 'Restaurant with a View', which will allow guests a view of the city's main skyline, which stands approximately two miles away.

As previously mentioned, Knighthead have certainly done their utmost to embrace themselves in various cultural aspects associated with the West Midlands.

It has been revealed that the Sports Quarter will provide thousands of jobs for locals, whilst also increasing the economy. In the meantime, the investment firm has also acquired stakes in Birmingham Phoenix alongside former Blues academy graduate and Real Madrid star, Jude Bellingham.

The effort which has gone into such a transformative project has not been underestimated by Blues fans, and it has been particularly evident how they feel about Wagner in his recent returns to St. Andrew's after unfortunately suffering a stroke in January.
