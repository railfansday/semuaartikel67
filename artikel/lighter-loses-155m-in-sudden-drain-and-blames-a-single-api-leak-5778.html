Lighter loses $15.5M in sudden drain and blames a single API leak

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Perpetual futures exchange Lighter suspended withdrawals on Nov. 16 after a security incident that resulted in an estimated loss of roughly $15.5 million.

Bitquery traced 10.39 million USDC leaving its Ethereum contract, and Lighter stated in a subsequent enhancement that its investigation indicated a leaked API key for a specific trader's account caused the incident.

According to recent reports, it said the protocol and smart contracts were unaffected and that it was targeting a resumption of withdrawals by the end of Nov. 16.

According to Bitquery, the larger loss estimate concerns two market-maker accounts, which did not verify the internal account-loss allegations.

Sources indicate that bitquery identified one withdrawal of approximately 10,393,057 USDC on Nov. 16. The investigation separates that transfer from a second alleged loss of over $5 million. Bitquery says that if those funds left the second account, they had not left Lighter within its Ethereum observation window.

Bitquery's morning Ethereum trace follows one withdrawal through Lighter's contract.

The reported account loss does not establish how much left the exchange or how much can be recovered.

Internal account-loss allegations remain unverified. Ethereum data end around 12:13 UTC on Nov. 16; later movements and recovery are outside this trace.

Lighter's trades happen on its own rollup, which Bitquery indicates it does not read, so its Ethereum trace cannot verify every change to a trading account's balance.

According to Bitquery, five orders on the CoW Swap exchange converted 10.36 million USDC into Ethereum between 10:13 and 10:17 UTC.

Recent findings suggest that bitquery also traced twelve payments of 160 ETH and associated accounts on the Hyperliquid exchange buying the XMR1 token. The firm reported 3,845 ETH remaining in the recipient wallet at its 11:55 UTC snapshot.

It has been revealed that lighter noted it enabled what it calls Guardian mode and prioritized recovering funds involved in an additional withdrawal attempt. Its patch said withdrawals remained paused and that the Lighter Security Council would upgrade the contract.

The revision did not announce a recovered amount or a completed reopening, leaving both the eventual recovery and users' ability to withdraw separate from the funds already traced out.
