Jorge Jesus insisted his Portugal team remained focused on righting the wrongs from their defeat to Denmark in their final Group A4 clash with Wales in the Nations League.

Portugal have already confirmed their spot in the quarter-finals of the competition and make the trip to the Cardiff City Stadium to face a Wales side guaranteed to finish bottom.

However, the Selecao arrive into Tuesday's encounter off the back of a 4-1 defeat to Denmark, with Rasmus Hojlund and Pierre-Emile Hojbjerg both starring in the victory.

Portugal have not lost back-to-back matches since between September and October 2014 (Albania and France), but have a strong record against Wales in their recent meetings.

They have won four of their five matches against Wales in all competitions (L1), including the last three in a row without conceding a goal, though this is the first time they have been hosted in the nation since May 1951, when they were beaten 2-1.

Jesus' side will finish top of the group if they avoid defeat by Wales, or Denmark do not beat Norway, and Jesus is hoping to see a response from his players.

"When you lose, the focus is on reversing the situation in the next game," Jesus told reporters.

"A 4-1 defeat makes everything heavier, and we can't ignore what we did wrong, but we also can't forget the journey we've been on.

"Nobody talks about the fact that we qualified two matches ago, that we could finish in first place, or that we could end up with 15 points.".

Portugal have won 12 of their last 16 competitive away matches (D2 L2), and have lost just three of their last 26 on the road in competitive fixtures (W17 D6).

They face a Wales side that have lost four of their last five competitive matches (W1), which is as many defeats as they suffered across their previous 25 (W12 D9 L4) between March 2023 and March 2026.

Wales lost 1-0 to Denmark in their last home Nations League game but have never lost consecutive home games in the competition, while they last did so in competitive games in defeats to Croatia (1-2) and Serbia (0-3) in March and September 2013 respectively.

Despite their progression already being confirmed, Jesus revealed he would not make wholesale changes to his team.

"Throughout this journey, I have always made changes to the team, usually three or four. We have been playing every three days," Jesus said of his potential starting line-up.

"I'm going to replace two or three of the players who have accumulated the most minutes, because I know some haven't fully recovered.".
