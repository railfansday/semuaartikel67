📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

U.S. officials suspect Iranian hackers may be behind as many as 50 cyberattacks targeting shipping vessels in recent weeks, according to a report by The Wall Street Journal.

According to recent reports, the ships carrying oil, liquefied gas, and other explosive chemicals were hacked as they traveled across Asia and Europe. Several oil tankers headed toward the U.S. coast in August also had their navigation systems breached, prompting cybersecurity teams within the FBI and Coast Guard to board the vessels.

The report remarked officials are investigating if Iran is expanding its recent wave of hacks against the U.S. by targeting large vessels to sow fear among shippers, disrupt routes, or to cause an explosion at a port. (Ships have backup controls in the event that automated systems are hacked or fail.).

According to recent reports, hackers may have been abusing a bug dubbed FortiBleed, which allows hackers to break into networks by compromising vulnerable Fortinet-made routers. A joint FBI-Coast Guard security alert from September warned that Fortinet routers are used on shipping vessels.

Iran has been firing back on all sides since the start of the U.S./Israel-led war earlier this year, which killed the country’s supreme leader. Many of Iran’s retaliations have been in the form of cyberattacks, with U.S. officials blaming Iran for hacks targeting hundreds of American water providers and U.S. medical tech companies.

A spokesperson for the FBI did not respond to a request for comment about the shipping hacks or whether it had a more accurate figure.
