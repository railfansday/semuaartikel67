As luxury Amazon trips grow in popularity, operators are having to go further to deliver what they sell: remoteness. Gateway cities, including Brazil’s Manaus, are sprawling, and lodges that once felt remote no longer are.

As luxury Amazon trips grow in popularity, tourism is adding pressure to the very thing it’s sold on: remote, authentic experiences.

Sources indicate that tour operators increasingly define luxury as access to remote locations and unique experiences. As the industry grows, the gateways to these experiences, and especially to the Amazon in Brazil, are becoming more urbanized — threatening the very thing travelers are there to experience.

According to recent reports, one notable pressure point is waterways. CroisiEurope will debut luxury cruises from Brazil to Colombia next year, and Carnival Corporation’s Seabourn announced a new cruise featuring Amazon river sailing for 2028. Lindblad also chartered a 42-guest ship to expand its presence in the Amazon for this year, doubling its capacity, and Ponant bought a majority stake in Aqua Expeditions, which runs Peruvian river cruises.

Amazonastur, the tourism agency in Brazil’s Amazonas state, expects to see 17,000 visitors including crew.
