Top performers report stronger AI returns, more reliable forecasts and better payback on developing their sellers.

PHILADELPHIA, Nov. 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Richardson Sales Performance, a global leader in sales training and performance improvement, today announced findings from a survey of 1,105 senior sales leaders at large global organizations. The study finds that while AI may have leveled the playing field, it has not leveled the results.

Top performers who met or exceeded quota report better results from AI, with 62% indicating a positive financial return vs. 39% of laggards. Additional findings:.

In a recent development, "Leaders at every performance level see the same market and share many of the same worries about AI," said Andrea Grodnitzky, chief marketing officer at Richardson Sales Performance. "What set top performers apart was what they did next. We conducted the research behind The Revenue Edge so sales leaders can see those practices clearly and compare them with their own.".

The Revenue Edge organizes these divergent approaches with others in forecasting, staffing and skill development into four distinct operating disciplines.

Underneath it all, leaders agree that selling stays human. Seventy percent say humans outperform AI on the activities that matter most in complex deals. Only 4% say AI does. Eighty-eight percent expect the skills AI can't replicate to set their best sellers apart in 2027.

Respondents were based in North America or Europe, hold a revenue leadership, general management or sales enablement leadership role at the vice president level or above, and worked at organizations with at least $750 million in annual revenue. Field dates were Oct. 12 to Oct. 20, 2026. Quota attainment is self-reported, so all comparisons are correlational. Top performers are leaders whose organizations met or exceeded quota (100% or more, n=497). Laggards are those below 90% of quota (n=338). Subgroup margins of error are about 4 points for top performers and about 5 points for laggards.
