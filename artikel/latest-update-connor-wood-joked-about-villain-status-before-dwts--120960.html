Dancing With the Stars’ Connor Wood is sharing his reaction to the internet’s perception of him.

In an interview with The Hollywood Reporter published on Tuesday, November 24, ahead of the season 35 finale, the comedian said that while he was a fan favorite to some viewers, “The rest of the internet, totally different story right now. We’ve gotten to a point where I have become a bit of a villain for some people.”.

Wood, 31, continued, “I think that they think that I personally voted their favorite off, and it’s like — I’m aware of my level of dance skill. I have nothing to do with me staying here.”.

“That’s just been just the dark side of making it as far as we made it,” he went on. “My understanding is that the more that people go home, the more the fans of those people will go to someone else’s page and villainize those people. That’s sad, and it’s just a testament to how invested people are in this show. A cool part of this show is that the fans are so invested.”.

Wood, 31, and professional partner Rylee Arnold came third in the DWTS season 35 finale on Tuesday night, behind eventual winners Ezra Klein and pro partner Daniella Karagach and second-place runners-up Amber Glenn and Pasha Pashkov.

The podcaster caught the attention of Jennifer Lawrence earlier in the season after he revealed the Hunger Games star was his celebrity crush in an Instagram video. Lawrence, 36, went viral for quipping in the comments section, “I saw you on 8th avenue drinking a smoothie and you paid me no mind.”.

Wood told The Hollywood Reporter that “the 8th Avenue thing, it’s fun for people to be a part of things,” but “being on the other side of that is not as fun.”.

In a recent development, “It’s part of the game. I signed up for it. I’ve never experienced that,” he shared.

Despite struggling with some of the social media commentary about his DWTS run, Wood stated he’s proud to have made a lasting impression.

Sources indicate that “This is weird and sick in the head, [but] I am going to miss every part of this experience. If you have haters — you’ve been around long enough to have a hater — that means you’re doing something really, really well,” he stated. “I’ve been lucky enough.”.

It has been revealed that but the comedian conceded, “I will say, I’m not cut out for public scrutiny. This is not something for me. This will be my last reality show.”.
