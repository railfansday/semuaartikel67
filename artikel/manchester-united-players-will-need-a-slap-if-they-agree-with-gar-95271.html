‘Wake up,’ squawked Gary Neville as he launched into an evisceration of a Manchester United defence that had just capitulated in all too familiar fashion.

The Anfield washing machine, as Mikel Arteta once described Liverpool’s fortress, has accounted for sterner rearguards than the one Mauricio Pochettino has inherited, but there was something particularly abject about this particular 4-0 humbling.

Whether it was the grim acceptance that the eventual scoreline was deemed passable after the most farcical of beginnings to a United managerial tenure, or the immediate removal of any notion that Pochettino has a honeymoon period to enjoy, Neville struggled to compute the reality of the league table as it stands.

‘You play for Man Utd,’ Neville barked, having slapped himself in the face, in surprisingly aggressive fashion, to symbolise the wake-up call his club are in need of.

While he provided an early frontrunner for meme of the season, Neville failed to grasp the full gravity of the situation Pochettino has landed himself in.

Considering the likelihood of United finding themselves in a fully-fledged battle to preserve their Premier League status, Neville was dismissive to the point of arrogance.

‘I don’t believe they’re in a relegation fight,’ he said, without providing a modicum of evidence to explain his confidence.

‘It’s as bad as it’s been for 55 years but I do think they’ll come out of it and move up the table.’.

Listing the likes of Andrey Santos, unable to nail down a starting slot since his £48million move from Chelsea, Amad Diallo, no Premier League goal in approaching a year, Patrick Dorgu, without a place in the redundant wing-back role he was recruited to fill in Ruben Amorim’s doomed system, and Bryan Mbeumo, immediately sacrificed when United were reduced to 10 men yesterday, as the players best suited to fulfil the requirements of Pochettino’s high-intensity ethos should have prompted an immediate Neville U-turn, even before he threw the name of Bruno Fernandes into the mix, while adding the caveat that ‘he can run around, but whether he knows where he’s running is a bit more debatable’.

Make no mistake about it, United are in a dogfight and the situation will only deteriorate further if the dressing room shares Neville’s assertion that this group of players are too good to go down.

Time on the training ground to organise a defence that is now officially the worst in Europe’s top five league is limited and while January will surely bring reinforcements, there is precious little to suggest United have either the funds, or a cohesive enough recruitment strategy to identify the players they require if they are to dig themselves out of trouble.

Sitting second bottom after 11 games is not a small sample size to be dealing with either, especially when you consider the majority of these players were in-part responsible for the multitude of unwanted records that were broken during Amorim’s wretched stint.

Recent findings suggest that it should also be remembered that Spurs had 18 points after 11 games last season and only stayed up on the final day. United have eight points and have already been beaten by two goals or more on six occasions this term in all competitions.

While Neville’s sparring partner Jamie Carragher may have gotten a little ahead of himself in terms of where Liverpool are at this juncture in Andoni Iraola’s rebuild, he was on the money when assessing how United compare to the teams they should be focused on competing with at present.

‘No-one believes they are, no-one will say that they are (in a relegation fight),’ he stated. ‘But the strength of the Premier League this season, what we could call teams that we expect to be mid-table or near the bottom, I think it’s as strong as it’s ever been.

‘We can talk about teams at the top and say it’s nowhere near as strong. Think about teams in the Champions League. Our teams in this league are not in the top four or five in the Champions League, I don’t think. But when you look at the Europa League or Conference League, we expect our teams to walk it!

‘Look at Fulham, going away to Manchester City, 1-1. Leeds go to Chelsea, 4-1. There are no poor teams. There aren’t many great teams in the league but there are no poor teams. And that’s a problem for Man United.

‘Their next league game is Brentford at home. Manchester United are one of the biggest clubs in the world, Brentford are well-run. If Man United beat Brentford at home, I think we’d all look at that and say ‘great result.’ That will not be an easy game.

He added: ‘The only game they’ve won is Ipswich, a newly-promoted team. In that game they went 1-0 down, Ipswich had another chance. That’s the only game they’ve won.’.

Indeed, United are second bottom, not second top, and until everyone connected with the club accepts that reality and accepts last season’s Michael Carrick-inspired renaissance for what it was, a flash in the pan, the route out of this mess will be a bumpy one.
