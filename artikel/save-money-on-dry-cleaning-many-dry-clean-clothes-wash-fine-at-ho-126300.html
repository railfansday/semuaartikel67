Save Money on Dry Cleaning – Many Dry-Clean Clothes Wash Fine at Home

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A “dry clean only” label warns that a garment can’t be washed, and the maker needs evidence to print it. Plain “dry clean” just names one safe way to clean the garment. Wash cotton, linen, and polyester pieces with that label at home, air and steam suits between cleanings, and ask your cleaner about bundle pricing for the rest.

Sources indicate that cleaners charged an average of $4.17 to launder a shirt and $18.89 to clean a two-piece suit in January 2026, according to a survey of 867 dry cleaners commissioned by Cleaner’s Supply. Sending out five shirts and a suit every week costs about $159 every four weeks at those prices.

It has been revealed that both wordings come from the Federal Trade Commission’s care labeling rule, which lets a garment that can be washed or dry cleaned list just one of the two methods. The fabric on the tag and the way the piece is built tell you which plain “dry clean” pieces you can wash yourself.

Cotton, linen, polyester, and cotton or acrylic knits wash well by hand or on a gentle cycle in cold water. Tailored suit jackets, silk, wool, leather, and rayon still belong at the cleaner, since water can shrink, spot, or misshape them.

Sources indicate that make each cleaning last longer, too. Let a wool suit hang on a wide hanger for at least a day between wearings, brush off lint, and blot spills right away. A handheld steamer takes out wrinkles between cleanings, and changing out of work clothes when you get home keeps dinner off them.

At the counter, ask whether the cleaner offers bundle pricing, a loyalty card, or a credit for hangers you bring back. For shirts you still send out, ask for the shirt price on a woman’s cotton button-down rather than the blouse price, which averaged $8.12 compared with $4.17 for a shirt. Paying more for the same job because a shirt is a woman’s is a pink tax.

Industry observers note that start with the shirts, which a cleaner washes in water just as your machine would. Washing those five shirts at home and having the suit cleaned every four weeks cuts that $159 to about $19, in exchange for one more load of laundry a week and the ironing.
