📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Few remnants of fashion history manage to stir up emotions and draw headlines quite like pieces from Alexander McQueen’s revered runways. Rare, technical, and sensorial, these are certainly not your average, everyday pieces of clothing. McQueen runway pieces are often flexed at the world’s top fashion exhibitions at museums around the world. That’s just one of the reasons why the multicolor silk minidress from Alexander McQueen spring/summer 2010 “Plato’s Atlantis” is the star of Christie’s December Luxury Week, where the runway gem will be hitting the auction block with a $150,000 to $300,000 estimate. The sale runs online from November 25 to December 10, 2026.

“Based on what we know about McQueen’s distribution at the time and the types of garments that went into production after a runway show, we estimate there were probably only fewer than two dozen examples of this exact dress produced,” Max Brownawell, head of handbags at Christie’s, tells ELLE. “Then on top of that, the importance of ‘Plato’s Atlantis,’ as McQueen’s final fully realized collection, really can’t be understated. He himself noted that his previous collection was his final as a young man. And so this was his most mature, fully fledged collection that he’d ever produced.”.

Industry observers note that the dress was the fourth look in the runway collection, styled with the iconic Armadillo boots. Designed with a futuristic sculpted drape, covered in prints from the natural world (think: a collage of abstracted imagery pulled from butterfly wings, snakeskin, and insects), the silhouette is a favorite of fashion fans and archivists. Brownawell shares that the piece still has the tag attached and has likely never been worn. The ensemble may very well exceed the estimate: for reference, a “Plato’s Atlantis” look complete with those aforementioned boots sold for $1.5 million including fees at a Fair Warning auction in New York on Sept. 17, 2026, setting an auction record for McQueen.

According to recent reports, “He was going for something that was entirely fresh,” says Brownawell. “This dress is a really prime example of that, the way that the print has been designed to lay perfectly within the architecture of the garment itself is really a spectacular sort of couture level of creation. On top of that, you have this unbelievably architectural draping, which, you know, McQueen has, it’s been said that he really actually draped a lot of these pieces directly onto the forms themselves because it was so complex.”.

Aside from the dress itself, Alexander McQueen’s spring/summer 2010 show was ahead of its time in every way. It was the first show to be livestreamed in real time on the internet, and it was the first fashion show to debut a major pop single during a show; Lady Gaga’s “Bad Romance” premiered during the show’s finale. “McQueen’s legacy has been just growing stronger and stronger every year,” adds Brownawell.

It has been revealed that the dress is hitting the auction block at a time when Christie’s is launching its inaugural fashion auction. “We really believe that the market is at a point right now where people do appreciate the important designs,” he states. “There’s a lot of clothing out there, but there’s also a lot of very important pieces that define what we wear and where fashion is going and where it’s been.”.
