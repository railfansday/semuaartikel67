Croatia U21 vs. Germany U21 preview: Team news and predicted lineups

Germany U21 will look to finish their international calendar on a positive note when they travel to Rijeka to face Croatia on Tuesday evening.

Antonio Di Salvo’s side have already secured qualification for the 2027 UEFA European Under-21 Championship, but November’s fixtures against fellow qualifiers Belgium and Croatia represent an notable opportunity to measure themselves against some of Europe’s strongest teams at this level.

Germany suffered a disappointing 2-1 defeat to Belgium on Friday, leaving them with a point to prove as they prepare for another demanding encounter against a Croatian side that also topped its qualifying group.

Germany entered the November international break having secured their place at next summer’s European Championship, with three consecutive victories in their final qualifying matches ensuring direct qualification.

It has been revealed that however, their preparations for the tournament have not started entirely as planned. Despite enjoying plenty of possession and creating several promising opportunities, Germany were beaten 2-1 by Belgium in Essen on Friday.

Julien Duranville gave the visitors an early lead before Muhammed Damar equalised with an excellent free-kick in the 25th minute. Germany continued to create opportunities after the break, with Damar particularly unfortunate to hit the crossbar twice, but Romeo Vermant ultimately secured victory for Belgium.

The defeat highlighted an issue Di Salvo will be keen to address against Croatia: turning territorial dominance and promising attacking situations into goals.

Germany’s head coach has already indicated that the November fixtures are an opportunity to experiment with different approaches, particularly in their pressing game, while continuing to develop the team’s understanding ahead of next summer.

In a recent development, there have also been opportunities for several less-established members of the squad. Hennes Behrens and Cajetan Lenz made their U21 debuts against Belgium, while Maurice Krattenmacher and Louey Ben Farhat also received their first appearances at this level.

It has been revealed that germany will once again be without several notable names. Nicolo Tresoldi, who scored 10 goals during European Championship qualifying, suffered a concussion in training and has been ruled out of both November fixtures. Goalkeeper Mio Backhaus and defender Hendry Blank are also unavailable following injuries.

Furthermore, six players eligible for the U21s, including Said El Mala, Brajan Gruda, Tom Bischof and Lennart Karl, have been given opportunities with the senior national team.

According to recent reports, nevertheless, there is still considerable quality available to Di Salvo. Muhammed Damar will be one to watch following his impressive display against Belgium, while Paris Brunner and Dzenan Pejcinovic offer further attacking options.

Joshua Quarshie, who captained Germany against Belgium, and Nnamdi Collins will also be notable figures as the visitors look to improve defensively.

Croatia will provide a stern examination for Germany, having also secured qualification for next summer’s European Championship after an impressive qualifying campaign.

Ivica Olić’s side finished top of Group H with six victories, one draw and one defeat, collecting 19 points from their eight matches.

Their qualification was secured with a 3-1 victory over Hungary in September, while they subsequently finished their campaign with a 1-0 win away to Lithuania.

Tuesday’s encounter represents Croatia’s first major opportunity to begin preparations for the European Championship, with Olić keen to test his players against high-quality opposition.

The Croatian coach has spoken positively about facing Germany, describing them as one of the strongest teams at this age group alongside Spain. He also highlighted the quality of their young players, several of whom have already attracted attention from the senior national team.

In a recent development, there will be some notable absentees for the hosts, however. Šimun Hrgović and Marin Šotiček have joined Croatia’s senior squad for their Nations League fixtures, while injuries have also forced changes to Olić’s original selection.

Lovro Zvonarek is among the players expected to provide important quality in midfield, while Adriano Jagušić is another name worth monitoring after scoring five goals during the qualifying campaign.

Croatia will be particularly motivated to make a positive impression against one of Europe’s traditional youth football powerhouses, especially with the European Championship draw scheduled for November 26.

Croatia U21 (4-3-3): Pavlešić; Hodak, Prpić, Mlačić, Krušelj; Zvonarek, Pavić, Jagušić; Tunjić, Verinac, Canjuga.

Germany U21 (4-2-3-1): Seimen; Baum, Collins, Quarshie, Gechter; Lenz, Aseko; Kade, Damar, Brunner; Pejcinovic.
