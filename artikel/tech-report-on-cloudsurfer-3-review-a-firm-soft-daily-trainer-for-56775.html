The Cloudsurfer 3 is a real step forward for On, even though it’s most definitely a daily trainer built with max cushioning for easy miles. It doesn’t have the pop of the brand’s bouncier shoes, and I don’t think it’s trying to. What it has in abundance is a steady, firm-soft (not a contradiction) cushion that holds up over long, easy miles, plus a fit that works beautifully for my feet.

Right now, it’s only available in-store and at selected retailers, but it releases nationwide February 2027.

The headline is Surreal, On’s first supercritical midsole foam, which is EVA-based and sits inside a redesigned CloudTec Phase structure. Supercritical processing infuses the foam with gas under high pressure and temperature, then lets it expand in an autoclave—a machine that uses steam to superheat the environment. That’s the same basic-ish idea behind the “super foams” that have taken over daily trainers, but On had never made it work with its hollow Clouds.

Industry observers note that scott Maguire, president and COO of On, explained why in an interview with Runner-in-Chief Jeff Dengate. “We’ve only ever been able to do CloudTec in EVA in the past,” he noted. “We’ve never been able to do it in a supercritical processing.”.

It has been revealed that the trouble was scale. “Putting Clouds in that, it becomes super small and it has to become very big,” Maguire said. Predicting where each Cloud will form, how far apart they sit, and how they interact “has been impossible for us to do for years.”.

Sources indicate that on’s own numbers put Surreal at roughly 15 percent softer, 15 percent lighter, and 23.3 percent more responsive than Helion, the foam that came before Surreal. The redesigned CloudTec Phase geometry is credited with 15.5 percent deeper compression and a 9.9 percent cut in peak braking force. Those figures come from finite element analysis simulations at On Labs Zurich, so they are modeled results, not data from everyday runners; a grain of salt should be well-applied. What I felt in the ride was a foam that’s soft but has a firm backbone, kind of like a good honeycomb-cushion feeling.

Here’s where my opinion comes in. The upcoming foam is an improvement, but the return doesn’t have quite the oomph of other shoes in On’s lineup. If you’re hoping for a springy kickoff, look elsewhere.

In a recent development, what I love is the character of the cushion. It’s firm-softness—if that’s a thing—which I declare it is. Over long miles, it never felt like it compacted, and it never gave so much that it slowed me down. Since I run more miles than speed, that’s my best case.

Dengate, who has been tough on On’s hole-based tech in the past because slabs of modern foam were beating it, came around: The Cloudsurfer 3 “really does feel like a big improvement in comfort and ride for a daily training shoe.” Gear Editor Amanda Furrer, who admits she found past On trainers kind of blah, rated this one near-perfect. As a cushioned trainer, she remarked she’d have to quote me, saying: “it’s aces.”.

Sources indicate that on gets fit so right. From its super shoes to its ultra trail runners like the Cloudultra Pro, they’re somehow both forgiving and secure, with a heel-lock eyelet placement that’s my personal favorite bit of the brand. (That, and the Zendaya commercials. I love her.).

I have high arches and a correspondingly high instep, and this shoe suits both. There was no rubbing and no hot spots, and I never felt the need to loosen my laces at the midfoot. The warp-knit upper is plush through the collar and tongue and secure without clamping or getting that weird bunching pleat thing that I loathe.

It has been revealed that furrer saw the same thing from the wide-foot side. For runners with wide feet and high insteps, she says, the “Cloudsurfer is fitted and not tight, but also secure.”.

This is not a speedwork shoe. If you want a rocket for tempo days, keep looking. It’s also on the heavier side, which is the trade-off for all that stack.

Industry observers note that but as an everyday trainer for long, easy miles, it’s excellent. The foam is better, the fit is better, and the ride stays consistent from the first mile to the last. For high-arched runners especially, I’d put it near the top of the try-on list.
