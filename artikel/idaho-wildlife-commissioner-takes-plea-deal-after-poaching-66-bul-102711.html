Idaho Wildlife Commissioner Takes Plea Deal After Poaching 6×6 Bull Elk, Trespassing

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

An Idaho Fish and Game commissioner just made a deal to avoid poaching and trespassing charges related to an illegal elk hunt last season.

In a recent development, on Nov. 9 eastern Idaho’s representative, Brody Harshbarger, agreed to plead guilty to two of seven charges of wildlife violations in exchange for having the remaining charges dropped.

His guilty pleas are for hunting without a tag and shooting across the road, according to local news outlets. The dismissed charges include two for poaching, trespass, motorized aid and failure to retrieve game. Both of the killed animals were bull elk, one antlerless and the other a 6×6 which was on private property. Harshbarger was accompanied by another man, Eric Murphy, at the time.

Sources indicate that “Neither Brody Harshbarger nor Eric Murphy called the landowners to request permission to retrieve the six-point bull and did not make a reasonable effort to retrieve the six-point bull,” according to investigation documents obtained by the Idaho Capital Sun. “Neither individual had permission to be shooting onto the private property in violation of Idaho Code.”.

Harshbarger had already filled his elk tag the weekend before the two bulls were shot. His hometown, Ashton, is in the same county where the violations took place. That same county, Fremont, borders Yellowstone National Park and is in the region Harshbarger represents on the state’s seven-person commission. That commission sets statewide hunting, fishing, and trapping seasons plus bag limits and other regulations based on recommendations and research supplied by Idaho Department of Fish and Game scientists.

In a recent development, each member of the commission, most often a resident hunter and/or fisherman, is appointed by the governor for a term of four years with a two-term maximum. The commission meets at least four times a year. Harshbarger hasn’t attended those meetings since his case opened after a tip received through the department’s Citizens Against Poaching in December. He hasn’t resigned and the Governor hasn’t dismissed him, but his profile has been removed from the commission’s website.

In a recent development, “The Governor’s office is aware of Commissioner Harshbarger’s plea agreement,” Gov. Brad Little’s spokeswoman Emily Callihan told the Sun. “Gov. Little will be working with the Idaho Department of Fish and Game and stakeholders in the coming days to ensure the responsibilities of the position are fulfilled.”.

Harshbarger was appointed to the commission in 2024. His sentencing for illegal activities in 2025 is scheduled for Dec. 9. With the upcoming plea deal and the number of charges reduced, the fine is expected to be $500 plus a one-year loss of hunting privileges.
