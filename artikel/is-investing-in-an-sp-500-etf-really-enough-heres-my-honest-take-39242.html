Industry observers note that exchange-traded funds are a great way to gain exposure to many stocks at once -- and one that tracks the S&P 500 could be particularly interesting. After all, the S&P 500 has been a winning investment over time, delivering an average annual return of 10% since the index's launch in the late 1950s.

A look at the famous benchmark's performance over the past three calendar years also is reason to give us confidence. It's climbed 78% over that time period as the bull market advanced -- and even amid certain headwinds this year, the S&P 500 is heading for a gain.

In a recent development, so, is investing in an S&P 500 ETF really enough? Here's my honest take.

In a recent development, first, let's talk a bit about ETF investing. As mentioned, these assets allow you to gain exposure to many stocks, and this is according to a theme such as biotech or retail, or an ETF may reflect the performance of an index. In each case, ETFs offer a certain degree of diversification.

According to recent reports, and with diversification in mind, an S&P 500 ETF, such as the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO +0.91%), is an excellent choice. This is because the major index is well-diversified across 11 industries, so an ETF that tracks it will have the same composition. Of course, today, tech stocks are heavily weighted in the S&P 500, so they more heavily influence performance than an industry with a lower weighting, such as utilities. But this may change over time as the composition of the index evolves.

And this brings me to my next point: By investing in an S&P 500 ETF, you'll always be exposed to the key companies driving the day's economy. This is because the major index rebalances on a quarterly basis, removing companies that no longer meet membership criteria and adding new ones that do. ETFs that track the index must follow these moves, and as a result, investors benefit from the growth of the day's most significant industries and companies.

NYSEMKT · As of 9:39 AM ET 11/19/26.

Finally, an S&P 500 ETF is a low-maintenance investment: You can invest and hold over time without closely inspecting earnings reports or corporate comments.

According to recent reports, one thing to keep in mind is that ETFs come with fees, expressed as an expense ratio, so you'll want to go for one with a ratio of less than 1% to optimize your gains. The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF, with an expense ratio of 0.03%, fits the bill. And ETFs trade daily on the market just like stocks, so they're easy to buy and sell.

Considering all of this, you might think that all you really need is an S&P 500 ETF. But is investing in such an asset enough? Here's my honest take. ETF investing is fantastic for all of the reasons I've mentioned above. And an S&P 500 ETF is a great fit for any investment style -- from cautious to aggressive.

In a recent development, but I wouldn't stop my investing story right here. It's vital to also buy individual stocks, as they can truly supercharge your performance over time. Though a company like Apple is the heaviest-weighted in the S&P 500, and therefore in the funds that track it, you can benefit more from Apple's gains by directly holding it than from holding an index fund. For example, Apple is heading for a 29% gain this year, while the S&P 500 is advancing about 8%. And over time, the difference can be greater too, with Apple and the S&P 500 rising 133% and 57%, respectively, over the past five years.

Industry observers note that so it's wise to buy an S&P 500 index fund, but in my honest opinion, investors shouldn't stop there. According to your investing style and comfort with risk, you should also build out a broad portfolio including quality individual stocks. An S&P 500 ETF and a mix of solid stocks are complementary and together could help you build wealth over the long term.
