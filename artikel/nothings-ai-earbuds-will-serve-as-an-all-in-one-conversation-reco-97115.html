Nothing’s AI Earbuds Will Serve as an All-in-One Conversation Recorder

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Phone-maker Nothing Technology is breaking away from its typical retro-inspired designs with a upcoming pair of AI-powered earbuds, as reported earlier by Bloomberg. The company confirmed via an X post that its Ear Note wireless earbuds will be available starting Dec. 8.

Industry observers note that the Ear Note earbuds are designed primarily to maximize the recording functionality. While other AI earbuds have already hit the market, the Ear Note is the first to record both phone calls and in-person conversations.

Sources indicate that the earbuds can capture and transcribe phone calls, while the charging case has its own microphone to record in-person conversations. To mitigate privacy concerns, the case only records with a physical button press — a tell that could reveal when audio is being captured around the Ear Note user.

Nothing shared that the Ear Note will work with multiple popular AI agents, but the full list of compatible software has not yet been released. The Ear Note works in tandem with an app that stores recordings from the earbuds and case, creating transcriptions and summaries similar to those created by the popular Otter AI app.

The Nothing brand has developed a cult following for combining lifestyle and fashion trends in its suite of smartphones, headphones, speakers and watches.

The full product lineup is built with modern hardware, but mimics warm, nearly retrofuturist design sensibilities. The blocky Ear Note charging case continues this trend while taking the manufacturer’s tech in a new direction.

In the lead-up to the Ear Note’s reveal, Nothing CEO Carl Pei described the earbuds in an X post as “a tool, but one built with the personality and care that have come to define a Nothing product.”.

In a Bloomberg interview, Pei indicated that Nothing isn’t trying to create AI agents or software, but that the organization has a “really interesting opportunity to be the hardware layer to AI.”.

Sources indicate that it’s at the moment unclear how much the Ear Note earbuds and case will cost when they’re released next month. According to Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman, the company hasn’t settled on a price just yet. The most expensive earbuds currently available on the Nothing store cost $149.

It has been revealed that whether you’re covering your eyes, plugging your ears or slapping a timepiece on your wrist, there’s now an ever-present option to record the world around you. Multiple companies have created their own versions of smart glasses, earbuds and watches with augmented reality capabilities.

Industry observers note that now that AI is the flavor du jour, these existing features are being enhanced by new ways to scan and interact with the surrounding environment. That means a lot of visual and auditory data is being recorded by wearable devices, and very rarely with the consent of all parties involved.

The most immediate risk factors are associated with video recording – public distrust of smart glasses is so widespread that they’ve colloquially been coined “pervert glasses,” as folks fear they fundamentally break an unspoken social contract by obscuring public recording from other people.

But audio recording, enabled by Apple’s latest smartwatches, the Amazon Bee, Plaud earbuds and the fresh Ear Note, is also raising concerns, not least because many states require all-party consent before you can record conversations.

The Ear Note’s ability to record every bit of audio, from phone calls to in-person conversations, means it will be handling more sensitive information than other AI-powered earbuds. The company will need to face new challenges to ensure security measures are in place to prevent private chats from leaking.

It has been revealed that at the very least, it seems as though Nothing Technology is aware of these privacy risks. According to Bloomberg, the firm created a data and security team to address concerns that cropped up during the Ear Note’s development. The company has also “obtained the same privacy certifications as competing recording products and worked with law firms to assess potential risks and safeguards.”.

Pei called wearables with recording functionality a sensitive space, and noted the design team was “extra careful to not take any risks.” The Ear Note earbuds and case are largely controlled by user commands, including tactile button presses that begin the recording process.
