Freiburg’s Derry Scherhant celebrates dream Berlin homecoming

Industry observers note that SC Freiburg attacker Derry Scherhant enjoyed an unforgettable evening in his hometown of Berlin on Monday, scoring Germany’s opening goal in a commanding 5-0 Nations League victory over the Netherlands. The 24-year-old subsequently reflected on his unlikely journey to the national team and took particular satisfaction in proving those who had once questioned his ability to become a professional footballer wrong.

According to recent reports, scherhant had arranged approximately 50 tickets for family members and friends ahead of the encounter at the Olympiastadion. The former Hertha BSC youngster, who grew up just five minutes from the venue, marked the occasion with an outstanding individual performance and a spectacular first-half goal.

Scherhant opened the scoring in the 41st minute after receiving a pass from Joshua Kimmich, cutting inside and dispatching a powerful effort into the upper-right corner. The Freiburg professional had already struck the outside of the post earlier in the half and subsequently played a decisive role in Germany’s fifth goal.

The performance represented another significant milestone in a remarkable international breakthrough that has gathered momentum since Jürgen Klopp took charge of the German national team.

Recent findings suggest that as Get German Football News detailed in its comprehensive October feature on Scherhant’s unusual career trajectory, the winger’s development was anything but straightforward. Scherhant struggled to establish himself at Viktoria Berlin and Tennis Borussia before eventually finding his way into Hertha BSC’s academy in 2020. His early difficulties included repeated questions about his physical development and whether he possessed the necessary qualities to succeed at the professional level.

He ultimately earned a Bundesliga debut with Hertha, although regular opportunities remained elusive during the club’s turbulent relegation campaign in 2022/23. The subsequent spell in the 2. Bundesliga proved notable to his development, and SC Freiburg eventually secured his services in the summer of 2025. The move to Breisgau provided Scherhant with a more stable environment, even if initially displacing established attacker Vincenzo Grifo presented another considerable challenge.

GGFN‘s 2026/27 tactical preview of SC Freiburg identified Scherhant as one of the club’s most impressive performers during preseason preparations. The former Hertha attacker subsequently consolidated his place in Julian Schuster’s starting XI.

A particularly impressive Bundesliga performance against Eintracht Frankfurt followed his first senior Germany nomination in September. Scherhant contributed a goal and an assist in the 2-2 draw, providing further evidence of his rapid development.

Sources indicate that the remarkable progression continued at international level. Scherhant earned his senior Germany debut during Klopp’s first international window and impressed sufficiently to retain his place when the Bundestrainer reduced his squad ahead of November’s fixtures. Get German Football News subsequently reported on Scherhant and Freiburg teammate Philipp Treu’s appreciation of the supportive environment at their Bundesliga club. Both players credited Freiburg’s collective mentality and stable working conditions with helping them establish themselves at international level.

In a recent development, scherhant also spoke enthusiastically about the prospect of representing Germany in Berlin. The winger celebrated his 24th birthday during the November international break and regarded the opportunity to return to the Olympiastadion as an extraordinary personal occasion. His performance against the Netherlands ultimately exceeded even those expectations.

The Freiburg attacker became only the third Berlin-born player to score for Germany in an international match staged in the capital, following Thomas Häßler against Bulgaria in November 1995 and Hans Ruch against Scotland in June 1929.

It has been revealed that klopp was particularly impressed by the contributions of Scherhant and fellow attacker Brajan Gruda. The Bundestrainer praised not only their technical abilities but also the considerable defensive work and intensity they contributed to Germany’s convincing victory.

Speaking after Monday’s victory, Scherhant recalled how frequently his chances of reaching professional football had been dismissed during his youth.

It has been revealed that “A few years ago, people told me that I wouldn’t make it as a professional,” Scherhant explained. “Then I became a professional. And even then, some people were still saying that it wasn’t enough, that the Regionalliga was the highest level I’d ever reach. It’s simply unbelievable that I’m now standing here, with the satisfaction of having proved those people wrong. 99 percent would have said never. One percent might have said perhaps, if he works really hard.”.

Klopp offered his own assessment of Scherhant’s contribution alongside Gruda.

“Derry Scherhant and Brajan Gruda, the way those two play football — my goodness,” Klopp remarked afterwards. “But the amount of work they put in and had to put in today was extraordinary. That’s what ultimately makes a team. Because there are other players who can play football too. But it’s the commitment and passion you bring to it that ultimately matter.”.

Scherhant himself struggled to contain his delight after scoring in the stadium he once called home.

“What can I say?” Scherhant said. “It couldn’t have gone any better, scoring the opening goal here in my own city. You couldn’t have dreamed it up any better. It’s truly unbelievable. I had a tremendous amount of fun and I’m simply overjoyed.”.

With his postponed birthday celebrations still awaiting him, Scherhant could finally look forward to an evening with family and friends after a homecoming that will remain among the most memorable moments of his young career.
