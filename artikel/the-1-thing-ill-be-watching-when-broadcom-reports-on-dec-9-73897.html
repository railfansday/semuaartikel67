Recent findings suggest that broadcom (AVGO -12.19%) shares have dropped about $135 apiece since their record close on June 2, putting the stock roughly 28% under that peak at about $347 as of this writing. The chip designer reports results for the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (the period ended Nov. 1, 2026) after the market closes on Wednesday, Dec. 9.

There'll be lots of numbers in that release. But one line matters more to me than the rest: artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductor revenue. I'll be checking it twice -- once for the quarter Broadcom just wrapped up, against the $21.7 billion management forecast in September, and once for what the tech giant expects in the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

After all, AI chips made up 56% of Broadcom's revenue in its latest quarter, and they accounted for over 80% of its year-over-year revenue growth.

Broadcom has made a habit of landing a bit above its own AI forecasts this year. In the first quarter of fiscal 2026 (the period ended Feb. 1, 2026), AI semiconductor revenue was $8.4 billion, against the $8.2 billion management had forecast. The fiscal second quarter brought $10.8 billion, against a $10.7 billion forecast. And the fiscal third quarter (the period ended Aug. 2) delivered $16.7 billion, over the $16.0 billion management had guided for.

Growth has accelerated the whole way, too. Year-over-year AI revenue growth rose from 106% in fiscal Q1 to 143% in fiscal Q2 and 221% in fiscal Q3. For fiscal Q4, management expects 236%.

Sources indicate that in other words, Broadcom's guidance has leaned a little conservative. But the beats have been small -- between around 1% and 4% above the forecast. I'm not expecting a blowout in fiscal Q4, then. AI revenue at or a bit above $21.7 billion would show me the organization is still on the schedule management laid out.

A result under $21.7 billion would break that streak for the first time this fiscal year.

Recent findings suggest that management's goal for next year is about $115 billion of AI revenue in fiscal 2027 -- almost double the $58 billion or so it expects for fiscal 2026. And on its September earnings call, Broadcom mentioned it doesn't strategy to update that figure every quarter. That arguably makes each quarterly forecast the best checkpoint investors have.

Do the math, and $115 billion comes to almost $29 billion a quarter on average. That's about a third higher than the fiscal fourth-quarter forecast. If AI revenue rose at an even pace from $21.7 billion, the first quarter of fiscal 2027 would likely need to land somewhere near $24 billion to keep the year on track.

A first-quarter forecast in the mid-$20 billion range, then, should be a sign the doubling is on schedule. A forecast closer to $22 billion might mean more of next year's growth is being shifted into the second half of fiscal 2027.

Management sounded confident in September.

"Our demand actually exceeds this outlook, and we will work to improve supply," remarked CEO Hock Tan.

It has been revealed that if that holds, the first-quarter forecast might land above my rough math. Either way, Dec. 9 is the first chance to check.

Industry observers note that after a 28% fall, I don't think Broadcom needs a major upside surprise to work from here. Of course, the tech giant has kept growing through the slide. Fiscal third-quarter revenue rose 86% year over year to $29.6 billion, and non-GAAP (adjusted) operating income climbed 92%.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

In a recent development, that growth hasn't made the stock cheap, though. At around $347, each share costs about 44 times Broadcom's earnings per share over the past four quarters. This price-to-earnings ratio only makes sense if AI revenue continues to rise as management expects.

In a recent development, what the stock needs is evidence that next year's doubling is still on track. And the AI revenue line, along with the forecast behind it, is where that evidence should first appear.

Granted, one good report won't settle everything. Much of next year's AI revenue hinges on deployment looks from a few big AI customers, and those plans can change. But the first-quarter forecast is the closest look investors will get at whether these plans are holding.

According to recent reports, in the end, if fiscal Q4 AI revenue comes in at or above $21.7 billion and the first-quarter forecast calls for the mid-$20 billion range, I think today's price looks fair for a growth stock with sales climbing this fast. A forecast well below that would make me more cautious, even at a lower stock price.
