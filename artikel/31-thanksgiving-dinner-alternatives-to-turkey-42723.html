Recent findings suggest that honestly, anything can make for an alternative Thanksgiving dinner. It’s your house, your rules. Our only suggestion: Make it festive, fun, and at least a little decadent.

Industry observers note that A classic Thanksgiving menu is all well and good, but one of our colleagues swears by serving nachos on the fourth Thursday of every November. If a substantial bloomin’ bird just isn’t ca-caw-caw-calling your name this year, serve a whole roast fish, pork tenderloin with sour-sweet cherry sauce, or a flaky carrot Wellington instead.

Or go completely rogue with lobster pasta or a major pot of chili. You can do that. Below, find vegetarian centerpieces, celebratory roasts, chicken dinners, and seafood mains for a Thanksgiving that looks a little different. BA’s Best Pumpkin Pie can stay.

Can carrots really count as a main course? They can when they’re roasted, glazed, and tucked into puff pastry with goat cheese, Gruyère, and walnuts. This Wellington feeds 8 to 10.

Industry observers note that red wine, browned mushrooms, pearl onions, and carrots give this meatless braise plenty of heft. Ladle it over mashed potatoes for a vegetarian anchor to your Thanksgiving menu.

Recent findings suggest that ruffled savoy cabbage, melty cheese, and a buttery cream cheese crust make this free-form tart worthy of the holiday table. Cut generous wedges for dinner and stick with savoy: Ordinary green cabbage won’t work here.

A make-ahead casserole, like this pumpkin-infused strata scented with nutmeg and herbs and balanced by sharp cheddar, is just what your holiday schedule needs. Assemble it the night before; bake it on Thanksgiving.

Sources indicate that turkey isn’t the only holiday cooking project worth clearing the counter for. Delicate homemade crepes are rolled around ricotta, mozzarella, and Parmesan, then baked in marinara. Make the batter and filling a day ahead to split up the work.

Sources indicate that kale, pinto beans, and charred poblanos make these cheesy enchiladas a satisfying holiday main for four to six. Get a head start by making the charred salsa verde up to three days ahead.

Sources indicate that this autumnal risotto works as a standalone dinner or alongside your favorite Thanksgiving sides. Set the table with shallow bowls and pick a fall salad full of color and crunch to balance the buttery rice.

British beef Wellington meets Cantonese barbecue-style pork in Peter Som’s pastry-wrapped centerpiece. A shiitake mushroom filling and gravy made from the marinade complete the picture; assemble the Wellington a day ahead.

According to recent reports, these impossibly tender short ribs are begging to be served with mashed potatoes and buttery milk buns with garlic butter. Give them what they want—and give yourself a head start by braising the ribs the day before Thanksgiving.

For a roast-and-sides Thanksgiving without the turkey, garlic-and-rosemary-rubbed beef tenderloin makes an excellent centerpiece. Rosemary butter dresses the sliced roast, while tangy horseradish yogurt gives everyone something to swipe their next bite through.

Sources indicate that toasted rice, fried onions, and garlic form a dark, peppery crust on this slow-braised pork shoulder. Set out the major platter and pass the coconut-vinegar pan sauce; this roast feeds 8 to 12.

According to recent reports, maybe save the brown sugar-glazed ham for Christmas? This whole leg of lamb makes for a centerpiece that’s just as showstopping. And, like ham and turkey, leftover lamb can be deliciously repurposed in sandwiches.

A platter of sliced pork tenderloin with glossy cherries and browned shallots has plenty of holiday presence without a long roast. Honey and red wine vinegar give the sauce its sweet-sour bite, and frozen cherries are welcome here.

Sources indicate that if you’re going to make tamales, it’s a good idea to make a lot of them. Better yet, turn the process into a party, with everyone spreading masa and folding corn husks around the red chile pork filling.

Recent findings suggest that this pasta packs a whole spectrum of holiday flavors into each bite, with crumbles of Italian sausage, shreds of brussels sprouts, and toasted pecans. Streamlining dinner into one dish leaves you more time for Thanksgiving pies.

Recent findings suggest that for some people, spending all Thanksgiving in the kitchen sounds like a good time. For everyone else, there’s this beef-and-bean chili. A can of pumpkin purée thickens the broth, keeping things seasonal without calling for a single roasting pan.

Give homemade fried chicken an even more festive angle with sage and thyme in the dredge and cranberry-mustard sauce for dipping. Start the buttermilk marinade the day before.

Recent findings suggest that this whole roast duck comes with a built-in Thanksgiving side: The duck fat drippings go into basmati rice that cooks while the bird rests. Plan for an overnight dry brine before the glossy pomegranate-honey glaze gets its moment.

Cooking for a smaller crowd? This skillet delivers the stuffing and the bird—chicken thighs, in this case—in one tidy package. Rendered chicken fat enriches the boxed stuffing mix, with dried cranberries and lemon zest brightening things up.

Two diminutive birds make a festive roast for four. A fiery guajillo chile, ginger, and vinegar marinade flavors both the hens and the potatoes beneath them, so the platter arrives with its own very good side.

You just need to make green beans, because this Thanksgiving main is already a two-in-one: Thinly sliced potatoes cook beneath a whole chicken, catching its drippings and crisping around the edges in the same cast-iron skillet.

If your Thanksgiving table could use a little red sauce (and spice), skip the whole bird in favor of this take on chicken parm. Gochujang and gochugaru bring the heat; melted mozzarella keeps things gloriously gooey.

Presenting a whole fish at the holiday table is wildly celebratory, even when you’re cooking for just two or three. Bring out lettuce wraps, kimchi, rice, and ssamjang so everyone can build each bite a little differently.

It has been revealed that A large roasted salmon fillet looks right at home on a holiday platter, especially under a crunchy, garlicky nut salsa. Make the salsa up to four days ahead, then roast the fish when it’s time for dinner.
