Night one of sales checking. The counter did not move.

Sources indicate that it has been one week since I pressed publish on the VitalEdge Gold EA and put my trading robot up for sale on the MQL5 Market for $69.99. I told myself I would not check the sales page every day. I checked it every day. Sometimes twice. And every single time, the same number looked back at me: zero.

Recent findings suggest that this is the part of the entrepreneur story nobody puts on the highlight reel. The launch was supposed to be the finish line. It turned out to be the start of a much longer, quieter road. So let me tell you honestly what a week of zero sales actually feels like, what it taught me, and what I am doing about it.

In a recent development, the night my robot passed the MQL5 Market validation server, I sat in the dark genuinely proud. Four PASSED marks on symbols I had never tested. A file I built with my own hands, from nothing, working on a machine I do not control. That feeling was real and nobody can take it from me.

But here is what I have learned about introduces: the euphoric part lasts about one night. Then the market asks its cold, indifferent question. Who is going to buy this?

On day one, I refreshed the product page like a man watching a pot that refuses to boil. A view here, a view there. No orders. Day two, the same. Day three, I stopped refreshing in the morning and moved it to the evening, as if changing the hour would change the result. It did not. By day seven, the counter still read zero, and I had to sit with that honestly instead of explaining it away.

I could hide behind excuses, but a diary that lies to itself is useless. So I did the uncomfortable thing and listed the real reasons.

Nobody knows who I am. The MQL5 Market is not a field of dreams. Building it does not mean buyers come. There are thousands of Expert Advisors for sale there, most of them made by sellers with years of reviews, ratings, and history. When a buyer drops real money on a trading robot, they are not buying code. They are buying trust. And I walked into that marketplace with zero reviews, zero ratings, zero sales history. I am a stranger asking strangers for $69.99.

According to recent reports, I have no live proof. Every serious seller in that market runs a live signal account, a real account connected in real time, so buyers can watch the robot trade before they pay for it. I do not have one yet, for the simple reason that it takes capital I could not spare this month. But from a buyer's point of view, that is a red flag, not a sob story. They cannot see inside my wallet, they can only see an empty signals tab on my product page.

My traffic channels were down at the worst time. The same week my product went live, my Instagram business account got restricted by Meta. That account is one of my main funnels for everything I build. So I debuted my first paid product during the exact week one of my loudest megaphones went silent. Some weeks the universe has a sense of timing.

It has been revealed that sitting with this list was uncomfortable, but it was also strangely freeing. None of these problems are about the product itself. The code passed validation on its own merit. The problems are about trust, proof, and reach. Those are solvable problems.

Recent findings suggest that the road after launch is longer than the road to it. I am still walking.

Building is not selling. I spent a weekend building the robot and roughly an hour planning how to sell it. That ratio is backwards. The greatest product with no distribution is a secret. The market does not reward effort, it rewards evidence.

Industry observers note that the product is only half the job. A buyer needs to see a track record, a live signal, reviews from other humans, a face behind the name. Trust is built out in the open, over time. My first week of zero sales was really a tuition payment in this lesson.

It has been revealed that patience is a strategy, not a consolation. A week is nothing. Some of the top sellers on that marketplace spent months or years posting free tools and signals before their first sale landed. The difference between them and everyone who quit is that they kept showing up after the counter still remarked zero.

My why has to be bigger than the sale. If the only reward was money, this week would have broken me. But it was not. I proved to myself that I can take an idea from nothing to a product on an international marketplace. That proof changes how I walk into every room from now on.

It has been revealed that zero sales is data, not destiny. Here is what I am doing about it, in order.

First, launch a live signal account. This is the single biggest trust gap between me and the buyer. As soon as I can fund a small live account, I will connect it to the product page so anyone can watch the VitalEdge Gold EA trade in real market conditions. Reviews can be doubted, but a live signal cannot lie.

In a recent development, second, keep building in public. Every post on this diary, every video on my YouTube channel, every honest report about what worked and what did not, all of it accumulates into the one thing I cannot buy: a reputation. When people can watch the whole journey, they stop seeing a stranger.

According to recent reports, third, widen the income so no single product carries the dream. This is why I run the affiliate store alongside the trading products. One stream feeding another. The blog brings readers, the store pays the bills, and one day the robot sells because the audience already exists.

According to recent reports, fourth, revisit the price point once there is proof. Sixty-nine dollars and ninety-nine cents is fair for what the robot does. But fair pricing needs social proof to work with it. Once the live signal is running and the first reviews land, I will look at the price again with real data instead of hope.

If you are somewhere behind me on this road, sitting with your own zero, let me say what I needed to hear this week. The zero is not the verdict. It is just the opening scene. Every seller you admire once refreshed a page and saw nothing. The ones who made it are simply the ones who went back to work the next morning anyway.

My robot is live. It works. It passed tests I never even planned for. Right now, nobody has bought it, and that stings, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. But a week from now I will still be here. Building, documenting, and knocking on doors. The sale will come when the trust arrives, and the trust is under construction every single day.

Recent findings suggest that see you in the next entry. The counter will not read zero forever.
