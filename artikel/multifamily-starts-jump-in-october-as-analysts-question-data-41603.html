Multifamily starts jump in October as analysts question data

🌐 Source: Housingwire • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (514 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, the Census Bureau reported a surge in multifamily construction starts in October. Apartment industry analysts don’t buy it.

Industry data show a shrinking apartment construction pipeline. Developers say they’re breaking ground on fewer projects and expect to start even fewer in 2027.

Starts in buildings with five or more units rose 25.5% from September to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 433,000, the Census Bureau and the Department of Housing and Urban Development reported Wednesday. That was 22% above the year-earlier rate. Permits fell 5.2% to an annual rate of 475,000, reversing a September increase and leaving authorizations 0.4% below their year-earlier level.

“The vibes among multifamily builders today are as bearish as I’ve seen since about 2010,” apartment market analyst Jay Parsons wrote on LinkedIn. He pointed to the Federal Reserve’s September rate hike and signals of further increases. Higher rates make projects harder to finance, he noted, by raising borrowing costs while putting downward pressure on property values.

Recent findings suggest that parsons said the federal figures don’t square with what developers are telling him or with private-sector data. He rejects the idea that the discrepancy could be chalked up to monthly volatility and questions whether the Census Bureau’s survey of permit holders reliably captures multifamily starts.

Sources indicate that “The pullback in construction activity is broad-based,” Cushman & Wakefield noted in its third-quarter U.S. multifamily report. Construction pipelines shrank from a year earlier in more than 70% of the 90 markets the firm tracks.

Sources indicate that the steepest declines came in markets that built most aggressively during the boom. In Sarasota, Florida, units under construction as a share of existing apartment inventory fell by 7.9 percentage points. Savannah, Georgia, and Austin, Texas, also posted sharp declines.

Recent findings suggest that pipelines expanded in just 25 markets. Cushman & Wakefield singled out Palm Beach and the Bay Area, where it noted stronger rent growth had improved the economics of latest development.

Recent findings suggest that another relevant data indicator, the AIA/Deltek Architecture Billings Index® (ABI) score remained well below 50 at 44.9 for the month, as billings have not been in consistently positive territory for more than four years now. This index is a leading indicator primarily for latest Commercial Real Estate (CRE) investment, including multi-family residential. According to Calculated Risk, a housing blog hosted by analyst Bill McBride, “multi-family billings have been below 50 for 49 of the last 51 months.”.

Meanwhile, apartment demand through September ran at its strongest pace since 2021, and the national vacancy rate fell to a three-year low of 8.6% in the third quarter. Net absorption totaled nearly 350,000 units, up 14% from the same period last year, while deliveries fell 25% to just over 250,000 units. Asking rents rose 2.3% from a year earlier to $1,950, the fastest pace of growth since early 2023.

“A resilient economy continues to support household formation and renter demand, while higher interest rates and elevated home prices have widened the affordability gap between renting and owning,” Cushman & Wakefield stated.
