Recent findings suggest that P3 State, an Australian foundational AI startup, unveiled an A$9.5 million (US$6.5 million) raise led by Main Sequence, with participation from US deep-tech investor DCVC. The manufacturer’s news index dates the post November 23, 2026, while the release itself is datelined November 24, 2026, in Australia.

According to recent reports, the funding will expand P3 State’s research and engineering team in Australia and advance its core technology toward a public release. The startup, a spin-off of the Australian Institute for Machine Learning (AIML) at Adelaide University, is working to lower the cost of developing and operating AI without reducing performance.

P3 State’s central argument is that adding capacity has become an increasingly expensive way to improve AI. The dominant approach applies more compute and data to ever-larger models, requiring substantial energy and capital for training and operation. Those requirements restrict frontier development to a small group of organizations, while data-center projects face growing resistance.

Industry observers note that existing efficiency techniques also tend to involve a compromise: faster or cheaper operation often comes at the expense of accuracy or capability. P3 State intends to remove that trade-off by rethinking how AI models work at a foundational level.

Chief executive and co-founder Simon Lucey explained why that requires more than refining existing systems: “You can engineer a system to be ten times better, but if you want it to be dramatically more efficient than that, you have to go back to the foundations.”.

Industry observers note that the founding team combines academic research with large-scale deployment experience. Lucey is an Adelaide University professor and former AIML director who has held faculty roles at Carnegie Mellon University. Chief technology officer and co-founder Arpit Garg has trained and deployed AI models at AIML and TikTok. Chief scientist and co-founder Hemanth Saratchandran researches efficient, reliable AI systems and holds a pure mathematics PhD from the University of Oxford.

According to recent reports, P3 State’s leadership biography describes chief operating officer Iain McCowan as a two-time deep-tech founder with experience in speech and language AI research and commercial AI product teams. That background spans theory, model deployment and company building, connecting the startup’s academic origins to the practical work of preparing a public release.

Main Sequence investment manager Danielle Haj-Moussa said the firm sees P3 State as Australian AI research becoming a commercial venture, with the founders’ first-principles approach offering the potential for a “step change rather than a marginal gain.” Main Sequence also identifies compute as one of six systems the world depends on, making its efficiency important to every industry that uses it.

In a recent development, the announcement did not include performance benchmarks or a release date, and P3 State said further details would follow. The central question for that release is whether the tech giant can demonstrate lower development and operating costs without sacrificing accuracy or capability, the compromise its efficiency thesis is intended to avoid.
