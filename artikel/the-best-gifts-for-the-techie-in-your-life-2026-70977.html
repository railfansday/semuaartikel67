The Best Gifts for the Techie in Your Life (2026)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Techy gizmos make great stocking stuffers or Secret Santa gifts for friends, family, or coworkers. In fact, we've collected them in our comprehensive list of the Best 40 Techy Gifts under $100—that should give you plenty to parse through for impulse buys and stocking stuffers.

But this list is not that. These are the massive tech products that really got us amped up—many of which debuted this year. They're more expensive than what you'd buy for an acquaintance, but if you're looking for a big reaction on Christmas morning from your kids, spouse, or significant other, one of these should do the trick.

Updated November 2026: I've added the iPad Mini, Sonos Era 300, and the Lego Smart Play Star Wars Throne Room setup.

In a recent development, oura has a lock on the world of smart rings, and we've been loving the latest model, which came out this July. Because the Ring 5 is smaller and more comfortable than before, WIRED reviewer Boutayna Chokrane states she often forgets she's even wearing it. That's crucial for something you're supposed to wear at all times, including while sleeping or during workouts. It's billed as a “proactive” health and fitness platform, giving you data on everything from sleep patterns to predictive blood pressure signals. It's likely not worth upgrading from the previous version, but for the fitness and health nut in your life who doesn't own one yet, the Oura Ring 5 is the best smart ring you can buy.

We already have smart speakers. We have iPads. We have digital photo frames (or, at least, your parents do). So, a dedicated device that serves merely as a digital calendar might seem superfluous. Maybe it is, but reviewer Nena Farrell absolutely swears by them. As someone with a busy family who struggles to sync our calendars, I get the appeal: a calendar on a larger screen in your kitchen or living room, visible to everyone who walks by, certainly sounds convenient. It's not just a list of events, either. It also has a meals page to blueprint and organize family dinners, plus tasks to keep everyone on top of chores. And this latest 15-inch model, the Skylight Calendar 2, is the best so far.

It has been revealed that it's not only our favorite Keychron keyboard—it's the best overall keyboard you can buy. Rolled out in early 2026, this mechanical keyboard has a mid-century modern vibe, made of wood and metal. The 75 percent layout gives you everything you'll likely need in a compact size, with all the usual Keychron features, such as hot-swappable switches, double-shot keycaps, and the usual excellent build quality. Unlike other mechanical keyboards you may have tried, this one is low-profile, which means the travel distance on each key isn't quite as long, and it's designed for typing first and foremost. It's a great office companion, and it'll be a smoother transition if you're coming from a more generic rubber-dome model like an Apple Magic Keyboard.

Recent findings suggest that the iPhone Duo might be the talk of the town, but the fresh iPad Mini is the more practical purchase. First of all, it doesn't require trading in the iPhone you're already familiar with to risk it on a first-generation product. It also isn't nearly as expensive if you already have a phone that you like. And what you get is easily the best small tablet money can buy. The deliciously vibrant OLED display is brighter and bigger than in the past, and if you opt for the nano-texture screen, it becomes the ultimate outdoor tablet. It might be a bit too expensive for a tablet of its size, but it just might be the ultimate travel companion for the right person.

It has been revealed that this Sonos speaker is built different, quite literally. It’s made specifically with Dolby Atmos spatial audio in mind, with six speaker drivers pointed in multiple directions to spread out sound like no other home speaker can. Outside of Atmos, it’s an excellent stereo speaker that sounds crisp and detailed, and can connect with Sonos soundbars to make a full surround sound setup. —Harry Rabinowitz.

Recent findings suggest that you know what they say: If it ain't broke, don't fix it. The Logitech MX Master 3S has long been the most popular productivity mouse you can buy, so expectations for its successor were high. Thankfully, the MX Master 4 (9/10, WIRED Recommends) delivers without rocking the boat too much. There's now haptic support in the thumb rest, and you can press here to activate the virtual Actions Ring. You can customize the ring in the Logi Options+ app to launch apps, open websites, insert symbols, or even type text—the options are nearly endless.

Industry observers note that it might not be worth upgrading from the MX Master 3 or 3S, especially since the polling rate hasn't changed, but the MX Master 4 has taken the reins as the go-to wireless mouse for serious office workers. If you know someone who would appreciate a mouse that not only offers great ergonomic support but also feels extremely high-quality in the hand, look no further.

Whether it's the Rabbit R1 or Humane Pin, AI hardware has been a big flop so far. But this year, we finally started to see devices that are actually interesting and potentially useful. WIRED contributor Christopher Null tried out all the latest product category of AI notetaking devices, which can dictate, record, summarize, and translate seamlessly using large language models. Pocket was among the top recommendations. It magnetically attaches to the back of your phone and can even record phone calls without needing to keep it on speakerphone. Many AI notetakers require a paid subscription, while Pocket offers a substantial free option and only requires Pocket Pro as an upgrade for unlimited history.

With all the earbuds we review, you might think our audio experts are above something as common as AirPods. But Apple really nailed it this year with the AirPods Pro 3 (9/10, WIRED Recommends). It's been almost three years since Apple updated its noise-canceling earbuds, and the latest model adds a fresh ear tip design, supports real-time translation, and delivers vastly improved noise canceling and longer battery life. According to our testers, the earbuds last up to eight hours with noise canceling on, enough for more than a full day of work. All that makes this pair the best AirPods ever made.

GoPro has a upcoming premium camera, and it's one of the most expensive action cameras you can buy. But our camera expert and outdoors enthusiast, Scott Gilbertson, says it's totally worth it. The new model, the GoPro Mission 1 Pro, has a larger 1-inch sensor than the Hero models, letting it shoot up to 8K at 60 fps. That might sound like overkill, but 8K lets you crop and zoom in on what you capture on your adventures while exporting to 4K without a major hit to image quality (unless you're fortunate enough to own an 8K TV). Scott also praises the new slow-motion mode, shooting up to 4K at 240 fps—even when zoomed in. The older GoPro Hero 13 Black is still a great option if you want to save some money, but the Mission 1 Pro's upgrade in image quality is noteworthy for a camera this small.

Sources indicate that the best espresso machines cost well over $1,000 and take up your entire kitchen counter. They aren't exactly very “giftable.” The Breville Bambino challenges that assumption. It's only $300, yet it has many of the same features as more expensive machines, such as a thermoblock for instant heating. Of course, as a smaller, budget option, it's missing some things, such as automatic milk frothing. But according to former WIRED reviewer, Matthew Korfhage, pairing the Bambino with a solid grinder gives you “beautiful cups with counter space to spare.” Who wouldn't want that?

Smart Play is Lego's next big move into adding some serious tech into its classic brick-building. Despite looking like standard Legos, each Smart Brick has a custom chip, light array, speaker, and a variety of sensors. You can probably imagine how all this tech plays out in a set as immersive as the Star Wars Throne Room Duel. If you want to experience the latest in the world of Lego, it doesn't get more cutting-edge than this (even if our reviewer found that the tech took some of the magic away).
