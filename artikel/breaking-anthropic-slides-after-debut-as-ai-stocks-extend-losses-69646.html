The reversal came one day after that Nasdaq debut. After underwriters took up an option to purchase more shares, Anthropic’s initial public offering (IPO) totaled $93.5 billion. Reuters reported that this exceeded the $85.7 billion raised by SpaceX, making Anthropic’s listing the largest on record.

Wall Street’s main indexes had ended sharply lower on November 19, and futures pointed to further declines before the November 20 open. Among large technology companies, Nvidia and Meta Platforms each dropped more than 3%; Amazon fell 2%, and Microsoft slipped 1%.

Semiconductor shares were also hit: AMD and Sandisk each shed more than 6%, while Intel retreated more than 3%. “Investors still believe in AI but are becoming more selective about what they will pay for that growth,” stated David Miller, chief investment officer at Catalyst Funds.

In a recent development, that selectivity matters because investors had treated Anthropic as a stand-in for the broader AI trade and had hoped its arrival would help the sector recover from a turbulent stretch. Kaush Amin, head of private market investing at US Bank Asset Management, noted he favored patience and evaluating the organization as it reports public results, adding that the valuation “assumes years of rapid growth.”.
