So You Are competing in the Melbourne Cup at Flemington Racecourse on 3 November.

The body of a top Australian racehorse has been found dumped in a skip bin, hours after its trainer revealed the horse had died from colic.

In a video shared online, only two weeks after So You Are raced in the Melbourne Cup, the deceased racehorse appeared to be lying in a large metal bin surrounded by other household rubbish in Sydney.

Trainer Bjorn Baker - who shared news of the horse's death from colic on Monday - has mentioned he was "deeply distressed" by the video, and called for a "full explanation".

Racing NSW, the state's industry regulator, remarked a preliminary investigation had found the horse was later properly buried, but called the way its body was handled "completely unacceptable".

"Like everyone who has seen the footage, I am deeply distressed by the handling of So You Are's remains," Baker mentioned in an statement shared online.

"He was a much-loved horse, and he deserved to be treated with dignity and respect after his death.".

So You Are had a successful run during spring, winning four times at respected races including the Rosehill, Randwick and the Bendigo Cup.

The five-year-old recently finished eight in the Melbourne Cup, which was won by Point of Law, a horse previously owned by King Charles III.

The racehorse had received urgent treatment after a severe bout of colic, but was "unfortunately unable to be saved" and was euthanised under anaesthesia, Racing NSW stated.

"So You Are was then transported for burial, and was being held temporarily, awaiting burial, when these images were recorded," it said.

Burial at a licensed landfill is typically recommended for animals that die from euthanasia due to the chemicals involved in the process, according to the racing body.

"That burial, however, should have occurred in a timely and respectful manner," it added.

In a statement, REC Equine Specialists - the hospital where the horse died - mentioned a contractor had been hired to dispose of the So You Are's body.

"We did not carry out the burial ourselves, but we chose the provider and the arrangements, and we take full responsibility for that.".

"We have spoken with So You Are's owners and trainer to apologise personally.".

The company added that it had stopped using the contractor, and was reviewing its processes to make sure they known exactly how horses are handled after leaving their hospital.

According to recent reports, "No owner should have to go through what So You Are's connections have experienced this week. We will make sure it does not happen again.".
