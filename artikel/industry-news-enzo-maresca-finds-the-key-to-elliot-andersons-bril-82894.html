A £116m transfer fee would automatically place anyone under the spotlight. Of late, it has appeared that Elliot Anderson has assumed a prominence anyway, and not always in ways he would have wished. A first return to Nottingham Forest was notable for the challenge he lost to Xavier Schlager that led to Liam Delap galloping clear to score. A meeting with Spain’s World Cup winners was curtailed by a red card for fouling Lamine Yamal; naively, really, given it brought the dual punishments of a dismissal and a penalty.

Sources indicate that so if it is safe to say Manchester City needed Anderson’s equaliser against Fulham, it feels equally true that he did. “Nice moment for him, nice moment for us,” mentioned Enzo Maresca. City were facing up to the prospect of a fourth straight league defeat. Anderson’s first top-flight goal since an extraordinarily expensive move averted that.

It offered an illustration of his considerable qualities – and, his mishap at the City Ground notwithstanding, Anderson has been one of City’s finest players in their decidedly mixed season – as well as an idea of how best to deploy them. “We used him today for the first time since the season started as an attacking midfielder,” Maresca explained.

Sources indicate that A problem for much of this season is that Anderson has seemed to be playing centre midfield on his own which, even for a player who commanded a nine-figure fee, posed certain difficulties when he was outnumbered. It had felt all the more needless when City’s midfield rebuild came at a record cost.

In a recent development, with Enzo Fernandez reduced to a cameo, his wanderlust taking him to the bench, and Ayyoub Bouaddi starting, Anderson had a partner. “Ayyoub and Elliot were both really good,” noted Maresca. It was not a one-stop solution to bring City solidity: they still conceded, for the 18th occasion in their last nine games, and looked too open. “We need to find a way to keep clean sheets,” Maresca admitted.

But Bouaddi’s presence helped liberate Anderson and there were two moments to demonstrate what he can do when unleashed in the final third. The first was his leveller, a crisp, clean volley from the edge of the box, almost as impressive technically as his strike at the Etihad Stadium for Forest last season. “Fantastic goal,” said Maresca.

It came after Fulham had cleared a corner so arguably Anderson would have been in the same position if Fernandez was not on the pitch. Not when he almost got a winner, though: eight yards out, angling a shot just wide after linking up with Erling Haaland, following the kind of box-crashing run that players like Ilkay Gundogan and Kevin De Bruyne have made when used as No 8s by City. They have instead tended to be Fernandez’s remit this season.

And if Fernandez is the conundrum, rather than Anderson, there is nevertheless the case that a double act really brings the best out of him. Until his early exit, he excelled alongside Declan Rice in a duo in Madrid. “It has been an interesting week,” said Anderson, which rather underplayed it.

In a recent development, A problem for Maresca is that, even when identifying a way of using Anderson, City still floundered. This was another incoherent display as the longest winning streak between two clubs in English football came to an end. “We knew we lost the last 20 times we played Manchester City but I told [the players] we would have the chance to beat them if we play with courage, with character,” said Alvaro Arbeloa.

According to recent reports, fulham’s first point against City since 2011 was a sign of the progress under the Spaniard, following draws against Liverpool and Manchester United. Arbeloa knocked City out of the Champions League last season when with Real Madrid. He showed his prowess with lesser players as Fulham pressed man-for-man, played a variant of 4-2-2-2 and deservedly led.

The instigator was Oscar Bobb, sold by City in January, with a driving run on the right. Rodrigo Muniz’s first touch took him past Abdukodir Khusanov. His second was placed in the top corner. The striker celebrated by putting the ball up his shirt to mark his wife’s pregnancy. Muniz admitted he struggled last season when she had a miscarriage. It is too trite to say he looked a player reborn, but he gave Fulham a threat.

The opposing No 9 thought he had levelled. A disputed decision ensured otherwise, referee Thomas Bramall ruling Haaland had kicked the ball out of Bernd Leno’s hands when he bundled it over the line. “I had the ball in my hands,” the goalkeeper noted. “I had the feeling I controlled the ball and Haaland crashed into me and slid into me.” City disagreed. “From my point of view it is quite clear that it is not a foul,” said Maresca. He said Haaland concurred, but the City top scorer may wish he had met Iliman Ndiaye’s cross with a better finish at the first time of asking.

It was not the only example of wastefulness. Haaland blazed over when released by Foden. Only three of City’s 15 shots were on target. They could have lost it. After recent errors, there was a redemptive element to Gianluigi Donnarumma’s saves from Bobb and Muniz. City’s winless streak nevertheless continued. “We don’t like this kind of moments in terms of four games in a row,” remarked Maresca. For Anderson, though, a different game brought a different status, as scorer and saviour.
