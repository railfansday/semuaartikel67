It has been revealed that this beloved American fast-food chain is known for its ButterBurgers and frozen custard. Can you guess?

Pop the cheese curds, Wisconsin! Forbes just released its first-ever America’s Favorite Restaurants list, and the No. 1 spot didn’t go to Chick-fil-A, McDonald’s or any coastal cult favorite (lookin’ at you, In-N-Out). It went to Culver’s.

To build the ranking, Forbes partnered with data and insights firm HundredX to poll more than 100,000 Americans about 100 restaurants, from sit-down chains and fast-food spots to coffee shops that serve food. The survey considered eight categories: taste, price, food quality, menu, value, atmosphere, cleanliness and speed.

In a recent development, the Midwestern chain known for its ButterBurgers, frozen custard, crinkle-cut fries and breaded cheese curds scored extra well in taste and food quality. Honestly, anyone who’s ended a long day with a Concrete Mixer already knew that.

According to recent reports, we’ll admit to some hometown pride here. Taste of Home is based in Wisconsin too, so watching a homegrown favorite take it all feels a little like seeing your cousin’s pie win the county fair.

Recent findings suggest that culver’s was founded in 1984 in Sauk City, Wisconsin—about 100 miles from Taste of Home HQ. Today the organization is based down the road in Prairie du Sac and has more than 1,000 locations in 26 states. Almost all of them are owned by local franchisees.

In-N-Out Burger came in second, earning strong marks in the menu, price and value categories. Forbes noted that both the No. 1 and No. 2 spots have something in common: They’re burger joints.

Texas-based iced tea chain HTeaO landed in third place, Texas Roadhouse took fourth, and Chick-fil-A rounded out the top five.

In a recent development, the biggest downside for burger fans is that Culver’s isn’t in every state yet. But if you’re planning a holiday road trip, it could be worth checking whether there’s a Culver’s along the way. A ButterBurger and a scoop of the Flavor of the Day make a detour worth every mile. And if there’s not one nearby, these Culver’s copycats will do the trick.
