Recent findings suggest that we’ve just seen American formally retire its Boeing 777 first class, and with that, no airline in the United States has a “true” international first class anymore. It’s the end of an era, because going back many decades, premium products were largely pioneered by US carriers. So I think this raises the question — why can US carriers not make first class work in a way that’s economically viable, especially as they keep talking about how premium they are?

Recent findings suggest that before we get into this, let me make sure we’re all on the same page when it comes to terms. This post isn’t about the premium cabin on domestic flights, which is often referred to as first class. Instead, it’s about international flights that have a cabin above business class, when it’s marketed as first class. So this would include airlines on my list of the world’s best first class products.

It has been revealed that airline executives are divided on the merits of having an international first class cabin. Some insist that there’s no need for such a cabin, as business class has improved so much. Meanwhile others absolutely see merit to such a product.

So how is it that carriers like Air France, Lufthansa, SWISS, etc., can make money offering first class to the United States (primarily targeting Americans), but US airlines seemingly can’t?

According to recent reports, the main problem with international first class on US carriers is that it's international (for once the American obsession with the word is relevant :)), therefore competition from non-US carrier exists. US simply doesn't have service culture on level needed to deliver true premium experience, and it's twice as true in aviation. Internally it works because you have various lackluster products competing against each other, but no one sane will pay top buck for an AA experience when they can fly AF. This consequently leaves US airlines with the more price sensitive segment, which simply doesn't work in first class.

One thing to look at is that any top product sits at the top of the iceberg. And it has thus to be credible with respect of the rest of the iceberg. You can't sell a Coco Chanel Suite in a shabby 3 class Hotel, even if the suite itself has all the bells and whistles. The US airlines would not have to just deliver a good first class they would have to improve their other premium products too.

Industry observers note that from a French perspective, the failure of US international First Class goes way beyond airline economics, it’s a cultural impossibility. Money buys convenience and private jets, not elegance. You see it every day in how Americans travel abroad: loud, sacrificing elegance on the altar of comfort, boarding premium cabins in Juicy Couture tracksuits. True First Class requires discretion, quiet refinement, and centuries of art de vivre and hospitality heritage. Without that DNA, a US carrier can never deliver a genuine First Class experience, just an oversized Business Class. 😉.

US airlines (which do not have a long range approach) have spent 10 years building business class only not to start degrading it with “basic” business fares. Psycho strategy in action.

I'm not one who can justify to myself the cost of a revenue long-haul FC ticket, but I can see why some people might. Like US airlines, Qantas offers fewer first-class cabins than in the past, and they are absent from newer jets. They at the moment offer 14 F seats on A380 flights to LAX, DFW, LHR, JNB and SIN from Sydney. First is coming back on the new A350-1000ULR aircraft slated for their flagship Sydney to New York and London non-stop routes, with six seats. Those aircraft will also operate some other routes. I doubt Qantas decided this lightly. I've flown first on QF12 from LAX (on points, quite a lot of them), and the difference from J was noticeable, I can see why people would pay for it, and I would repeat (for points). On my flight, the cabin was full. Looking at the next few months, award F seats to and from the US are not unicorns, but they are not common. There are none available on QF1 to LHR or SIN. I suspect there's going to be a halo effect from these extreme long-haul flights, and a second from them offering first class. What that will be worth remains to be seen. Whether QF or the US3 have made the better business decision also remains to be seen.
