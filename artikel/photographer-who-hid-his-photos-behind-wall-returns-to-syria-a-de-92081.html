A Syrian photographer has finally been reunited with his personal photo archive more than a decade after hiding it behind a wall he built during the country’s war.

Photographer Ahmed al-Bekkar fled Syria more than a decade ago, seeking refuge in Turkey amid widespread destruction, looting, and attacks by the terrorist group ISIS (Daesh) and forces loyal to Syria’s now-topled Assad regime, according to a report by Anadolu Agency.

‘Every piece of paper has a history, every page holds a memory’.

🇸🇾 Syrian photographer Ahmed al-Bekkar recovers personal archive in Raqqa after more than a decade.

● Bekkar, 66, sealed his library before fleeing war to protect books, photographs and personal documents.

● He sought refuge in Türkiye amid attacks by Daesh (ISIS) and forces of ousted Syrian regime.

— Anadolu English (@anadoluagency) November 19, 2026.

Before leaving Syria, Bekkar built a wall to conceal the entrance to his personal library in the northern city of Raqqa, hoping to protect his extensive photographic archive. The archive contains approximately 3,000 printed photographs of Raqqa, as well as a much larger digital collection documenting historical sites, cities, and markets across Syria.

During his decade in Turkey, the now 66-year-old photographer continued his photographic work, helping establish the House of Migrants association and the Turkish-Arab Art House. Although Bekkar spent productive years doing photography in the Turkish capital of Istanbul, he says nothing could replace his hometown in Syria.

Anadolu Agency reports that Bekkar returned to Raqqa this year and reopened the library he had concealed behind the wall more than a decade earlier. The photographer describes watching the bricks being removed one by one from the wall as an emotional experience, having long dreamed of setting foot inside his archive again.

“Every piece of paper has a history, every page holds a memory. I spent time and effort obtaining and preserving every document,” the photographer tells the news outlet. “I feel that those days are preserved in these papers, whose owners are no longer with us, but which have remained behind.”.

According to recent reports, bekkar recalled growing up across from an old mosque in Raqqa, where the surrounding historical buildings inspired his interest in photography and Syria’s cultural heritage. Since returning to his hometown, Bekkar has revisited historical sites he photographed years earlier to document them now.
