November 24: A Super Beaver Moon occults the Pleiades.

Sources indicate that the distant ice giant Uranus reaches opposition tonight at 6 P.M. EST. Opposition is the best time to view a planet in our sky, and it’s visible roughly from dusk until dawn. Because Uranus is faint — magnitude 5.6 — you’ll want to give it a few hours after sunset to climb away from both light pollution and turbulent air near the horizon. Around 10 P.M. local time, you’ll find Uranus midway between the bright star Aldebaran and the Pleiades star cluster in Taurus the Bull, about 60° above the southeastern horizon. It sits some 1.5° south of 4th-magnitude 37 Tauri.

Industry observers note that although Uranus can be visible to the naked eye under clear, dark conditions, the bright waning Moon is nearby, so pull out binoculars or a telescope to view the far-off planet tonight. Once you land on its location, look for a “flat,” grayish-colored star. Uranus’ disk spans 4”, so you will likely be able to tell it is circular, rather than a pinpoint of light like any background stars.
