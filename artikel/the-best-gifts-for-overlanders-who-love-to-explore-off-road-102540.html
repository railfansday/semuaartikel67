It has been revealed that overlanding is a pretty niche activity, so it can be hard to find the perfect gift for the driver in your life. Fear not. As an overlander myself, I’ve compiled a comprehensive gift list that has suggestions for overland rookies and veterans alike. I’ve included a wide range of price options, with some gift ideas so significant they’ll take up half your living room and others small enough to fit under the tree.

Tires are the most key overland upgrade. A good set of all-terrain tires like the BFGoodrich KO3s helps drivers safely get through snow and mud, and up and down rocky backcountry roads. Their tougher construction also helps resist flats. I love the KO3s for their legendary off-road reliability combined with their quiet ride on pavement.

It has been revealed that overlanders love to get stuck so they can get back out, which makes recovery gear essential. Maxtrax boards slip under tires that have lost traction, allowing the vehicle to slowly inch out of snow, mud, sand, or whatever else it’s bogged down in. Go for the more expensive Xtreme version because it comes with metal traction teeth that last significantly longer.

Industry observers note that airing down tires is often the easiest way to get unstuck because it increases the tires’ contact patch. I love the Rhino kit because it airs down four tires at once. I also recommend this ARB air compressor that attaches to a car battery and can reinflate the tires before you hit pavement again.

Sometimes self-recovery isn’t an option after getting stuck, and I’ve had to get yanked out by a friend. This device fits in the two-inch rear receiver on any truck or SUV and creates a safe place to attach a D-ring and recovery strap. Heads up: Make sure whoever you gift this to knows how to use it properly before heading out.

Folded down, this shovel is only 18 inches long. Fully extended, it goes to 34 inches and is perfect for digging your truck out of a snowbank or excavating a cathole near camp. I’ve tested an earlier version of this shovel for years, and the carbon steel head has put up with rocks, trees, and firm ground without ever bending or chipping.

Anyone who’s overlanding for more than a couple of days will love an electric fridge. It eliminates ice runs and keeps food from drowning in meltwater. Dometic makes some of the most reliable fridges on the market. I like this one because it has two zones: one a fridge, the other either a fridge or a freezer.

In a recent development, coffee tastes better at 9,000 feet. To brew it there, Yeti’s indestructible French press is my go-to. I’ve thrown it in a kitchen box or just let it roll around in the back of my truck, and it still works perfectly every time. I also love that it has a ceramic lining so the coffee doesn’t taste like metal.

Recent findings suggest that water can be a precious commodity on a backcountry trip. That’s why I use this battery-powered faucet that pulls water from a jug and then dispenses just the right amount so I can wash my hands or do dishes. One charge lasts for weeks, and all the hoses are easy to clean at home. Pair it with a Dometic water jug for the ideal setup.

Recent findings suggest that the REDARC GoBlock is my go-to overland battery (for fridges, lights, etc.) because it’s designed to be charged in multiple ways. I can connect solar panels to add juice at camp, or I can wire it to my alternator so it gets topped off while I’m driving. I recommend the kit version, which includes a dock that bolts to a truck bed and gives the battery a secure place to live at all times.

iKamper nailed the details with this version of the Skycamp rooftop tent. It has the smartest mounting system I’ve tested. The bombproof hardshell cover will put up with years of sun and weather, and inside it’s extra roomy thanks to a clever tent design. It’s pricey, but this is one of those buy-once, cry-once purchases that you’ll have for at least a decade.

Two layers of enhanced memory foam make the Foamy the most comfortable camping mat on the market. Nothing else I’ve tested even comes close. Perfect for larger tents or truck beds, the Foamy almost guarantees a good night’s sleep because it’s so plush. Pair it with a camping blanket and the setup will be almost as comfortable as what you’re used to at home.

According to recent reports, overlanding requires a lot of gear. The best way to keep it all organized is a Kitworks Gear Box. Infinitely customizable, the Gear Box is the perfect storage method for a camp kitchen, recovery gear, dry food, even clothing. Made from honeycomb panels covered in a 900-denier ripstop shell, the box has proven just as durable as any hard-sided competitor.

Recent findings suggest that don’t invest in a dash-mounted radio—get two of these instead. With an ultra-reliable, multi-day battery and huge range, these radios are the best way to stay in touch with buddies when cell phones don’t work. When I’m not using the radios in my truck, I use them to keep in contact while backcountry skiing, hunting, and other off-grid adventures.
