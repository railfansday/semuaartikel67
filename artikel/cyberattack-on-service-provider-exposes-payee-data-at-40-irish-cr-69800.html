Cyberattack on service provider exposes payee data at 40 Irish credit unions

Minister calls notification delay 'not acceptable' after October attack on Irish banking player Wellington IT came to light in November.

An attack on software provider Wellington IT exposed saved payee details held by around 40 Irish credit unions, prompting a minister to criticize the tech giant's delay in notifying them.

Robert Troy, Ireland's minister of state with responsibility for credit unions, said the breach happened in October but many affected institutions only learned of it in recent days.

Troy told Irish broadcaster RTÉ that the breach occurred "sometime in October," and Wellington IT's delay in providing details to its credit union customers was "not acceptable.".

News of the problems affecting Irish credit unions broke on Tuesday, although the government mentioned at the time it was only investigating a "possible cybersecurity incident" in the first instance, and did not name Wellington IT until Wednesday.

Affected credit unions said attackers exploited a vulnerability in their provider's online banking platform between October 2 and 4. The flaw was closed on October 6.

Troy stated the affected credit unions were only informed for the first time in recent days.

Industry observers note that the exposed records concern payees saved in members' online banking accounts. Those records can also be linked to the members who created them.

The information exposed varies between institutions and individual records, but disclosures list the following data types:.

Names on payee bank accounts and saved nicknames.

Business Identifier Codes (BICs).

International Bank Account Numbers (IBANs).

Internal reference numbers that do not identify customers.

Credit unions including St. Canice's said online banking passwords, PINs, and login credentials were unaffected, and that the exposed payee details did not provide access to members' accounts or funds.

In a recent development, st. Canice's warned members not to transfer money in response to anyone claiming it needed to be moved to safety because of the breach.

The CEO of Belfast-based Wellington IT, Colin Mitchell, mentioned in a statement that the company notified the affected organizations "as soon as we confirmed the potential risk to customer data.".

He said Wellington IT has engaged outside cybersecurity experts to help investigate the breach, and reported the matter to the relevant authorities.

Mitchell added: "We are directly supporting our customers in their assessment of the potential impact on affected individuals and to help them meet any applicable regulatory obligations.

It has been revealed that "We recognize the importance of this matter to our customers and their members and are treating it with the utmost seriousness. We remain focused on completing a thorough investigation and communicating directly with our customers as the investigation progresses.".

Owned by Canadian manufacturer Vencora, Wellington IT has offices in Belfast and Dublin, and provides credit unions with software, mobile apps, private cloud infrastructure, and fully managed services.

Around 40 credit unions in Ireland were affected, roughly a quarter of the national total, Troy stated.

Credit unions operate similarly to traditional banks, offering savings accounts and loans, but are owned by their members and are not-for-profit organizations.

Troy told RTÉ that cybersecurity is a constant challenge for financial institutions, as technology and threats evolve over time.

"What's most notable is that financial institutions take all the right measures to mitigate against these threats and that is what is happening," he said.

Separately, the UK's Financial Conduct Authority, Prudential Regulation Authority, and Bank of England opened a joint consultation on cyber resilience guidance on Thursday.

The FCA remarked October brought its second-highest monthly tally of reported cyber incidents in five years, and the highest in 18 months. The proposed guidance clarifies how firms should meet existing requirements rather than introducing fresh rules. ®.
