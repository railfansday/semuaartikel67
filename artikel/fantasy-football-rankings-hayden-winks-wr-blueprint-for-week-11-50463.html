The Blueprint is my weekly column and basically everything that I use to build out the most accurate rankings I can. There will be an RB column, this WR column and a combination column on the onesie positions. You will find film notes and endless charts. Maybe enough charts to stress test the Yahoo website. I kid.

It's impossible to get every ranking thoroughly early in the week, so be sure to keep checking in on these columns through Sunday morning. As always, my full rankings at every position are available.

It has been revealed that 1. Ja'Marr Chase (at WAS) - 93.5 proj. receiving yards.

Chase is back to averaging 98 YPG this year after a WR1 overall stretch over the past month. The Commanders are a pass-funnel defense without a strong secondary. They've allowed the fifth-most passing YPG, and Chase hung 118-2 against them in his only game against Washington back in 2024.

It has been revealed that 2. CeeDee Lamb (vs. TEN) - 90.5 proj. receiving yards.

Lamb has been pretty up-and-down this year, but on average, he's the WR3 on WR2 overall usage as a no-doubt Round 1 value. He's leading the position in target share (39%) and air yards (145) over the past month, as the Cowboys defense has continued to allow anyone through the front door.

Sources indicate that now he gets a dream matchup against a Titans defense allowing 35.0 half PPR points per game to WRs (first). They've allowed the most points under 20 air yards, specifically, where Lamb does almost all of his damage. They've been even worse of late without DT Jeffery Simmons and S Amani Hooker. Dallas enters as 8.5-point home favorites with a slate-leading 31.5-point team total (first).

3. Justin Jefferson (at SF) - 82.5 proj. receiving yards.

The Vikings passed more in neutral situations last week with Kirk Cousins compared to over the past year and a half with underwhelming QBs. That'll help Jefferson find his mid-range WR1 groove, on top of those throws being far more accurate. Jefferson had three screens last week, plus other underneath targets, allowing him to post a season-high 19.3 expected half PPR points (WR5). They didn't rip the explosive play, but those will be coming. In the short term, Jordan Addison won't be out there either.

This week is a great spot for Jefferson. The Vikings are slight dogs, yet still have a 21.5-point team total in what should be a down week across the NFL. The 49ers were shredded by the Cowboys' pass game last week and have been given up 27.7 half PPR points to WR rooms on average this entire season. Most of those will go to Jefferson here.

4. Amon-Ra St. Brown (vs. TB) - 76.5 proj. receiving yards.

In a recent development, st. Brown was on a five-game streak without a touchdown before finishing last week with 23.8 half PPR points in Germany with a couple of long scores. He's been a target monster all year long and remains a positive regression candidate. The slot star is the WR5 on WR4 usage and WR4 targets per game this year.

It has been revealed that the Buccaneers have been stout against WRs overall, ranking 24th in half PPR points allowed (24.1 per game). However, they rank seventh in points allowed to receiver targets under 10 air yards, fitting St. Brown's underneath profile. Major slots like Luther Burden III (WR9) and Germie Bernard (WR6) have had spike weeks against them recently. Detroit enters as seven-point home favorites with a 25.25-point team total (ninth).

It has been revealed that 5. Tee Higgins (at WAS) - 74.5 proj. receiving yards.

It has been revealed that it'll be hard to run as pure as he has so far. Higgins is the WR6 on WR16 usage and WR14 targets per game. The good news is Higgins has the best film of his career, and Joe Burrow is firmly in his absolute prime. This is a pairing that should be dunking all over models. On top of that, the Bengals have the highest neutral pass rate (67%) in the NFL. When games are close, Higgins will see eight+ targets per game. The Commanders are an eruption spot.

Recent findings suggest that 6. George Pickens (vs. TEN) - 69.5 proj. receiving yards.

We're so back. Pickens is the WR12 on WR10 usage this season, on WR11 targets per game. He's not even running hot either. He finally had one of his signature blowup games last week, thanks to the Cowboys' unwatchable defense. Pickens is on the WR1/2 border depending on the matchup. He is best when facing single high, as Dak Prescott can take perimeter shots to him with more success.

(Chart Hayden Winks/Yahoo Sports).

Sources indicate that the Titans don't have anyone in the secondary to worry about. They've allowed the most fantasy points to WRs, including the third-most points on targets with 15+ air yards. They won't have FS Amani Hooker roaming deep now either. Dallas carries a 31.5-point implied team total (first) as 8.5-point home favorites.

Industry observers note that 7. Nico Collins (vs. IND) - 71.5 proj. receiving yards.

Industry observers note that collins ran more routes last week — thank you!!! — and it worked. He turned 15 targets into a WR7 week. The Browns held their own in the second half by blitzing and playing tight man coverage behind it. Collins will have more space to operate in future matchups, and the Texans offense is trending towards being more and more pass-heavy. They're now fifth in neutral pass rate after usually being bottom-10 under the DeMeco Ryans regime. Collins is a fringe WR1/2 with plenty of weekly upside.

Sources indicate that collins is looking to extend his four-game streak of 100+ yards against Indy. The Colts won't have CB1 Sauce Gardner, nor CB2 Charvarius Ward. This should be BBQ chicken. Houston enters as 12.5-point home favorites with an implied team total of 28.5 points (fifth).

Recent findings suggest that 8. Zay Flowers (at CAR) - 68.5 proj. receiving yards.

It would've been best if Lamar Jackson was playing like the MVP, but PPR scam artist Jacoby Brissett is a great backup plan for Flowers' fantasy appeal. We instantly felt that last week when they trailed the Chargers. They had a season-high neutral pass rate (73%) in an offense that's been last in dropbacks per game since 2022 when Flowers entered the NFL. We'll trade some downfield completions for more volume moving forward. I'm throwing out the Week 8-9 sample and putting more faith into last week's 6-74-0 line on seven targets.

(Chart Hayden Winks/Yahoo Sports).

According to recent reports, we'll see if the Panthers are still going to be without their top-2 CBs later in the week. They've been middle of the pack against fantasy WRs this year, with Jordyn Tyson turning 13 targets into 80 scoreless yards last week as the most recent example.

9. Parker Washington (at NYG) - 64.5 proj. receiving yards.

Washington can get game-scripted out of production in second halves, but that is a good problem to have, as he can either go crazy early or walk into full-game production when games are tighter. He's inside the top 15 in every per-route metric essentially, and Washington is still the WR13 despite being the WR25 in targets per game. It's clear that he's both Trevor Lawrence and Liam Coen's favorite target. How could he not be? He's smart, physical, explosive and can be used inside and out. Star.

Sources indicate that the Giants do have a strong secondary with a great CB1 in Deonte Banks. Washington gets moved around into favorable matchups, and I trust Coen to find spots for him. But the Giants are 30th in passing yards per game allowed (196). They've shut down deep targets in particular, so it's possible Washington racks up more underneath and intermediate targets this time around. Jacksonville travels as 6-point road favorites with a 26.25-point team total (seventh).

In a recent development, 10. Malik Nabers (vs. JAX) - 65.5 proj. receiving yards.

Recent findings suggest that nabers is the WR12 on WR4 overall usage since Week 5 after knocking off the rust early. He's still not 100% himself and does seem to be mad offline, but the spike-week potential is fully on the menu when the ball is going his way. Over that span, only JSN, Puka Nacua, Ja'Marr Chase and Justin Jefferson are ahead of him in target share, so when the Giants are forced into dropbacks, expect huge games down the stretch. J.J. McCarthy is pushing the ball on these deeper out routes relentlessly and looks generally improved, even if he's volatile.

In a recent development, the Jaguars will be a real test. Travis Hunter is in the mix for the second-best CB tape right now, and he'll be joined by FS Jessie Bates III now. The only good news is the Giants are expected to be trailing, and offenses facing the Jaguars have the second-highest neutral pass rate (59%). For those reasons, the Jaguars are 15th in fantasy points allowed to WRs. The Giants enter as 6-point home underdogs carrying an implied team total of 20.25 points (18th).
