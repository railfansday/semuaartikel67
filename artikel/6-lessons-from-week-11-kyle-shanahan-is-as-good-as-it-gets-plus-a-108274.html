Industry observers note that when I think about Thanksgiving, I think about going to my grandparents' house growing up. We'd leave immediately after my dad got off work on Wednesday and drive from my hometown in rural Virginia to my mom's parents in New Jersey. A quick stop at the McDonald's was the only time we'd leave our route. We'd drive extra miles to avoid traffic. I'd try, often unsuccessfully, to fight off sleep on the drive up.

Around midnight — sometimes later — we'd arrive at my grandparents' doorstep, weary but happy. They'd always greet us with smiling faces, no matter the hour.

Sources indicate that and then there was Thanksgiving Day, which meant football. My grandpa, a die-hard Giants fan, would lead our collective yelling at the TV, hoping the Cowboys would lose, and we'd pity-root for the Lions, who seemingly never won.

According to recent reports, the results fade, but what I remember is my family being together. It wasn't perfect. It got harder as my grandparents got older. We'd laugh, we'd cheer, we'd wish it could last forever.

It's been several years since then, but I still remember my grandparents waving us goodbye, my grandpa good-heartedly reminding my dad to go south, not north, on the turnpike. It felt like just minutes earlier, they had been standing in that doorway welcoming us in. It always went too fast.

If all you can do is call or text, great. It doesn't have to be perfect. Whether you watch every football game this week or none at all, I hope you're surrounded by people you love.

Recent findings suggest that coach of the Year has become so misconstrued, and not just in the NFL. Every individual player award honors the best player, but for some reason Coach of the Year honors the coach whose team exceeds expectations most.

So, once again, Kyle Shanahan's magnificent job will likely go unrewarded, simply because he's done too good a job for too long. An early congratulations to either John Harbaugh or Todd Monken for winning. They've done magnificent jobs.

But Kyle Shanahan is the best coach in the NFL this season. The 49ers just dismantled the Vikings to move to 9-1. They have won games in Australia and Mexico and Seattle and Dallas, all places with their own unique challenges. They have won games using wide receivers they never imagined having to count on.

Industry observers note that thought you saw the best of Brock Purdy? Think again. I've written at length about why Purdy deserves MVP and is much more than just a product of the Shanahan system, but there's no doubt Shanahan has raised his game immensely. This offense is so well-constructed, so prepared for each and every task each and every week and so connected.

The Vikings love to blitz. Purdy shredded them with quick passes: 12-for-13 for 161 yards and two touchdowns on throws released in 2.5 seconds or faster. Minnesota entered the night leading the NFL in sack rate and pressure rate. It managed just one sack and a 29% pressure rate, both season lows.

This is nothing newly introduced, by the way.

Oh yeah, and Shanahan also made the excellent hire of defensive coordinator Raheem Morris, who has helped turn this defense from quite poor to elite.

It's silly that we'll look back and likely see someone else win Coach of the Year. Shanahan has never won the award, by the way, even though he's been the best or among the best in the profession for nearly a decade. Andy Reid, who's fourth all-time in wins, has won it once, back in 2002 with the Eagles. Sean McVay has won it once since becoming a head coach in 2017. Kevin Stefanski, a perfectly solid coach but nothing more, has won it twice.

Again, even if he doesn't win "Coach of the Year," Shanahan is very much the defining coach of this year. He'd gladly take a Lombardi Trophy over any individual award, anyway.

Sources indicate that one day after Brian Flores' normally excellent defense was shredded, one of his disciplines had his best performance yet. Commanders defensive coordinator Daronte Jones and his unit were terrific in a 22-20 upset win over the Bengals.

In a recent development, jones, like Flores, brings extra pressure, massive time. Washington is third in blitz rate this year. Joe Burrow has destroyed blitzes all year, but on Monday night, not so much.

Jones was without his best cornerback, Amik Robertson, but refused to back down. His corners played physically, aggressively and well. The safeties followed in kind. And while Cincinnati's running game has never been a strength, Washington turned it into a decided weakness. The Bengals' 14% rushing success rate was the worst by any team in any game this year.

Industry observers note that jones has this defense moving in the right direction, too. In November, the Commanders rank 15th in defensive EPA per play; they were 23rd before that. They've done a good job stopping the run and limiting explosives, largely because they have the third-lowest missed tackle rate in the league. Washington hasn't allowed more than 23 points in a game since Week 5.

There will be big-picture coaching questions in Washington if the team doesn't make the playoffs, but Jones has done solid work under the radar.

Industry observers note that I wrote about this in our Week 11 overreactions, but the Steelers are once again a team with a winning record and little chance of winning a playoff game. Here, though, let's talk about the style.

Aaron Rodgers is now completing 21.6% of his passes thrown 20+ yards downfield. While he is throwing way more of them this year — only Jordan Love has more than Rodgers' 51 attempts — they're mostly performative "See? I'll still take shots downfield" throws that are low-percentage floaters. Only Cam Ward and recently released Daniel Jones have a lower completion rate on these throws.

Industry observers note that the run game can be quite good. In fact, the Steelers are second in rushing EPA per play this year at 0.06. Earlier in the season, it seemed they finally had one truly sterling offensive aspect. This made them different.

In a recent development, but when the run game isn't going well, as has been the case the last two weeks, Pittsburgh has nothing else. The Steelers are 4-0 when their EPA per rush is above their overall season mark and 2-4 when it is below it. Pittsburgh was at -0.19, second-worst of the season, in the Week 11 loss to the Eagles.

The Steelers have been the same as they've been during this playoff win drought: poor quarterbacking, good-not-great defense.

The Chiefs' three-game winning streak has coincided with a soft schedule. There's not a ton to take away from it in terms of Kansas City's standing in the championship-contending pecking order.

Sources indicate that but on an individual level, Emmett Johnson is a beast. In his two starts with Kenneth Walker III out, Johnson has 134 yards from scrimmage and three touchdowns.

According to recent reports, johnson currently leads all running backs in 7.0 yards per touch (min. 50 touches). He also leads all running backs in tackle avoidance rate (36.8), which is also first and slightly higher than tackle-avoiding machine Walker III.

Johnson has a true three-down skill set. Only 11.3% of his rushes go for 0 or negative yards, the fourth-lowest rate among all running backs with at least 50 touches, and he has turned several potential losses into gains.

Walker is a stud. He'll be the feature back when he returns from his groin injury. But Johnson has proved he not only deserves a legit complementary role down the stretch and beyond, but with his pass-catching acumen, he can actually be a weapon, too.

Sources indicate that the Colts got thumped 26-3 by the Texans on Thursday, and that somehow wasn't the worst aspect of their week. Not even close.

One day after that, Indianapolis released Daniel Jones, and shortly after that, reports surfaced that at the trade deadline, the Colts had turned down the Ravens' offer of a fourth-and fifth-round pick for Jones (the fifth would be returned if Baltimore made the playoffs). Now, they get nothing for Jones and will carry a $35.2 million dead cap hit in 2027.

It has been revealed that if interim GM Ed Dodds wasn't already filling in for a fired GM (Chris Ballard), this would be a fireable offense. Maybe it is, anyway. The Colts sold away many of their franchise pillars but stopped at Daniel Jones?

According to various reports, the Colts turned away multiple suitors for Jones, including Baltimore's potentially lucrative one; multiple suitors for Jonathan Taylor, who fetched offers for a second-round pick plus more; at least one offer that included a fourth-round pick for veteran defensive tackle Grover Stewart, who's 33 and in a contract year.

According to recent reports, I gave the Colts a D+ in my trade deadline grades and, upon first reflection, thought I might have been a little harsh. Indianapolis did get decent draft capital back for the moves it did make. But now, I think that mark might be generous. Every meaningless hard hit Taylor takes, and every meaningless double-team Stewart takes on, is just that. Meaningless.

According to recent reports, the Colts have very little young, cost-controlled talent. They don't have their first-round pick in 2027. They will have a upcoming GM and, likely, a new coach. They are arguably the worst team in the NFL in 2026. They will be in the running for worst team in the NFL in 2027 and potentially 2028. They just turned down draft capital for a player they didn't even want. If there were a class in mismanaging an enterprise worth billions of dollars, the Colts' front office could do a great job teaching it.

If it feels like there's been a little bit of a midseason lull, it's because there has been. In November, there's been a ton of blowouts — seven games decided by 15+ every week from Week 8-10, and then a lot of byes and semi-competitive games in Week 11.

But the league is cranking it up a notch this coming week.

According to recent reports, the Bears have won five straight, but their last two are over the lowly Buccaneers and Saints, with a bye in between. The Lions have won six straight, but the last three are over the Dolphins, the Patriots (mostly without Drake Maye) and the Buccaneers. I'm a huge believer in "you can only beat who's on your schedule." But in terms of learning about these teams, the past few weeks haven't been very helpful.

That all changes this week. On Thanksgiving, the 8-2 Lions host the 8-2 Bears (on CBS and streaming on Paramount+, by the way!), which should be an absolute banger.
