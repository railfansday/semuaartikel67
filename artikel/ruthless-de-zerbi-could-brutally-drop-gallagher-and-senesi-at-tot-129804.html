📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

After a disappointing start to the season, Tottenham Hotspur have the opportunity to make it five wins from six Premier League matches when they face lowly Sunderland at the Stadium of Light on Sunday afternoon.

Tottenham were bottom of the table after six league games and some pressure was beginning to build upon Roberto De Zerbi following a £300m-plus summer transfer spend.

However, their fortunes have turned ever since they beat Coventry City 3-1 at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium on October 19, and they now lie in 10th position and just six points adrift of the Champions League spots following Saturday's routine 2-0 home victory against Ipswich Town.

The win against the Tractor Boys was the latest sign of their improving home form, and they have now taken 10 points from a possible 18 on their own patch, but their away results need to improve if they want to seriously compete for a spot in the top four.

What is clear is that the Premier League chase for European football is wide open this season, with Manchester City and Chelsea dropping off in recent weeks, and Liverpool still showing a lack of consistency at times.

It means that Brighton and Hove Albion, Bournemouth and Brentford currently lie in the top six, and there is a significant opportunity for Tottenham too, if they can put a proper run together.

The north London outfit have only picked up one win on the road so far, scoring three times in five matches, with a goal difference of minus two.

De Zerbi will see a trip to 18th-place Sunderland as a major opportunity to record another three points, but he has some big selection decisions to make for the match.

The Italian coach left captain Micky van de Ven on the substitutes' bench against Ipswich following the international break, while Sandro Tonali was omitted because of illness and Mathys Tel missed out through injury.

The likes of Luca Williams-Barnett, Marcos Senesi and Conor Gallagher shone in the absence of those players, and now De Zerbi will be considering ruthlessly axing two of those on Wearside.

It's a positive for De Zerbi that he was able to give his captain a rest and that Tottenham could still deliver a comfortable three points against Ipswich, but it now means he has a bit of a dilemma.

Clearly, Van de Ven and his Dutch compatriot Jan Paul van Hecke are the first-choice and strongest partnership based on the early weeks of the campaign, boosted by the fact they also play together for the Netherlands.

But Senesi staked his own claim for the starting spot following an 8/10 performance against Gary O'Neil's side last weekend, where he looked both strong and assured alongside Van Hecke.

Perhaps what was most impressive about the Argentine was the ability he revealed in picking out passes into the midfield, which made a big difference to Spurs' attacking play.

Still, it will be difficult for De Zerbi to leave his captain out again, and you would expect Van de Ven to return to the XI in the north-east as the club look to sort out their away form.

Meanwhile, Gallagher was another who produced an 8/10 display alongside Mateus Fernandes in the heart of the midfield, impressing with his high work rate as usual.

It was an all-round performance from the Englishman though, who produced some vital blocks but was also good in possession, which isn't always one of his main strengths.

However, with £100m man Tonali likely to return following illness, you would expect him to walk straight back into the XI alongside Fernandes.

Gallagher can also play further forward too of course, but James Maddison's return to fitness and his display againt Ipswich surely mean he is another guaranteed starter at Sunderland.

De Zerbi would rather have these conundrums than not as it reveals the strength of the Spurs squad, but it also means he must make ruthless calls on a weekly basis.
