📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As part of a summit meeting in Manchester yesterday, the IWGB Game Workers union have joined up with other unions spanning the Republic of Ireland's GWUI, Germany's GDRT, Italy's FIOM-CGIL, France's STJV, and Spain's CSVI to publish a manifesto that aims to "bring to light the risks and dangers genAI poses to the workforce" for consumers, workers and publishers alike.

The manifesto states that "GenAI is currently harming the games industry, its workers and the games they produce," and suggests actions that can be taken by all to reduce harm done to the industry. The manifesto specifies the "severe harm to the environment, the destruction of communities and the exploitation of the Global South" as wider harms of the technology, but maintains its focus on the industry itself.

The effects of the technology on gamemakers, such as "directly and indirectly replacing workers", "worker fatigue, cognitive decline and burnout", and "a marked decline in junior positions in the industry" are listed as core harms to be avoided and fought against, with the manifesto also noting that it also "removes a sense of ownership from workers [...] the things they produce no longer feel like they have the workers' stamp on them, and therefore they can no longer take pride in them." As it states, "games are best made by people.".

The manifesto goes on to explains the unions' stance that genAI is "sold to employers as an alternative to workers", and its "predisposition towards sycophancy and lack of real world context can lead to an increase in unreasonable, damaging requests when used by employers to set policy, timescales, workloads, etc., as well as to task expansion and a general degradation of conditions." In addition, its nature as a tool built on the theft of stolen third-party data is highlighted as an inherent danger, and the so-called loophole in only training models on internal data is equally highlighted as resulting in employees and creators rarely being credited or compensated for their work that is fed into the machine. This glitch also ties into the inherent implicit and explicit discrimination that exists in genAI content due to datasets' "intrinsic and exclusionary Western bias that limits diversity and marginalises non-dominant languages, cultures, and ethnicities", which leads to "a homogenisation of culture.".

In a recent development, as for action, the manifest recommends that workers should "avoid usage of genAI services whenever possible", "distance yourself from genAI usage", and "make it clear to your employer why you do not want to use genAI", as well as, of course, to "organise. Unionise. Do it now, not when it is too late and you are already under threat." Similarly, employers are recommended to "ensure that no worker is required to use genAI", and to "disallow the use of genAI in the company code of conduct, even for early stages of development." Finally, for consumers, it simply advises to "support games made without genAI", and to "be vocal about disliking genAI in games.".

It's an incredibly clear and concise statement that calls to mind the statement made by a selection of IGF jurors on the state of genAI in the industry and their decision not to vote for any game that uses genAI in any capacity. As both of these statements are joined in saying, "we do not have to accept the use of genAI to make video games; it is not inevitable.".
