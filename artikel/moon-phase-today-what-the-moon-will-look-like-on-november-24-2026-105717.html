Moon phase today: What the Moon will look like on November 24, 2026

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Full Moon is here, and this one's a Supermoon. That means the Moon is closer to Earth than usual, making it appear slightly bigger and brighter in the night sky.

It has been revealed that as of Tuesday, Nov. 24, the NASA's Daily Moon Guide tracker tells us the Moon is in the Full Moon phase, with 100% of its surface visible.

In a recent development, if you don't have any visual aids, you can still see the Mares Imbrium and Crisium, as well as the Copernicus Crater. With binoculars, you'll also see the Archimedes Crater, the Endymion Crater, and the Alphonsus Crater. And if you have a telescope, you'll get to see all this plus the Apollo 15 landing spot, the Schiller Crater, and the Rima Hyginus.

The next Full Moon will take place on November 24.

In a recent development, it takes around 29.5 days for the Moon to move through all its phases, according to NASA. Throughout this period, the Moon's position relative to Earth and the Sun determines how much of its illuminated surface we can see. Starting with a New Moon, the visible portion gradually increases until it reaches a Full Moon, before shrinking again as the cycle comes back around.

New Moon - The Moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (in other words, it's invisible to the eye).

Waxing Crescent - A small sliver of light appears on the right side (Northern Hemisphere).

It has been revealed that first Quarter - Half of the Moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-Moon.

Waxing Gibbous - More than half is lit up, but it's not quite full yet.

Full Moon - The whole face of the Moon is illuminated and fully visible.

Sources indicate that waning Gibbous - The Moon starts losing light on the right side. (Northern Hemisphere).

Third Quarter (or Last Quarter) - Another half-Moon, but now the left side is lit.

Waning Crescent - A thin sliver of light remains on the left side before going dark again.
