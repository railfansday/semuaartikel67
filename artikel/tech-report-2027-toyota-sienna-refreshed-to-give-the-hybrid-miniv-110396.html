In a recent development, some mid-cycle updates focus on aesthetics, bringing some newly introduced style (and hopefully some new eyes) to a slightly older product. The 2027 Toyota Sienna skipped those pleasantries and focused on the meat and potatoes: its hybrid powertrain.

Industry observers note that the 2027 Toyota Sienna looks no different—unless you count the Cypress paint color, which was dropped and swapped with Storm Cloud—but it’s what’s lying under the body that matters more here. The Sienna adopts Toyota’s sixth-generation hybrid powertrain, which first arrived in the upscale Lexus ES Hybrid. A new electric motor packs more efficient silicon-carbide conductors, and it pairs with a 1.15-kWh battery that ditches the old nickel-metal hydride chemistry in favor of lithium-ion. In addition, there’s a whole new electrical platform underpinning it all, with new ECUs and software to manage everything.

Combine all that with a 2.5-liter four-cylinder gasoline engine that has been retuned for quieter low-speed operation, and you have a Toyota Sienna that produces a whopping six horsepower more than before, rising from 245 horses to 251. Engine torque is unchanged at 176 pound-feet, and Toyota did not specify e-motor torque. That small power bump is enough to affect its acceleration: Toyota estimates a 7.5-second sprint to 60 mph in front-wheel-drive Sienna variants, rising to 7.6 seconds with AWD. That’s 0.3 or 0.4 second quicker than before, respectively.

Even with that bump in speed, Toyota claims improved fuel economy in the 2027 Sienna, to the tune of +3 mpg in the city for FWD models and +4 mpg in the city for AWD models. Combined estimates rise 1 or 2 mpg. For context, the 2026 model is EPA estimated at 36 mpg city/36 highway/36 combined for the front-driver, and 35/36/35 for the AWD.

There have also been some tweaks to other electrical-adjacent components. The electronic power steering promises less effort at low speeds, while improving on-center feel at highway speeds. That adjustment includes a latest steering-wheel layout that will eventually migrate to other sixth-gen-equipped hybrids. Toyota also fiddled with its brake electronics, resulting in less pedal effort and a smoother blend between regen and friction braking. The Sienna also includes something Toyota calls Vehicle Braking Posture Control, which applies braking to individual wheels with the aim to reduce body roll during cornering.

In a recent development, creature comforts get some upgrades as well. A 12.3-inch infotainment screen is now standard across the lineup (the LE grade ditches its 8.0-incher), as is a 12.3-inch gauge cluster (LE and XLE get this instead of the old 7.0-inch getup). There’s newly introduced LED cabin lighting, plus a revised rear-seat entertainment system that now features native apps on a new touchscreen. Upgraded dashboard sound abatement promises to lower the cabin volume by one decibel.

According to recent reports, toyota also slimmed down the Sienna’s trims for 2027. The Limited grade has been dropped in favor of adding more features to the sporty XSE variant, which rises to become the trim underneath the Platinum. As a result, the 2027 Sienna XSE picks up niceties like ventilated front seats with memory, a panoramic-view monitor, and a head-up display. The rugged Woodland Edition trim also picks up the surround-view tech. The upgraded rear-seat entertainment is available on the Woodland, XSE, and Platinum trims.

It has been revealed that the 2027 Toyota Sienna reaches dealerships in early—you guessed it—2027. Pricing starts around $44,500 for the front-wheel-drive LE base trim, which represents a price bump of less than $2000 over a base 2026 Sienna. Not too shabby for a whole newly introduced generation of powertrain.
