£2m West Brom loanee getting what he needs in League One loan – but one glaring issue remains

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

West Brom signed Alfie Gilchrist from Chelsea in a deal worth up to £2m in the summer of 2025. He enjoyed a positive spell on loan at Sheffield United in the 2024/25 campaign, so there was hope that the promising defender could become a valuable asset for WBA.

It has been revealed that however, a tough debut campaign resulted in Gilchrist dropping down the pecking order. As such, the 22-year-old was sent out on loan to Leyton Orient for the 2026/27 campaign, giving him a chance to play regularly in his preferred role as a centre-back.

According to recent reports, gilchrist has found the game time he needed in his strongest position, and he’s earned praise for his performances too. However, there are some glaring weaknesses that will worry those back at The Hawthorns.

Industry observers note that gilchrist made his first two Leyton Orient appearances off the bench before a spell out through injury. It meant he had to wait until October 10th for his full League One debut, and the West Brom loanee has not relinquished his starting berth since.

According to recent reports, in the last five third-tier games, Gilchrist has helped keep five clean sheets with Orient winning two, losing two and drawing one. He also played the full 90 minutes in the 5-1 FA Cup win over Harborough Town earlier this month.

Industry observers note that with Gilchrist on the pitch, the O’s have conceded 0.53 goals per 90. He has averaged 12.27 defensive contributions per game, along with 9.07 clearances as well.

However, at WBA, there were worries over whether Gilchrist had the physical and aerial presence to deal with powerful Championship forwards. He’s a committed defender, but in terms of winning duels, his struggles have continued out on loan.

Recent findings suggest that gilchrist is averaging a duel success rate of 53.8% with 3.74 won per 90, ranking him in the bottom 18% and 10% of League One centre-backs (all stats via FotMob). The Baggies loanee has shown his ability in one-on-one defending, but when it comes to winning physical battles, he continues to struggle.

From his breakthrough at Chelsea, everyone knows Gilchrist has strong potential as a defender. He’s old-fashioned in the way that he likes to get stuck in and highlights no hesitation in clearing the ball to guard his box.

In a recent development, however, when the game is as physical as it is now, teams need dominance in duels from their centre-backs. Gilchrist could not offer this at West Brom, and it is a weak part of his game at Leyton Orient as well.

It will raise questions over whether Gilchrist can cement a place at centre-back at WBA in the future. Perhaps his defensive ability will see him adapt to a full-back role in time, but if he wants to play at the heart of defence, he seems unlikely to do so in James Morrison’s 4-4-2 setup at Championship level.

Gilchrist will strive to improve in this area though. His mentality and commitment to defending means he can still progress into a fine centre-back, but questions will persist over his chances of doing so at The Hawthorns or in the Championship.
