Taiwanese officials raise alarm over interference by China in elections that will shape fortunes of ruling party.

Taiwanese voters go to the polls on Saturday in closely watched local elections that will shape the fortunes of the ruling Democratic Progressive party (DPP) as it navigates an increasingly precarious set of relationships on the global stage.

Saturday’s vote will choose mayors, council members and other local leaders across 22 districts. Unlike the presidential elections, in which foreign policy is often a key matter, the local elections, held every four years, are largely focused on bread-and-butter issues such as housing affordability and wealth distribution.
