Aspects of GTA 6 are inspired by Red Dead Redemption 2's honor system because its player agency 'was a hard thing to ignore and walk away from'

Industry observers note that grand Theft Auto 6 doesn't just mark a huge, and at times unbelievable, step forward for graphics; it's also at the forefront of what an open world game can be, just look at the existential crisis it's given Cyberpunk 2's creative director. With thousands of enterable buildings, unpredictable and slightly scary NPCs, and systems that help you determine what kind of criminal you want to be. It all really does feel like a milestone for what videogames can do.

Industry observers note that "As the resolution goes up, you want the sophistication of the experience to go up, and obviously the sophistication of the mechanics," Rob Nelson, Rockstar's head of development, states in an interview with Eurogamer. "So it just naturally flowed that we give players choices to interact with the world beyond just violence, beyond just 'down the barrel of a gun', because it's fun.".

It has been revealed that the sophisticated mechanics that Nelson is talking about here pertains to GTA 6's Criminal Profile, a system that evaluates your escapades across Leonida and gives you a rating. There's Professional, Hardened, Violent, Psycho, and Irredeemable. The more stealthy and non-lethal you are, the higher your profile will be. It's similar to Red Dead Redemption 2's Honor System.

Recent findings suggest that "There was maybe more emotional resonance in the story of RDR2 than we had, certainly in a GTA before," Nelson indicates. "So we liked that idea. We didn't necessarily come to GTA 6 going: 'We must have emotional resonance,' but all of those things combine. It's more that those systems and that element of player choice and agency, and the way that affected the narrative, was a hard thing to ignore and walk away from.".

In a recent development, nelson explains that most of the groundwork started as "simple things" in principle, "but they create a lot more work in terms of development of the sophistication of the systems." It's all about choice in GTA 6. You choose whether you want to be a criminal with a conscience or a loose cannon.

According to recent reports, "It's still a world of violence, but you could threaten somebody by aiming a gun at them and just robbing them, and they have a chance to comply. So you don't have to beat them to death to get their money.".

The part that sticks out the most is that you "don't have to" approach crimes in a certain way, there's still the option to go into every encounter guns blazing because redemption only works from a storytelling perspective when it's the player's choice, and not as Nelson puts it, something that is "foisted upon you".

It has been revealed that "You can still go on those rampages and basically smash your Criminal Profile until it breaks," Nelson adds. "And then you enter this irredeemable state, which basically says, 'I reject your criminal profile system! I don't want it.'".

According to recent reports, am I brave enough to reject the criminal profile system entirely? Hell no. I replay sections of dialogue if I accidentally upset someone, and cannot do an "evil run" of a game without giving myself stomach ulcers because of the guilt—but I appreciate having the option.
