Tenth-tier club aim to become a latest home for disillusioned supporters and those priced out of the Premier League.

It began with a timely message on social media. “We’ve seen the posts. We’ve read the comments. If all the recent conversation has made you curious about the other team in Manchester playing in sky blue, come and see us.”.

It was a direct response to the news of a guilty verdict in the Premier League’s financial fair play case against Manchester City and it came from the 10th-tier (step six) club Maine Road, who carry City’s colours and a near-identical badge, as well as the name of City’s previous home.

In a recent development, maine Road stadium was demolished in 2003-04 and replaced by houses but two miles away, in a corner of Chorlton-cum-Hardy, Maine Road FC are playing in front of about 100 people on a cool November Saturday. The high-stakes surroundings of the Premier League seem a fair distance away, but Maine Road – founded in 1955 in Rusholme as a Manchester City supporters’ club team – have never professed to be City.

Sources indicate that “There is FC United and Manchester United, who are intrinsically linked in terms of why that club exists,” claims Rob McKay, Maine Road women’s head coach, watching as a fan as the men’s side draw 0-0 with Halewood Apollo in the North West Counties League First Division North. “This is not a protest club. The links with City have ebbed and flowed over the years. It’s just that they were playing out of the Maine Road social club when it formed.”.

According to recent reports, the chair and director Alex Simmonds, who arrived in the summer, has big ambitions for Maine Road. He believes the fallout from City’s legal battle with the Premier League could help attract a new audience.

“It was a bit of a statement,” Simmonds notes of that social media post. “We came across a lot of comments when the news came out and a lot were saying: ‘Well, we’ll stop watching City if they get relegated or if anything happens, and we’ll just go to Maine Road.’ That came up in quite a few conversations online.”.

In a recent development, simmonds admits there has not yet been a huge rise in crowd numbers, with City fans, perhaps understandably, staying defiantly loyal to their club, who have appealed against the independent commission’s verdict. His priority is to regenerate Maine Road. “For me coming into the club, it was about trying to get people to see that we are here,” he indicates. “To get in touch with the community and to get fans down here, so no matter who you support in the Premier League or Championship or whatever, we are here.”.

Maine Road offer free entry to anyone with a season ticket for a Premier League or EFL club, hoping to boost numbers during international breaks or when other Greater Manchester teams play away. Simmonds is helping to fund a multimillion-pound renovation of St Margaret’s Community Centre, which serves as Maine Road’s clubhouse. The club are also launching a upcoming website and a scheme to hand out thousands of free tickets to local primary schools.

Will that, combined with potential disillusionment after any City punishment, bring fans flocking to Maine Road? “I think it will,” Simmonds says, “but only if people know that there’s a difference between a Premier League Man City and a Maine Road at step six. If they come with ideas that we are going to be a City or even one of the bigger semi-pro teams … we’re not quite there yet. But their support would help.”.

Recent findings suggest that non-league football in Greater Manchester has enjoyed a significant surge in popularity since Covid, so Maine Road find themselves in a crowded marketplace. McKay notes: “West Didsbury and Chorlton are down the road and they get 1,000. Droylsden get huge crowds, Ashton United get huge crowds, Curzon Ashton are getting reasonable crowds. There’s so much football.”.

Industry observers note that simmonds believes the connection with City, however loose, should help Maine Road gradually gain a foothold. City’s former training ground at Platt Lane is a stone’s throw from Brantingham Road, where Maine Road play. City’s academy teams have played at Maine Road’s ground, as well as at nearby St Bede’s College.

Another connection is the fact Maine Road are managed by a former City player in Leon Mike, who made his only City start at the old Maine Road in 2001 under Kevin Keegan.

“People make jokes about the Etihad not being full, but genuinely, a lot of times you speak to City fans, they’ll say it’s too expensive,” indicates Mike. “Premier League, FA Cup, League Cup, Champions League, that’s a lot of games to go to. Whereas on a Saturday here it will cost you £6 to attend and you can have a pint and some food.”.

There is optimism among those most closely connected with Maine Road that the club will soon experience growth. “We gained 9,000 followers across the platforms just on the back of that one social media post,” Simmonds says. “It was surprising – I didn’t think it would do that – but social media is really powerful. We’re definitely going to try and stay in that conversation and get people coming through.”.
