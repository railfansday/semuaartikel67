He was a late addition to the rumor mill, but Robert Pattinson has officially landed the role of James Bond.

While industry insiders had previously claimed it was a three-way race between Jack Lowden, Josh O’Connor and Callum Turner, on Friday, November 13, Puck’s Matthew Belloni revealed that Pattinson was “studio Amazon MGM’s preferred choice for the role.” The other lads were apparently out, and despite the studio requiring the next 007 to commit to a four-Bond-film contract—Pattinson’s contract called for a three-film deal—he has been cast as the iconic secret agent.

In a recent development, “James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honour of a lifetime,” Pattinson said in a statement. “I’m deeply grateful to Denis, Amy, David, Tanya, and everyone at Amazon MGM Studios for their belief in me. To say this role is a dream come true is an understatement. I couldn’t be more excited about this partnership and the work ahead with this fantastic team.”.

Pattinson, born in Barnes, London, in 1986, has a wealth of experience behind him. After an early breakthrough as Cedric Diggory in 2005’s Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, he became a global teenage heartthrob as vampire Edward Cullen in the hugely successful Twilight franchise. Based on the bestselling books of the same name, the series ran across five films from 2008 to 2012.

Pattinson went on to take a series of more esoteric indie roles, including in David Cronenberg’s Maps to the Stars in 2014—his second film with the director, after 2012’s Cosmopolis—and Robert Eggers’ The Lighthouse in 2019.

He also caught the attention of one of Hollywood’s most lauded directors, Christopher Nolan, and starred as Neil in 2020’s Tenet. Pattinson then took on another major franchise role as the Caped Crusader in Matt Reeves’ 2022 film The Batman. The sequel is due out in 2028.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, Pattinson also moved into the A24 realm, starring in Marty Supreme, alongside Timothée Chalamet, and then The Drama, acting opposite Zendaya. He also starred with her in his second Nolan film, The Odyssey, in which he played the villain Antinous, which has picked up Oscar buzz. Then there's his recent acclaimed performance in Primetime, playing To Catch a Predator host Chris Hansen.

As a result, Pattinson has become one of the industry’s most in-demand and most celebrated actors. Some critics might have argued that it was impossible for one actor to be both Batman and Bond—to front up two of the most legendary roles in pop culture—but the appointment of Pattinson proves that the studio bosses have no doubt that he can make the distinct transition and that he’s the best person to breathe newly introduced life into the franchise.

It has been revealed that interestingly, back in 2012, Pattinson said he would “definitely like to go for Bond,” but wanted to wait around twenty years. “There’d be nothing worse than, like, ‘Let’s get a fresh-faced Bond!’” he added, according to NDTV. “That would be the worst idea in the world. It would be ridiculous to reinvent it as some young posh kid.”.

Sources indicate that pattinson will take over from Daniel Craig—who was in five films from 2006 to 2021—making him the seventh official Bond. The as-yet-untitled Bond 26 will be produced by David Heyman and Amy Pascal, while Dune’s Denis Villeneuve will direct and Peaky Blinders’ Steven Knight is writing the script. Pattinson will make his debut as the secret service agent when the film is expected to be released, which at this moment is said to be 2028.
