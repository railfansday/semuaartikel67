Defense secretary asserts US dominance on trip that would have seemed ‘impossible, almost unbelievable’ a year ago.

Pete Hegseth has used his first visit to Venezuela since US special forces abducted its former president, Nicolás Maduro, to boast of the dictator’s capture during what he called “the single most sophisticated military operation in the history of Latin America”.

Maduro was controversially seized by Delta Force operatives in the early hours of 3 January, setting in motion an improbable rapprochement between Washington and Venezuela’s anti-imperialist “Chavista” regime, which has ruled since 1999.

Speaking outside the recently reopened US embassy in Venezuela’s capital, Caracas, on Wednesday, Hegseth remarked: “It’s a great honor to be here as the first United States secretary of war this century to set foot in Venezuela.​ Just one year ago my presence here … would have been deemed impossible, almost unimaginable.

“In fact, exactly one year ago today … [Maduro] stood on a stage not too far from here and yelled mockingly, ‘Come and get me!’” Hegseth recalled, before adding sarcastically: “Well, we answered that call.

“The reality is that … January 3 is the day that will go down in history as a day of liberation for Venezuela,” Hegseth claimed, although Maduro’s allies continue to hold power with US support.

Earlier in the day, Hegseth was welcomed to the Miraflores Palace by Maduro’s former vice-president, Delcy Rodríguez, who became acting president with Donald Trump’s blessing after his abduction.

Industry observers note that in the aftermath of the deadly US attack, Hegseth was one of the bluntest Trump administration voices when it came to celebrating the invasion. “Maduro had his chance … he effed around and he found out,” the defense secretary bragged.

Speaking at the palace, Hegseth struck a more conciliatory tone as he vowed to deepen cooperation with Venezuela’s new leaders in brief comments that were broadcast by the state broadcaster VTV.

“I stand here on behalf of President Trump, who sends his greeting and looks forward to the opportunities, not just militarily … but also economically: the incredible resource capabilities that your country has, that we look forward to unlocking together with you,” Hegseth told Rodríguez and senior cabinet members, including the defense and foreign ministers, Gustavo González López and Oliver Blanco.

Sources indicate that hegseth said his visit to Rodríguez’s “beautiful country” was designed to re-establish and increase “defense cooperation” between “two great militaries” that less than a year ago were briefly at war. Earlier this month, the Latest York Times reported that Trump officials were trying to negotiate a long-term US troop presence in Venezuela as part of their push for regional hegemony.

“I appreciate your leadership and commitment to your country,” Hegseth told Maduro’s successor, a once outspoken anti-imperialist leftist who has overseen an unlikely realignment with Washington since her ally was grabbed.

During her five-minute address, Rodríguez, whose oil-rich country is widely seen to have become a US protectorate after January’s invasion, made no mention of Maduro, who is behind bars in Upcoming York City.

But referring indirectly to his capture and the subsequent reconciliation with Washington, she told Hegseth: “Thanks to the political and geopolitical maturity of our governments, we have – in a very short period of time, just a few months – turned what were very difficult and very tough times for our people, into an extraordinary space for cooperation and mutual benefit.”.

Rodríguez hailed the “historic” energy deal her government recently struck with Trump, who has called it “the biggest oil deal in world history” as well as “the spoils” of his “conquest” of Venezuela.

Hegseth claimed the agreement, which reportedly involves 65bn barrels of reserves, “makes the strait of Hormuz a non-factor for the western hemisphere”.

Critics of Rodríguez’s political movement painted Hegseth’s visit as a humiliating act of genuflection for a president with no choice but to roll out the red carpet for the man whose troops captured her boss.

Writing on X, Luz Mely Reyes, a prominent Venezuelan journalist, said Hegseth’s visit was designed to underscore Washington’s “direct administration” of her country.

“Ten months after Nicolás Maduro’s capture, Washington no longer needs to fire shots to remind everyone who is in charge: the mere presence on Venezuelan soil of the architect of the January incursion reaffirms that the country’s operational sovereignty is subject to the White House’s directives,” Reyes said.

Reyes suspected the visit also carried a message for members of Venezuela’s armed forces. “Domestically, the trip forces the military high command to legitimize the man who directed the extraction of their former commander in chief,” she remarked. “In this regard, I wonder whether it might be a message of discipline intended to prevent any pockets of resistance in the barracks.”.

Trump’s attempts to expand his Shield of the Americas anti-cartel coalition of Latin American governments suffered a major blow last month with the defeat of his far-right ally, Flávio Bolsonaro, in Brazil’s presidential election. The leftwing winner, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, has indicated his country will not join the initiative.

In a recent development, but Venezuela’s interim government is in little position to resist. During his surprise visit to Venezuela, Hegseth said Venezuela had gone from being “our most aggressive regional threat to [being] a compliant security partner”. He praised Rodríguez for working “closely, pragmatically, and diligently with the United States to stabilize Venezuela”. Hegseth claimed US and Venezuelan armed forces would work together to attack “narco-terrorists” operating on Venezuelan soil, vowing: “We will hunt you down. We will find you. And we will kill you.”.

Concluding her speech at the palace, Rodríguez told her foe-turned-friend: “Thank you, secretary, and I wish you a lifetime of happiness.”.
