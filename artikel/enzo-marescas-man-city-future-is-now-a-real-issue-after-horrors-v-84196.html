Life after Pep Guardiola was always going to be difficult, and to begin with, the transition appeared to be seamless.

Enzo Maresca had put his Chelsea struggles behind him, with Manchester City winning their first eight games of the 2026-27 campaign.

That streak was brought to an end by PSG, and even after getting back to winning ways, the Citizens' performances haven't looked the same at all.

City have won just one of their last six games across all competitions, the latest reality check being a 1-1 draw against Fulham.

Heading into the game, Football Insider understood that Alvaro Arbeloa was still on the verge of being sacked at Craven Cottage.

During proceedings at the Etihad Stadium, fans could hardly tell which team, and even which manager, was under more pressure this season.

The flaws and downfalls of Maresca's tactics were on show for all to see in the 3-1 defeat to Nottingham Forest at the City Ground.

After plenty of time to reflect in the international break, though, fans would have been confident of victory against Fulham this weekend.

Just minutes into the game, though, and supporters were getting frustrated, The players had no clue what they were supposed to be doing either.

Industry observers note that were they supposed to be pressing Fulham's defence and goalkeeper? Should the back four been higher up the pitch? It doesn't seem like anyone knew the answer to those questions.

It isn't just this result. The entire season so far has sparked questions, and now Maresca's future is a major talking point at the Etihad.

Keith Wyness told Football Insider that Maresca's job remains safe at the Etihad, and that's hardly surprising.

After a decade of Guardiola, the last thing the club want to be doing is search for a brand-latest manager already, but cracks are appearing.

The Cottagers may even feel that they deserved all three points, and that would be difficult to disagree with after watching both performances.

Industry observers note that maresca failed to adapt to what was in front of him. It's as simple as that. The second half was better, but it was not a classic Man City display.

Recent findings suggest that it's been a long time since the crowd has reacted like they did this weekend. That should be a very worrying sign for the manager, who has been in charge for just 18 games.
