Google has rolled out a upcoming upgrade for Android Auto that improves support for wider display formats, but there’s a good chance you won’t be able to see it.

According to recent reports, android Auto has to scale to a lot of different display shapes and sizes, and its functionality varies depending on what you’re using as the platform adapts to what your display can handle. This can sometimes mean you are locked out of a specific feature or need to access it another way. A couple of infamous examples of this include the “Report” option in Google Maps being hidden on smaller displays, as well as the weather card only being on larger, taller displays.

That stated, Google is now making an effort to improve how apps on Android Auto adapters to wider formats.

In the latest app update (v18.2), Android Auto is tweaking apps to switch from a top-bar layout to a sidebar for navigation. So, in Spotify, this means the “Home,” “Recents,” “Browse,” and “Library” shortcuts move to a sidebar rather than being up top.

According to recent reports, it’s a good use of space, as it allows more content to be visible on wide but short displays. Android Authority spotted the change across Spotify, YouTube Music, and Phone, implying this is probably a platform-level change rather than being tied to individual app updates.

It’s a great change, but one you might not be able to see.

In a recent development, as mentioned, Android Auto adjusts to take advantage of whatever display space it has available, and that often includes putting Google Maps off to the side of other apps. While you can easily set Maps to take up the full display, other apps are less likely to be able to do that, with Maps semi-permanently off to the opposite side. In a car with a wide display we tested for this newly introduced format, Maps prevented any other apps from going wide enough to show the new layout, so your results may vary. In many cases, this new layout may simply not be visible. In a way, though, that’s a good thing – it shows that Google is addressing even the most niche aspects of Android Auto, and that’s a win for everyone.
