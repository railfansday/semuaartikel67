Industry observers note that LAS VEGAS—Consumers are rapidly adopting consumer health AI. Roughly 1 in 3 U.S. adults have turned to an AI chatbot for health information, according to a KFF survey, and the use of health AI is even more prevalent among younger adults.

But questions remain about whether consumers understand and trust AI-generated health advice, and whether it influences how they manage their health or seek care.

Sources indicate that tech giant Apple rolled out the Apple Health AI Usage Study today with prepares to recruit 200,000 participants in the U.S. to gain a better understanding of how people interact with conversational health AI tools.

In a recent development, the study is open to individuals 21 or older who live in the U.S., and participants must have an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone (iPhone 15 Pro or later) running iOS 26 or later. Apple will run the virtual research study on a dedicated Apple Health AI Study App, separate from the Apple Health and Research apps. Participants may remain in the study for up to 12 months, Apple remarked.

Industry observers note that by examining what drives user trust, the quality of responses, comprehension and everyday health actions, the study aims to inform the responsible design of health AI experiences for Apple and the industry, the manufacturer said. There are also privacy implications that need to be explored, Apple representatives said. Researchers also want to evaluate how AI chatbots complement the traditional healthcare system and the handoff to physicians.

The tech organization is collaborating with OpenEvidence on the research initiative, as the startup provides the underlying clinical AI technology for Apple’s Health AI Usage Study. OpenEvidence is a widely used AI-powered clinical decision support tool and medical search engine for clinicians. The company's platform pulls directly from licensed, peer-reviewed literature and clinical guidelines through formal partnerships with The Upcoming England Journal of Medicine (NEJM), JAMA, Nature, Cochrane, and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN).

Apple said it selected OpenEvidence as a partner for its high-quality medical sources, robust privacy controls, and advanced search models.

According to recent reports, through Apple’s study app, participants can ask everyday health questions and receive answers in approachable language, with links to peer-reviewed clinical sources, Apple said. Users can ask about symptoms, general health questions, recovery from procedures, fitness and other health concerns. They can rate the AI's answers and indicate what was useful or lacking, Apple said.

Industry observers note that study participants can also choose to share HealthKit data types—such as resting heart rate, activity and sleep—to get more personalized responses to health questions. If study participants have imported clinical records into the Health app and separately agree to share them with the Health AI Usage study, the app can incorporate that information, primarily lab results, Apple representatives mentioned.

Industry observers note that "At Apple, we believe responsible innovation starts with scientific rigor and clinical grounding. Health is deeply personal, and conducting a dedicated research study is the most thoughtful way to understand how people engage with, comprehend, and evaluate health AI in everyday life. Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey," Sumbul Desai, M.D., Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness, said in a statement.

Apple described the research initiative as a formal study overseen by an independent review board.

Participants will complete a mandatory onboarding process that includes informed consent, and they'll be asked to share demographic information such as age, race and education so researchers can examine how these variables affect participant preferences and communication needs, Apple mentioned.

The company mentioned study participants will receive baseline and monthly surveys, allowing researchers to examine how people use the information they receive and what happens afterward.

The Health AI Usage study app also includes tools to help people prepare for healthcare appointments, along with surveys after visits to gauge whether the use of health AI chatbots makes patients feel better prepared or empowered and improves their interactions with providers, Apple stated.

The study is designed from the ground up with privacy protections, transparency and clinical guardrails, the manufacturer noted. Study data will not be used to train AI models, Apple confirmed.

Industry observers note that apple said it has implemented security measures to protect the study data it maintains, as well as the confidentiality of participants’ information. User queries on the Health AI Usage Study app are first processed on the iPhone to remove identifying information. The queries then flow to a secure study environment managed by a third-party study support contractor where they are de-linked from individual participant IDs and screened again before being sent to OpenEvidence where answers are generated using supporting reference materials, Apple said.

The study app also features safety guardrails that are intended to detect certain concerning questions and suggest that a participant follow up with a provider or other resources if needed.

Industry observers note that consumers are increasingly using general AI chatbots powered by LLMs to get answers to health questions. But research indicates a wide gap exists between an AI's technical accuracy and a patient's ability to safely use that information.

"Patients and clinicians are both turning to AI, but the tools they use are being built and tested in separate worlds. When patients and doctors draw on versions of the same trustworthy tools, patients arrive prepared, conversations improve, and next steps happen seamlessly. Connecting these tools, and rigorously studying what works, is how we’ll get there," Robert M. Wachter, M.D., UCSF’s professor and chair of the Department of Medicine, noted in a statement.

According to recent reports, the Health AI Usage study builds on Apple's growing research initiatives. In 2019, the company released its Research app and three medical studies to test how well its Apple Watch can track a person's mobility and cardiovascular health, changes in hearing and women's health and fertility. Last year, it began the Apple Health Study to use its devices, including iPhones, Apple Watch and AirPods along with third-party devices, for a longitudinal, virtual study to monitor changes in participants’ health, spanning a wide range of health and disease areas.
