By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER.

Published: 12:32 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 12:48 EST, 21 November 2026.

What Martin O’Neill wouldn’t give to see more of this until the point where the cavalry appears on top of the hill in January.

Celtic wins with bells and whistles attached have been as rare as hen’s teeth since this campaign began.

That the supporters witnessed one in trying circumstances felt significant given the arduous schedule which awaits in the coming six weeks.

According to recent reports, despite having no option but to piece together a midfield, O’Neill watched his side create a slew of opportunities. They took four with as many different scorers and might well have had more.

A display brimming with enterprise and imagination boded well for what lies ahead. For the first time since September, Celtic have chalked up three straight wins. Ahead of the trip to face Torreense in Portugal on Thursday, it appears that a bit of wind is back in the sails.

In a recent development, this was as comprehensive a win as the scoreline suggests. The champions were never in danger from the moment Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain edged them in front just after the half hour mark.

Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain celebrates Celtic's first goal against St Mirren.

Sources indicate that in danger of becoming something of a forgotten man, the former England man rolled back the years here with a display which reminded you why he once wore the shirts of Liverpool and Arsenal. On this evidence, he still has a contribution to make at Parkhead.

Industry observers note that the outcome was more or less settled by the interval. Camilo Duran continued his outstanding first season in Glasgow’s East End with another assured finish.

Luke McCowan, a man who’s not exactly had his critics to seek in recent times, netted the third before the break, his goal a just reward for a tidy afternoon’s work.

On a day when O’Neill would have had concerns about his midfield, Callum McGregor also netted with a crisp strike from the edge of the box.

St Mirren’s consolation, coming from Killian Phillips’ penalty, was too little too late.

On Friday, Buddies boss Craig McLeish had made some salient points about the self-interest which is holding back Scottish football.

It has been revealed that it’s hard not to like McLeish’s attitude and the style of football his side are playing. But they are continuing to lose goals far too easily. They could find themselves getting dragged into trouble if they don’t quickly address it.

Celtic's Camilo Duran celebrates after scoring their second goal in the 4-1 win.

On Friday, O’Neill had rejected the suggestion that Haissem Hassan had been omitted from the squad which faced Kilmarnock because he needed a kick up the backside. Yet, curiously, there was no sign of the Egyptian again here.

With Benjamin Nygren and Mika Baur injured, O’Neill hoped that a combination of McGregor, McCowan and Oxlade-Chamberlain would have enough.

In a recent development, kasper Hogh’s chances of ending a run of 16 games without a goal weren’t helped by him starting on the bench. He didn’t leave it.

With just one win in nine coming into this, Craig McLeish’s side were short on points yet they were not short on confidence.

Although they played some stuff which was very easy on the eye, an inability to keep the ball out of the net cost them dear again.

McCowan had an early shot blocked before Colby Donovan had a weak effort saved. McCowan also hesitated to take a first-time strike with Louis Jackson doing enough to protect Jacob Chapman’s goal.

Recent findings suggest that celtic actually had the ball in the net twice before they made it count. Duran planted a header and a shot in the visitors’ net only to discover that the offside flag had been raised.

Sources indicate that jota sparked a sweeping move which saw Yang Hyun-jun tee up McCowan. Paul Nsio became the latest Saints player to frustrate the Celtic midfielder with a block.

Recent findings suggest that saints were aggrieved that Yang won a free-kick by going to ground too easily just before the opener. They may have had a point. That didn’t excuse the defending which followed.

Cameron Carter-Vickers rolled the ball in Yang’s direction. The winger burst away from Declan John and assessed his options.

His cut-back for the unmarked Oxlade-Chamberlain was inch-perfect. The former England midfielder swept home his first goal since netting against the same opponent in April.

It has been revealed that saints might well have levelled immediately. Ismeal Kabia wriggled away from Donovan and picked out Eseosa Sule. Liam Scales did well to prevent the forward netting from seven yards.

Celtic then cut through Saints with embarrassing ease. A straight pass sent Yang clear on goal. He should have gone for glory, but instead tried to square the ball to allow Duran a tap-in. Alex Gogic had other ideas and knocked the ball away for a corner.

Celtic midfielder Luke McCowan and St Mirren's Paul Nsio battle for the ball.

McLeish’s hopes of making it to the break still in the contest unravelled in the final five minutes of the first period.

It has been revealed that kieran Tierney’s decision to swap flanks paid dividends with a brilliant in-swinging cross. Duran ghosted onto it and cushioned the ball beyond Chapman with his left foot to claim his 14th goal of his debut season.

Jota might well have made it three-nil seconds later and he fired Oxlade-Chamberlain’s lay-off straight at the keeper.

There was no hope for Chapman as Celtic all but wrapped up the win three minutes before the interval.

Tierney was again the provider, his energy taking him to the line and allowing him to cross. McCowan calmly stroked home his second goal of the season.

Saints kept going seeking a goal which might have made it interesting. Kabia’s strike forced Sam Johnstone into a smart stop down to his right.

According to recent reports, yang ought to have killed the contest but blazed over while leaning back. Skipper McGregor indicated him how to do it as he buried a right foot shot low in the bottom left-hand corner from the edge of the box after a defender had taken the ball off Duran’s toe.

Saints did get on the board thanks to Phillips’ penalty conversion, the award coming after Johnstone clattered Calvin Ramsay as he punched clear.

O’Neill was still able to send on Shim Mheaka and James Forrest with Austin Trusty returning to warm applause. Joel van den Berg had already been introduced for his debut along with Callum Osmand. The Dutch teenager demonstrated up well.
