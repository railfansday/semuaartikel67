📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Koto evolves the GoFundMe brand identity around one idea, Help Adds Up, turning the goal bar into a progress circle that radiates outward.

Sources indicate that the progress circle is the whole idea. GoFundMe's most recognizable asset, the goal bar, becomes a ring, and the ring throws off an arc, a ray, that bends through the entire system. It indicates up as fingerprint-like rings listing real causes, Pacific Palisades Wildfire, Hurricane Helene, Surfrider Foundation, and as words set on a curve. The brief came from a real shift: after acquiring Classy, the leading nonprofit fundraising platform, GoFundMe's product reached deeper into nonprofit giving while its brand still spoke in its early peer-to-peer voice. The palette stays close to the ground: deep green, pale lime, yellow, beige, dark brown. Green carries the trust, yellow carries the energy, and nothing else competes.

The typeface is GoFundMe Sans, a custom face built for the system, and the specimen sets it substantial on green with letters riding a radial curve. That curve is the grid. Words bend along arcs everywhere: Help Fund Give Go! stacked in a bounding box, Change takes action on a yellow poster, And every bit of help adds up curling around a beige field. The voice lines are short and plain, set at a size where the letterforms do the shouting.

According to recent reports, the system lands in the product first. Goal tracking, donation flows, and campaign pages carry the same progress language, and the GoFundMe Pro dashboard turns it into numbers: $28.5k raised online, 227 total donors. Outside the product it holds the same register: a flower macro for the environment discovery page, iPhone screens for the Spring Valley Community Garden fundraiser, campaign photography where the radial type does the talking. The identity also stretches to cover GoFundMe Pro, so the nonprofit platform sits under the same story instead of beside it.
