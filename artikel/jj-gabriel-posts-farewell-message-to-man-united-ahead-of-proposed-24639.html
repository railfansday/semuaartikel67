Teenage wonderkid JJ Gabriel has appeared to confirm his Manchester United exit in a video posted on social media.

The 16-year-old, who has never played a competitive match for the first team, joined United as a 10-year-old in 2020.

He has been absent from the club since requesting in September that his registration be cancelled, shortly before his 16th birthday on 6 October.

Gabriel made no mention of where he intends to go next, but widespread speculation has linked him with Real Madrid, and his goodbye video on Instagram appears to have edged him closer to a move to the Spanish giants.

Industry observers note that gabriel noted in his video: “Dear Manchester United, I arrived in 2020 as a 10-year-old boy with massive dreams. Here I grew as a player and a person. From scoring at Old Trafford to receiving the Jimmy Murphy Award.

“You gave me moments I will always treasure. To every coach and every member of staff, to my team-mates and everyone who supported me, thank you for believing in me and helping me grow.

Sources indicate that “As I begin my next chapter, Manchester United will always be a part of my story. Thank you for everything, JJ.”.

The teenager is the son of former Republic of Ireland international Joe O’Cearuill.

Last season he became the youngest ever winner of United’s Jimmy Murphy Award, given to the club’s young player of the year.

Despite his absence, Gabriel had remained registered to United, who the Press Association understands are ready to explore legal options against the youngster’s next club over possible broken regulations.

United had initially hoped to persuade him to stay.

It now appears his hat-trick against Sabah on his Uefa Youth League debut on September 10 will be his final appearance for the club.

Gabriel shone in the 5-0 victory, but his representatives were disappointed he was not registered to play later that day in the Champions League fixture against the champions of Azerbaijan.

He had been due to appear in the Carabao Cup tie against Brighton the following week, but had upped sticks by that point.

Had he featured in that tie, Gabriel would have become the club’s youngest player in a competitive match, having already appeared off the bench for Michael Carrick’s side in an August friendly against Atletico Madrid.
