Sources indicate that liquid Glass was one of the most controversial and polarizing UI changes Apple has made for a long time. The company listened to the feedback and made a number of changes, the most significant of which was to introduce a sliding transparency scale.

Apple is presently testing a change some will consider insignificant but I personally find very welcome: softer edges when scrolling ….

Apple’s introduction of the Liquid Glass UI split the Apple community, some hailing it as one of the best improvements seen for many years, and others rejecting it as a usability disaster.

In a recent development, as is often the case with any change, the controversy dampened significantly once people had a chance to get used to it. That is not the same as everyone being happy with it, however. Apple later tried to please most of the people most of the time with a slider that allows people to customize the level of transparency, from a very frosted glass look to an almost completely clear one.

Code sleuth pdfu has spotted that the tech giant is presently testing a small but welcome additional choice.

They heard the feedback: Apple is testing the return of iOS 26’s softer scroll-edge effect in navigation bars when glass tint is set to 25% or lower.At the moment disabled behind UIKit/clear_glass_soft_pocket. Applies to the default/auto style. pic.twitter.com/D2NvKfESYa.

The difference is somewhat subtle, and some may consider it irrelevant, but as someone who has transparency maxed-out I’m a fan of the softer fade and would welcome the option to be able to reinstate this.
