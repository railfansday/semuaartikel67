8 Metroidvanias Where Every Choice Matters

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Choice one of the mechanics that does a lot to set video games apart from other forms of media, and can take many different forms. It’s what draws me to RPGs, but even genres that aren’t known for their story choices like Metroidvanias have choice play some kind of a role, even if it’s something like how you build your character and approach combat.

Metroidvanias focus primarily on exploration, abilities, and exploration, and there are plenty of games in the genre that take things a step further by giving you meaningful decisions that influence your adventure. Even within this framework, choice matters as builds, abilities, weapons, and exploration all play a role. These are Metroidvanias where those choices play a bigger role than is typical of the genre.

UNSIGHTED includes a game mechanic that makes choice one of its most notable features. Characters, including yours, are all on a time limit before they disappear, or become “unsighted”. Your time can be extended thanks to meteor dust, a limited resource that forces you to decide who gets to live and who has to die.

This turns resource management into a moral dilemma, particularly because your decisions have consequences beyond individual character interactions. Saving certain NPCs can preserve access to shops, quests, and useful items, while allowing others to become unsighted can permanently change what you have access to. It's a clever way of making your decisions feel important without relying exclusively on dialogue options or branching cutscenes. Every time you discover a upcoming character, you have another difficult decision to make on the horizon.

Sources indicate that death’s Gambit: Afterlife has a lot of RPG elements with a strong Metroidvania influence, which puts it in an awkward in-between category. But, because of this, choice is more key here than is typical of Metroidvanias. It features multiple endings, with some of the most important decisions tied to your encounters with Death and Thalamus. Depending on your progression and the decisions you make during the adventure, you'll have opportunities to pursue different narrative outcomes.

I particularly like games that connect RPG mechanics to storytelling in this way. Rather than treating character development as something that exists independently of the main narrative, Death's Gambit: Afterlife makes your progression part of the decision-making process.

It has been revealed that nine Sols is already great if you're looking for a challenging Metroidvania that comes with a good story. It also goes the extra mile by making the relationships that you develop throughout the game incredibly relevant, and unlocks a different ending.

In a recent development, you’ll encounter several characters who join you at the Four Seasons Pavilion, most notably Shuanshuan and Shennong. Both these characters introduce questlines that require you to invest time in helping them. Completing these quests, alongside other optional discoveries, is necessary to unlock the game's Shooting Star Ending, also known as the True Ending. Having a reason to invest in a game’s characters, beyond it being its own reward, is an example of game design done well, and it means that the ending you get is influenced by the choice you make to invest in them.

Blasphemous 2 is a particularly good example of a Metroidvania that rewards you for looking beyond its main storyline. While exploring Cvstodia, you'll encounter numerous optional challenges, hidden collectibles, and characters with their own stories to uncover.

Some of these discoveries are particularly important if you want to unlock the game's true ending. It makes the choice to explore and complete side quests more important, and neglecting them means getting the less satisfactory ending. What's interesting is that you can technically reach the final encounter without completing any of the side objectives. However, doing so means missing out on an entirely different conclusion.

One of Salt and Sanctuary’s more interesting mechanics involves the different Creeds you can pledge allegiance to throughout the game. Creeds offer different benefits, resources, and services, meaning your allegiance can influence how you approach progression. Choosing to follow one Creed over another can affect which items and services are available to you, while interacting with rival sanctuaries can have consequences of their own.

You can also choose how to develop your character, with an extensive skill tree and a wide variety of weapons, armour, and spells allowing you to experiment with different builds. While its choices are more closely tied to character progression and faction allegiance than branching narrative decisions, Salt and Sanctuary still gives you plenty of opportunities to change your adventure based on your choices.

Vigil: The Longest Night is another dark fantasy Metroidvania that takes inspiration from Soulslike games and Salt and Sanctuary. You play as Leila, a warrior tasked with investigating the strange events affecting her homeland. As you explore, you'll encounter characters who have their own problems, motivations, and quests to complete.

In a recent development, one Vigil’s more interesting aspects is how its optional content encourages you to investigate the circumstances surrounding the people you encounter. Rather than simply following the main storyline, you can spend time helping NPCs and uncovering additional information about the world. The game also features multiple endings, giving you additional reasons to investigate its mysteries and pursue optional content. There’s a lot of character and environmental story here, and your experience is largely shaped by your decisions.

In a recent development, ENDER LILIES: Quietus of the Knights is a particularly atmospheric Metroidvania that follows Lily, a young priestess attempting to uncover the truth behind a devastating plague known as the Blight. As you explore the ruined kingdom, you'll encounter the spirits of fallen warriors who can help Lily in combat. Each spirit has its own abilities, allowing you to experiment with different combinations and develop a combat style that suits your preferences, bringing choice into play.

Alongside its gameplay, choice also plays a massive part in its endings, with three possible outcomes. The best outcome involves thoroughly exploring and finding all seven Stone Tablet Fragments, which allows you to craft the Liminant Aegis Curio, an artifact that protects Lily. It neatly ties together narrative with exploration, and gives more incentive for exploration.

Infernax is one of the best examples of a Metroidvania where your decisions have consequences. This is because of a morality system, which always makes a game that is influenced by your choices. The morality system is determined by how you choose to help the people of your kingdom. Infernax gives you multiple options with how to deal with situations, and with both noble and selfish options available.

According to recent reports, in addition to affecting your alignment, your choices also determine which ending you’ll receive. Infernax is a fantastic example of a game that gives you the freedom to shape your character's journey.
