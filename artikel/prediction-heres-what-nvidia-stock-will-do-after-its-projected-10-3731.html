Prediction: Here’s What Nvidia Stock Will Do After Its Projected $108 Billion Quarter Results Are Revealed on Nov. 17

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The news for Nvidia (NVDA -4.56%) won't stop coming for a while. The focus at the moment is on former CEO and co-founder Jensen Huang. While that news cycle will likely continue for a bit, there will be some fresh news for Nvidia on Tuesday, Nov. 17, when its Q3 earnings results are released after the market closes. There are many pointed questions about the health and strength of the business without Huang in the leadership role, but the reality is that none of Q3's results will necessarily answer them since they are tied to a quarter that ended Oct. 31.

Analysts covering the stock expect a strong quarter, and Nvidia management forecasted revenue in Q3 to reach $108 billion. If Nvidia meets that internal projection, what will the stock do? Let's take a look.

The reality is that Nvidia's Q3 figures likely won't mean much for the stock unless they are either incredibly good or incredibly bad. I doubt management will have negative financial results to report, as the artificial intelligence build-out is progressing at full steam ahead and Nvidia's GPUs are a huge part of that.

Recent findings suggest that with the negative sentiment surrounding both the stock and the company right now, it would also take a gigantic earnings beat and expectation raise to send the stock soaring. However, it's not out of the question. Competitor Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -6.08%) raised its outlook for 2027 recently due to huge demand, boosting its stock. Analysts covering Nvidia stock expect it will beat the $108 billion forecast with 91% revenue growth to $108.8 billion. For Q4, analysts have revenue guidance of $124.8 billion, indicating an 83% growth rate.

Industry observers note that if Nvidia reports Q3 revenue that misses guidance, I won't be surprised to see the stock get hammered in the days after. Nvidia cannot afford a misstep in its results with what's going on in the CEO role, and if it does, it could lead to a rough day for the stock.

NASDAQ · As of 3:36 PM ET 11/16/26.

Sources indicate that nvidia has a strong track record of beating expectations. If Nvidia can come out and say that business is strong and that management already has its eyes on a few replacement options for Jensen Huang, the stock could soar. I think this is the most likely option. Nvidia stock has already been beaten down to an incredibly attractive valuation.

Even before the sell-off related to Jensen Huang's resignation, Nvidia's stock wasn't getting much respect from the market. It has posted incredible results and has high visibility to future revenue, but the market still deemed it necessary to give it a discounted valuation. That fact has gotten even more exaggerated, and now Nvidia trades for merely 10 times next year's estimated earnings.

NVDA PE Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts.

That is an absurdly low price, and investors should be doing everything they can to take advantage of that mismatch. For reference, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -2.06%) trades at about 18 times forward earnings. If Nvidia meets expectations for next year, it could lead its valuation rising to a market-average multiple. In that case, the stock would nearly double. That suggests a potential buying opportunity.

The negative headlines surrounding what has transpired in the CEO role will pass over the next few months. And several complications remain to be resolved, including legal investigations that are inevitable in the wake of this news. There is definitely uncertainty surrounding the business and the stock that needs to be considered.

In a recent development, however, Nvidia's business is still largely intact. It remains a compelling AI investment option in the market. Admittedly, the share price could be a bit volatile as the market digests developments, but I continue to think Nvidia is a top long-term option to buy and hold now.
