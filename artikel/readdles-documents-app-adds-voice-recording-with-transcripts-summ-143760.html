Readdle updated its Documents app for iPhone and iPad this week with Voice Notes, a fresh AI-powered feature that lets customers record conversations and automatically generate transcripts, summaries, and follow-up notes. Here are the details.

While the habit of recording meetings, lectures, or even one’s own thoughts is not particularly new, modern AI tools and models have made it considerably easier to extract useful information from these recordings without having to listen back to them in their entirety or take copious notes that can distract from the actual conversation.

This week, Readdle’s Documents introduced Voice Notes, which “lets you record audio and turn it into a summary, follow-ups, and a full transcript,” as the manufacturer put it.

According to recent reports, users can start a latest recording session by tapping the “+” button in the app and selecting Voice Notes. Alternatively, they can start recording from the iPhone’s Action button, which is a nice touch.

Once they’re done, Readdle indicates Documents can generate:.

Owners can cycle through these three tabs, where they can also listen to the original recording or copy and share the information.

Readdle notes Voice Notes can recognize multiple languages in the same conversation, with the actual transcript preserving the languages as spoken, “while the summary and follow-ups are generated in the most frequently spoken language.”.

It has been revealed that each voice note is saved as a single file in Documents that contains the original recording, transcript, summary, and follow-ups. Consumers can organize these files into folders, share them, sync them with their cloud storage, and save the transcript as a separate .txt file.

However, if users give a voice note’s AI-generated content a thumbs down, Readdle may retain its transcript and summary for up to six months, or until the user objects to their processing, whichever comes first.

The firm also indicates user data isn’t used to train AI models, although third-party AI providers may retain inputs for up to 30 days for abuse and misuse monitoring, or longer if required by law.
