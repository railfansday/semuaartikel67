"Stop buying coffee out" has become commonplace personal finance advice, but is there a way to invest and cover your monthly Starbucks (SBUX -1.28%) bill? Let's say you go to Starbucks a few times a week, which, over the course of a year, can really add up: 150 trips a year at $4.96 per cup, or just under $5, comes out to $744 annually.

Industry observers note that that remarked, for a five-figure investment, you could pay for these coffees from your dividends alone. However, in terms of potential gains from holding the stock, is it worth it? Not necessarily.

According to recent reports, following Starbucks' latest dividend increase, shares in what is one of the largest consumer discretionary companies now pay $0.63 per share quarterly. That comes to $2.52 per share in annual dividend payments. Divide $744 by $2.52 per share, and you get 296 Starbucks shares needed to cover this expense. Starbucks stock currently trades for around $106.50 per share, meaning you would need around $31,524 to enter this position.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Industry observers note that any way you slice it, that's a tall order. Even as annual dividend growth has averaged nearly 8% over the past five years, the latest dividend increase was only 1.6%. If low dividend growth persists, the price of a cup of Starbucks coffee could outpace the payouts. Also, don't assume buying today means trading lower dividend growth for the potential for greater gains down the road.

According to recent reports, the organization's "Back to Starbucks" turnaround, focused on improving the in-store experience, has been in motion for over two years now. Recent results, including a 7.2% increase in same-store sales during the September-ended quarter, have investors very confident about further improvements in growth and profitability.

However, after this rally, the stock trades at 33.5 times forward earnings, a massive premium to peers like McDonald's. Although further strong quarterly results could help sustain this valuation, any sort of earnings hiccup could quickly send shares back in the wrong direction. Considering this, I would skip buying Starbucks shares for now.
