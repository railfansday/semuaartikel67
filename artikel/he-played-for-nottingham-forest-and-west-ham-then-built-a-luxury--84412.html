Marlon Harewood enjoyed a glittering 18-year career in the professional game, and when he retired from playing he entered into a business in an area that's close to the hearts of many players.

Footballers, it's reasonable to say, have always been keen on their cars. The sight of Baby Bentleys and Land Rovers arriving at training grounds became a familiar one on transfer deadline days, an inevitable consequence of young men earning more money in a week than many of us do in a year.

So it makes perfect sense that going into the car industry makes perfect sense for a recently-retired professional footballer, and in the case of the former Nottingham Forest and West Ham United striker Marlon Harewood, an 18-year career in the professional game gave him the knowledge to be able to establish himself in a particular niche that was close to his very own heart.

Over a playing career that lasted from 1998 to 2016, Marlon Harewood played for fourteen different clubs in England, from the Premier League to the National League North, but it's for his spells with Nottingham Forest and West Ham United that he's best remembered.

In a recent development, harewood came through the youth system at Nottingham Forest and went on to make just over 200 appearances for them between 1998 and 2004, with a brief spell back at The City Ground in 2011. His first spell began at the worst possible time. Having made his League debut as Forest sailed to promotion back to the Premier League, his first full season saw his team finishing bottom of the table on 30 points, eleven adrift of safety. He managed one goal in 23 League appearances.

Things did improve for him. Harewood developed a strong partnership with David Johnson, and Forest reached the Championship play-offs off the back of his 20 league goals in the 2002-03 season. But they didn't go up, and in November 2003 he left for West Ham United.

He was more successful at West Ham than he had been at Nottingham Forest. He scored 13 in 28 in his first season in the Championship for them, and the following season scored 17 times as the Irons returned to the Premier League, while he also featured in the FA Cup final against Liverpool, where West Ham lost on penalties following a thrilling 3-3 draw. In his first season back in the Premier League since that disastrous first season with Nottingham Forest, he scored 14 times in 37 appearances.

Industry observers note that harewood's career started to slow when he moved from West Ham to Aston Villa in 2007, turning down a move to WIgan Athletic in order to do so. He found his opportunities at Villa Park to be severely limited, and in 2010 he was sent on loan to Wolverhampton Wanderers.

It has been revealed that in sharp contrast to the first half of his career, which had only taken in two clubs, the second was far more nomadic. There was another permanent deal for him with Blackpool in 2010, but there were also spells on loan at Newcastle United and Barnsley, and even a spell with Guangzhou RF in the China Super League.

Recent findings suggest that the last five years of his EFL career saw him play for four different clubs; a return to Forest, then Barnsley, Bristol City and Hartlepool United. And he called time on his playing career in 2016, after scoring ten goals in a season for Nuneaton Town of the National League North.

Recent findings suggest that the automotive trade was an obvious next step for Harewood, who'd long had an appreciation for cars, and it was one that he'd been slowly working on behind the scenes for years. Initially founded while he was still playing for Forest, it was run by his business partner while his football career continued. When he retired from playing, he was able to move seamlessly into it.

But the business that he built up tailored to a very specific market of which he was a member; designing and customising cars for players through his company, AC13 Premier. By the end of 2022, his clients included Harry Kane and Kyle Walker. Speaking to The Daily Star in December of that year, he explained: "Everyone’s got an interest in cars at the highest level - people like their nice stuff and cars did it for me.".

In moving from professional football to cars, Marlon Harewood has been fortunate to work in two industries that he enjoys, though this is also a reflection of his own range of talents.
