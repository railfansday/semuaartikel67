Industry observers note that copy your routing number from your bank’s app or online banking rather than from a web search. Massive banks assign routing numbers by the state where you opened the account and often use a separate one for wires, so confirm which one your payment needs.

It has been revealed that bank of America, for one, ties the routing number to the state where the account was opened, which may not be the state you live in now. Wells Fargo’s routing numbers also depend on that state, and both banks list a separate number for incoming wires.

Industry observers note that so a number copied from a search may belong to accounts opened in another state. The routing number tells a payment which bank to go to, and the account number tells the bank which account is yours. Give the wrong routing number and the payment can bounce back to whoever sent it, which delays your money.

Recent findings suggest that if your account number happens to match another customer’s account at the wrong bank, the money can land there instead. The IRS tells taxpayers whose refund went to the wrong account to work with the bank to get it back.

Sources indicate that in the app or online banking, open the account and look at its details, where the routing number is listed with the account number. On a check, it’s the first number along the bottom, before your account number, and some statements print it too. To confirm that a number is your bank’s, enter it in the American Bankers Association’s lookup, which names the bank it belongs to.
