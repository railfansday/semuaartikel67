Does Anyone Care How Yellowjackets Ends Anymore?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Believe it or not, Yellowjackets is based on real events. In 1972, a high school rugby team, along with their families, boarded Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571 and crashed in the Andes. Only sixteen of the forty-five passengers survived when they were rescued seventy-two days later, and they all harbored a dark secret: To survive, they ate the flesh of the people who had died.

In a recent development, yellowjackets, in a way, explores what might have happened had the group never revealed just how they managed to survive in the wilderness. Much like Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571, the Showtime series follows a high school women’s soccer team that survives a plane crash by killing and eating each other. Cocreators Ashley Lyle and Bart Nickerson blended in potential supernatural elements such as a dark presence that possibly forces them to kill, classic Lord of the Flies homages, and a killer soundtrack. Yellowjackets also cut ahead to each survivor’s life as an adult, featuring the acting talents of Melanie Lynskey, Christina Ricci, and Juliette Lewis. For a while, it was engrossing TV.

According to recent reports, the series explored survival in all its forms, from the lasting effects of grief to the need for women to escape their mundane, suburban lives for a little danger. It was critically acclaimed, nominated for awards, and topped many “Best of the Year” lists. But then, five years and two more chaotic seasons went by that really seemed to test its audience’s … well, appetites.

According to recent reports, now that the fourth and final season is upon us—season 4 premiered on Friday, November 20—the answers to Yellowjackets’ burning questions will finally be revealed. Namely: Is something forcing them to kill each other, or can everything be explained by science? Will the public find out that they resorted to ritualistic murder and cannibalism? And, most importantly, is there anyone left who’s still interested in finding out?

To be fair, there’s already something of an answer to that last question. Despite critical fatigue for the series, over two million viewers tuned in to the season 3 premiere last year, per Paramount. That number then increased a bit to 3 million by the season 3 finale. Sure, those numbers pale in comparison to The Pitt season 2’s 15.4 million average weekly viewers or even the Landman season 2 premiere’s 9.2 million viewers. But there is a fan base willing to see this series to the end, whatever happens.

What is that ending? Where the series currently stands, our girls are finally returning home after being rescued. They’ll likely adjust to normal life again by committing another hunt back in their hometown. And in the present, the promise of “last one standing” in the pre-roll recap likely points to a final bloodbath. Is that even what fans want? Until that finale, how everything will resolve, and what it all really means, remains a mystery.

Industry observers note that for the Uruguayans, they faced a moral and religious crisis when they returned. When news broke that the rugby team ate the flesh of their dead passengers, one of the survivors defended their actions by comparing it to that of Jesus Christ and the Eucharist. According to the survivor, the group made a pact to sacrifice their bodies for each other if they perished so that they could eat their flesh to survive. The media circus completely flipped, and they were heroes once again. Pope Paul VI even wrote them a letter to sanctify their actions. It’s a fascinating end to their story—but one that the Yellowjackets cocreators likely found made for an entirely different sort of TV show.

Outside of the pagan imagery and supernatural threats that the Yellowjackets cast face, religion has yet to enter the picture. The cannibalism mostly seemed inevitable until it finally happened halfway through season 2, and besides that, the women have done much worse since returning home. After three seasons of one cold-blooded murder after another, the body count just to keep their secret hidden is enough for all of them to face the electric chair. And even if there is a malevolent force controlling them, it may take a metaphorical release of pain and grief on par with Hereditary or Weapons to fully satisfy viewers in the series finale.

“To some extent, I do feel as though if we were to just 100 percent jump both feet on one side of [the supernatural/reality] line, it would disperse some of what makes this show interesting,” cocreator Ashley Lyle told CBR ahead of season 4.

Sources indicate that “I think that we are fully aware that we are either going to delight or anger both sides of that equation in some way, shape, or form,” they added. “But I don’t know that that could have been avoided.”.

At this point, some version of watching the women all kill each other, or even watching them all defeat the evil they believe is haunting them, just doesn’t feel like enough. The show’s biggest problem might have just been that it ran too long. A simpler ending to a shorter story might have left viewers delighted. But we shall see! If Yellowjackets can land the plane (sorry) in the end, I’m willing to eat (apologies again) my own words on this one.
