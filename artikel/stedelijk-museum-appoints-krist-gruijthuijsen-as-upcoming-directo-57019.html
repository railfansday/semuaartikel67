Stedelijk Museum appoints Krist Gruijthuijsen as upcoming director

The Stedelijk Museum in Amsterdam has unveiled that the Dutchman Krist Gruijthuijsen will take over as its new director from July 2027.

Moving from Finland, where he has since 2025 been director of the Espoo Museum of Modern Art (EMMA), he remarked he wants to bring an outsider’s perspective and a focus on how the modern city connects with the world.

“The Stedelijk museum’s strength is in bringing large and Modernist exhibitions to the Netherlands,” he tells The Art Newspaper. “People go to the Rijksmuseum for Rembrandt and to the city [Stedelijk] museum to understand how Amsterdam has developed, to understand Modernism through the lens of art and design.”.

Recent findings suggest that the Stedelijk Museum enjoyed success last year with an Anselm Kiefer show—organised with the neighbouring Van Gogh Museum—and an exhibition of work by the Dutch photographer Erwin Olaf, increasing annual ticket revenue compared to 2024 by almost a quarter, to €10.8 million. Its current Yayoi Kusama show has been extended to the end of January due to its popularity.

According to recent reports, this success stands out against a backdrop of Dutch museums reporting financial pressure and a fall in visits compared to pre-pandemic levels. The Rijksmuseum’s most recent annual report describes internal discussions that took place about the fact visitor numbers fell 4% in 2025 to 2.3 million, while the Rembrandt House Museum recently warned that rising tourist taxes will damage cultural tourism.

Sources indicate that but Gruijthuijsen noted he is more interested in variety than visitor records. “I’m not a visitor numbers director,” he says. “It is quality rather than quantity: what we need economically, and the rest is a plus. It is very difficult these days because people only choose what they know. My goal is first and foremost to support artists and art.”.

Gruijthuijsen takes over from Rein Wolfs, who has been the director since 2019.

In a recent development, from one of the first exhibitions to establish Van Gogh’s work in 1905 to experimental highlights of the 1960s, the Stedelijk Museum created an international reputation for contemporary art, which Gruijthuijsen wants to make relevant today. “The DNA of the city museum is a modern day and global perspective—trying to find connections,” he claims.

From the Finns, he will also bring an vital lesson in terms of improving diversity and tackling under-represented subjects, he claims: “Less talking, more doing.”.
