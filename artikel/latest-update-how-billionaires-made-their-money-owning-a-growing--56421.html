📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, most billionaires got rich by founding or holding a major stake in a tech giant that kept growing, not by earning a salary. You can use the same method on a small scale. Own stock through low-cost index funds and hold it for decades, and keep as much ownership as you can in any business you start.

Even a $1 million salary, saved in full, would take 1,000 years to add up to $1 billion.

It has been revealed that A stake in a tech giant is different, because its value rises as long as the company grows and you hold on. Jeff Bezos founded Amazon in 1994 and still owns 8% of it, the largest part of his fortune. Elon Musk’s fortune is mostly the 38% of SpaceX he owns, counting options to buy shares, with less in his nearly 11% of Tesla.

Forbes counts 71% of the 2026 Forbes 400, its list of the richest Americans, as self-made, meaning they built their fortunes rather than inheriting them. The rest started with an inheritance, like Alice Walton, whose fortune comes from her stake in Walmart, the company her father cofounded.

Recent findings suggest that the part you can copy is ownership. If you start a business, keep as much of it as you can, because each share you sell early gives its future gains to the buyer.

Don’t copy how much of their wealth they keep in one manufacturer, though, since few single stocks pay off like that. Finance professor Hendrik Bessembinder found that four of every seven U.S. stocks since 1926 returned less over their lifetimes than one-month Treasury bills, which are short-term U.S. government debt. The best-performing 4% of companies produced the stock market’s entire net gain over that span.

A broad, low-cost index fund, such as one tracking the whole U.S. stock market, owns thousands of companies, so it holds the few big winners without you having to pick them. At an assumed 7% average annual return, $10,000 left in one for 30 years grows to about $76,000, because each year’s gain earns returns too. Building wealth on an ordinary income works the same way, by spending less than you earn and investing the gap for decades.

Start investing in stocks with one such fund this month, since in that example the last 10 years add about $37,000, more than the roughly $29,000 the first 20 add.
