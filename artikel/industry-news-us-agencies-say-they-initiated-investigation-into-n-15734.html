Industry News: U.S. agencies say they initiated investigation into Nvidia partner Megaspeed in 2024

According to recent reports, the U.S. Commerce and Justice Department on Monday stateside said they had initiated an investigation into Singapore-based manufacturer Megaspeed in 2024 to "address inaccurate information circulating in public reporting.".

Megaspeed International, a cloud computing firm that operates data centers across Southeast Asia, has been drawn into the fallout surrounding the leadership shake-up at chip giant Nvidia.

In a joint release, the departments outlined a timeline of their investigations into Megaspeed, saying the probes began in 2024 over potential export-control violations.

In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its "no sell" list, after Commerce Department investigators requested records from Nvidia concerning direct or indirect sales to the manufacturer.

Sources indicate that in the same month, U.S. officials and Singapore police conducted an in-person inspection of Megaspeed and interviewed Alice Huang, who was allegedly involved in a relationship with Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang.

"Following this enforcement action, the Department of Justice took the lead role in the criminal investigation of Megaspeed," the statement mentioned.

In a recent development, on Wednesday, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick also reposted the statement, adding, "The Department of Commerce works to protect the U.S. economy and our critical industries every day. We are very proud of the work of the Bureau of Industry and Security.".

Sources indicate that in response to the statement, Chris McGuire, senior fellow for China and emerging technologies at the Council of Foreign Relations wrote on X, "Very, very strange statement tonight... about Megaspeed. The facts here just don't add up, and this does nothing to explain why Commerce did not cut Megaspeed off from chips."McGuire said that Megaspeed kept buying chips after Nvidia put them on their no-trade list, and asked if Nvidia also blacklisted Megaspeed's subsidaries Speedmatrix and PT Megaspeed International. "I would bet they did not! If they kept shipping to them after blacklisting Megaspeed, that would be very concerning," McGuire added.

Sources indicate that the statement comes after Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs said the first time authorities there learned of U.S. interest in Megaspeed and Alice Huang was in June. Singapore had already been conducting its own independent investigation and said she was not investigated for espionage.

The city-state barred Alice Huang from re-entering the country in February 2026 after authorities found that she had knowingly provided inaccurate information about her role at Megaspeed.

Alice Huang was reportedly recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to gather information on Jensen Huang. The Justice Department has not reached a conclusion on whether she was a Chinese spy.

According to recent reports, jensen Huang resigned as CEO last week after revelations about multiple extramarital relationships, including with two Nvidia employees. The tech giant has appointed Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO.

Sources indicate that the agencies' statement also comes as Nvidia prepares to report its third-quarter results Tuesday afternoon U.S. time, with Kress set to lead her first earnings call.

Earlier on Monday in the U.S., the Commerce Department moved to close a loophole that has allowed Chinese companies to access Nvidia's advanced computing power through data centers in third countries.

According to recent reports, the interim final rule from the department's Bureau of Industry and Security is aimed at restricting remote access to advanced U.S. computing technology.

It also comes amid growing scrutiny of Lutnick for not having done enough to prevent restricted Nvidia chips and computing capacity from reaching Chinese companies.

— CNBC's Anniek Bao and Dan Mangan contributed to this report.
