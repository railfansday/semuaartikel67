Sources indicate that every few months, Grinding Gear Games resurfaces with livestreams to showcase all the newly introduced toys in the latest updates for Path of Exile 1 and 2. They play out quite differently from your usual game announcements, cutting straight to showing new gameplay features ahead of any preamble.

In a recent development, according to Path of Exile co-game director Jonathan Rogers, this is a key part of Path of Exile's success over the last 13 years. Speaking at games industry conference DevGAMM FTW!, he explained how GGG came up with a winning formula for PoE's seasonal update schedule.

Sources indicate that the announcement streams aren't just marketing, he explained, they help dictate what kinds of things the team should focus on with every enhancement. If it doesn't sound exciting enough to rally players around a new league, it doesn't get made.

After a teaser trailer reveals the theme and title of the league a few weeks before the full announcement, the stream dives right into the juiciest details—an approach that Rogers believes is paramount when it comes to selling the update.

Rogers says he's seen some "truly terrible livestreams for game announcements," before slipping in a quick jab: "Blizzard I'm looking at you.".

According to recent reports, I've watched enough Diablo 4 upcoming season streams to know exactly what he's talking about even if I don't quite agree that it's strictly a bad way to do it. Blizzard typically likes to lead with some introductions before having individual developers break down what's coming in an revision.

In a recent development, admittedly, these can drag on for too long, especially if you don't particularly care about percentage changes to some item or stat. But I appreciate hearing the thought process behind each decision from the people responsible for that aspect of the game. In a time when certain very vocal segments of the audience prefer to think of studios as machines, it's a good way to remind people that real human beings are behind it all.

That said, I think Rogers' specific point about too many studios wasting everyone's time by holding the best announcements for last is often true. For something like a latest update to an ongoing game, everyone is watching to find out why this one is worth logging back in for. I want to see the standout details so I don't feel like I wasted 45 minutes for something I'm not that into.

Sometimes I do like the build-up and eventual payoff for a substantial reveal, though, like the way Blizzard dropped Diablo 5 at the end of the Diablo portion of this year's BlizzCon or how Larian surprised everyone with Divinity last year. Certain games earn the theatrics. If anything, Rogers' approach just underlines how important it is for studios to read the room and craft their announcements around the things that will actually spark interest rather than putting everyone to sleep.
