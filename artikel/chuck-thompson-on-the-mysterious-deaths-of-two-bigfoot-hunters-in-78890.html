📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Skamania county rarely, if ever, captures the attention of the national news media. Let alone the international news media. But during the traditionally slow Christmas holiday, when even serious outlets stoop to tracking Santa’s progress across the northern hemisphere and presenting expert commentary on decorations at the White House, the story about the bodies found in the backwoods of the Pacific Northwest arrived like a Christmas gift from News Kringle.

Industry observers note that on Christmas morning 2024, two men from Portland, Oregon, were reported missing in the wilds of Washington state. After a three-day search that involved more than sixty volunteers from two states, dogs, drones, and helicopters working through harsh terrain, icy conditions, and a snowstorm, their frozen bodies were recovered. On December 28, Portland’s CBS television affiliate KOIN 6 was among the first to pick up the story.

“Two Portland men searching for Sasquatch and missing since early Christmas morning were found dead in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest—likely from exposure and being ill prepared,” the station reported.

According to recent reports, did someone say Sasquatch? Every editor in the world knew clickbait when they saw it. Within days, near-identical headlines appeared around the world, backed by the biggest names in news.

CNN: 2 Oregon men die from exposure in a forest after they went out to look for Sasquatch.

Fox News: 2 dead after search for Sasquatch in Washington national forest.

NPR: 2 men were found dead from exposure after looking for Sasquatch.

BBC: Two men found dead while searching for Bigfoot.

Nine News Australia: Two men found dead in US after going on Christmas Eve hunt for Bigfoot.

Hundreds, perhaps thousands, jumped in. The Associated Press. MSN. CBS News. HuffPost. Newsweek. Today. Yahoo News. USA Today. Newly introduced York Post. Los Angeles Times. San Francisco Chronicle. The Times. Daily Mail.

The story was such a surefire traffic pusher that even specialized outlets ran with it. People. Sports Illustrated. AccuWeather. The celebrity and entertainment website TooFab. Spain’s national sports tabloid Marca. Newly introduced Zealand’s Stuff. Taiwanese tabloid CTWant. The massively popular 52hrtt, a Chinese-language website that serves the global Chinese diaspora.

There was no video, so each outlet looped the same images from a package of photos released by the Skamania County 23 sasquatch country Sheriff’s Office: four SAR volunteers on a snow-covered trail carrying an extension ladder through the forest; six volunteers in safety-orange jackets and helmets, one pointing a finger into the woods, using an extension ladder laid on the ground to cross a stream; a guy on hands and knees crawling across the ladder over a stream; a group of seven searchers using downed logs to cross a stream; and a Coast Guard helicopter hovering above an opening in the forest canopy.

In a digitally powered echo chamber, local, regional, national, and international outlets repeated the story almost word for word. All the way across the continent, News Center Maine television led its December 30 national news roundup in typical fashion:.

Recent findings suggest that we’ll start in Washington state. Two men were found dead there after going on a search for Bigfoot. They went out on their search a few days before Christmas Eve. When they didn’t return, family members contacted police. They were found after a three-day search. Local authorities there say it seems as though they likely died of exposure because of the weather conditions at the time and not being prepared.

Sasquatch hunters. Christmas search. Poorly prepared. Died of exposure. That was all anyone seemed to know.

According to recent reports, in fact, those elementary pieces of the story were all anyone in the news media actually did know. As even a cursory examination of the torrent of articles and broadcasts about the two bodies found in the forest showed, the stories were noteworthy mostly for their striking uniformity and lack of information. But something about the story wasn’t right. Something didn’t add up.

It has been revealed that the rule that credible journalists are meant to follow is that the details in a story must support its headline—particularly if that headline makes an audacious claim. Two guys froze to death while looking for Bigfoot? Wow. Bring on the details.

According to recent reports, but that was the thing about the story of the Christmas Sasquatch hunters. After the stun-gun heds and ledes, there were no details about the supposed Sasquatch hunt. Not scant details. Not vague details. No details whatsoever. As if by mandate, every outlet that picked up the story made sure to include Sasquatch in its headline and opening grab. Then they dropped the Bigfoot angle altogether.

It has been revealed that how long had the men been out looking for Sasquatch? Did they have a track record as Sasquatch hunters? Were they carrying special equipment, such as video cameras, long-range microphones, or telephoto lenses? If they were out searching for Sasquatch in rugged terrain and winter conditions, why were they so ill prepared?

On these and other questions related to the Sasquatch hunt teased in the headlines, every story was the same—strangely mute.
