Lufthansa is turning airplane etiquette into a clothing collection, including a latest shirt reminding passengers that there is no need to stand up immediately after landing. I like the idea and agree with much of the advice, though standing up is not necessarily the problem…pushing past everyone else is.

Lufthansa has announced that four items from its Travel Etiquette Collection will go on sale through the WorldShop beginning November 27, 2026.

The collection debuted in August and was not originally intended for sale. Lufthansa indicates the response prompted a retail edition, including a newly introduced shirt based on the most popular suggestion from its social media community.

Each design is limited to 250 individually numbered pieces. Lufthansa will donate its licensing proceeds to help alliance, the Lufthansa Group’s aid organization (that is the licensing revenue, rather than necessarily the entire purchase price).

Prices were not included in the announcement.

First off, I love these…simple yet stylish.

It has been revealed that as to the messaging itself, I understand why the shirt appeals to people. We have all watched passengers spring up as though standing in the aisle will somehow make the jet bridge attach faster.

And if the aircraft is still moving or the seatbelt sign remains illuminated, it should go without saying that you stay seated.

Industry observers note that but once the aircraft is parked and passengers are permitted to get up, I have no objection to someone standing. After several hours in an economy class seat, stretching your legs is perfectly understandable. An aisle passenger can also retrieve a bag and be ready to leave when the rows ahead have cleared.

The problem is the person who charges forward from row 28 and wedges himself beside row 19, apparently convinced that everyone else is sitting there for his convenience.

According to recent reports, stand if you want. Just wait your turn to leave.

Sources indicate that (The only exception I would make are the circumstances in which the crew asks passengers to remain seated so travelers with tight connections can get off first. That requires everyone to cooperate, including those of us who might otherwise prefer to stand…but I also have a problem with the “free riders” in those situations…another problem for another day.).

My favorite in this collection is the “Recline Responsibly” message, which strikes me as common sense, but something so many passengers are so rude about.

If an airline installs a reclining seat, passengers should generally be able to use that feature. But look behind you first and recline slowly. Giving the person behind you a moment to adjust a laptop or move a drink costs you very little.

During meal service, the seat should be upright, but other than that, recline away…just gently.

Industry observers note that we spend so much time arguing about whether reclining is acceptable that we sometimes overlook how much the manner of doing it matters. A slow adjustment is very different from throwing your seat back without warning.

It has been revealed that lufthansa will begin selling four limited-edition etiquette items on November 27. I like the collection, particularly the reminder to recline responsibly. But I can’t say I’ll be up late waiting to buy it…how about you?
