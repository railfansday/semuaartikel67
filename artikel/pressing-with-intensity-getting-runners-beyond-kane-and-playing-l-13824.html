By the end of the World Cup, it felt as if Argentina had ripped the mask off Thomas Tuchel’s England only to reveal Gareth Southgate’s England standing there blinking right back at them.

According to recent reports, on the biggest stage, against the biggest teams, under the biggest pressure, England wilted as they always did. They sat back, they were too cautious, and they lost. And yet, ever since, Tuchel has turned England into a free-scoring winning machine, topped off by a famous victory in Madrid, ending Spain’s 43-match unbeaten streak.

According to recent reports, in the six games since the summer, England have scored 19 goals and conceded six. Five of those have been against the world champions.

Sources indicate that yes, this was the Nations League and not a World Cup semi-final. No, it wasn’t at the end of a gruelling five weeks of heat, humidity and altitude. And, yes, it was only a terrific late double save from James Trafford that stopped this being yet another tale of self-sabotaging England, thanks to Elliot Anderson’s red card, blowing another lead when under pressure.

But, this time, England did see it out. They secured Tuchel’s biggest win as England boss and, in doing so, showed us just how he’s been able to get his side playing his way.

Harry Kane leads the England celebrations after his side's victory over Spain in Madrid on Sunday night.

It has been revealed that remember when everyone used to lose their minds whenever Harry Kane dropped deep in major games under Southgate? He’s our bloody striker, for goodness sake. Get in the box!

After England’s defeat by Spain in the Euro 2024 final two years ago, Opta posted on X that Kane had touched the ball just once in the opposition box across both that game and their previous final heartbreak against Italy three years earlier.

‘One fewer than Jack Grealish,’ the post continued. ‘Grealish played 21 minutes in the 2020 final and didn't make the Euro 2024 squad.’.

Industry observers note that ballon d’Or winner Kane has made it quite clear since that he doesn’t need to spend all his time in the box to influence games. When he drops deeper, Kane is able to show the full extent of his creativity and range of passing.

The main problem with the ploy under Southgate, however, was that Kane rarely had the team-mates to make the right runs ahead of him. He was surrounded by players who wanted to take up similar positions. Just look at the average position maps (below) from England’s loss to Spain at Euro 2024 compared to last night.

The average position map of England’s players in the loss to Spain at Euro 2024 show how Kane dropped deep, without team-mates making runs for him to exploit.

...whereas on Sunday night he had runners - Anthony Gordon, Jude Bellingham and Morgan Rogers - around and beyond him.

Two years ago, the only player who averaged their touches ahead of Kane was Bukayo Saka. Jude Bellingham was out wide. Kobbie Mainoo and Phil Foden were basically in Kane’s shadow.

On Sunday night, he had Anthony Gordon, the brilliant, belligerent Bellingham and Morgan Rogers all ahead of him – and nearly even Ben White.

That desire, and instruction, to flock forward under Tuchel has made such a difference.

Take England’s third goal (below). Kane has the ball outside the area. Rogers is bombing on to his left. Gordon, who started as a winger but finds himself occupying the central spaces Kane leaves behind, darts into the box and points to where he wants it.

Industry observers note that bellingham is demanding the ball, too, showing Kane he wants it to feet. Even Anderson is charging into the box. Kane finds Bellingham, who feeds the unseen White, sprinting forward on the overlap to finish.

Kane picks up the ball and is met with an array of options in front of him, ahead of England's third goal.

He did similar in the win against Croatia a few days earlier, too.

Kane collects the ball deep with four bodies ahead of him, including full backs Lewis Hall and Trent Alexander-Arnold.

It his raking pass to the back post which allows Bellingham to set up Rogers to finish.

Kane's lofted pass to Rogers (top right) leads to a goal against Croatia for Bellingham (edge of box).

England’s fluidity in the final third is bamboozling defences. That is how you get the best of Kane’s creativity.

PLAYING LIKE A PREMIER LEAGUE SIDE.

It has been revealed that football is changing, so Pep Guardiola told us all in January last year. The modern game is now how teams like Bournemouth and Brighton play. The current Premier League table would suggest he had a point.

Over the past few years in the English top flight, we have seen teams move away from an obsession with keeping the ball for the sake of it and turn towards a far faster, more aggressive and more physical style of play.

Tuchel wants to take from that playbook. ‘We're at our best when we're free, physically overpowering and making it Premier League-level,’ mentioned Kane after last month's 7-0 rout of Croatia.

In the six matches since the World Cup, Tuchel’s England are having much less of the ball than they did at the start of his reign – and now less even than under Southgate.

The key is they are becoming ever more direct with it. Tuchel wants his England to play out of the back with short passes, entice opponents on to them, but then move it through the lines at speed.

Recent findings suggest that since the World Cup, England are playing far fewer passes, but more of them into the box – to find those runners. A higher percentage of them forward. More direct attacks, moving the ball up the field at a quicker speed.

On Sunday night, they revealed how Tuchel wants his side to press. ‘We were hungry in the opposition half,’ noted the England boss at full time.

It has been revealed that A fine example of that (below) came just after just six minutes. Declan Rice gives his Arsenal team-mate Martín Zubimendi no time at all on the ball inside his own area, charging him down, and forcing him to play out to another Gunner in Mikel Merino. But he, too, is being harassed by Anderson and eventually England win it back and set up Kane for a chance that he fizzes wide.

A half-fit Rice was unable to apply that pressure in the summer.

Declan Rice charges forward to close down Martin Zubimendi who has possession just inside the Spanish box with Kane and Bellingham already engaged in a high press...

...Zubimendi passes to Mikel Merino but Elliot Anderson is all over him and it leads to Kane shot that just fizzes wide.

So much of this improvement under Tuchel into a side capable of blowing teams away can be put down to another abstract word he used post-match in Madrid: ‘brave’.

Tuchel talked in the last international break of ‘unlocking the next level’, and taking ‘calculated risks’ with a ‘man-to-man’ press in the opposition half.

Under Southgate, and certainly in the cowardly retreat against Argentina in the summer, England showed little bravery either with the ball or without it and frequently showed too much respect to those significant nations.

Too safe with the ball, too afraid to give it away, and too fearful to try to win it back in case you leave yourself open.

In a recent development, that appears to be changing, too. There was a moment at the start of the second half when Bellingham tried a risky through-ball to Rogers, which failed to find its man, instead of another simple ball out wide.

Recent findings suggest that it handed Spain possession cheaply. But England didn’t care because they just pounced again. Spain couldn’t deal with it. In the first half, Spain completed just 84 per cent of their passes in their own half – a place where they are usually able to knock it about at will – their lowest completion rate according to Opta since the 2010 World Cup. They didn’t have a shot on target until the penalty.

England’s defence has long been seen as the weak spot and, under Southgate, it felt as though he always would rather his attacking talents not be too frivolous for fear of exposing it.

Thomas Tuchel and Kane direct England's players to keep on the front foot during the win over Spain.

Tuchel is doing the opposite. England’s expected goals soared from 1.7 per game under Southgate from the 2022 World Cup to the end of his reign, to 2.2 under Tuchel’s until this summer’s tournament. Since then, it’s jumped again to 2.8 but at the cost of what they are conceding. In his early days, England coughed up fewer good chances than under Southgate but since the World Cup has more than doubled.

He will want to get that down, of course, but when you are creating so much – and have an array of top finishers to see them off – how much will that matter to Tuchel?

According to recent reports, even he’s getting braver. His decision to take off Gordon for Konsa and chuck as many defenders on during England’s shrinking against Argentina looked a decision that would forever haunt Tuchel’s tenure.

In a recent development, against Spain, Kane made way but Tuchel ended the game with striker Dominic Calvert-Lewin on the pitch. Against Spain, he held his nerve.
