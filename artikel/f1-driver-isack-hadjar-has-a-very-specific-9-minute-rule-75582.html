F1 Driver Isack Hadjar Has a Very Specific 9-Minute Rule

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that ISACK HADJAR HAS a problem. The 22-year-old Red Bull Formula 1 driver has finally gotten his hands on Grand Theft Auto VI, and he's slightly concerned about what that might do to his free time.

In a recent development, “It's going to ruin my life,” he jokes to Men's Health in the paddock ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix. He's only managed to squeeze in about 25 minutes of gameplay so far this weekend, but his verdict is already in: “It's amazing.”.

Sources indicate that for now, at least, Hadjar has his priorities straight. “I'm a professional,” he says with a smirk. “When I know it's time to go to sleep, I go to sleep.”.

Sources indicate that that level of discipline extends well beyond bedtime. In a sport that demands lightning-fast reflexes, intense concentration, and serious physical endurance, Hadjar has developed a pretty specific routine to get his brain and body ready to perform.

Take his pre-race ritual, which he has down to a science. He stretches and warms up with his trainer, including juggling and reaction-time tests to prime the brain. He listens to rap music—French, American, or British—to get himself hyped.

Sources indicate that then, exactly nine minutes before the race begins, he starts putting in his earpieces. “Not 10, not 11, nine," he notes.

In a recent development, it's not a superstition or a lucky number. (That would be six, the number he drives with for Red Bull.) It’s simply what he’s found to be the perfect amount of time to get in the car and get ready to race. “I hate staying in the car doing nothing,” he says. “So for me, nine minutes is the maximum time.”.

While he enjoys the pre-race preparation, there's one part of training he'd happily skip: zone 2 cardio, running at a moderate pace to power up his endurance.

Industry observers note that “It’s just boring. I like conditioning more,” he says. To get through it, he puts on a movie, TV show, or music. “It goes by promptly in the end, but it's just, I feel like it's a waste of time. But it's very crucial.”.

In a recent development, when the race is over, Hadjar's routine is considerably more relaxed. A good meal helps him switch his brain off—specifically, a burger with caramelized onions, cheddar, tomato, and lettuce. He’ll also watch anime and FaceTime his parents, especially after a good result.

In a recent development, and now, of course, there will be GTA VI waiting for him. Whether he'll be able to stick to that bedtime routine is another question.
