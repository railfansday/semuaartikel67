How Studio Freight Reimagined Thesis Brand Identity

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Studio Freight builds the Thesis brand identity from zine culture: an asterisk mark, dot-grid Harber type, and electric lime.

The system starts from a position, not a logo. Thesis builds brands on Bitcoin, backing its conviction with capital and code, and Studio Freight answers with a visual language pulled from indie hacker zines and early computing: black and white, gritty textures, hand-drawn spheres and gears, and one electric lime doing all the shouting. The asterisk sits at the center, somewhere between a footnote and a flag, and the whole identity reads like a samizdat pamphlet that learned to behave on a screen. The voice matches, what the studio calls adult punk: spirited defiance tempered with wisdom, unafraid to make guesses about restoring autonomy in tech.

According to recent reports, two typefaces carry the system. Harber, a dot-gridded display face, holds the texture of a printout from a different internet, with dotted letterforms that spell the name at poster scale, in the site navigation, and ghosted behind the asterisk on the homepage. Antique Legacy Semibold does the clear work, setting plain statements in a voice that stays readable while the display type makes the noise.

In a recent development, the grid is the dot grid itself. The asterisk mark, the dotted THESIS letterforms, and the dotted figures in the launch graphics all sit on the same dotted construction, so every panel reads as one argument. The studio coined a word for the position behind it, counterventure, naming a studio that creates ideas from the inside instead of waiting for them to walk in, operating against the extractive defaults of the venture model.

Industry observers note that the material language is print, even on screen. Hand-drawn spheres and gears, gritty textures, and a black-and-white field give the system the feel of print passed hand to hand, and the electric lime cuts through it in exactly one place at a time: a poster, a headline, a hand-drawn illustration on a lime field. The stationery keeps the same discipline, a white card that carries the name and the counterventure label, and nothing else.

Sources indicate that the system stretches across the launch site, where the asterisk fills a monitor above the tagline, and into product work, where a phone screen for tBTC carries the dot-grid mark and a line about decentralized cryptography. A poster fills its frame with one word in flat lime, and a manifesto sheet sets the studio's own words in typewriter type over the dotted asterisk, including its line about making bitcoin accessible to all.
