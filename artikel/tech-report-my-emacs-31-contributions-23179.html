All that is to say, if the upstream Emacs contribution/onboarding process seems less than ideal, cut it some slack. The project as a whole primarily runs on the blood, sweat, and tears of unpaid contributors. If you can, fund the maintainers you rely on (or better yet, get your employer to do so).

If you've been thinking of contributing to Emacs upstream, don't procrastinate like me and just give it a try. Compared to the typical GitHub pull-request process, your first Emacs contribution may feel a little daunting and dated, but there's a lovely fuzzy light at the end of the tunnel. You see, Emacs has been around for 50 years. Very few software projects get to live this long. Landing contributions on a project with such a rich history is a real treat!

I do enjoy a visual (and functional) tweak here and there, and a Dired tweak felt small enough to be the perfect candidate for my very first Emacs contribution.

With that in mind, I tweaked dired-hide-details-mode by adding the dired-hide-details-hide-absolute-location user option, which hides the directory's absolute path, typically displayed at the top of Dired buffers (bug#72272, commit).

More on this in my Hide another detail post.

Industry observers note that at some point, I ran into a snag trying to run macosrec from my beloved eshell. The process just crashed with a cryptic abort trap: 6, so the glitch wasn't super obvious. It turned out the utility required granting an OS permission that can only be requested by the host app (Emacs) if it includes the relevant description in its Info.plist file.

As of version 31, Emacs includes NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription in its Info.plist (bug#72827, commit).

context-menu-mode now includes a "Send toâ¦" item, enabling sending the current file(s) or region text to external apps or services. You can also invoke M-x send-to directly. On macOS, it's powered by the native sharing picker (I use it frequently to AirDrop things to my iPhone). Elsewhere, it falls back to shell-command-do-open (bug#76120, commit, follow-up fix). These two implementations plug into new generic plumbing (send-to-handlers), so you could, for instance, add a handler sharing via Android's native share sheet (android-browse-url can do the heavy lifting) or KDE Connect on Linux (kdeconnect-cli --share).

More in my Emacs send-to (aka macOS sharing) merged upstream post.

Sources indicate that emacs 30 lost macOS dictation when migrating to NSTextInputClient. Implementing selectedRange in nsterm.m brought it back (bug#79070, commit). With LLMs/agents being a thing these days, dictating into your Emacs buffer may be just the thing to complement your AI workflow.

I covered this in my macOS dictation returns to Emacs (fix merged) post.
