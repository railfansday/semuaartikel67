Emile Heskey is standing in a warehouse, dressed in a black adidas tracksuit, trying to sell you a jar of curious sweet treats.

He smiles into the camera with the gentle-giant warmth that characterised his football career – but Heskey is in unfamiliar territory.

Behind him, stacked on shelf after shelf, are containers full of Supreme CBD gummies.

The man who scored in England's 5-1 thrashing of Germany in 2001, who scored 110 Premier League goals for Leicester, Liverpool, Birmingham, Wigan and Aston Villa plus seven more for his country, is, remarkably, trying to flog you an assortment of products containing cannabidiol, a legal but controversial ingredient derived from cannabis.

Today, he is promoting a tub of CBD strawberry gummies, as well as oil. If you fancy an exclusive discount, there's a special code bearing his name.

‘I swear by these products for my sleep,’ he writes in the caption on Instagram. ‘They have been fantastic for me for years now!’.

Emile Heskey spends his days trying to flog controversial Supreme CBD products (Instagram: theemileheskey).

He is one of multiple former footballers promoting the products for ex-Olympic boxer Anthony Fowler - despite their illegal claims about the supposed health benefits.

It has been revealed that heskey, 48, was once one of English football's most respected players. He won 62 caps for England, appearing at two World Cups. He won five trophies at Liverpool and two League Cups at Leicester.

Occasionally mocked for his lack of goals – he averaged roughly one every five games during his club career – he carried himself with humour and grace. Since retiring, his reputation has helped him secure roles with England's youth pathway and Leicester's women's team, as well as occasional punditry work for the BBC.

In September, he scored in an England legends match against Poland, playing alongside John Terry and Robbie Fowler. This month, he was a guest of honour at Liverpool's Anfield VIP Experience. He remains a regular on the speakers' circuit, with more appearances booked before Christmas.

All of which begs the question: what is he doing promoting Supreme CBD? Why is he filming several videos a month for a organization with such a troubled regulatory record?

Where once he shared a dressing room with Steven Gerrard and David Beckham, he now shares an ambassadorial roster with Katie Price.

It is unclear whether Heskey is aware of the concerns surrounding Supreme CBD products, or the repeated occasions on which the company has fallen foul of advertising rules.

Its founder, Anthony Fowler, a former Team GB Olympic boxer, has made unsubstantiated claims that his cannabidiol products can help treat mental health conditions and autism in children.

Industry observers note that there is likely a financial incentive for Heskey, although the terms of any arrangement are unclear. Equally, he appears genuinely convinced of the products' benefits. He claims the oil has done wonders for his sleep and eased his aches and pains.

Heskey's wife Chantelle has issued divorce proceedings - and claims he cancelled a holiday with their kids to go away with his fresh partner instead.

Heskey and his new partner Jade Slusarczyk spent two weeks together in Cyprus and Greece.

Chantelle branded Heskey 'vile' and claimed he deleted her comment on an Instagram post about the new couple's holiday.

Sources indicate that that may well be true. The NHS acknowledges that CBD has some medical uses, but warns that the ‘quality and content’ of products sold online can be uncertain and that they ‘may be illegal and potentially dangerous’. Nor is there any guarantee that products sold in health stores will be of ‘good quality’.

Industry observers note that supreme CBD have repeatedly breached advertising rules. In June, the Advertising Standards Authority took action over the tech giant's continued use of prohibited claims that CBD could treat, prevent or cure human disease.

The regulator has also upheld complaints about celebrity promotions that failed to make clear they were advertisements.

Sources indicate that supreme CBD have made claims that their products can treat anxiety, insomnia and depression. Such medicinal claims are prohibited in advertising for products that have not been authorised as medicines. It is uncomfortable ground for Heskey to find himself on.

Last year, Fowler also spoke about refusing chemotherapy for his two-year-old daughter's tumour, claiming CBD oil had had a 'massive' effect on it instead. Price, a paid brand ambassador, amplified those claims on her own social media channels, including posted to her 1.6million followers on X that the tumour had 'shrunk' as a result.

British medical and food safety authorities advise that CBD should not be given to those under the age of 18.

CBD can also affect how other medicines work, meaning anyone using it alongside other treatments should seek medical advice. Certain medicinal CBD treatments require regular monitoring because of their potential effects on the liver.

Recent findings suggest that it is not unusual for former sportspeople to struggle with insomnia, depression or persistent pain after retirement. In a digital world where anyone can extol the virtues of a product to thousands of followers, it is perhaps unsurprising that cannabis oil has attracted so much attention. Some people say it has helped them.

Heskey celebrates with David Beckham after scoring for England against Denmark at the 2002 World Cup.

Many of Heskey's almost 100,000 Instagram followers appear equally enthusiastic, with comments beneath his posts endorsing the benefits he describes.

And Heskey is hardly alone among former footballers. Supreme CBD products have also been endorsed by former Arsenal and England playmaker Paul Merson – who has his name on a bundle of his 'favourite' products available to buy on the company's website – Heskey's old Liverpool team-mate Chris Kirkland, Southampton hero Matt Le Tissier and ex-Nottingham Forest goalkeeper Mark Crossley, while ex-Liverpool striker John Aldridge has insisted the product was ‘good for me’.

Life after football has, at times, been rather messy for Heskey.

In 2009, his wealth was estimated at £12million. He had built a clean-cut image and maintained a relatively low profile away from the pitch. Yet just a year later, in 2010, he was declared bankrupt.

Industry observers note that his subsequent business dealings have brought further difficulties. Earlier this year, Heskey became caught up in a legal dispute surrounding an allegedly fraudulent £15m takeover of Cypriot club Apollon Limassol by British businessman Craig Gabriel.

Sources indicate that heskey had been lined up to become the face of the takeover and a club director. There is no suggestion of wrongdoing on his part.

According to recent reports, in 2023, Heskey was found to have defaulted on £92,000 in tax relating to his work as a football development officer between 2017 and 2020. He was handed a £42,000 penalty.

That same year, a celebrity bar and restaurant he had funded, and which was overseen by his wife Chantelle, was wound up by the courts with debts totalling £163,000.

Former England footballer Heskey has found love with former Miss Blackpool Slusarczyk.

Parea, which opened in 2018, had been a regular haunt of footballers and television personalities, including members of the Real Housewives of Cheshire cast. But a local catering company applied to have the business wound up over unpaid debts.

The restaurant had previously made headlines in 2019 when its first anniversary party featured sushi served on a semi-naked woman lying on a table, her modesty largely preserved by strategically placed leaves and pieces of salmon.

In 2024, Heskey faced further financial difficulties linked to his involvement in a celebrity investment scheme that became embroiled in a £1.6billion tax dispute.

Recent findings suggest that the scheme was reportedly operated by Ingenious Media, a financing company that backed dozens of films. Investors were said to include Beckham, Gerrard, Jeremy Paxman, Robbie Williams and Heskey, with a minimum investment of £50,000.

Gerrard and the composer Andrew Lloyd Webber were among those who released legal proceedings against HSBC in 2018, alleging that the bank had ‘dishonestly assisted’ Ingenious Media.

In the same year, Heskey was ordered to pay almost £200,000 in a dispute involving an unpaid £1.63m tax bill.

Industry observers note that his personal life has also become complicated. Heskey appears to be estranged from his wife Chantelle, whom he married in 2014 and with whom he has four children. In August, she branded him ‘vile’ following a holiday he took with his new partner.

Recent findings suggest that heskey had gone public with his relationship with Jade Slusarczyk, a former Sky Sports walk-on girl accompanying darts players to the oche, in July. Within a month, the couple were holidaying in Cyprus and Greece, sharing photographs of their trip on Instagram.

Parea, a popular Cheshire bar loved by footballers and celebrities and owned by Heskey and his wife, was wound up with debts of £163,000.

According to recent reports, ‘The best two weeks away with you,’ he wrote. The posts provoked an angry response from Chantelle, who has begun divorce proceedings and accused Heskey of letting his family down in comments beneath the photographs. She subsequently claimed that Heskey had deleted her comment and described him as ‘vile’.

Daily Mail Sport has contacted Mrs Heskey's representatives on multiple occasions, but they have declined to comment.

Emile and Chantelle have two sons, Reigan and Jaden, both footballers, and two younger daughters, Milanna and Mendez. Jaden made his Manchester City debut in the Carabao Cup last season and is now on loan at Sheffield Wednesday, while Reigan was sold to Cologne in the summer.

In a recent development, speaking to Daily Mail Sport last year, Heskey praised his sons' athletic ability and suggested that both could one day play for England's senior team. He was happy to talk on that occasion, speaking as he helped coach children at a football camp.

This time, however, Daily Mail Sport has been met with silence. We have made repeated attempts, through multiple reporters, to put questions to Heskey about his involvement with Supreme CBD. His representatives have either declined to comment or failed to respond.

Industry observers note that we wanted to know whether he was aware of the advertising rulings against Supreme CBD, and whether he had any concerns about the medical claims made by its founder. We would also have welcomed his account of the personal and financial difficulties described here.

But those questions remain unanswered.

And so we are left watching Heskey from afar, returning to the warehouse for video after video, smiling and cracking jokes with Fowler.
