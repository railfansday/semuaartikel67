Dumbarton have made history by appointing the youngest manager in the history of the SPFL.

Martin Fellowes has been installed as the new head coach of the League Two side at the age of 28.

He leaves his position as assistant manager and head of football operations at league leaders Stranraer to join a team six places further down the table.

Fellows spent almost 10 years with East Kilbride, were he started as a teenage kit boy, working his way up to interim manager in 2023, and winning the Lowland League Cup during a two-month spell in charge.

He has a day job in office furniture sales and holds a Uefa A licence.

Industry observers note that "You have to earn people's respect as a person," Fellowes told BBC Scotland. "When you are a young guy and you're leading a football club, I don't think you can come in and change everything and lead with an iron fist.

Industry observers note that "You have to get people's respect on and off the pitch. I know there will be plenty of criticism and there will be plenty of people sitting here today doubting that I'm capable of doing this job at 28.

"But now really the work for me has started on, by the end of the process, having gained those people onside to say, 'he was more than capable of doing this job and we're delighted to have him here'.

"I can only do that through winning games of football.".

Fellowes claims the mantle of Scottish football’s youngest ever head coach from Jim Duffy, who was 29 when he took charge of Falkirk in 1988, while Rhys McCabe was appointed player/manager of Airdrieonians at the same age four years ago.

Former St Mirren and Clyde manager Danny Lennon, now Dumbarton director of football, revealed age was not a factor when considering who to appoint as a replacement for Frank McKeown, who was sacked last month.

“What really impressed us about Martin was his football knowledge, the clarity of his coaching philosophy, his understanding of player development and the passion and ambition he has for the game,” Lennon stated.

“Throughout our conversations, Martin demonstrated a real understanding of how he wants his teams to play, the standards he expects and how he intends to help players improve, both individually and collectively.

“He is a young and ambitious coach who has worked extremely hard to develop his knowledge and prepare himself for an opportunity as a head coach in the SPFL. I believe football should always be about recognising ability and potential, regardless of age or background.

Sources indicate that “Martin has earned this opportunity through the commitment he’s shown to his development and the qualities he’s demonstrated throughout the recruitment process. We fully appreciate that managing at this level comes with challenges, and it’s vital that we provide him with the right environment and support to succeed.

“We have a group of players who have shown a genuine willingness to learn and improve, and we’re excited about the opportunity to continue that development under Martin’s leadership.

“Our ambitions remain clear. We want to improve performances, compete towards the top end of the league and establish a strong football identity that our supporters can be proud of.

Sources indicate that “This appointment is about both the present and the future of Dumbarton Football Club. We’re looking forward to working closely with Martin, supporting his development as a manager and, most importantly, helping him bring success to the club.

“I’d ask our supporters to get right behind Martin and the team as we begin this next chapter together.”.
