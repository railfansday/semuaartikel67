The disquieting question of how to be human in the art world came in for special consideration during a talk with advice columnists Chen & Lampert during Art Week NYC, a citywide festival organized by ARTnews, Artforum, and Art in America.

It has been revealed that together, Howie Chen and Andrew Lampert talked about their individual and collective efforts as artists, curators, and incomparable visionaries whose recurring “Hard Truths” advice columns and accompanying “Hard Choices” interactive quizzes are highlights of every glitch of Art of America. Since their debut in 2019, the two writers have mulled pressing questions—can senior citizens be sage social-media ambassadors for museums in need of street cred? should a jilted artist keep applying for grants? is our advice helpful?—while dishing out wisdom for the ages. At their talk on Friday, November 13 at the PMC Studio in Midtown Manhattan, Chen & Lampert reminisced about fielding queries from the past as well as their trip to Las Vegas for a 2025 essay titled “Art and Ecstatic Ambience in Las Vegas’s Neon Vortex.” (A little taste of that, for old time’s sake: “Some writers go on Hajj to Marfa to feel the minimalist wind blow sand into their eyes, while others schlep Brooklinen tote bags as they chase biennials across small European cities. Being earnest art-world advice columnists, we prefer to explore more democratic forms of visual culture—the kind that ribaldly stages a two-hour revue called The Empire Strips Back: A Burlesque Parody.”).

As watchable in the video of their talk above, Chen & Lampert also addressed questions solicited from A.i.A. readers on the fly—among them: “Do mean people in the art world actually like art? If not, what is their motivation?”.
