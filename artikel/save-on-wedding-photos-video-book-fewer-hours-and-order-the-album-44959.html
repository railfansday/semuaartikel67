Industry observers note that book your wedding photographer from the ceremony through the first dance, toasts, and cake, and a videographer for the ceremony and toasts or not at all. Take digital files you’re free to print instead of an album package, and hire a newer pro whose complete wedding galleries you’ve seen, not a friend with a camera.

For couples who married in 2025, a photographer cost an average of $3,000 and a videographer $2,300, according to The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study.

Each hour of coverage adds to the price, and so do the extras. Many photographers charge separately for a second shooter, the extra photographer who works alongside the first. An album sold on its own can add several hundred dollars. The moments worth paying for fit in a few hours: the ceremony, family portraits, the first dance, the toasts, and the cake cutting.

Book coverage from the ceremony through the cake cutting, and add the getting-ready hours only if you want those photos. Ask your DJ to schedule the first dance, toasts, and cake early in the reception. Skip the second shooter for a small wedding, where one photographer can cover it all. For video, book just the ceremony and toasts, or skip it.

In a recent development, for a lower rate, look at newer photographers building a portfolio, or ask photographers what they charge for a Friday, Sunday, or winter date. Judge each by two or three complete wedding galleries, not a portfolio of best shots, and get a contract listing the hours, the overtime rate, when the files arrive, and your print release. Even a newer pro beats a friend with a camera, since a friend signs no contract and is a guest that day too.

According to recent reports, ask each photographer you like to quote both the full package and five hours with files only. The difference is what the extra hours and the album cost you.
