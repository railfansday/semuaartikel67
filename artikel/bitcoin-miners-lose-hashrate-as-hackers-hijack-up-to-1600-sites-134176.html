Bitcoin miners lose hashrate as hackers hijack up to 1,600 sites

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On the 25th of November, Bitcoin mining firm Braiins warned that attackers had hijacked Bitcoin miners’ work through unencrypted pool connections.

In a recent development, machines kept running while someone else received credit. That basically left operators paying electricity bills for work credited elsewhere.

Its investigation documented four episodes on the 20th of November, mostly in Europe. Each affected 1,050–1,600 mining sites and diverted 0.6–1.9 EH/s for six to 49 minutes.

Attackers forged internet routing announcements, known as BGP hijacking, to intercept connections and impersonate the pool. Miners reconnected to the same address, making the switch difficult to spot.

Earlier episodes hit Latin America between the 7th and 12th of November.

It has been revealed that responding to an X user, Braiins mentioned the attacks exploited a weakness in Stratum V1, the protocol connecting miners to pools. Stratum V2 contributor Pavlenex added,.

This is an attack on MINERS and their infrastructure and is not a vulnerability in Braiins Pool.

The exposed protocol dates back to 2012, and warnings have been issued for years.

Sources indicate that braiins had flagged hashrate theft on the 18th of April 2020. It argued that preventing stolen revenue and reducing operating expenses would encourage upgrades more effectively than decentralization alone.

That history prompted Turtlecute, who works in product marketing at Braiins, to write,.

For 14 years, we ignored SV1 issues.

When an X user asked whether this was the first publicly confirmed case, Pavlenex recalled earlier reports of interception attacks.

By November, the financial incentive for upgrading had become harder to dismiss. Braiins reported that miners’ machines indicated normal activity while their pool dashboards recorded no credited hashrate.

Six months earlier, seven mining organizations had joined the Stratum V2 Working Group. These included ANTPOOL, Foundry, F2Pool, SpiderPool, Block, MARA Foundation, and DMND.

In a recent development, reportedly, the organizations represented roughly 75% of the global hashrate at the time. Their backing gave the encrypted replacement substantial reach, but deployment remained uneven.

It has been revealed that as of the 26th of November, the official directory listed Braiins Pool and DMND as production pools. It separately listed several solo pools and MARA Foundation’s industrial solo operation.

Foundry remained marked as work in progress, despite reported testing in November. ANTPOOL, F2Pool, and SpiderPool were absent from the production listings.

It has been revealed that the exposure extended beyond V2 adoption. Braiins said most major pools’ public endpoints still offered only unencrypted Stratum V1 during its checks on the 23rd of November.

Pools, Miners, fix your URLs today.

V2 encrypts mining traffic and verifies the pool’s identity, preventing an impersonator from receiving miners’ work. The November attacks showed the revenue consequences when industry support had yet to reach those connections.
