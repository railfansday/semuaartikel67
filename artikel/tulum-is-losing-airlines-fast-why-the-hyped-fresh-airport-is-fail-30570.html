Tulum Is Losing Airlines Fast: Why The Hyped Fresh Airport Is Failing

It has been revealed that in late 2023, we saw the opening of the Felipe Carrillo Puerto International Airport (TQO), in Tulum, Mexico. For those not familiar, Tulum is located on the eastern coast of the Yucatan Peninsula, and for years, it was a growing tourist destination.

While it could previously be accessed via Cancun International Airport (CUN), the hope was that by opening a latest international airport, visitor numbers would expand massively. Unfortunately what has actually happened is exactly the opposite, as airline after airline has pulled out. What exactly is going on here? I think it makes for an interesting story, and thanks to TravelinWilly for flagging this.

According to recent reports, in 2023, we saw airline after airline announce flights to Tulum. Among US carriers, we saw American, Delta, JetBlue, and United, all launch flights to Tulum, while Spirit announced flights, but never rolled out them.

That was only a small part of the overall international service to the airport, as we also saw flights to Tulum on Air Canada, Air Transat, Avianca, Copa, Discover, and WestJet, along with flights on domestic carriers like Aeromexico, Mexicana de Aviacion, Viva, and Volaris.

In a recent development, less than three years after the airport opened, where do things stand? When it comes to international year-round service, we’ve now gone from service to 12 airports, to service to four airports, which is an absolutely massive reduction:.

Recent findings suggest that just to put this into numbers, in the first half of 2026, the airport saw a 33.2% reduction in passengers. June 2026 was the worst month to date, with a 44.8% drop, as there were just 47,600 passengers, compared to 86,200 passengers the year prior.

It goes without saying that for a newly built airport, this is rather catastrophic, and not what you hope to see.

It has been revealed that in some ways, it feels like the whole concept behind Tulum Airport was sort of unnecessary. That’s also easily explained by the fact that the airport was well over a decade in the making, so while I can see how it might’ve seemed like a good idea at some point, tourism trends do evolve over time.

It has been revealed that the reality is that there was a point where Tulum was cool, edgy, and a bit off the beaten path, compared to… whatever it is now. But when it comes to the struggles that airlines have had with flying to Tulum, it seems like the problems have been endless:.

It’ll be interesting to see if Tulum can reverse any of these trends, and regain visitors….

Recent findings suggest that A few years ago Tulum Airport opened to some fanfare, as a good number of airlines rushed to add service there. Unfortunately the excitement didn’t last long, as the airport has gone from a dozen year-round international routes, to just four.

This reduction in demand particularly evident from the United States, as soon there will just be nonstop service on American and Delta, and even that will be with reduced frequencies and routes.

Tulum Airport is facing endless challenges, not the least of which is a decline in visitors to Mexico from the United States, combined with increasing competition for those fewer visitors. Mexico just isn’t a value destination in the way that it used to be in places like Cancun, Tulum, Los Cabos, etc., and that’s understandably impacting demand.

I’m curious if Tulum can find a way to turn things around, but it’s not a good situation.
