President Donald Trump signed an executive order late Friday instructing federal contractors to notify the White House of congressional information requests and withhold material the administration deems privileged, a move that comes as Democrats prepare to launch oversight investigations.

In a recent development, the order applies to all federal contractors and subcontractors. Companies that fail to comply risk losing their existing contracts or being suspended from future work, according to the order.

Trump framed the directive as a pushback against lawmakers whom the order accuses of "harassing private companies" to get confidential executive branch information.

It has been revealed that the order builds on a directive issued earlier this week by White House Counsel Will Scharf, who instructed federal agencies to clear employee congressional testimony with the administration first. Scharf added that some Democratic investigations could cross into criminal conduct, drawing pushback over what critics call a broader effort to shield the executive branch from oversight.
