📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

This briefing is based on the T3 Transportation Think Tank study commissioned by T&E. This study examines the environmental footprint of travel influencers, and the role they and social media play in the advertising strategy of airlines. The first part analyses travel influencers’ associated emissions, travel frequencies and routes. The second part focuses on airline digital advertising investments in five European markets: France, Germany, Netherlands, Spain and the United Kingdom.

Travel influencers are digital opinion leaders that travel frequently and who shape the destination’s image, attitudes and behavioural intentions of their audiences through personal narratives. Most often, air travel is their main mode of transportation and they cover large distances by taking long-haul journeys. They post content all year-round, meaning they travel a lot and generate a substantial climate footprint. Through their posts, they display luxury, comfort and aspirational locations.

Industry observers note that this constant posting of glamorised photogenic locations shapes their audiences’ destination preferences and can incite them to fly long-distance, fuelling air travel demand and contributing to overtourism and crowding of tourism hotspots. However, influencers are confronted with transparency concerns: research suggests that too much sponsored content can undermine an influencer’s credibility, and at the same time identifies a lack of hashtags, used to explicitly disclose sponsored content (#ad or #sponsored), in such content.

Sources indicate that airlines’ advertisements promote carbon-intensive behaviours through frequent and long-distance flying. Although airlines don’t publicly disclose their digital advertising expenditure, social media forms one of the largest portions of their digital budgets. Social media is a powerful driver for travel inspiration, destination promotion and direct bookings. It exerts a high level of influence on consumer choices. Partnering with travel influencers offers airlines high returns on investment. Likes, shares and reposts ensure airlines secure continuous, long-term exposure and significant quantifiable marketing value.

According to recent reports, right now, regulatory standards specifically targeting influencer content and brand collaborations remain underdeveloped. There is also no regulation asking airlines to disclose their advertisement budget spend. In addition, the EU lacks a unified and robust regulatory framework that taxes, restricts or prohibits advertising for carbon-intensive advertisements, such as airline ads. Finally, aviation taxation and carbon pricing, needed to curb excessive emissions such as those from long-haul flights, are weak. To effectively fill these gaps, existing policy mechanisms as well as those currently being developed must be strengthened and expanded.

Travel influencers often disregard the significant carbon footprint of their long-distance trips, and encourage environmentally damaging lifestyles and excessive travel among their audiences. Airlines publicly commit to sustainability while actively funding social media campaigns and influencer promotions that encourage frequent, long-haul flying.

Together they drive carbon-intensive behaviours and air travel demand, which also contributes to overtourism. Regulatory measures need to be robust, strengthened and unified across the EU to enforce accountability of both parties:.
