Designers Say These 9 Unexpected Bed Styling Tricks Can Wake Up Your Whole Bedroom

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A standard pillow setup—two king pillows against the headboard, with two standard pillows in the front, and a square accent pillow in the center—is a snooze. Designers are treating the bed as an opportunity to showcase a bit of flair. Not to mention awaken the entire bedroom.

Industry observers note that what kinds of changes are happening? Throw and accent pillows are now treated as if they are fashion accessories. We’re seeing everything from edgy studs to romantic ruffles in contrasting colors to graphic lines that hypnotize. In fact, mismatched pieces work great and can feel “intentional, collected, and completely joyful,” notes designer Betsy Wentz.

Here, nine unconventional ways to play with your bed pillow arrangements.

If the rest of your bedroom is neutral, designers are saying it’s OK to go more bold with your bed pillows. In the above space, it’s impossible not to notice the hypnotizing hue of Yves Klein blue pillows on a striped duvet cover.

“There’s something about that particular blue,” says designer Kate Anlyan, founder of Yasu Home. “It’s so vivid and almost electric, but it also has a depth to it. I love how it cuts through all the other tones without feeling disconnected from the rest of the room.” The hue works as a counterpoint to the wood paneling in the background and provides a balance to the visual weight of the black headboard.

Sources indicate that the interconnection between the contrasting stripes on the square pillows and the round velvet ball feels both fun and sophisticated, says designer Beth Diana Smith. The stripes feel especially striking, bringing a sense of energy and definition to the space, when set against the moody purple-brown Benjamin Moore's Cinnamon Slate wall color. To keep the rest of the bedding from competing, Smith tames the room with a muted light gray coverlet.

In a recent development, “Match up the headboard, throws, lamps and artwork with your throw pillows to balance out color throughout your room,” says designer Renée Gaddis. The encapsulated look creates instant cohesion. Starting with a bold hue, like a mustard yellow, will make your job easier because it has personality from the start.

Industry observers note that faux fur is a classic way to add rich texture, but you don’t need to reserve it for a château. Interior designer Mialani Collard uses a neutral linen palette and tactile embellishments that sandwich the fluff to make it less prominent, allowing the bed to work even in tropical climates like Florida.

In a recent development, when you want a home with casual living as the main objective, fill surfaces in a way that doesn't look too pristine or orderly. Interior designer Tyson Ness of Studio Ness mixed different patterns and colors for the bedroom pillows so there’s no “right way” to make the bed after it’s been used.

“The different combinations create a slightly different look each time, keeping the overall feeling light, airy, and totally casual,” Ness adds.

Choosing pillows that match the bedding is easy, but Cate Gutter, owner and principal designer of CWG Design, prefers to add a bit of je ne sais quoi when she styles the bed.

“This bedroom is tucked away in a mountain home here in North Carolina, and we wanted it to have a playful, nostalgic feel reminiscent of summer camp,” Gutter says. “We carried that inspiration into the pillow designs, giving them an almost handmade, craft-like quality that feels whimsical.”.

The oversized pillows are made in Imogen Heath's Arty Check, a cheerful yellow check finished with a fuzzy green fringe. In front of it, a smaller bolster is a playful take on a traditional shape, but the muted, multicolored stripe with a contrasting ruffled flange bolsters extra personality. The added texture breaks up the monotony of solid linens.

According to recent reports, “Studs are an unexpected detail on bed pillows because they’re so versatile,” claims Sean Carter, senior interior designer at Sapphire Pear. “They look just as great on a neutral fabric as they do with bold colors, prints, and patterns.” The metallic element adds a little edge without overpowering the overall design.

Wentz is no stranger when it comes to combining patterns and layering different scales in the same color family. Look for prints with their own personality that won’t compete with each other.

“The smaller confetti and stick stripe pillows give your eye a place to rest against that major graphic headboard, so they feel like friends, not strangers,” Wentz claims.

Pillows gone wild can still balance restraint. Creating serious contrast—in pattern direction—will step up your bedscaping game.

In a recent development, “The geometric patterns in the pillows complement the strong linear elements,” indicates interior designer Nicole Johnson. “The vertical wood slats draw the eye upward, while the horizontal lines of the bed and furnishings provide balance.”.

Linear patterns in the pillows at a smaller scale create a picture-in-picture effect that won’t overwhelm the overall design.
