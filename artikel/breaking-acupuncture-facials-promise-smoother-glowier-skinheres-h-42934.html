Sticking needles in your face is a choice, but many who have done it, along with the experts administering them, will say the benefits of injectable fillers and muscle relaxants like Botox are worth the prick. Facial acupuncture takes a more holistic approach to smart aging—one that also claims to have wellness perks beyond simply aesthetics. Kim Ross, founder and CEO of Ora Method, explains the benefits, plus how and when to incorporate facial acupuncture into your routine.

In a recent development, acupuncture is a practice rooted in traditional Chinese medicine that uses ultrafine needles to stimulate circulation and support the body’s internal balance. Ross claims she first turned to the practice for her own digestive challenges and saw firsthand how effective it could be. That experience led her to start Ora Method, a NYC-based wellness practice specializing in acupuncture.

Facial acupuncture applies the same principles to the face. The treatment aims to gently boost circulation and support collagen production. “Many of our facials also include full-body acupuncture, because what shows up on the skin is often connected to what’s happening internally, like stress or hormonal fluctuations,” she explains. The treatment can also be layered with medical-grade red light therapy, along with guided meditation, which Ross notes works hand in hand with the acupuncture.

There are several. Ross notes that after a single acupuncture facial session, clients often notice less puffiness, smoother texture, and a subtle lift from the boost in circulation and lymphatic drainage. “Most people leave with slightly flushed, glowing skin,” she adds. The treatment can also help balance oil production, clear up mild breakouts, and improve skin texture.

It has been revealed that the bigger benefits build with consistency. “Acupuncture stimulates collagen production, which helps improve elasticity over time. It works with the skin and the muscles underneath instead of dramatically changing the face, so you end up with a healthier, more lifted version of your own features. That’s why so many of our clients see it as a long-term investment in their skin,” Ross adds.

Beyond the face, she also points out that how your skin looks has a lot to do with how your body feels on the inside. So, along with the face, there are perks to simultaneously doing full-body acupuncture.

Industry observers note that the treatment works across the whole face, but Ross states there are visible results for puffiness around the eyes and along the jawline, where fluid tends to build up. For TMJ sufferers, it also helps release tension in the jaw and facial muscles. The practice supports both lymphatic drainage and collagen production, so it’s a good fit for anyone who wants to refine their facial contours or improve tone and elasticity over time. “Many clients also notice their fine lines start to fade,” says Ross.

It has been revealed that you can actually do acupuncture even if you already have filler or have injected a muscle relaxant; Ross says it’s really about timing. “We ask clients to wait at least weeks after Botox or filler before booking a facial acupuncture treatment. That gives your injectables time to settle and keeps the treatment from interfering with your results,” she advises. It’s also crucial to note that each of these treatments yields different results. Acupuncture, filler, and neurotoxins like Botox each target different layers of the face and address signs of aging through completely different mechanisms. Botox temporarily blocks nerve signals to specific facial muscles, softening or freezing them so they don’t contract and leave indents in the skin, particularly where expression lines form on the forehead and around the eye area.

Sources indicate that acupuncture, on the other hand, uses hair-thin needles to create tiny microtraumas in the skin. Over time, the tiny punctures signal the body’s natural healing response and stimulate circulation, collagen, and elastin production. Dermal fillers work by filling in areas of the face that have hollowed out, lost volume, or need more sculpting with a substance like hyaluronic acid or poly-L-lactic acid.

Ross states that facial acupuncture is generally very gentle and requires little prep. Since the treatment uses ultrafine needles, most clients leave with slightly flushed, glowing skin. There’s typically no downtime, so you can go right back to your day. Skin tone and type are not factors; however, if you are sensitive, the pricks may cause more redness or irritation than in others.

Much like other professional treatments such as dermal fillers and Botox, Ross recommends pausing retinoids and any active exfoliants for five to seven days before an acupuncture facial, so your skin isn’t sensitive going into the treatment.
