A “Saturday Night Live” skit about caring for aging relatives touched a viral nerve after it aired over the weekend, including with Melinda French Gates, who offered her own “fact check” on the too-close-to-home comedy.

The sketch, a fake commercial, stars host John Mulaney as a man who has found an easy way to care for his aging father without upending his own life: “Sister,” an “elder care system” that is free and is, in fact, his sister.

French Gates wrote on Instagram this week that the sketch “is funny because it’s a little too true.” Using research from Pivotal, her investment and philanthropic organization, she went through the ad’s claims one by one and found that most held up:.

Sister will provide. True, French Gates said, citing AARP and the National Alliance for Caregiving. Among adults ages 55 to 64, women are nearly 40% more likely than men to care for an older relative, and nearly 40% more likely to provide constant care.

She’ll quit her job. “Mostly true,” at least the job part. Catalyst found that 42% of women who voluntarily left jobs in 2025 cited caregiving as the main reason.

Sources indicate that paid help is pricey. “True, unfortunately.” Fresh America puts the average home health aide at more than $6,600 a month, and 24-hour care can top $25,000.

Recent findings suggest that sister may break down. Also true. In the Caregiving in the U.S. 2025 report, nearly two-thirds of caregivers said they were under moderate or high emotional stress, and women felt the strain more than men did.

According to recent reports, “Free” is misleading. French Gates said Sister isn’t free; she’s the one paying for the care, in lost wages, stalled careers, thinner retirement savings and less time for her own life.

French Gates has made women’s health and caregiving central to her work since she stepped down as co-chair of the Gates Foundation in 2024.

According to recent reports, she left with $12.5 billion to put toward her work on behalf of women and families. Much of that work runs through Pivotal, the Seattle-area organization she founded to handle her philanthropy and investments. In June she pledged another $215 million for women’s health, which brought her giving in that area to more than $600 million over two years. The pledge included $10 million to the Menopause Society for better menopause care in the U.S.

The sketch has been shared widely across the country this week, in part because it lines up so closely with the research.

In its own analysis, The New York Times cited studies showing that daughters generally provide at least twice as much care to aging parents as sons do. The U.S., unlike many wealthy countries, offers no federal paid leave or long-term care insurance for family caregivers.

French Gates stated she welcomed the conversation.

“I’m so glad we’re talking about this, and laughing about it,” she wrote on Instagram. “We need more honesty about what caring for loved ones really takes and why so much of that work falls to women. But I’d love for the day to come when these jokes don’t pack quite the same punch — because we’ve made it so much easier to care for the people we love.”.
