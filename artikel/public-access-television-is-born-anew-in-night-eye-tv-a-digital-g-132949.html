Industry observers note that I am both a bit too young and a bit too very not American to have ever experienced public-access television, but it's something that's always fascinated me. For those too young to have even heard of it, or just not in the know, it essentially allowed anyone to broadcast their own shows through cable TV. A proto YouTube, if you will. It's obviously not what it was any more, given the advent of the internet, but in NIGHTEYE.TV, it may be making a digital, more interactive return.

Recent findings suggest that NIGHTEYE.TV is a strange little place. I call it a place, it's a fake TV floating in a Unity default texture void, lending it a Backroomsian vibe, but even if there's not much there to ground yourself in, the goggle box is enticing all the same. It beckons you to turn it on, a series of buttons all linking to different "channels." What awaits you isn't television, however. Instead you'll find games, mostly of the weirdo, freako, sicko variety, promptly setting the tone.

🌕NIGHT EYE TV’s initial form is live!🌑 Check out the site and follow our Indiegogo campaign at www.nighteye.tv![image or embed].

In its current incarnation, it's mostly just a collection of clips of these various art games, with direct links to them hidden away in a menu, but a good collection it is. You'll find Nathalie Lawhead's Everything is Going to Be OK, Jeremy Couillard's more recent Soul Injector Commando, and Taylor McCue's He Fucked The Girl Out Of Me. Some clips are longer than others, acting as sort of segments of playthroughs, but it all feels like an exciting evolution of what public-access television was.

The ultimate goal is for it to be a curated platform where you can buy a rotating selection of games. Most of the sale goes back to the original artist, and 15% gets put into a community pool to be split amongst all of the contributors, with the last 15% going towards keeping everything running. As it stands, that's not quite ready yet. You can help make it happen, though, as right now an Indiegogo campaign is running to help it all get fully operational. Worth chucking a few quid I reckon!
