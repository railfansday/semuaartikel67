Latest Update: DC online casino and sweepstakes ban bill gets Nov. 18 committee vote before Dec. 15 cutoff

🌐 Source: Readwrite • 📅 15 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (573 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • It has been revealed that the DC Council’s Committee on Human Services will mark up and vote on B26-0656 at 3 p.m.
  • The District’s online casino bill is moving again, with little time to spare.
  • Sources indicate that the calendar is tight.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that the DC Council’s Committee on Human Services will mark up and vote on B26-0656 at 3 p.m. on Nov. 18, the bill’s first movement since its only hearing on May 4. The bill would legalize online casinos and ban dual-currency sweepstakes casinos, and few legislative days remain this year.

The District’s online casino bill is moving again, with little time to spare.

Sources indicate that the calendar is tight. Council Period 26 closes at 11:59 a.m. on Jan. 2, 2027. Any permanent bill that has not received final reading by the Dec. 15 legislative meeting lapses at the end of the period and must start over in Council Period 27.

The committee’s makeup may help. Its members include the bill’s sponsor, Wendell Felder, along with Christina Henderson and Zachary Parker. The committee also oversees the Office of Lottery and Gaming, which would regulate the new market.

Industry observers note that felder stated in his introduction letter that the bill is meant to authorize, regulate and tax internet gaming in the District. If it passes, DC would join the small group of jurisdictions with legal online casinos.

Sources indicate that the bill would also ban dual-currency sweepstakes casinos, and it comes with enforcement tools. The Office of Lottery and Gaming could send cease-and-desist letters. The DC Attorney General could go to court and seize profits.

Sources indicate that launch would not be immediate. The introduced bill says internet gaming cannot start until the District’s chief financial officer certifies that the regulatory system is ready.

Recent findings suggest that the licensing terms favor operators already in the District. Suppliers would pay $50,000 to apply and $25,000 to renew. There is no cap on operator licenses, and each operator could run up to two skins.

It has been revealed that holders of a DC sports wagering operator license or another gaming license would get expedited review. The Office of Lottery and Gaming could rely on earlier background checks. BetMGM, Caesars, DraftKings, Fanatics and FanDuel all offer online sports betting in DC.

Recent findings suggest that industry support was visible at the May hearing. Caesars, DraftKings and BetMGM all backed the bill. Thomas R. Burnside, executive director of the Office of Lottery and Gaming, testified and attached proposed changes to the bill.

Sources indicate that the regulator also flagged a trade-off for its own business. Its director acknowledged that online casinos could eat into iLottery sales. He said online casino revenue would offset any losses.

Any amendments adopted at markup will show whether Burnside’s proposed changes made it into the committee version. Once the committee has voted on Nov. 18, the full Council’s remaining legislative meetings fall on Dec. 1 and Dec. 15.
