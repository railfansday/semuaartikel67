This is not your average “gifts for foodies” round-up. We know that you’ve probably been given a few too many wine glasses and cookbooks. “As a professional, I don’t want to be gifted introductory-level items that will ultimately go unused,” claims Storm Brunni, private dining director at Barolo Grill in Denver. That’s why, for our guide, we’ve consulted you, the hospitality pros, about what you actually would like to receive this year.

It has been revealed that new shoes for service. “I’m getting all of my favorite people the new high-top kitchen shoes by Chuck Taylor in leather anti-slip. They’re cool enough to complete your streetwear look, functional enough to wear in the dining room, kitchen, or work, and comfy enough to wear as a commuter.” —Jonathon Sawyer, chef at Kindling in Chicago.

An industry-favorite hand cream. “Between constant handwashing, citrus, chiles, and hot pans, hospitality hands take a beating. Add a heated neck massager to use on the couch after a midnight close, and you’ve basically given someone a spa day.” —Antelmo Ambrosio, executive chef at Casa Oaxaca in Fresh York City.

Something for the coffee nerds. “It’s definitely a splurge, but I buy myself the Onyx Coffee Advent Calendar every year. A lot of hospitality pros nerd out about coffee, and Onyx is one of the best roasters. They fill the calendar with individual portions of beans (enough for two people) with different origins, roasts, and processes every day. Everything is roasted on a schedule so that when you’re brewing each one, the coffee is at its peak that day. The calendar has some of their signature coffees, alongside one-offs that you’ll never be able to get again, and I’ve tried some of the best coffees of my life in past calendars.” —Katie Renshaw, owner of Bar Bambi in Chicago.

Industry observers note that an upgrade for kitchen organization. “I love this automatic tape dispenser. Gift it with a nice fresh box of Sharpies.” —Kevin Beary, beverage director of Gus’ Sip & Dip, Kitty’s Cosmopolitan Club, Three Dots and a Dash in Chicago.

The gift of longer-lasting produce. “Vejibag is a family-owned business, run by a small team of women who create 100 percent organic cotton bags designed to keep produce fresher for longer at home. They genuinely work and are an amazing addition to any kitchen.” —Lauren DeSteno, executive chef at Ai Fiori in Latest York City.

It has been revealed that A tool for operators who know there’s always something that needs fixing. “I was given a precision screwdriver from HOTO Tools and now I’m obsessed with the brand and their design aesthetic. So many things in the restaurants need little fixes all the time and the 3.6V Electric Screwdriver Kit Pro has all the bits you could need. I also love their rotary drill for polishing plateware with chips/scratches for work.” —Zach Engel, chef at Galit and Cafe Yaya in Chicago.

It has been revealed that gifts with a personal touch. “As a general manager some of the top staff requests I’ve received are for nice wine keys (it’s an even nicer touch if they’re engraved) and custom server books.” —John Schneekloth, general manager at Toro in Chicago.

Recent findings suggest that nice dishware is worthwhile at home, too. “I’d love to receive items from Ivo Angel, a beautiful ceramics manufacturer out of Italy. I am a huge fan of the splatter design and always want a pinch bowl; I can’t have too many. Also the olive bowl with an attached vessel for pits is perfect for hosting.” —Julia Jaksoc, chef and owner of Bar Roza, Cafe Roze and Roze Pony in Nashville.

The pepper mill that chefs swear by. “It’s one of those tools that gets used constantly, makes a real difference, and is something every cook or bartender would appreciate having.” —Marco Torres, Chef at Brass and The Tusk Bar in Latest York City.

Recent findings suggest that fun socks. “Being locked down in heavy black pants, unforgiving non-slip shoe leathers, and a stiff chef coat means you have to find your personality somewhere. A pair of loud, ridiculous socks underneath always makes me feel a little more human. I’ll never forget getting this pair of bright yellow Spam socks as a Christmas gift one year. Honestly, it’s one of the best presents I’ve ever unwrapped, and it brings a little joy each time I slip them on.” —Soo Ahn, chef/partner at Adalina Prime, Arla and Adalina Italian in Chicago.

Recent findings suggest that something to take the edge off. “For the industry pro who enjoys a little puff puff and a good nap (especially after a Saturday morning shift with lines out the door), you can’t go wrong with Tokin’ Jew rolling papers. We sell them at the deli because, come on, but they’re also a fantastic gift. And for the pipe people, their pickle pipes are another way to keep it kosher.” —Jake Schneider, chef and co-owner of Schneider Deli in Chicago.

Sources indicate that A beautiful book for recipe writing. “I would gift this brown leather journal cover from Shinola along with this 12-pack of pens. I think the notebook is a worthwhile splurge, and while I know the idea of a more expensive and elegant pen is both compelling and sexy, it is also impractical for when you’re on the go or as prone to losing nice pens as I am. These ones write fluidly and beautifully.” —Róbert LeBlanc, creative director and founder of LeBlanc + Smith in Fresh Orleans.

Sources indicate that the kitchen fit of your dreams. “I could wear this jumpsuit 24/7! It’s comfortable, easy to move around in, and perfect for those long days in the kitchen when I’m constantly running around and want something that looks as good as it feels.” —Andrea Belfiore, chef and founder of EVÈNTO in New York City.

A tool for foraging. “I love my ARS pruning shears. I take them pretty much everywhere when I’m out foraging, and they’re great for everything from cutting herbs to collecting branches and other ingredients for drinks. They’re one of those tools I use all the time.” —Danny Childs, co-owner of Field Day in Philadelphia.

According to recent reports, A trio of pastry chef staples. “On any given day, a pastry professional will produce dozens of recipes, and we need tools to measure mass/weight, time, and temperature. Even when we have our own, backups or better ones are always welcome! My top picks include: this scale, which has a pull-out screen when you’re scaling on to sheet trays or large bowls; this kitchen timer has four color-coded channels for timing multiple projects at once; and this thermometer, which is fast-reading, accurate, and has a magnet on the back for easy access on any magnetic surface.” —Orlando Soto, executive pastry chef of Le Bernardin in Newly introduced York City.

Recent findings suggest that the perfect vessel for an industry-favorite amaro (or 50/50 shot). Some of the best hospitality happens in an ounce and a half: a pour of Cynar for a regular who had a long week, a welcome taste for someone fresh at the bar. I’d recommend these little ceramic artichoke shot glasses to make that moment feel even more considered.” —Steven Waters, owner and operator of Fino in Denver.

Recent findings suggest that many knife options. Patrick Dooley of Harry’s Bar in Paris recommends Le Grand Tradi, which is sized between a paring and chef’s knife, making it a great workhorse option, while Nancy Santiago, beverage director at NYC’s IGC Hospitality, recommends a Japanese petty knife “for precise citrus work and garnish prep.” Amir Babayoff of Ophelia Lounge in NYC is partial to Tojiro’s chef knives.

According to recent reports, and knife storage and upkeep options. Schneekloth of Toro recommends a knife backpack, while Devin Denzer, chef at Atsumeru in Chicago, recommends a whetstone and ceramic sharpening rod, especially for newer line cooks.

Recent findings suggest that A thoughtful gift card. “Here are a few gifts that I have received that stood out to me: a hefty gift card to a sought-after restaurant with guaranteed reservations on a night off, which is such a good way to give someone a night out; a gift card to a shoe store that carries high-quality work shoes, like Red Wings, Dansko, or Blundstones; and a gift card to something wellness-related—my business partner Jesse got me three sessions at a body-stretching spa downtown.” —Phila Lorn, chef and co-owner of Mawn and Sao in Philadelphia.
