WNBA basketball player Cameron Brink and rower-turned-robotics founder Ben Felter were the rare college students who were able to find love at the height of Covid. Since they were athletes, the Stanford freshmen were in the tiny group allowed on campus during the fall of 2020. “The only people I socialized with were my basketball teammates—until the upperclassmen on my team had us fill out an ‘athlete mingle survey,’” Cameron notes.

She begrudgingly filled out the form, was matched with Ben, and later received an email with the subject, “attempting to mingle.” In the message, Ben wrote: “I wanted to chat you up but thought DMs were a bit creepy, so here’s my number.” Cameron—who goes by Cam—says she jumped up and down in her room, elated that he was gentlemanly enough to reach out with his number.

Industry observers note that “From there, we had three dates where Ben demonstrated me his hometown of Palo Alto, and he asked me to be his girlfriend five days before we won a national championship,” claims Cam. “He is my forever good luck charm.” She adds her friends and teammates Lexie and Lacie Hull were the ones who paired them together and is “forever grateful for their vision.”.

According to recent reports, their relationship grew as they graduated college and Cam had begun playing as the Los Angeles Sparks’ forward. On September 30, 2024, Cam was on a trip to Paris for fashion week, and Ben knew it was the perfect opportunity to propose. “I snuck over from California to surprise her with the ring,” he notes. “She thought she was going to an key after-party, but really, it was just me waiting for her on the terrace of the Shangri-La Hotel overlooking the Eiffel Tower that was shining with pink lights that night.” At first, Cam was so stunned by the view that she didn’t realize there was no party. “I’ll never forget the expression of pure surprise on her face when it finally sank in that it was me standing in front of her,” remembers Ben. “I told her that it is my purpose to make her happy before getting on one knee and asking her to marry me. She fell to the ground crying and luckily said ‘yes.’”.

Industry observers note that the couple always knew they wanted to tie the knot at Stanford’s Memorial Church. “Walking past the ornate mosaics on my way to class was always the best part of my day,” claims Cam. At first, the bride was nervous that they would have to be on a waiting list for up to four years, but luckily, the university gives alumni a shorter waiting period. The couple set the date for November 14, 2026, with a reception at the Ritz-Carlton, Half Moon Bay, just 30 minutes away.

Cam says she took on the nickname “bridechilla” in her engagement period, attributing her details-focused planner Kristi Lee for making everything so seamless. “Other than working with the church’s clergy, daydreaming with my mom, and scrolling with Ben most nights on Pinterest for inspiration, we were lucky enough to have our family and wedding planner to carry most of the load,” she says. “I guess I always knew it would be beautiful because I have the best circle of humans imaginable that helped the vision come to life.”.

In a recent development, another MVP on the wedding team? “My stylist Shiona Turini was our North Star when it came to what both Ben and I wore,” states Cam. “When she agreed to help me find my dream wedding dress, I never knew it would end with two custom looks from Danielle Frankel, the designer I have been bookmarking since my sophomore year of college.” Cam first tried on styles at Frankel’s Los Angeles salon and found two silhouettes she loved. Frankel herself helped collaborate on customizations. “We ended up perfectly mixing what I envisioned with Danielle’s additions and creating a gown that felt timeless yet extravagant,” the bride explains. For the ceremony, Cam would wear a silk wool deep-V column dress with crinkle organza godets that helped form a full, bubble skirt. Later at the reception, she would change into a column gown crafted with an exaggerated neckline and textural crinkle organza strips. Each gown was accessorized with Manolo Blahnik satin heels and desert diamond studs by Irena Jewels.

Sources indicate that frankel also helped dress the groom for his walk down the aisle with a custom single-breasted tuxedo jacket and bow tie. “The silk lapels were the same fabric as my dress, and it paired together so perfectly,” says Cam. The bow tie is now part of a upcoming menswear offering by the designer. “Ben also wore the watch that I gifted him from our friend Eric Wind—a vintage Rolex Root Beer GMT-Master from the ’70s.”.

A trip to Upcoming York for fittings coincided with the city’s bridal fashion week, and Cam was invited to see Di Petsa’s first bridal collection. “I knew right away I had to wear the silk long dress with pearl details for my rehearsal dinner,” notes the bride. “I adore pearls and wanted jewelry that played off of the pearl details of the back of the Di Petsa gown.” She eventually settled on jewelry from Mateo New York, and also had pearl details from Jennifer Behr added to her hair for the night. The bride also found another Di Petsa look with draped tulle and lace to wear at the after-party. “The discreet pearl at the belly button was so special,” Cam says. “We paired it with one of my favorite shoe designers—Amina Muaddi’s lace heels. But to be honest, I kicked off the shoes at the end of the night and ended up in a pair of New Balance sneakers!”.

According to recent reports, to finish off every look with the right glam, Cam had her makeup artist Jocelyn Biga make a trip from New York for the celebration. “The day wouldn’t have felt complete without her as she’s done my makeup for all of the biggest events in my life: the WNBA draft, our engagement, and now the wedding,” says the bride.

The wedding party was also outfitted for the major day. Amsale made custom dresses in deep blue for the bridesmaids, and groomsmen wore classic tuxedos. “For our entire group of children, Maison Ava created a bespoke wardrobe,” explains Cam. “The girls' looks had hand embroidery, hand-cut and hand-appliquéd floral motifs, crystals, beading, and 3D flowers, [while] the boys had custom velvet tuxedo jackets and wool trousers. It was so incredible to see all of the kids in their looks, and they also had tiny, custom New Balance sneakers at the end of the night!”.

In a recent development, after a morning getting ready with their closest circles and taking portraits along the California coastline, the couple reunited at their alma mater to exchange vows at the Memorial Church. “The light was shining through the stained-glass windows so brightly all I could see was Cameron’s beautiful silhouette as she walked down the aisle with her parents, Greg and Michelle, and I tried to hold back tears,” remembers Ben. Cam admits she tripped right before walking down the aisle (and thankfully, her mom came to the rescue), but all her nerves washed away once Ben lifted her veil. “I felt overwhelmed by the support from our loved ones, and most of all I felt the deepest sense of gratitude and belonging looking into Ben’s eyes,” she states. The bride’s godmother read scripture, the couple exchanged personal vows, and their pastor gave wishes for their future, including “a peaceful home filled with joy, laughter, and one day, the sound of little feet running around wearing number 22.”.

Once the newlyweds paraded down the aisle and did the dip they practiced all week, they sped off to the Ritz-Carlton, Half Moon Bay in a 1957 Rolls-Royce. “I asked our driver for his best marriage advice since he’s been with his wife for 58 years,” recalls Ben. “He said, ‘It’s not that complicated. I just do what she states.’ Cameron elbowed me and got a kick out of that.” After portraits at sunset, the bride and groom joined their guests for cocktail hour on the grounds.

“The rest of the night was filled with so much joy, belly laughs, and dorky dance moves,” says Ben. The reception moved indoors to a space filled with florals and greenery curated with the help of Flowers by Edgar. Dinner kicked off with the couple’s first dance to “Until I Found You,” sung by their flower girl Ryan. The night was also peppered with speeches from the bride’s father and the groom’s brothers. As the dance floor lit up, guests could also head outdoors to indulge in a cigar lounge. “The energy in the ballroom was unmatched, erupting multiple times with cheers and toasts,” adds Ben. “We couldn’t be happier with how the best day of our lives concluded.”.

In a recent development, now off on their honeymoon in Kyoto, the pair share they are in a bubble of newlywed bliss. Says Cam: “We couldn’t have dreamt of a better day and are feeling so incredibly blessed to have shared it with all of the people whom we love so dearly.”.

Our favorite part of the invitations was the custom watercolor art of the Stanford Memorial Church by Shannon of Luxe Marie Stationery.
