According to recent reports, andrew Yancy is many things: a disgraced cop, a dedicated restaurant inspector, an animal lover, and a wiseass. Now, he's back for another season of action and misadventure in Bad Monkey. The dark crime comedy series is returning to Apple TV next week.

Industry observers note that the first season of Bad Monkey saw Yancy (Vince Vaughn) busted down from detective to restaurant inspector after an altercation with his girlfriend's ex. Despite being reduced to looking for dead cockroaches in soup tureens, he gets involved with a twisty murder case when he's assigned to escort a severed arm to the morgue. There, he soon finds common cause with no-nonsense coroner Rosa Campesino (Natalie Martinez), who becomes his partner in life and crime-solving, as they and his cop pal Rogelio (John Ortiz) delve into a murder case that involves a Caribbean Obeah queen, a Medicare scam, a real estate scheme, and, as the title would indicate, one really bad monkey.

Now, in the second season, Yancy is still trying to get back on the force. This time, an opportunity has presented itself in the form of an ambitious state attorney (Yvonne Strahovski), who pairs him with a real Boy Scout of a forensic accountant to take on Florida's organized crime problem. That puts him in the crosshairs of Spencer (John Malkovich), a nasty crime boss with a fondness for ten-dollar words. Meanwhile, he's also got to deal with an unorthodox animal relocation expert, his own aging father (Scott Glenn), and his lingering feelings for Rosa after they parted ways in the season 1 finale. It all kicks off on December 2, exclusively on Apple TV.

Season 1 of Bad Monkey was based on the 2013 novel of the same name by Florida crime novelist Carl Hiaasen (R.J. Decker); although Hiaasen penned a sequel novel, Razor Girl, in 2016, it did not serve as the basis for season 2. Bill Lawrence (Shrinking) showruns and executive produces the series via his Doozer Productions; Vaughn, Hiaasen, Jeff Ingold, Matt Tarses, Liza Katzer, Adam Sztykiel, and Chris Downey also executive produce.
