In a recent development, siemens revealed on November 24, 2026 an expanded automation portfolio for AI-powered autonomous production at SPS 2026 in Nuremberg, including a goods-unpacking robot under development and AI agents for planning and engineering. Siemens expects the planner to reduce manual planning effort by 70%.

The portfolio is built around goal-based automation: manufacturers specify the outcome they want, and industrial AI determines how to achieve it or carries out the work through AI agents. “The next productivity leap will come from automating tasks that were impossible to automate before,” said Rainer Brehm, president of automation at Siemens.

Recent findings suggest that the shop-floor example is at Siemens’ Electronics Factory Erlangen, where the manufacturer is developing an AI-based robot application to unpack incoming goods. Electronics deliveries vary so widely in packaging type and size that fixed robot programs cannot handle them, leaving the work largely manual. The application is designed to open cartons and remove component reels for further processing.

Cameras capture the workspace in real time, while AI-based robot foundation models interpret the scene and determine the required actions. Siemens provides the automation and control systems, industrial PCs, software orchestration, and manufacturing expertise drawn from its own facilities. That combination addresses the central limitation of a fixed program: the robot must adjust its steps to each delivery instead of repeating one predetermined sequence.

The Autonomous Production Planner is being introduced within Intelligence Center X, Siemens’ industrial AI orchestration software. Several AI agents collaborate to create production schedules and optimize them against defined key performance indicators. Each schedule is checked in a physics-based digital twin before release to production, putting a simulation test between an agent-generated plan and factory execution. Siemens also expects the planner to lower costs by up to 20%.

It has been revealed that the Eigen Engineering Agent, connected to the TIA Portal engineering software, can now adapt automation code and verify it through simulation. Creating safety configurations remains a future capability. For process industries, the agent is now available with SIMATIC PCS 7, Siemens’ distributed control system, where it analyzes existing control logic, checks or creates Continuous Function Charts, and automatically creates control modules from engineering data. Siemens says these functions can accelerate engineering and help companies apply expertise across projects.

It has been revealed that during production, the Industrial AI Suite connects AI models running on Industrial Edge to SIMATIC S7 controllers through PROFINET. This enables real-time inference within machine automation and supports uses such as AI-based motion control. Production data can also be used to retrain, validate, and redeploy models on-premises, creating a closed loop from deployment and operation to continuing model improvement.

Industry observers note that standardized device information supplies another layer. Siemens is making Asset Administration Shell data available for more than 120,000 hardware products, allowing software tools and AI-enabled workflows to access and process product information directly. The standardized data also supports digital engineering and digital-twin workflows.

Security accompanies that expansion of AI into control systems. Siemens is progressively preparing relevant industrial products for applicable requirements of the EU Cyber Resilience Act, which sets cybersecurity requirements relevant to its industrial products and customers’ machines. It is also supporting machine builders as they prepare their machines for requirements applicable from December 11, 2027.

According to recent reports, the portfolio’s near-term significance is the connection between AI-generated decisions and the industrial systems that validate and execute them. Its current limits are also clear: the Erlangen unpacking application remains under development, and the planner’s benefits are expectations, making simulation validation, accessible device data, and cybersecurity central to the move toward more autonomous production.
