Florian Wirtz was quick to shut down any suggestion that Liverpool were spending too much time ‘thinking about’ Arsenal after their 4-0 win over Manchester United.

Mauricio Pochettino’s reign got off to a nightmare start in what was ultimately a mismatch between the two bitter rivals, with the Red Devils proving their own worst enemy and giving themselves a mountain to climb early on at Anfield.

Some calamitous defending from Matthijs de Ligt and Leny Yoro led to Dominik Szoboszlai’s tenth-minute breakthrough and saw the latter shown a straight red card by referee Samuel Barrott, a decision that only compounded the visitors’ woes.

The impressive Wirtz doubled Liverpool’s advantage with an audacious finish just three minutes later to send the home crowd into raptures, before a pair of second-half goals from Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz completed the rout.

Having cut a frustrated figure on the touchline for the most part, Pochettino made it clear he was less than impressed by the officiating on show as he faced the media shortly after the final whistle on Merseyside.

The Argentinian will no doubt be desperate to turn the tide when United return to Premier League action at home to Brentford in six days’ time, following a midweek trip to Sporting.

The result ensured Pochettino’s men finished the weekend just one point better off than rock-bottom Crystal Palace, with the Old Trafford giants now leading Europe’s top five leagues for most goals conceded (34 in all competitions).

Liverpool, meanwhile, moved four points clear of fifth-placed Bournemouth and just one point behind Arsenal’s two nearest rivals, Manchester City and Brighton, after ten games.

‘Really good. The atmosphere after the game was so good. We could enjoy it and it was just a nice afternoon,’ Wirtz – named man of the match – said of the victory in his post-match interview with Sky Sports.

‘I feel very good but it’s also because the team develops and gets better.

‘Just being part of this, you also get better, so I’m just enjoying that.’.

Asked if he was perhaps benefitting from being coached by Andoni Iraola and Jurgen Klopp at club and international level, Wirtz replied: ‘Both are very good coaches and I enjoy playing under both.

‘They both bring different qualities and I’m just happy and can be happy to have two of these coaches.’.

Victory in next Sunday’s Merseyside derby at Everton would see Liverpool move to within four points of Arsenal, who host Manchester City later that same afternoon.

When asked if the 4-0 thumping of United had taken Liverpool to ‘another level’, Wirtz replied: ‘Yeah, it was definitely vital today, also for the table situation.

‘I think a lot of teams on top of the table, they didn’t win this weekend so it’s good that we could use the chance.

‘The red card influenced the game a bit of course but we take that, we earned it because of the run of Alex [Isak] so yeah, very good.’.

Wirtz was then asked if he was ‘thinking about’ Arsenal and possibility of winning the league this season, and his answer was unequivocal.

It came after the Gunners – the reigning Premier League champions – surrendered a first-half lead to drop points away to Newcastle.

‘Uh, I wasn’t talking about Arsenal… all the teams, I don’t know, from one to three,’ Wirtz responded.

‘So we knew, we didn’t speak so much about it, but I think everybody knew it was a significant chance to make some points.

‘I’m happy we used this chance.’.
