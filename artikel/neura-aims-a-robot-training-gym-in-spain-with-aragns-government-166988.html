Representatives of NEURA Robotics, the Government of Aragón and Costa Group sign the memorandum of understanding.

In a recent development, german robotics firm NEURA Robotics has signed a memorandum of understanding with the Government of Aragón and agri-food group Costa Group to open a NEURA Gym in Aragón by mid-2027. The partners plan to invest in the double-digit millions of euros, with NEURA leading, and to add a zone for testing robots in real settings.

According to recent reports, german robotics tech giant NEURA Robotics plans to open a robot training centre in Aragón, Spain, by mid-2027, it said on Thursday. It has signed a memorandum of understanding with the regional government and Costa Group, an Aragonese meat and agri-food group.

The partners proposal to invest in the “double-digit millions of euros”, with NEURA taking the lead. They did not give an exact figure.

The site will be one of NEURA’s Gyms, where companies train robots on real tasks before putting them to work. Firms will bring their own processes, test them on different robots and check how well they perform.

Through NEURA’s Neuraverse platform, companies can keep the robot skills they develop private or license them to others for a fee.

“A company that develops a valuable skill should benefit when others use it,” stated David Reger, NEURA’s founder and chief executive.

Sources indicate that costa Group will supply real problems from its farms and factories. The first projects will aim to cut physically demanding and repetitive work, before moving into logistics, cars and manufacturing. The University of Zaragoza will add research and train staff.

The partners also plan a “Physical AI Model Zone”, where robots can be tested in real working settings. Its scope and locations are still to be agreed, and any testing in public areas will need permits.

NEURA raised up to $1.4B in June, partly to expand its Gyms. It has since bought two companies. In Spain, Barcelona’s THEKER raised €73M in June for factory robots that learn on the job.
