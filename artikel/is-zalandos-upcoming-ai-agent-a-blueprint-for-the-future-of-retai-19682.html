Is Zalando’s Upcoming AI Agent a Blueprint for the Future of Retail?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

What happens when a multibrand fashion retailer starts selling products it doesn’t stock? European e-commerce giant Zalando is joining the AI shopping agent race with aims to let shoppers buy from other retailers through its platform. Its latest offering, Zalando Extended, will use AI agents to complete purchases on customers’ behalf, potentially transforming Zalando into a shopping gateway beyond its own catalog.

Zalando is testing the service with premium home and living retailer Westwing. Its products already appear in Zalando’s search and browsing results, but shoppers currently have to click through to Westwing’s website to buy them. From 2027, Zalando’s AI agent will handle those purchases on customers’ behalf, allowing them to check out without leaving the Zalando app.

Retailers can join through a relatively simple integration, providing something similar to an affiliate product feed and entering into an additional agreement with Zalando. The company has not disclosed what commission or fee, if any, it will earn from these transactions. It's a notable move for a retailer that has spent years building its own assortment and relationships with thousands of brands, from Gucci to Burberry and Jacquemus. But as the likes of OpenAI, Google and Meta race to make general-purpose personal AI agents, the battle is increasingly over who controls the customer relationship, rather than simply who stocks the product.

According to recent reports, zalando’s AI assistant currently helps shoppers purchase from the multibrand retailer’s product catalogue. Photo: Courtesy of Zalando.

It has been revealed that zalando’s response is to position itself on both sides of that coin. Its three-pronged strategy aims to make its own platform the first stop for fashion discovery, expand its assortment through external retailers and also reach shoppers through third-party AI agents. It aims to offer checkout within Google’s AI Mode and Gemini app when its agentic commerce releases in Europe, and has tested advertising inside ChatGPT in the UK.

Sources indicate that this also places Zalando in competition with specialist AI shopping platforms such as Daydream, which also launched a upcoming AI shopping agent this week. Both are betting that fashion requires expertise beyond that of general-purpose AI agents. While Daydream operates in the US and Zalando in Europe, the latter enters the race with a significant advantage in scale.

But for rival luxury and multibrand fashion retailers, Zalando’s AI strategy raises a bigger question: is the future of fashion e-commerce about selling your own assortment, or becoming the gateway to everyone else’s?

At the Web Summit conference in Lisbon, Vogue Business sat down with chief technology officer Graeme Smith to discuss the company’s ambitions and who stands to own the customer relationship in the age of AI agents.

Vogue: Why allow Zalando customers to buy products from other retailers through Zalando Extended?

Smith: We’ve got over 63 million active customers at Zalando, and through our Lifestyle AI [Zalando’s umbrella term for its AI-powered personalization technology], we’ve really got to know their preferences — the brands they like, the price points they shop at, the brand values like sustainability they look for, and how their needs change over time. We can use all this to create a personalized experience that can even become proactive.

So we thought, why limit ourselves? We already have our wholesale and partner platforms, but with AI agents, we’ve found a really lightweight way to bring in other retailers whose products you wouldn’t normally find on Zalando.

Our first partner is Westwing. Today, you can discover its products on Zalando and click through to buy them on Westwing. But early next year, you’ll be able to buy that sofa or lamp directly on Zalando, using your existing account and payment details, without leaving the app. We’ll buy it on your behalf.

Imagine how much more assortment across fashion and lifestyle we could offer this way, without stocking it ourselves. We can then build that into our personalization, learning more about customers’ interests beyond our existing catalog and helping them discover more products.

Industry observers note that vogue: With general-purpose AI agents increasingly capable of shopping on consumers’ behalf, are you competing directly with the likes of OpenAI, Google and Meta? Why would shoppers choose Zalando instead?

It has been revealed that people are going to use a variety of agents, but our mission is always to make Zalando the best place to start the fashion and lifestyle shopping journey. That comes down to the customer experience we can provide that others can’t, which starts with data.

We’ve been using AI for over 15 years, including for personalized shopping recommendations, and we’ve invested heavily in size and fit. We get tens of thousands of body measurements a week, and we have so much data from customer returns about which items run small or large. So we’ve got over 10,000 size charts, and we’ve put AI on top of that. No third-party agent has got that. So if you care about getting the fit right the first time, Zalando’s agent will be the place to come.

We’ve also invested in recommending whole outfits based on our understanding of trends, how people shop for different occasions and years of customer behavior at scale.

Vogue: How quickly do you expect Zalando Extended to grow? Are you planning to onboard lots more retailers in the coming months, or is this more of a test-and-learn phase?

It’s a little bit of both. We’re testing and learning, but it’s not a particularly complex thing we’re doing, and we’re very excited by what we’ve seen so far. Assuming it’s successful, this has the potential to scale very fast because the integration is so lightweight.

Most of these retailers already produce an affiliate feed, which is essentially what we need. We can make the sign-up and operations very simple for them, and it’s very light for us too. So there’s potential to scale this massively.

Recent findings suggest that vogue: Trust is fundamental to AI agent adoption, and attitudes vary between markets. Why would consumers trust Zalando Assistant more than a general-purpose AI agent?

It has been revealed that zalando is a brand that people trust, and we’ve worked very hard to earn that trust. The company has just turned 18, and we’ve built that trust through how we look after the customer experience, including how we handle their data. We’re a European company, so the data we hold is subject to high European standards for data protection.

We’ve seen customers enthusiastically adopt Zalando Assistant. Interestingly, questions about what we know about them haven’t been a big part of the conversation — people have just started using it. We’ve had 22 million customers engage with Zalando Assistant this year, including 13 million in the last quarter.

Recent findings suggest that customers are twice as likely to buy something in a session where they use Zalando Assistant compared with one where they don’t. And of course, we have all the consent mechanisms required in Europe. Customers can also request that their data be deleted.

According to recent reports, graeme Smith, Zalando’s CTO. Photo: Courtesy of Zalando.

Vogue: Is there a risk that Zalando could lose its direct relationship with customers by bringing other retailers onto its platform?

It has been revealed that no, we don’t think of it like that. We want people to come to Zalando more and more, and for it to be the place where they start their shopping journey. We’ve got 70% organic traffic, which is very high, and we work hard to maintain that.

We think we can do that by building really great experiences tailored to fashion and lifestyle shopping. Bringing in more retailers actually gives people more reasons to come to Zalando, because they know they can access a wider variety of products. They can buy a sofa on Zalando, as well as everything else. So we think this could actually drive more traffic.

Now, could someone go directly to Westwing to buy their next sofa? Of course, and that’s fine. We don’t sell sofas on Zalando. What matters is that we’re the best place to discover fashion and lifestyle products, wherever they come from.

Vogue: You’re also piloting advertising in ChatGPT and preparing to participate in Google’s Universal Commerce Protocol. How does that fit with your ambition to make Zalando the starting point for shopping?

There are three strands to our strategy. The first is getting people to keep coming back to Zalando as the best place to start their shopping journey. The second is what we call inbound agentic commerce, and the third is Zalando Extended, which allows us to massively expand the assortment available through outbound agentic commerce.

It has been revealed that inbound agentic commerce is what most people talk about when they discuss AI shopping agents. People are using ChatGPT, Google’s AI-powered search and other platforms. We had to decide early on how we felt about that, and we saw it as an opportunity. Our customers are going to use these services anyway, so we want Zalando to be there and meet them where they are. We also want to reach latest customers.

We’ve become very good at generative engine optimisation (GEO) — we’re the number one fashion destination in Europe referred to by AI agents. But we haven’t stopped at organic visibility. We’ve also piloted advertising inside ChatGPT in the UK, and we’re now looking at the results and deciding where to go next.

According to recent reports, with Google, it’s even more interesting. When Google launches agentic commerce in Europe, Zalando will be there from the start. Customers will be able to discover Zalando products and even check out within Google if they want to.

But they’ll still need a Zalando account. That’s very key to us. We want to engage with these AI platforms on our own terms and maintain the customer relationship.

According to recent reports, vogue: Fashion shopping is often emotional and highly considered. Do you think consumers will really want to complete purchases through AI agents, rather than visiting a retailer’s website?

In a recent development, we don’t know how this is going to play out. I’m very excited by it, but fashion is very personal and emotional.

What we see is that when customers arrive at Zalando from another channel, whether that’s an AI agent, Instagram or TikTok, they often don’t buy the item they landed on. They explore three or four more products, look at similar items and browse around before making a purchase.

In a recent development, it’s another signal that fashion is different, it’s a high-consideration purchase. So if I had to bet — and it’s always difficult to make predictions — I think people will use agents to research products, but they’ll still want to click through to the retailer, like Zalando, to ask: is this item quite right for me? What more can I learn about it? What similar products are available? And then maybe they’ll buy from there, but let’s see.
