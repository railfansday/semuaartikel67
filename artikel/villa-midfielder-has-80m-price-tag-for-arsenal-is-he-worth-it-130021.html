It has been revealed that arsenal transfer news: Aston Villa do not regard Boubacar Kamara as available, even with Arsenal interested, according to CaughtOffside. The report says an offer of around £80m could change that stance, although Villa negotiate from strength because the 26-year-old’s contract runs until 2030.

Recent findings suggest that january looks highly unlikely. Villa have already lost Amadou Onana to a torn ACL suffered at the World Cup, and the Belgian is not expected back until around April. Unai Emery cannot lose a second holding midfielder in the middle of a season. The summer is different. CaughtOffside says Villa could entertain a significant bid then, provided they can reinvest the money in a proper replacement and strengthen the squad around him.

In a recent development, TBR Football reported in July 2025 that he sat on Arsenal’s long list of midfield targets, and his numbers explain why. Kamara has started all 10 of his Premier League games this season, completing 86.3% of his passes, including 83 line-breaking balls, while making 28 tackles and 40 recoveries. He wins 56% of his duels and can play at centre-back, which would give Mikel Arteta cover in two positions.

The weaknesses are just as clear. He has no goals this season, only three shots and an xG of 0.14, and he has committed 20 fouls and conceded a penalty. He also missed the closing weeks of 2024/25 through injury.

Industry observers note that the role he would play is obvious: a ball-playing six next to Declan Rice. Martín Zubimendi occupies it now, and Arsenal lead the league with 27 points from 10 games, six clear of Manchester City, with seven clean sheets.

No, not at that price. Arsenal spent £75m on Bruno Guimarães in August, and Arteta still has Rice, Zubimendi, Mikel Merino, Martin Ødegaard and Myles Lewis-Skelly competing for the same central spaces.

FotMob values him at €49m, roughly £43m, so Villa would be asking close to double his market value for a player who turns 27 this month and offers little in the final third. Villa’s insistence on reinvesting the fee also hands them the leverage, which means Arsenal would pay a premium on a seller’s timetable.

Recent findings suggest that the only sensible trigger is a departure, perhaps Zubimendi or the 30-year-old Merino, leaving Arsenal short of a proven Premier League six. Kamara is a better holding midfielder than most of the market, but a title-leading side already has that job covered, and £80m would buy cover rather than improvement.
