In a recent development, this sounds like an idiotic suggestion at first, right? Surely all you need to do is plug the HDMI cable that came with your Series X into your television, and you'll be ready to enjoy the best Xbox games, no? It's not always that simple.

According to recent reports, though your current TV may support HDMI 2.1, the input that ensures the Series X can display its maximum resolution and framerate, that doesn't mean all of your TV's ports are 2.1 compliant. This latest high-def standard really matters, as without it, cutting-edge visual features like Variable Refresh Rate are off the table.

It's important to know the specifics of your current TV. For instance, if it's an aging model that only supports HDMI 2.0, you can stop reading this article now, and content yourself with a top output of 1440p/120 fps or 4K/60 fps from your Series X. However, if you own a semi-modern budget flatscreen, there's a good chance only one or two of its ports will be compatible with HDMI 2.1, enabling 4K/120 fps and framerate-smoothing VRR from the Xbox.

Let's get to know your TV's HDMI sockets a little better, discuss whether you should upgrade the cable that came in the box with your Series X, and go over why HDMI 2.1 is so important for current-gen gaming.

But remember, even with a newish TV, there's no guarantee that all of its ports are HDMI 2.1 compatible.

Most modern high-end sets will have three to four HDMI 2.1 ports total, so if you've bought a premium OLED or MicroLED panel in the last couple of years, it's likely that all of its HDMI inputs will comply. If your set is a good few years old and was one of the first models to support HDMI 2.1 features, there's a decent chance that only one of its ports can take advantage of the Xbox Series X's 4K/120 max output.
