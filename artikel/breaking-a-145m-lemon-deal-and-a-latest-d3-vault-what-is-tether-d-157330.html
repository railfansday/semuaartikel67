Stablecoin giant Tether [USDT] has been making some moves lately. Here’s the rundown.

It has been revealed that tether has backed Argentine fintech app Lemon, with the former not putting a number on it when it revealed the deal. Lemon did, however, saying that the package is worth $14.5 million.

According to Lemon CEO Marcelo Cavazzoli, only $5 million of that is an actual investment in the tech giant’s shares. The other $9.5 million is an incentive initiative linked to products the two will build together.

In a recent development, there’s a lot that’s unclear right now though. Neither side has shared Lemon’s valuation or how big a stake Tether is getting. There’s also no word on how or when the $9.5 million gets paid out. Tether is after more consumers for USDT and Tether Gold [XAUT] across Latin America. Lemon gives it a decent shot at that. The app is said to have 4.5 million verified users, and its main balance runs on USDT.

Worth noting, however, that Lemon’s not the only home for Tether this month.

Recent findings suggest that on the 9th, D3 debuted a USD vault for Tether’s stablecoin. Customers can deposit USDT, keep an eye on their position, and withdraw without leaving the platform.

Sentora built the system using its Earn OS and Upshift tools, which decide where deposited funds go. Money moves with the market, within a set of risk rules. It is said that the setup has already handled more than $2 billion.

Sources indicate that domain buyers usually come in, make a deal, and leave. Earning on stablecoins gives them a reason to keep their money on the platform.

Industry observers note that just don’t mistake automated for guaranteed. Returns depend on the lending markets the vault uses. So, customers are still trusting a whole stack of infrastructure with their dollars.
