It has been revealed that the uncertain 2026-27 Major League Baseball offseason is fully underway. The ongoing CBA negotiations and the near-certainty of an owner-implemented lockout on Dec. 2 loom over all, and it may indeed mean a flurry of trades and free-agent signings leading up to that date. That was certainly the case leading up to the last lockout of 2021-22, and this time around we've already seen some leading examples.

Thanks to that prospect of a hastened pace, offseason rumors are flowing. Speaking of which, we'll be here each day to round up what's out there. You can find Friday's supply just below.

The buzz at the GM Meetings last week was that the Mets offered top free agent Tarik Skubal a nine-year contract worth almost $400 million, reports MLB Network Radio. If the deal is worth at least $390 million, it would set upcoming records for total guarantee and average annual value given to a pitcher. Here are the current pitcher contract records:.

It has been revealed that 1. Yoshinobu Yamamoto, Dodgers: $325M.

2. Justin Verlander, Mets: $43.3M.

3. Stephen Strasburg, Nationals: $245M.

3. Zack Wheeler, Phillies: $42M.

Sources indicate that 4. Framber Valdez, Tigers: $38.3M.

Given the player and the deep-pocketed teams involved in the bidding (Blue Jays, Dodgers, Mets, etc.), Skubal is well-positioned to shatter Yamamoto's record. He could beat Scherzer's and Verlander's record as well. The expected lockout will begin after the collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on Dec. 1. Skubal is likely to sign before then.

The Diamondbacks are set to acquire righty Johan Oviedo from the Red Sox for minor-league lefty Luke Dotson and a player to be named later, reports the Arizona Republic. Boston had four pitchers for the No. 5 starter's spot (Oviedo, Brayan Bello, Kutter Crawford, Tanner Houck) and always seemed likely to trade one to ease the logjam.

Sources indicate that acquired from the Pirates last offseason, Oviedo made just one ineffective appearance around elbow trouble in 2026. He returned from Tommy John surgery in 2025 and looked excellent, and Arizona will hope Oviedo rediscovers that magic as he gets further away from his injuries. Dotson is not a top prospect. It's a low-cost flier for the D-backs.

The Tigers have signed right-hander Troy Melton to a six-year extension worth $46.5 million, reports ESPN. There is a club option for a seventh year and escalators can push the total guarantee to $80 million. Melton still had five years of team control. The upcoming extension buys out his five team control years and one free agent year with an option for another free agent year.

Sources indicate that melton, 26 next month, missed the start of the season with elbow inflammation and finished the season on the injured list with more elbow woes. Between injuries, he made 20 starts with a sub-3.00 ERA with strong under-the-hood numbers. The Tigers know him better than anyone and are obviously comfortable with Melton's medicals to lock him up long-term.

The Newly introduced York Yankees have agreed to a two-year contract with veteran right-hander Jonathan Loáisiga, ESPN reports. The deal also includes a club option for 2029 and is worth roughly $18 million, according to MLB.com.

In a recent development, loáisiga, 32, is coming off that strong season above for the Arizona Diamondbacks, and for his career boasts an ERA+ of 128 across parts of nine MLB seasons. He's worked in relief for almost all of that span, and eight of those nine seasons came with the Yankees.

According to recent reports, newly introduced York Yankees general manager Brian Cashman was recently asked if he'd be willing to play Spencer Jones in center field. In response, he told media, including SNY, this:.

"I would be comfortable with that. At the same time Jasson Domínguez is pounding the ball in winter ball. The entire industry is noticing that based on the phone calls I'm getting.".

The 23-year-old Domínguez still has significant-time power potential, and defensively he's best suited to center field. For his career in the Bronx, he has an OPS+ of 93, but his 751 plate appearances have been doled out erratically across four seasons. As things stand now, he'll once again be subject to a playing-time crunch. Aaron Judge and Cody Bellinger are installed at the outfield corners, and Giancarlo Stanton, if healthy, should get much of the playing time at DH. That leaves center field for Jones or Domínguez. Other teams no doubt see that bottleneck and are seeing if Cashman and the Yankees are willing to relieve it through trade.

Lefty ace and two-time American League Cy Young winner Tarik Skubal remains far and away the top talent on the free-agent market. While the New York Mets and incumbent Los Angeles Dodgers seem like the favorites to secure his services, the San Diego Padres also have significant interest in him. That interest took the next step on Thursday with an in-person meeting, The Athletic reports. The Padres are the fourth team to meet with Skubal, who turned 30 on Friday. Previously, the Dodgers, Mets, and Toronto Blue Jays met with him.

The Padres have a pronounced need in the rotation, especially if free-agent right-hander Michael King signs elsewhere. Last winter, they also lost Dylan Cease to the Blue Jays via free agency.

The Padres and left-handed platoon hitter Gavin Sheets have agreed to a three-year, $11 million contract extension that includes two opt-outs, FanSided reports. Sheets, who'll turn 31 in April, had been slated for free agency after the upcoming season. Instead, he and the Padres will now be potentially committed through the 2029 season, depending on whether Sheets exercises any of his opt-outs. While he's not much of a hitter for average, he does draw walks and hit for power. In two seasons with San Diego, he's tallied 36 home runs and 44 doubles in 855 at-bats while seeing DH, first base, and the outfield corners.

The New York Mets have agreed to a three-year, $12 million deal with right-handed reliever Darwinzon Hernández, reports José F. Rivera.

Hernández, who turns 30 in December, hasn't pitched in MLB since 2022 and instead has revived his career with four strong seasons in Japan. Over that span for Softbank of the NPB, he's put up an ERA of 2.46 in 131 ⅔ innings with 168 strikeouts and 42 unintentional walks. Prior to rebuilding himself in Japan, Hernández had a 5.06 ERA and a 1.82 K/BB ratio over parts of four seasons with the Boston Red Sox.
