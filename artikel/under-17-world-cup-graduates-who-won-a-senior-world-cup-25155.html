The FIFA U-17 World Cup has long been a shop window for football's next generation, but going from teenage promise to a senior world champion is another matter entirely.

Sources indicate that since the first edition in 1985, just 25 players have been named in a U-17 World Cup squad and gone on to lift the real thing. Some starred as teenagers, others barely registered, and a handful suffered heartbreak at youth level before finding redemption on the biggest stage.

Here is every U-17 World Cup graduate to become a world champion.

Alessandro Del Piero (1991), Gianluigi Buffon (1993), Francesco Totti (1993).

Iker Casillas, Xavi (1997), Pepe Reina (1999), Andrés Iniesta, Fernando Torres (2001), Cesc Fàbregas, David Silva (2003).

Roman Weidenfeller (1997), Toni Kroos (2007), Mario Götze, Shkodran Mustafi (2009).

Emiliano Martínez, Nicolás Tagliafico (2009), Exequiel Palacios (2015).

Aymeric Laporte (2011, for France), Ferran Torres, Eric García (2017), Pedri (2019), Pau Cubarsí (2023).

Emmanuel Petit was the first U-17 graduate to become a world champion. He represented France in Canada in 1987, when the competition was still an under-16 tournament, and Les Bleus finished second in a group containing Brazil, Australia and Saudi Arabia before bowing out in the quarter-finals.

Eleven years later, the Arsenal midfielder was integral to Aimé Jacquet's side on home soil. His first international goal was the winner in a 2-1 group-stage victory over Denmark, and he saved the best until last in the final, supplying the corner for Zinedine Zidane's opener before sealing a 3-0 win over Brazil in stoppage time.

Ronaldinho, one of the greatest dribblers in football history, remains the only man to win both tournaments. Brazil swept through their group in Egypt in 1997, and the teenage playmaker converted a late penalty in a 4-0 semi-final rout of Germany before the Seleção beat Ghana in the final.

Five years on, he completed the 'Three Rs' alongside Ronaldo and Rivaldo. He scored from the spot against China, but his defining moment came in the quarter-final against England, a looping 50th-minute free-kick over David Seaman that settled a 2-1 win. He was sent off seven minutes later and missed the semi-final, but returned as Brazil beat Germany in the final.

Recent findings suggest that italy's 2006 triumph was built on two forgettable youth campaigns. Alessandro Del Piero was part of the host nation's squad in 1991 and scored in a 2-2 draw with China, but Italy failed to escape their group.

Two years later in Japan, a 15-year-old Buffon and Totti fared even worse, finishing bottom of their group with Totti scoring Italy's only goal of the tournament, against Mexico.

In a recent development, all three delivered in Germany. Gianluigi Buffon, one of the best goalkeepers of all time, conceded just twice in seven matches, Francesco Totti fired a 95th-minute penalty to knock out Australia in the last 16, and Del Piero sealed the extra-time semi-final win over the hosts before converting Italy's fourth spot-kick in the final shootout against France.

No nation has drawn more from the tournament than Spain. Iker Casillas and Xavi helped La Rojita finish third in 1997, Jose Reina kept goal in 1999, and Iniesta and Fernando Torres were in the side that exited at the group stage in 2001.

Industry observers note that then came the nearly men of 2003. Cesc Fàbregas won the Golden Ball and finished joint top scorer with five goals, playing alongside David Silva, but Spain lost the final 1-0 to Brazil.

According to recent reports, redemption arrived in Johannesburg as Spain's young talents grew into world-beaters, as they dominated international for years, putting together one of the longest unbeaten runs in international football and winning Euro 2008 and 2012, with the World Cup sandwiched in between those two triumphs. Casillas claimed the Golden Glove after twice denying Arjen Robben in the final, and in the 116th minute substitute Fàbregas teed up Iniesta, one of the greatest midfielders in football history, for the winner.

Toni Kroos confirmed himself in South Korea in 2007, captaining Germany to third place and collecting the Golden Ball after scoring five goals. Mario Götze and Shkodran Mustafi followed in Nigeria two years later, where Germany fell to eventual champions Switzerland in the knockout rounds, while Roman Weidenfeller had been part of the side that finished fourth back in 1997.

In Brazil, Kroos was imperious, scoring twice in the 7-1 semi-final demolition of the hosts and Götze came off the bench to volley the 113th-minute winner in the final. Mustafi's tournament ended when he tore a thigh muscle against Algeria in the last 16, and Weidenfeller went unused as Manuel Neuer's back-up.

Benjamin Mendy made three appearances at the 2011 tournament in Mexico, where a France squad also containing future Spain centre-back Aymeric Laporte reached the quarter-finals.

Industry observers note that his contribution in Russia was modest. After sitting out the opening two group games, the left-back came on in the 50th minute of a goalless draw with Denmark, and those 40 minutes were his only action as France went on to win the tournament.

In a recent development, emiliano Martínez and Nicolás Tagliafico were in the Argentina squad that met Götze and Mustafi's Germany in the group stage in Nigeria in 2009. Götze put the Germans ahead inside eight minutes, but Argentina hit back in the second half to win 2-1, an early meeting between two groups of future world champions. Exequiel Palacios had a tougher introduction in 2015, as Argentina finished bottom of their group after a 4-0 defeat to Germany.

In Qatar, Martínez became a national hero. One of the best goalkeepers in the world, he won the Golden Glove and, after a 3-3 draw with France, saved from Kingsley Coman in the shootout before Aurélien Tchouaméni sent his kick wide.

Industry observers note that ferran Torres and Eric García knew youth heartbreak. Torres scored twice in India in 2017, including in the semi-final win over Mali, before Spain surrendered a two-goal lead to lose the final 5-2 to England. Pedri's Spain were thrashed 6-1 by France in 2019, and Cubarsí's side fell 1-0 to eventual winners Germany in the 2023 quarter-finals. Laporte, meanwhile, had played for France in 2011.

Recent findings suggest that in New Jersey, all five featured. Cubarsí and Laporte started, Pedri came on in the 62nd minute, García replaced Laporte in extra time, and substitute Torres struck in the 106th minute to beat Argentina and give Spain a second star.

According to recent reports, not every U-17 graduate got a fairytale ending. Argentina's 2014 final starting line-up included four of them, and all four lost 1-0 to Germany.

Martínez is the latest addition. Already a world champion, he made a reported 11 saves in the 2026 final, but it was not enough to stop Ferran Torres.
