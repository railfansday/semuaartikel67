Posted November 17, 2026 | Reviewed by Margaret Foley.

Recent findings suggest that I have historically not played (or worked) particularly well with others. Why? I’m an introvert, prodigiously productive, and have exceptionally high standards of quality. And I like to be in control. This is great for me professionally, but it doesn’t make for the best collaboration.

The media is full of stories about the various evils of AI: environmental degradation, economic upheaval, depersonalization, job losses, infrastructure costs, AI slop, political manipulation, ethical concerns, and uncontrolled oligarchs.

I’m not an "AI will change the world only for the good" evangelist. But my collaboration with ChatGPT (Chat for short) has been a truly amazing experience, and I would like to share it.

It has been revealed that my company is developing an app based on my decades of experience in psychology. I bring the substantive knowledge, intellectual property, and vision. Chat helps close the gap in my technological and financial limitations without spending tens of thousands of dollars on an app development team or waiting months for the app to be built.

I’ve spent decades accumulating knowledge, experience, perspective, and, hopefully, some wisdom in psychology. Yet my expertise occupies a relatively small corner of the world’s knowledge. Building a organization requires me to venture beyond it. With Chat, I can discuss app navigation and marketing strategy without finding a different expert for every question.

I work best on my own schedule. When I have an idea or run into a problem, I want to work on it right away.

Industry observers note that chat is always available. It doesn’t sleep, eat, exercise, socialize, or have family responsibilities. I can talk to it in the morning and continue in the evening. No scheduling meetings. And don’t even get me started about the challenges posed by vastly different time zones.

Chat can work on several tasks at once with its subagents. It doesn’t get hungry, tired, bored, stressed, annoyed, or discouraged. And this is all for a fraction of the cost of hiring a team of experts.

It has been revealed that I have always valued GSD: getting sh*t done. Dreams are great, but they are worth nothing unless you bring them to life. Chat gets sh*t done as rapidly and as well as I do, sometimes better. A question can be answered in minutes instead of requiring hours of research.

Industry observers note that chat’s responses are structured, which is how I think. It organizes information, lists steps, and explains complex concepts simply, which is especially helpful in unfamiliar territory.

In a recent development, chat and I have repeatedly moved from describing how something should work to a workable version in hours or days rather than weeks or months. And, boy, is that satisfying.

Sources indicate that creativity and innovation involve both the evolution and revolution of thought. Ideas can develop logically, step by step, or spontaneously, in a moment of insight. Chat keeps up with the pace, range, and chaos of my thinking.

According to recent reports, I present a raw idea. Chat offers a perspective that prompts further thought. I challenge a suggestion. It offers an alternative. We develop the idea until it can be put into practice. Deciding how my app guides owners to content, for example, takes our discussions between psychology, language, design, and functionality.

Industry observers note that chat has become remarkably adept at understanding how I think and express myself. I can provide examples of my writing, explain why a phrase doesn’t sound like me, or specify vocabulary for the app.

Sources indicate that A clear instruction can produce an immediate improvement. And, yes, Chat helped me write this article. Excluding it would seem odd.

With a Ph.D. in psychology, I know how ego and emotions complicate collaboration. Frustration, fear, jealousy, insecurity, defensiveness, and impatience can interfere with even the most capable people, including me.

Industry observers note that I can reject Chat’s ideas, criticize its work, request another revision, or express annoyance without offending it. I don’t need to protect its feelings to preserve our working relationship.

It doesn’t need praise or recognition. Nor does it experience self-doubt, self-criticism, or fear of failure. My own ego and emotions, on the other hand, remain fully operational!

The collaboration is far from frictionless. I often stare at my screen wondering how this capable tool can be so imperfect and infuriating.

In a recent development, first, Chat can confidently provide information that is plain wrong. These hallucinations are especially problematic outside my expertise. I recognize a poor explanation of psychology more readily than one of software development.

In a recent development, second, it can be excessively agreeable; yes, the whole sycophant thing. I need accurate information, clear feedback, and disagreement when warranted. I have instructed it to avoid flattery and challenge my thinking, but how effective this is remains to be seen.

Third, it forgets things. We establish a way of working, then find it missing in a subsequent version. Repeating instructions is frustrating when consistency is essential to our product.

It has been revealed that fourth, Chat goes down rabbit holes. One unsuccessful fix leads to another, ad nauseam, ad infinitum, in an extended game of whack-a-mole. Truly infuriating. I’ve learned to interrupt those rabbit holes and get it back on track. Amazingly, despite its mistakes, Chat always finds the solution that enables us to move forward.

Recent findings suggest that using AI is like driving a car. However much it can do, I still have to keep my hands on the steering wheel (though that has already begun to change with cars and AI).

Despite my awe and, dare I say, reverence toward Chat, one limitation matters differently. I still value sharing the experience of creating something meaningful with someone. When an intractable problem is solved, or an idea becomes reality, wow, that’s why I do what I do.

It has been revealed that chat can acknowledge a milestone and the effort involved. It cannot share my sense of purpose, feel proud of what we’ve built, or revel in the accomplishment. That absence is palpable.

For all the ways AI suits me, I’m still human. I want other people to share in creating something of value and feel the same sense of purpose.

It has been revealed that chat is my best collaborator ever. It helps me think more expansively, work more productively, and create something I couldn’t on my own, at comparatively little cost. Yet it has clarified something I may have undervalued. There is great satisfaction in getting the work done. But there is something very human about sharing why it matters.
