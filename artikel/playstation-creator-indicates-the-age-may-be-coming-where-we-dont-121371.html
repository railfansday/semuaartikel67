PlayStation creator Ken Kutaragi isn't so sure we'll have consoles at home in the future – in fact, he believes we might not need them at all one day.

According to recent reports, speaking with The Game Business in a new interview, Kutaragi first explains that things are just… well, way too pricey. "Right now the machines are super expensive," as he admits. "It's not a healthy situation. You spend huge money on [them, because] graphics boards are super expensive right now." Then there are all the RAM shortages and other hardware issues (many spurred by the rise in AI) that are contributing to these high prices.

In a recent development, it doesn't help that, as Kutaragi notes, Sony and Microsoft have moved toward more PC-like architecture for PlayStation and Xbox consoles. Sure, it makes developing games that can launch on all systems a bit easier, but, again, the hardware costs a lot. "It worked, but right now PC is super expensive – it's the price of a car, it's not healthy." The former PlayStation executive touches on AI, too, which isn't all too surprising.

After all, Kutaragi himself now serves as the president and CEO of Cyber AI Entertainment, having previously predicted that, uh, AI daydreams could replace video games someday. As per the founding PlayStation father, the controversial tech (AI, that is) is "evolving [into a] much more elegant thing," and we'll see its impact in gaming. "The age may be coming where we don't need any consoles. It's a [real] danger.".

Recent findings suggest that the evolution of AI might lead to "some lightweight platform," he ponders – but it won't be akin to the PS5 or Xbox Series X|S. "It will not be a console, it will not be a smartphone." Kutaragi does clarify, though, "This is not the official comment from Sony Interactive Entertainment!" And, again, he feels pretty optimistic about AI and how it might change entertainment – even if that means the death of consoles.

Industry observers note that "Everything can be calculatable. The AI is elaborating, but it's at an intermediate stage. Maybe in the future, everything [will] be calculatable. Everything. If those things happen, what entertainment could we and our children create for the future? Those things will not take several decades. Maybe it will be coming very, very soon. Be prepared for that." I'm not sure I want to be, honestly – but I suppose anything is possible.

The shift away from physical media – just see Sony's highly controversial decision to end all physical disc production – doesn't help, I'm sure.
