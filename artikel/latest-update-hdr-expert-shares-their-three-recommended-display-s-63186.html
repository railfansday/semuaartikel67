The good (and hilarious) folks over at HDTVTest have been looking at the HDR Calibration settings for GTA 6 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, and they've discovered that the tool is actually slightly broken as things stand.

Some of the specifics go over my head a little bit to be honest, but in a nutshell, the figures that are displayed on the calibration tool don't fully translate into the game itself, leading to a potentially inoptimal picture.

As a result, they've published a latest nine-minute video (which you can see up above) to explain the challenges.

Recent findings suggest that "The HDR calibration screen in GTA 6 can be misleading, because Rockstar's Max Brightness, Paper White and Black Levels settings interact in unexpected ways. To be clear, GTA VI's HDR rendering isn't broken… it's the calibration procedure that can give misleading results.".

The video goes step-by-step on how they analysed the issues and tried to figure out the best possible HDR settings for GTA 6, and while their tests aren't fully concluded yet (there's another video to come in the future where they try multiple consumer displays), they've shared their initial best settings for displays capable of 1,000 nits or brighter.

Here are those three settings, but again, keep in mind that your TV must be capable of 1,000 nits of brightness or more, and that certain smart TV features or special HDR settings on your TV itself may interfere with the results.

If your display isn't capable of 1,000 nits, do a bit of Googling to try and find out the specifics for your TV/monitor, and then times that figure by two in-game. So, if you're working with a maximum of 600 nits, set the figure as 1,200.

Recent findings suggest that I have to be honest and say I'm not seeing a lot of difference here, but I've also got an old TV with a max brightness of just over 600 nits, not to mention I'm using a few image-altering features that may be playing havoc with the settings. It's also noted in the video that this is generally based on playing in the dark, rather than in a brightly-lit room.

Recent findings suggest that have a play for yourself and see what you think - there's clearly a lot of people who are very thankful for these settings on YouTube and beyond, so we wanted to at least make you aware of them. The reality is that you may need to still do a bit of tweaking to find the perfect sweet spot for your setup though.
