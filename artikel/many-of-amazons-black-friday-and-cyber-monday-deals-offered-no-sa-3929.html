📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that A blockbuster new investigation from Groundwork Collaborative exposes how Amazon shoppers may be getting hosed during the retail giant’s much-touted Black Friday and Cyber Monday sale events. The report, Deal or Decoy? Amazon’s Deceptive Discounts, examines products the retailer showcased as some the “best” discounts of the year to shoppers hunting for bargains during these sale events. A closer look at hundreds of these “deals” finds that Amazon frequently offered shoppers the same price, or in some cases a higher price, during recent holiday sale events than they could have paid just weeks earlier.

The report’s authors, Breyon Williams, Elizabeth Pancotti, and Lindsay Owens, find that nearly 1 in 3 of these Amazon “deals” offered no real savings – the exact same products were sold for the same price or less in the month before the sale. During Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales in the past two years, prices for nearly 1 in 10 products advertised as “deals” were higher than what customers could have paid for the products in the month leading up to the sales.

Recent findings suggest that groundwork’s analysis examined the prices of more than 350 products included on Amazon’s own deal pages for the Black Friday and Cyber Monday sale events in 2024 and 2025. These pages promote these deals as some of the “best” deals available. Amazon’s Black Friday 2025 deals page indicates, “With so many deals to explore, we’ve handpicked some of the best ones you’ll find.”.

Groundwork Collaborative President and CEO and report co-author Lindsay Owens, shared the following reaction:.

Sources indicate that “Millions of Americans wait all year for Black Friday and Cyber Monday to score the biggest bargains on stocking stuffers and household staples. Shoppers trust that the deals advertised to them deliver real savings because Amazon promotes them that way. But it turns out that these major sales aren’t all they’re cracked up to be. Instead, our investigation reveals that shoppers who wait for these major sale events may be playing rip-off roulette.

“Amazon dominates online shopping, and families count on its sale events to stretch their dollars. With the holiday shopping season upon us and prices continuing to rise, shoppers deserve to know they can trust every deal Amazon promises them. If Amazon can’t deliver that basic promise, lawmakers should hold them accountable.”.

Owens is the author of the recent book Gouged: The End of a Fair Price—And What That Means for Your Wallet, which exposes how corporations are sophisticated pricing schemes to overcharge consumers.

The fresh report builds on Groundwork’s work exposing corporate pricing abuses and delivering results for American consumers.
