A new set of Nintendo patents has been discovered that have paved the way for DS, 3DS, and Wii U games to return on Switch 2, so long as the player doesn’t mind shelling out for extra hardware.

The Nintendo DS was a massively popular system, selling 154 million units worldwide. However, the system’s dual-screen design has made it hard for its titles to be rereleased on modern systems. Konami managed it with the Castlevania series, but few other companies seem interested in reviving their DS games on the Switch or Switch 2.

One possible solution to Nintendo’s second screen glitch may have been revealed. According to RetroDodo, two patents have been discovered that will allow the Nintendo Switch and Switch 2 to play games from the previous console generations.

The first patent allows a game to be instantly transferred between two systems, while the second allows a different console to be used alongside one running a title, with the ability to use touchscreen controls. Taken together, these patents could emulate the Nintendo DS, 3DS, and Wii U GamePad.

The caveat is that it would require someone to own two Nintendo Switch 2 units, or one of each Switch console, to run old games. This would be fine in a household that owns both systems, but for those who don’t, it’s an expensive proposition just to be able to run DS, 3DS, and Wii U titles.

Sources indicate that this isn’t the first time Nintendo has brought dual-screen games to consoles; DS games were available on the Wii U, with the GamePad used as the bottom screen. This isn’t the most elegant of solutions, especially as it forces the player to keep switching perspective between screens, but it gets the job done.

There’s also the question of what games fans would even want to play on such a setup. The obvious answer is the Pokémon games, as fans would crawl through broken glass for modern ports of Pokémon HeartGold & SoulSilver that don’t cost hundreds of dollars on eBay. However, considering Game Freak seems averse to making money, there are still easier-to-port Pokémon games that aren’t on the Switch 2, and the DS games would take a ton of work to upgrade.

In a recent development, the Wii U also doesn’t have much, since pretty much all its best games are already on the Switch, and they received updated ports over the console’s lifespan. Sure, it’d be cool for Nintendo Land to return, but no one’s lining up for Star Fox Zero. The Wii U was such a bomb that Nintendo is unlikely to bring up its ghost, even through a costly hardware expansion.

Recent findings suggest that the Nintendo 3DS had a stacked library of amazing games, but some of them would lose the system's big selling point: 3D visuals. Super Mario 3D Land was a tremendous game, but it requires playing in 3D to get the full effect, especially for its stage secrets. The lack of Streepass and Spotpass would also take away a lot of the extra functions present in Nintendo 3DS games.

It’s entirely possible that these Nintendo Switch 2 patents won’t come to anything. After all, companies patent ideas all the time that never get made. The ability to play DS, 3DS, and Wii U games on the Switch 2 would be incredible, but the price of entry might be a bit too high for most fans, even if the titles are released for free on Nintendo Switch Online.
