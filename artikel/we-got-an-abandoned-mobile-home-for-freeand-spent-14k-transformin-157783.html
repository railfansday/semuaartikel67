‘We Got an Abandoned Mobile Home for Free–and Spent $14K Transforming It’

An Oregon family transformed a mobile home that had sat abandoned for nearly five years into a home for a loved one–all without paying a dime for the property itself.

Sources indicate that but getting the home for free was only the beginning. What followed was months of demolition, electrical and plumbing work, drywall installation, and a race to get the aging property ready for its latest owner.

Lyman Branson II, who documented the transformation on I Can Fix That, his YouTube channel, took on the ambitious renovation alongside his family with one goal in mind: creating a safe, comfortable home for his sister, Betty Branson, 42, and her 17-year-old twin daughters.

“I don't think I've ever had an opportunity like this before in my life, and so to turn it down would have been a shame,” Betty tells Realtor.com®. “And I know I would have regretted it, so this was probably one of the better decisions I've made in my life.”.

It has been revealed that the family spent approximately $14,000 transforming the 1977 mobile home—a fraction of what Betty spent on rent in a year. Betty finally moved in the first week of May 2026, and now, just six months later, she is hosting her first Thanksgiving in the home her family helped bring back to life.

Lyman has worked in rental property maintenance and renovation since 2001, so he knew about the home in his small town of Irrigon, OR, that had been abandoned by the owner about five years earlier.

Sources indicate that he discussed taking over the mobile home to renovate it and rent it out to someone in need. The landlord would be able to transfer the title to Lyman and later collect rent on the lot. Meanwhile, Lyman would be able to remove the property from the landlord’s possession.

“It was kind of like a ‘I scratch your back, you scratch my back’ kind of deal,” Branson tells Realtor.com.

In a recent development, initially, Lyman saw the renovation as another project for his YouTube channel, which he launched in early 2025. But the timing was also opportune for his sister, whose living circumstances had unexpectedly changed just days before he called.

Betty had been searching for another rental and considered buying an RV, paying approximately $800 a month for space at an RV park. Instead, her brother presented her with an opportunity to finally own a place of her own.

Betty could contribute what her brother calls “sweat equity,” working on the property before and after her regular job.

Sources indicate that the purchase price was zero, but the property was far from move-in ready. The floors and walls were rotted, and there were leaks, water damage, and missing sections of ceiling.

“Basically, it was ready for the dump,” Lyman notes.

It has been revealed that with more than two decades of experience in the industry, he recognized that the structure was salvageable and the renovation worthwhile. Betty, however, was less convinced.

“I was a little overwhelmed and kind of scared when I first saw it,” she recalls.

She saw the home's many problems, including the broken countertop and the hole in the wall where a window would later be installed.

Recent findings suggest that the home remained in its original location, eliminating the expense of transporting it. However, it was still sitting on flat tires and had never been properly set up. So the family's first task was to stabilize the structure on blocks.

The landlord waived the lot rent for six months while the family obtained the necessary permits. Renovation began in October 2025. Lyman and his father, with help from other family members, updated the almost 50-year-old mobile home to current building codes.

They replaced broken flooring, added insulation and drywall, and wrapped the outside of the home to protect it from the elements. Lyman shared the process on his YouTube channel.

In a recent development, walls were painted, the bathroom drywall was finished, and the overall interior was beginning to look like a home. But the project wasn't without setbacks.

According to recent reports, “There were a couple times that I was still in the middle of it all, and I'm like, 'We're too far in. I can't quit now,'” Betty recalls. “But this is hard and this is scary.”.

She had to depend on her brother and her father for help when various issues arose during the renovation.

“I'm so grateful because without them, this wouldn't have been possible for me,”  she indicates.

Betty received her certificate of occupancy two weeks before the permit expired.

“I was actually really amazed that we were able to make it in the time frame that we did with how much that was done,” she states.

The renovation offered Betty something she had never experienced in 20 years of renting. She got to design the layout of her living space. She got to pick out the locations of her light switches and outlets. Her daughters, who share a bedroom, chose paint colors and the arrangement for the closets.

“It was an experience for me that I'll never forget,” Betty notes.

The renovation of the free mobile home came with substantial expenses.

Industry observers note that leAnna Branson, Lyman and Betty's mother, who kept track of the receipts for the renovation, tells Realtor.com that the family initially budgeted between $10,000 and $12,000. However, rising material costs, unexpected damage discovered during demolition, and decisions to upgrade certain features pushed the total to approximately $14,000.

Betty also opted to spend a little more on higher-quality materials and finishes, knowing she intended to live there for years.

“I could have gone cheaper. Definitely, we could have cut some costs here and there,” she claims. “But I plan to be here for a while. I want it to last.”.

Sources indicate that betty used her savings to kick-start the renovation and lived in her sister’s RV, which allowed her to devote her income to the renovation for six months. Once she moved in and began paying rent on the lot, plus utilities, the pace of the work slowed a bit.

Now, Lyman estimates the renovation is approximately 90% complete. The remaining work involves the upper kitchen cabinets, wall tile, a window replacement, and several finishing touches.

The family also received support from viewers of Lyman's YouTube channel, who contributed an estimated $1,000 to $2,000. Even the handles that will adorn Betty's kitchen cabinets and drawers were paid for by a viewer.

In a recent development, “More support than I ever imagined,” Lyman says of the response to the project. His viewers “loved seeing the family aspect.”.

It has been revealed that for Betty, the financial benefits extend well beyond the renovation costs. She indicates she has more living space than she would have gotten in an apartment. And there's space for her daughters and her dog.

“We went over budget a little, but considering [Betty] was paying $20,000 a year to rent, $14,000 is not that bad,” LeAnna says. “I mean it's a chunk of money, but it's doable.”.

Lyman believes owning a mobile home, even when you must rent the land it sits on, can offer an affordable housing alternative to people struggling to keep up with rising rental costs across the country.

“That was key for Betty as well, so that she could afford to live the life she deserves,” he says.

Though Betty’s experience shows that acquiring a free or inexpensive mobile home is only a small part of the financial equation, her brother’s professional experience, the family’s help, and the landlord’s willingness to waive her lot rent during the renovation also helped keep costs manageable.

For her parents, seeing their daughter choose everything from her kitchen sink to her bathroom tile was particularly rewarding.

“It gave us a peace of mind to know, you know, that she finally has a place of her own to call her home,” her mother indicates.

In a recent development, some of the most special features of her new home are things visitors won’t see. Behind her new cupboards are messages from her family who helped her renovate her home.

Recent findings suggest that “I have places on the wall behind some of the cupboards where the family all, like, signed their names and left me love notes,” Betty claims. “I know they're there, even though some of them are covered up.”.

The transformation is a daily reminder of what her family made possible.

“The fact that it was my family that assisted me along the way makes it even more special,” she notes. “It wasn't just hiring outside help, but family and friends that came to put in the time and effort that they could to help. It makes it mean so much more to me.”.
