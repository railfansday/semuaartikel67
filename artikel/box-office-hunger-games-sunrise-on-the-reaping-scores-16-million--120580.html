Box Office: ‘Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping’ Scores $16 Million on Tuesday, ‘Hexed’ Earns $2 Million in Previews

It has been revealed that lionsgate’s “Hunger Games” prequel “Sunrise on the Reaping” continues to power the domestic box office, earning a leading $15.98 million on Tuesday. Those ticket sales rank as the franchise’s best Tuesday haul, above 2013’s “The Hunger Games: Catching Fire,” which grossed $15.96 million on its first Tuesday (not adjusted for inflation).

“Sunrise on the Reaping” is expected to tower over two newcomers — Universal’s comedy “Focker In-Law” and Disney’s animated adventure “Hexed” — through the competitive Thanksgiving frame. The dystopian prequel, set 25 years before the events of the original “Hunger Games” saga, has generated $148 million in North America and more than $250 million globally to date. Fueled by positive word-of-mouth, “Sunrise on the Reaping” looks to add $80 million to $90 million over the five-day holiday frame. In terms of newly introduced releases, “Hexed” got off to a slow start with $2.1 million in previews. Through the long weekend, the $200 million-budgeted fantasy comedy is aiming for $40 million to $45 million from 3,725 theaters. Tuesday returns are slightly above 2023’s “Wish” and 2021’s “Encanto,” two of Disney’s prior Thanksgiving releases. “Wish” collected $1.8 million in previews and then opened to $31.6 million over the five-day frame, while “Encanto” pulled in $1.5 million in previews before launching to $40.6 million between Wednesday and Sunday. Those are troubling points of comparison because both of those movies wound up as money-losers in their theatrical runs. (While Disney’s animated sequels like “Moana 2” and “Zootopia 2” are among the biggest Thanksgiving offerings of all time, the studio has struggled with original fare in post-pandemic times.) “Wish” tapped out with $254 million worldwide on a $200 million budget. “Encanto,” whose box office run was hobbled by Covid-related closures, left theaters with $260 million on a $150 million budget. Those aren’t embarrassing tallies, except that cinema operators keep about half of ticket sales. In the case of “Hexed,” which follows a teen girl who discovers magical powers, word-of-mouth will factor in the film’s box office longevity.

In a recent development, “Focker In-Law” didn’t have Tuesday previews; showtimes will begin on Wednesday. It’s similarly targeting a debut of $40 million to $45 million over its first five days. “Focker In-Law” was produced for $90 million (split between Universal and Paramount), a hefty budget for a theatrical comedy. The studios were willing to spend significant, however, because the three prior films in the “Focker” universe were major box office winners. “Meet the Parents,” which debuted 26 years ago and starred Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro as mismatched in-laws Greg Focker and Jack Byrnes, generated $330 million worldwide and inspired two commercially successful sequels, 2004’s “Meet the Fockers” ($522 million worldwide) and 2010’s “Little Fockers” ($310 million worldwide). In this installment, Ariana Grande joins the fun as the girlfriend of Greg’s son Henry (Skyler Gisondo) who meets the family as they gather for Jack’s 80th birthday party.
