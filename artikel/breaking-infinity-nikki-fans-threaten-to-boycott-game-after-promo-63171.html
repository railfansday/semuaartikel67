Infinity Nikki, the free-to-play, cozy, open-world dress-up game, is at the centre of controversy again, though this time not because fans disliked one of the game's updates. Instead, it's because of… Elon Musk's mother.

Industry observers note that some Infinity Nikki fans are planning to boycott the game following the release of the game's latest trailer, which is narrated by Maye Musk. The trailer is for a new update - dubbed Cicia - which adds the ability to drive futuristic hovering cars. The reveal comes days after a widely mocked demonstration of Musk's own Tesla "flying car", which doesn't actually fly.

While some fans are worried Tesla's cars may be coming to the game, many also simply don't want their game associated with Elon Musk and his family.

It has been revealed that "If they promote Tesla or mention him in the game I'm 100 percent dropping it," wrote one Reddit user (via The Gamer). "Why would they involve that man in a girly fashion game, and if they wanted a car brand partnership why not a Chinese one??!? Infold is addicted to negative attention I swear.".

"If Tesla is somehow involved in the design of the car (or worse, an in-game promotion), then I might have to let go," wrote another person in the same thread. "Please please please be wrong.".

Sources indicate that though Maye Musk's involvement might seem random, there may be a good explanation for it. "Here's something that might surprise people who only know her as Elon's mother: Maye Musk is a celebrity in China in her own right," Reddit user BarbieSpeedRacer explained.

"She's a successful model, an author, and a recognisable advertising personality. Her memoir became popular with Chinese readers, and she's appeared in campaigns and promotional events for brands including OPPO, Fila, and JNBY.".

Her image as "a woman in her seventies who continues to work as a fashion model" is one that resonates with the game's Chinese audience, apparently, especially "among women interested in fashion, beauty, and lifestyle content".

"A Chinese firm makes an advertisement featuring a celebrity popular in China, and an international fandom interprets that decision almost exclusively through the lens of American domestic politics," the post added.

Sources indicate that so far, calls for a boycott don't appear to have coalesced into a movement, at least judging by the game's subreddit. Even if Western players were to organise one, it's not likely to hurt Infinity Nikki much, as most of the game's revenue - estimated at more than 80 percent - comes from Chinese players.

In Eurogamer's Infinity Nikki review, Jessica Orr mentioned it advances the genre of open-world gacha games and brings competition to rival developer miHoYo, writing: "The changes in length, combat and progression all feel utterly invigorating compared to other Genshin-likes, and it's why Infinity Nikki feels like it could (and should) be on the same trajectory to shake up the gacha scene just like Genshin Impact did in 2020.".
