Sweden’s H100 Taps Coinbase Prime and Deribit for Its 3,506 Bitcoin Treasury as It Courts Family Offices

Swedish bitcoin treasury firm H100 Group mentioned Monday it will use Coinbase Prime and the Deribit derivatives exchange for custody, trading and financing of its roughly 3,506 BTC, a move it notes supports both its bitcoin-per-share goal and a push to serve wealthy investors.

H100, which trades on Stockholm’s NGM Nordic SME under the ticker H100, announced the partnership in a release Monday morning. The organization and its subsidiaries will use Coinbase and Deribit, which Coinbase owns, for custody, trading and financing, it said.

H100 valued its holdings at about SEK 2.3 billion, or $226 million, based on a bitcoin price of $64,600 on Nov. 15. It also has about SEK 219 million ($22 million) in outstanding convertible loans that carry 0% interest, mature on July 9, 2030, and equal less than 10% of the stated value of its bitcoin.

The release did not disclose fees, collateral terms or borrowing limits. It also didn’t say whether H100 has drawn on any financing, how much of its bitcoin will sit with Coinbase, or whether it is moving coins from another custodian.

H100 described Coinbase’s offering as the crypto version of a bank’s prime brokerage desk, bringing custody, financing and trade execution under one roof.

It has been revealed that the trading itself will be run in-house. PDI AS, a wholly owned trading and technology unit, builds the software and trading systems used to manage H100’s bitcoin, and the tech giant remarked those tools operate “with protecting H100’s Bitcoin holdings as the first priority.”.

“This partnership gives H100 stronger tools for our own balance sheet, and we use them on our own Bitcoin first,” Executive Chairman Sander Andersen said. “Bitcoin per share remains the measure we run the Company by, and these tools are there to help us deliver on it, not to add risk.”.

Andersen added that this is “the first of several” partnerships, “and the next is already under way.” He did not name the next partner.

Lauren Abendschein, head of institutional sales at Coinbase, said bitcoin treasury companies “have grown into an established part of our institutional client base,” and that their priorities are custody, “liquidity when the market moves, and financing against the same balance sheet.”.

Recent findings suggest that the second goal is bigger. H100 remarked it wants to become what majority shareholder Geir Harald Hansen, in a June 11 letter to shareholders, called “a leading European alternative and digital asset manager.” It will start with family offices and high-net-worth individuals looking for a partner on bitcoin investments and financing, and said it will share details as products launch.

Industry observers note that H100 began as a health-tech firm before adopting a bitcoin treasury strategy in 2025. It agreed in January to buy Swiss treasury firm Future Holdings and closed that deal in February.

According to recent reports, its holdings tripled in August. After shareholders approved the acquisition of two Norwegian holding companies in June, H100 closed the deal on Aug. 10, adding 2,455.37 BTC and lifting its total to 3,506.4 BTC, according to its second-quarter report.

Industry observers note that H100 paid for that deal with 790.5 million upcoming shares. That took its share count to about 1.13 billion and left Hansen with about 69.2% of the firm. Eirik Grøttum, who has worked with Hansen for years, became H100’s CEO the next day.

Grøttum wrote in the same report that simply raising money to buy bitcoin “is unlikely to be sufficient on its own” for treasury companies, adding that “BTC should be the hurdle rate, not the business model.”.

Sources indicate that the announcement comes as bitcoin trades near $64,000 after a weekend slide to its lowest level since July, and treasury companies face pressure to show they can grow their bitcoin per share without issuing too much stock. H100 has not noted whether it plans to buy more bitcoin.
