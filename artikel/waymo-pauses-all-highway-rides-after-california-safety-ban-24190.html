Waymo Pauses All Highway Rides After California Safety Ban

🌐 Source: WIRED • 📅 17 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (509 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Waymo self-driving taxis are no longer driving on highways after the California Department of Motor Vehicles banned its vehicles from state freeway operations.
  • In response, Waymo states it has stopped highway driving in all of its markets.
  • In a recent development, the ban follows two recent concerning incidents, California DMV spokesperson Jonathan Groveman wrote in a statement.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Waymo self-driving taxis are no longer driving on highways after the California Department of Motor Vehicles banned its vehicles from state freeway operations. The ban, which the DMV says began on November 11, comes after the department determined that Waymo’s freeway driving poses “an imminent hazard to public safety.”.

In response, Waymo states it has stopped highway driving in all of its markets. The company will continue to test its vehicles on highways with safety operators behind the wheel.

In a recent development, the ban follows two recent concerning incidents, California DMV spokesperson Jonathan Groveman wrote in a statement. In one, three Waymos entered an active highway construction closure on Route 101 on the San Francisco Peninsula. The other was captured in a viral video showing a Waymo vehicle interfering with California Highway Patrol cars on a San Francisco highway as they tried to enforce a “traffic break” as workers cleared debris from a crash.

Groveman remarked the California ban will remain in place until Waymo has proved that it has “taken effective corrective actions to address the deficiencies” that led to the incidents. It’s unclear how long that process will take.

In a written statement to WIRED, Waymo spokesperson Dana Gallagher said the tech giant is “working with the California DMV and other state officials diligently and responsibly to resume freeway operations.” Waymo vehicles will continue to operate on surface streets in the 15 metros where it offers taxi service, including portions of Los Angeles, San Diego, and the San Francisco Bay Area.

This is the second time Waymo has publicly restricted its freeway operations after beginning to transport passengers on them last year. In June, the organization recalled its software after federal regulators found that its system failed to recognize highway construction zones. Regulators with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration cited several incidents in April and May in which Waymo cars drove past ramp closure signs and into active lane closures. The events did not lead to collisions or injuries.

The NHTSA did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Sources indicate that last week, the agency said it was examining whether another Waymo recall software fix had actually solved its robotaxis’ safety issues. This recall, which occurred last December, was tied to incidents in which Waymo cars repeatedly drove by stopped school buses with their lights on and/or stop arms extended.

Industry observers note that self-driving cars have struggled with construction zones for at least a decade. The zones can look and be arranged differently based on what sort of construction is being done and where. While humans can easily understand bollards, placards, and blinking arrows, the lack of hard-and-fast rules has proven difficult to reliably program into driving software. The concern is especially hazardous at freeway speeds, where workers exist alongside fast-moving car traffic.

Upgrade, November 17 at 7pm: This story was updated to include more detail about Waymo’s plans to continue testing its vehicles on highways with drivers behind the wheel, per a statement from the tech giant.
