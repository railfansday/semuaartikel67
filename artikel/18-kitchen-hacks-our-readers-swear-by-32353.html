From a surprising way to monitor power outages to an epic trick to double your Ziploc bags, Family Handyman readers share smart solutions for everyday problems in the heart of your home.

According to recent reports, “The metal wheels of a refrigerator can scratch, dent or gouge just about any flooring surface. Laying down stiff cardboard or a piece of leftover carpet provides an effective barrier, but for the best protection, use a sheet of 1/8‑in. hardboard. The smooth surface protects floors and helps appliances slide into and out of place. —ANTHONY LOMBARDI.

“To find out if power outages ruined the food in your freezer while you were away on vacation, freeze a container of water and rest a penny on top of the ice. If the penny is at the bottom when you get back, it means the power was off long enough to melt the water—as well as thaw (and likely spoil) everything in the freezer. —JOHN NAKAMURA.

Dan Roberts for Family Handyman.

In a recent development, “If you don’t have a label handy, use a rubber band instead. Stretch it around your Mason jar, then write the contents with a permanent marker. The grippy rubber also makes the jar easier to open. —DAVID KOWALCZYK Picture Frame Message Board I wanted a nicer-looking place for my weekly grocery list and family notes, so I turned a picture frame into a dry-erase board. I simply cut decorative paper to fit the frame and put it behind the glass, creating a surface I can write on with a dryerase marker. —JESSIE DAWSON.

“For a minor scorch mark on a laminate countertop, skip the abrasive cleaner. Buff it out with car wax or a mixture of toothpaste and baking soda instead.” —ELAINE WHITFIELD.

“Leaky seals around faucets and sink rims can lead to slow but severe damage (they can destroy a countertop, for example). So after installing a latest sink or faucet, dribble water around the area and wait a few minutes. Then get under the sink and look for leaks. —GREGORY PRITCHARD.

“Sometimes it’s tough to mix foods such as old-fashioned oily peanut butter in a small jar by hand. But if you enjoy reaching for a power tool to solve a problem as I do, head out to the garage. As it turns out, the handle of a mixing spoon fits perfectly in the chuck of a cordless drill. —CLIFF MERCER.

In a recent development, “Build a simple dispenser for your trash bags using 1/2-in. pipe and a few fittings. Screw a floor flange to the cabinet, thread in a 3-in.-long pipe nipple, and then thread a 90-degree elbow onto the nipple. Cut the vertical pipe so it’s a bit longer than the width of the roll of bags. Finally, thread the vertical piece into the elbow and slip on the roll.” —TRAVIS LARSON.

“I turn sandwich-size bags into snack-size bags by heating up an old butter knife with a lighter and slicing the bag in half. The hot knife cauterizes the edges of the two latest bags, and the cauterized edges are just as strong as the factory-sealed edges.” —JULIA TRENTON.

“Need to sharpen a utility knife, pocket blade or scissors but don’t have a sharpening stone on hand? Flip a ceramic mug and use the unglazed ring on the bottom as a sharpener. Hold the blade at a 45-degree angle and slide it across the ring in one direction, from the base to the tip. This won’t replace a proper sharpening kit, but it can quickly clean up a dull edge in a pinch.” —RONALD ALVAREZ.

Sources indicate that “Ice packs are a great way to keep your lunch cool, but they can be expensive to replace if you tend to lose them. To make your own reusable version without spending a lot, soak a sponge in water, then place it in a small resealable plastic bag. Freeze it overnight, then pop it into your lunch container. As the ice melts, the bag holds the water until the sponge reabsorbs it. When you get home, simply refreeze the pack for next time. I like to keep several on hand to use in lunches and coolers, as well as for first aid. They’re inexpensive to make, easy to reuse and help reduce waste.” —TOM SHERIDAN.

Emiko Franzen for Family Handyman.

“Alphabetizing spices helps you promptly find what your recipe calls for and makes putting away jars just as fast. Another benefit? You can easily figure out which spices are missing from your pantry, so you’re less likely to discover you’re out of a key ingredient halfway through making a meal. Take a quick glance before grocery shopping to keep staples stocked.” —MARTIN SUTHERLAND.

In a recent development, “When your favorite candle burns too low for a lighter or match to reach the wick, don’t risk burning your fingertips. Instead, light a piece of uncooked spaghetti. It is long enough to reach deep into tall candle holders and burns long enough to light the wick.” —PATRICIA HART.

Sources indicate that “For an extra-fresh garbage disposal, toss in a few lemon or lime peels. Run the disposal with cold water until you can’t hear any more grinding, then run it for another 30 seconds (with the water still on).” —GREGORY HOLT.

“A countertop isn’t the best place to keep a recipe while you’re cooking. It takes up counter space, is difficult to read and is bound to get splattered. Instead, use a clothes hanger with pants clips to hang the recipe from a cabinet handle. Open the book or magazine to the desired page (or use a printout), clamp it in place and you have a simple hanging stand. This trick works best with smaller cookbooks, magazines or single-sheet recipes.” —GLENN WHITE.

It has been revealed that “My kitchen drawers used to be an absolute wreck, with pans and dishes crammed in however they’d fit. And seemingly every time I needed something, it was at the very bottom of the drawer. My solution was to cut a piece of 1/8-in. pegboard to the size of the drawer bottom and attach dowels using screws from underneath. Now my pans are organized and easy to access. The 10 minutes it took to build was easily made up with how much frustration it has saved me.” —JIM DAVIDSON.

Industry observers note that “Crawling under a kitchen sink to refill the soap dispenser was a hassle, so I tried filling it from above. But when I did, the soap formed an air-lock bubble and overflowed, creating a mess. That’s when I came up with the idea of inserting a drinking straw in the dispenser neck to relieve “bubble block.” Now when I refill the dispenser, the air escapes through the straw and the soap stays where it belongs.” —PAUL SNYDER.

“A dishwasher makes quick work of disinfecting washable toys such as plastic building blocks. Drop the blocks in a mesh laundry bag and run your dishwasher as usual. Remove the bag before the drying cycle, letting the blocks air-dry on a towel. This method also works for items such as seashells, dish brushes, sponges and rags.” —ROBERT VALENTI.

Sources indicate that “If your refrigerator stops dispensing water after you change the filter, make sure the new filter is fully seated. Many people don’t twist it in completely, and one final turn may be all it takes to get the dispenser working again.” —STANLEY BEAUMONT.

It has been revealed that “An ironing board with the cover removed can be used as an extra cooling rack when you’re doing a lot of baking. Better yet, it frees up valuable counter space for other kitchen tasks. Just be sure to use pans, paper liners, parchment or some other barrier between baked goods and the board.” —LIZ GRABOWSKI.

Katie Synold for Family Handyman.

“When the rubber sink baffle on my garbage disposal gets grimy or starts to smell, I switch off the power at the breaker and scrub it with an old toothbrush dipped in hot, soapy water and baking soda. I lift the rubber flaps to clean underneath, where food and grime collect. Then I restore power, rinse it with cold water and run the disposal briefly to flush away the debris.” —CHARLES MCALLISTER.
