Industry observers note that the most notable thing about Hyundai’s fresh Elantra is its radical redesign, but there are enough updates under the skin to make this new compact sedan compelling for other reasons. The 2027 Elantra will arrive first with a mostly carryover engine, but the 2028 Elantra Hybrid comes with upgrades to its gas-electric powertrain that improve its power and mpg figures.

We already saw the Elantra’s fresh sci-fi styling a few months ago when it was revealed globally, and the newly revealed U.S. version looks nearly the same. Angular lighting elements, sharply creased body lines, and futuristic details make this look far more distinctive than anything else in the mainstream compact segment. It’s also larger than before, with the wheelbase stretching by 1.2 inches, which provides extra legroom in the rear seat.

The standard model, arriving for the 2027 model year, has a carryover powertrain consisting of a 147-hp 2.0-liter inline-four and a continuously variable automatic transmission. The previous model offered a more powerful turbocharged engine in the N Line variant, but Hyundai has yet to announce any sort of performance version of the new Elantra—though we assume the full-bore Elantra N will eventually join the new generation.

The 2028 Elantra Hybrid has more power than before; its combination of a 1.6-liter inline-four and electric motors now makes 155 horsepower, an uptick of 16 horses compared with before. Hyundai estimates it will achieve up to 53 mpg combined, which is 1 mpg less than the outgoing model’s highest combined rating. It’s also notable that the hybrid model has a multilink rear suspension setup, compared with the nonhybrid’s twist-beam rear suspension.

Recent findings suggest that we like the look of the new interior, at least in the loaded Limited model we sat in, which incorporates nicer materials than you might expect in this segment. The dashboard layout is fresh and modern, with a standard 12.9-inch touchscreen protruding from the center of the dash. There’s also an optional slim digital gauge cluster that’s located near the base of the windshield. Hyundai’s latest Pleos Connect infotainment is in play here too, and it’s said to recognize more natural voice commands. Optional features include dual wireless charging pads and a Bang & Olufsen stereo.

It has been revealed that the ‘27 Elantra lineup starts with the base SE trim level and also includes SEL Sport and Limited trims. The hybrid lineup is similar, but the base model is called SE Blue. The outgoing Elantra is one of the most affordable cars on sale, and we hope the upcoming one’s starting price won’t rise much above $25,000. The ‘28 Elantra hybrid, arriving later on, will cost a few thousand dollars more than that.
