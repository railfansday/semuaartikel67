Gary Colliver Was a “Massive-Hearted” Climber Who Devoted Decades to Yosemite

Gary Colliver passed away on August 30, 2026, in Denver, Colorado. He was 83. A longtime Yosemite Valley resident, he spent decades caring for the park as a ranger, medic, rescuer, and advocate.

According to recent reports, throughout his life, Yosemite’s beauty was a current which drew him back, time and time again. Colliver discovered climbing in college and soon moved to Camp 4. He met his future wife, Reva, in Tuolumne Meadows, married her in the Yosemite Valley Chapel in 1965, and built a decades-long career serving the park and its visitors.

In the early days of climbing in the Valley, Colliver was a quiet giant. With Richard McCracken and John Evans, he made the fourth ascent of the Nose (5.9 C2; 3,000ft) on El Capitan. He later returned to the route for the film El Capitan (1978), and in 1969, he climbed the Salathé Wall (5.9 C2; 3,500ft) with Chris Jones.

Later, his ambitions also took him beyond Yosemite. In 1964, he and Glen Denny made the first ascent of Jirishanca Norte (19,735ft) in Peru. In 1970, he joined Jones, Bob Cuthbert, and Gray Thompson for the first complete ascent of Mount Adamant’s north ridge in British Columbia. The following year, he participated in the International Himalayan Expedition to Mount Everest, where his team attempted a new route on Everest’s Southwest Face.

To Dave Sessions, Colliver’s second cousin, those accomplishments belonged to an exceptionally modest man. “He was one of the most modest people I ever met in my life. Incredibly soft-spoken,” Sessions remarked. “Never, never sought out attention or, you know, just very kind-hearted and modest and soft-spoken.”.

Colliver’s work in Yosemite included serving as a wilderness and climbing ranger, park medic, and volunteer firefighter. In 1983, he and two fellow rangers received a Department of the Interior Valor Award for rescuing a visitor from swift water.

His National Park Service colleague Mark Finscher worked alongside him on an early effort to develop a climbing management initiative for the Valley, bringing together interest groups with opposing views on access, bolting, and responsible climbing.

It has been revealed that “There were some strong personalities in that group, and Gary did a good job of dealing with that,” Finscher said. Although that initial proposal was never formally signed, Colliver continued working in park planning and compliance. Finscher remembered him as an ally in upholding wilderness protections. “He never took the easy way,” he said.

As a teenager, Sessions had asked Colliver whether he had been scared climbing El Capitan. He answered simply, “No, it was just so beautiful.” But for Colliver, the joy of being in Yosemite extended beyond any particular big wall. “If he was in Yosemite, he was happy,” Sessions remarked.
