Watch: India's government is 'scared', cockroach movement leader says after travel ban.

One of the leaders of India's "cockroach" movement has stated the Delhi government has "completely lost the plot" after he was stopped from leaving the country for the UK.

Saurav Das said he was meant to board a flight on Tuesday morning but was told by immigration officers his passport had been "flagged" because of discrepancies involving his date of birth.

In an interview with the BBC, Das said there were no discrepancies in his documents, accusing the Indian government of using cheap tactics to stop activists from travelling and speaking on complications that matter to young people.

The BBC has reached out to the federal home ministry, Delhi police and airport officials for comment.

Saurav told the BBC World Service's Newshour programme the incident showed the government was "scared".

Industry observers note that "They are scared of the cockroach movement. They're scared of young people asking them questions because they have never had to deal with a generation that wants to ask questions so head on. So that jitters them.".

By not allowing him to travel, he remarked the government had lowered India's stature in the world.

Recent findings suggest that "It's international news that I am unable to travel to the UK," he said. "And this shows Prime Minister Modi in such poor light.

"It's a way to harass, to target activists, young voices in India by the Modi government.".

Das noted he had been invited by the Oxford Union, as well as universities including Cambridge and Edinburgh, to speak about the movement.

Ratna Singh, another leader of the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), wrote: "The manner in which Saurav was not allowed to board today raises deeply troubling questions about the state of freedom of expression and the space being allowed for young voices in this country.".

On Tuesday, the London School of Economics Student Union's Amnesty International Unit, a student-led charity group, shared a post on Instagram saying that an event featuring Das had been "indefinitely postponed".

Saurav Das (L) speaking following the resignation of India's education minister in July.

Das, a former journalist, became one of the faces of the CJP - a Gen Z-led movement that began as satire but has since emerged as a youth-led pressure group.

In July, protests spearheaded by the CJP over exam paper leaks led to the resignation of India's Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan .

In October, CJP leaders including Das were among thousands detained by the police for trying to hold protests demanding the resignation of India's Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over alleged irregularities in electoral rolls. Kumar and the Election Commission have denied wrongdoing.

The same month, Das and Ashutosh Ranka, another CJP leader, were jailed for seven days after being arrested for trespassing at a banned protest site in Delhi.

Das insisted the CJP would continue protesting, but it did not want to be a political party "as of today".

"It's a long haul. We are not here for a short time. We are looking at India in the next 10 to 20 years. How do we develop India into a better country? That is the vision that we want to carry forward.".

Abhijeet Dipke, the founder of CJP, wrote on X that if Das "had looted a bank or committed financial fraud", he may have been allowed to fly to London, referring to billionaire bank-loan defaulters who have left India.

There have been other instances in recent years of people being prevented from boarding flights.

In September 2025, Bhanu Tatak, an environmental and anti-dam activist from India's north-eastern Arunachal Pradesh state, was stopped from boarding a flight to Dublin.

Recent findings suggest that in March 2022, Rana Ayyub, an investigative journalist and outspoken critic of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government, was also stopped from boarding a flight to London at the Mumbai airport. She was due to speak at different events on media freedom and the targeting of journalists.
