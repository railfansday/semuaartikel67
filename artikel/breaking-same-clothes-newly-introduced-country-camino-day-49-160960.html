I woke up around 6:30 AM, and since breakfast wasn’t until 7:30, I had time to relax with a cup of instant coffee and take in the misty ocean view.

Every morning, we share the exact same joke. One of us asks, “What do you want to do today?” and the answer is always, “Let’s take a long walk.” The follow-up question is, “What should I wear?”—which is equally funny since we only own one set of clothes.

Today was a significant milestone: the day we finally catch a ferry out of Spain and cross into Portugal.

After a great buffet breakfast at the hotel, we started hiking at 8:30 AM. The fog was still rolling in off the water, leaving the ocean barely visible as waves crashed against the rocks below us. Through the mist ahead, a massive church suddenly appeared—the nearly 900-year-old Monastery of Oia, nestled right along the coast in a picturesque little town.

Recent findings suggest that most of the morning followed dirt paths running parallel to the main coastal highway, occasionally merging onto a wide, yellow-paved shoulder. Reaching the end of the peninsula, we took a shortcut through a quiet town and arrived at the ferry dock around 12:30 PM.

While waiting, I grabbed a squid sandwich and a café con leche, while Knockerz ordered tea and some rather overcooked chicken fingers. Our ferry reservation wasn’t until 4:00 PM, so we hoped to snag space on an earlier boat.

Soon after our food arrived, a small ferry pulled up—a boat small enough to hold only about six passengers. Fortunately, only two other people were heading across, so we hopped right on.

Once we stepped ashore in Portugal, I checked my phone and realized we had gained an hour in a newly introduced time zone. Better yet, we had officially made it to a new country, bringing a whole new culture and language to explore.

Right on the waterfront stood a striking cathedral that felt noticeably different from the churches in Spain. Inside, decorative tiles lined the ceiling alongside carved wood, model boats hung from a side altar, and an intricate retablo resembled a tree growing out of a saint.

Since Portugal is famous for port wine, I stopped at a local bar to try a 10-year-old tawny port. It was sweet with a distinct licorice-syrup flavor. Next, I ordered a local red wine that arrived in a massive glass filled all the way to the brim—for just $1.50. Most places only pour a small portion, so I was shocked by the value.

Sources indicate that while chatting with fellow southbound Camino hikers we had met on the boat, I accidentally knocked over and spilled my glass. As we wiped up the mess, the waiter walked right back out and refilled my glass completely free of charge.

Recent findings suggest that because Knockerz hadn’t eaten her stale chicken fingers on the ferry, she ordered a fresh meal at the table. Our entire bill came out to just €8. Soon, a group of pilgrims from Quebec joined our table for a lively conversation before we set off to find a grocery store and our apartment.

Industry observers note that along the way, we got distracted by an empty, historic church filled with centuries-old artwork. This town is truly stunning.

When we finally tried to locate our apartment, we couldn’t quite find the entrance—and immediately experienced how warm and laid-back Portugal is. Several locals stopped to help us search. One man—who looked like he worked for the local water company—asked for our host’s phone number, called them directly, and stayed with us until we reached the door. When we thanked him in Portuguese, he pointed to the Camino bracelet on his wrist and smiled, “Bom Caminho.” We were completely blown away by the kindness.

After dropping our packs and heading back out for dinner, I noticed the side wall of our apartment actually shared a wall with the back of an old church altar (key detail for tomorrows story!). We picked up a few groceries, sat down in the main square with a couple of glasses of wine, and looked out over the white-capped water separating Spain from Portugal. Even though we thoroughly loved Spain, we couldn’t be happier to finally be in Portugal.
