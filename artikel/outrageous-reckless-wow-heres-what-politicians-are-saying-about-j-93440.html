Industry observers note that jensen Huang is back. The Nvidia CEO and founder, who was ousted from his firm a little over a week ago after revelations of extramarital affairs (including, critically, a dalliance with a suspected Chinese spy), has apparently been deemed too big to fail.

Huang, who co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and subsequently grew the chipmaker into one of the most powerful companies in the world, has now been reinstated by the manufacturer’s board. The move was one that President Trump lobbied for. On Monday, after Huang’s return was confirmed, the president posted an AI-generated meme of the CEO holding a woman’s bra, an image that recalled the famous photo from Trump’s own assassination attempt.

Just how and why all of this has happened is a question that everybody is still scrambling to figure out. Because Nvidia is an economically important firm, it is also a politically important one. Yet there’s a lot of disagreement over just how important Huang, himself, is — and whether his importance outweighs the alleged dangers of Chinese espionage to which his affair with Alice Huang may have exposed the company.

Even if Huang is back for now, the Nvidia founder may face an uphill battle given the changing political landscape wrought by the “blue wave” of Democratic victories that swept the U.S. midterms. Huang, now aligned with Trump, will face increased scrutiny from congressional Democrats — starting with a public Senate appearance scheduled for next week. He is also expected to testify privately before several House committees next month, including the Intelligence Committee, the Foreign Affairs Committee, and the China Select Committee.

Industry observers note that if initial responses from current political leaders are any indication, Huang has his work cut out for him. As news of his return broke on Monday, politicians and public figures from across the political spectrum weighed in — and many didn’t seem too pleased.
