Sources indicate that arsenal have been on a meteoric rise back to the top of the Premier League under Mikel Arteta in the past few years. The Gunners haven't been shy about spending significant sums of money to get back to the top, though, and this is shown in their wage bill.

The reigning English champions have signed some of the biggest stars around in recent transfer windows and have also splashed the cash on securing long-term deals for their most vital assets.

Amid those key developments within the squad, below is a full list of the Arsenal players' wages – including the reported salaries of key summer signings Bruno Guimaraes and Ezri Konsa.

Recent findings suggest that david Raya's move from Brentford to Arsenal was made permanent during the summer of 2024, following an excellent spell between the sticks for the north Londoners, where he won the Golden Glove. After signing fresh terms in October 2025, the Spanish shot-stopper is now pocketing £100,000 per week after proving himself among the best goalkeepers in the business.

Sources indicate that the Gunners managed to shift Aaron Ramsdale's substantial £100,000-per-week salary off their books, selling their back-up goalkeeper to Southampton in 2024. In his place, Kepa Arrizabalaga, following loan stints at Real Madrid and Bournemouth, joined from Chelsea, and he earns £60,000 every seven days as a result.

According to recent reports, arsenal won the race to sign Illan Meslier on a free transfer this summer after his contract with Leeds United expired. The Frenchman was highly rated at the beginning of his career but has struggled in recent years. Despite losing his place as Leeds' starting goalkeeper, he was handed a £45,000-per-week deal by the Gunners.

William Saliba and Gabriel have grown to be the best central defensive partnership in the English top flight. The duo have been equally impressive on the pitch in four successive title charges - with the most recent being successful - and they've both been rewarded with massive contracts in recent times.

Gabriel is earning £180,000-per-week, while Saliba has more recently put pen-to-paper on a five-year deal worth £270,000-per-week.

Sources indicate that summer signing Ezri Konsa earns the same amount as Gabriel, and just more than Ben White. The latter spent much of last season sidelined through injury, and has had a fight on his hands to re-establish himself as a regular starter.

In a recent development, arsenal confirmed the arrival of Riccardo Calafiori from Bologna in the summer of 2025, with the Italian defender earning £140,000 every seven days. Piero Hincapie enjoyed a successful loan spell at the Emirates, and is now earning £120,000 weekly after making his move permanent.

Industry observers note that jurrien Timber earns the same amount as the Ecuadorian, though he is in line for a pay rise after his contributions last term, which saw him fill in all across the backline where key injuries left some gaping holes. It is believed he has agreed a contract extension, although it is not yet known how much he will earn when he puts pen-to-paper.

Sources indicate that the highly-rated Cristhian Mosquera, signed from Valencia last summer, is earning £70,000-per-week at the club. Still just 22 years old, he could be a key player for the club for many years to come.

Recent findings suggest that breakthrough talent Myles Lewis-Skelly is one of the highest-earning teenagers in world football. He put pen-to-paper on a five-year contract worth £50,000-per-week last summer, a cool sum for someone who is still in the infancy of his career.

Industry observers note that arsenal, despite having one of the best midfields in the world, thought they needed to strengthen the area further after winning the Premier League in 2026. They went all out for Newcastle captain Bruno Guimaraes, with the Brazilian joining the English champions in a £75m move. He penned a four-year deal (with an option of an extra year) worth £250,000 per week.

Declan Rice and Martin Odegaard are among the best midfielders in the Premier League. The pair have been vital in Arteta's engine room in the past few years, although Rice is set to surpass Odegaard's £260,000-per-week contract with a massive £350,000 a week deal, according to BBC Sport. That will make the England international Arsenal's joint-highest earner.

Eberechi Eze's performances for Crystal Palace and England convinced Arsenal to fork out £67.5m in the summer of 2025, and he put pen-to-paper on a four-year deal worth £200,000-per-week. Scoring five league goals against fierce rivals Tottenham in his debut season is enough to make him worth every penny for the Gunners.

The extremely versatile Mikel Merino, who has been required to play in an array of positions including striker and in defence during his Arsenal career, earns £140,000 every seven days.

Fellow World Cup winner Martin Zubimendi joined the Gunners' ranks in the summer of 2025, and the former Real Sociedad anchor - who was high on the wish lists of both Real Madrid and Liverpool - is reportedly earning around £90,000-per-week for the privilege of playing alongside Rice, Odegaard and Guimaraes.

*Declan Rice's current contract is worth £260,000 a week.

Industry observers note that it comes as no surprise that Bukayo Saka is Arsenal's highest-paid player. He signed a four-and-a-half-year deal worth a whopping £300,000-per-week in early 2026. Not a bad increase on his previous £195,000 weekly wages.

Before that deal was signed, Kai Havertz (£280,000-per-week) was making the most money at the club. His switch from Chelsea to Arsenal raised eyebrows, but was clearly a good move for the 27-year-old, professionally and financially.

Arsenal made it their goal to sign a top striker last summer and they landed on Viktor Gyokeres. The Swede is earning £220,000-per-week at the club following his £55 million move from Sporting CP.

Christos Tzolis takes home £90,000 every seven days after moving from Club Brugge, while Ethan Nwaneri's £80,000 p/w salary is at the moment off the books again following his loan move to Borussia Dortmund.
