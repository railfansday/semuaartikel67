In the late 1800s, travelers arriving on the West Coast found that the gold rush had dried up, but another kind of treasure was waiting in its wake: the health spa. Soon, doctors back east were prescribing trips to the Golden State for ailing patients in search of restorative sunshine, mineral springs, and fresh air. California’s reputation as a destination for serious R&R has persisted ever since. From beachfront properties in SoCal to redwood hideaways along the Big Sur coast to vineyardside estates in Napa Valley, the best resorts in California are a kind of gold standard all their own.

It has been revealed that of course, it’s a big state with hundreds of different landscapes, so finding the right resort is all about knowing what makes you come alive. Looking to spend time on the links? There are legendary fairways waiting at Pebble Beach. Craving an off-the-grid escape? Decamp to a secluded sanctuary in the Santa Rosa Mountains. Prefer five-star amenities with near-clairvoyant service, or a home base for wine tastings and lavish dining? Consider it covered. Read on for the best resorts in California, where the good life is the real gold rush.

Recent findings suggest that auberge Collection’s third property in Napa Valley is set on a historic Wine Country ranch founded in 1856 by Judge Edward Stanly. Guests can choose from 135 cottages, rooms, and suites—decorated in oatmeal and beige hues with leather and oak furnishings—all with private terraces and outdoor showers. Standout amenities include Guatemalan heated robes, yoga mats, and Theragun massage tools. Bay Area food lovers make the trek here just to sample chef Anthony Stagnaro’s ingredient-focused meals at the resort’s Avroko-designed restaurant, Bear; others prefer to sip rosé at the French countryside-inspired Basin Bar or order a scoop of ice cream at The Window. But don’t expect to drink wine all day: This wellness-focused resort can curate fitness optimization programs, send you out on a cycling adventure with a pro, or arrange a hot-air balloon ride for the whole family. —Jen Murphy.

Recent findings suggest that while it’s tempting to paint Sonoma as a sleepy sweep of California Wine Country, the truth is, the county is every bit as—if not more—booming than neighboring Napa. It’s got Michelin-starred restaurants, shiny fresh tasting rooms, and major-name hotels cropping up on the regular. But to try a little of that sweet Sonoma of yesteryear, there’s no better place than Farmhouse Inn. Located near the Russian River—and within easy driving distance to charming Healdsburg, as well as some of the region's best vineyards—this 25-room property feels delightfully laid-back and intimate. The Inn has five different room categories, all with a country-chic look; bright and airy with white wainscoting, pillowy bedding, and haute-rustic touches that might include a personal welcome message on a small blackboard, a fireplace, and comfortable overstuffed chairs. The Farmhouse Restaurant occupies a beautiful space and offers an ever-evolving chef’s tasting menu with hyper-local ingredients paired with, naturally, a stellar regional wine list. All in all, Farmhouse Inn looks and feels like a classic New England country charmer—with California Wine Country street cred. —Rebecca Misner.

It has been revealed that this outstanding Napa Valley hotel is set on 33 acres of vineyards and olive groves—needless to say, the views are spectacular. Sumptuous cottages done in sun and earth tones have wood-burning fireplaces and refrigerators stocked with local wines and cheeses; bathrooms come with skylights and flat-screen TVs. The restaurant serves Wine Country cuisine with French and Mediterranean influences, and its terrace has heaters for dining out on cool evenings. Enjoy Italian ices and chilled towels at the pool and Reiki at La Pagode, an Asian-style pavilion. —Todd Plummer.

Napa has a handful of excellent hotels and no shortage of fabulous wineries and tasting rooms, but this is the first full-on luxury resort that marries the two. The property’s 85 rooms and suites, as well as 20 private residences, are surrounded by acres of vineyards. The bar and dining space are gorgeous—soaring ceilings, a glassed-in kitchen, and a wall of glass doors that open onto a huge terrace with a firepit—but made cozy by comfy booths and warm lighting. Northern California’s Wine Country retreats often model themselves on faraway places; destinations with longer traditions and more old-world gravitas. This is too bad, because their location, between the Pacific and the Mayacamas Mountains, is pure magic, and by this point on the domestic wine timeline, there’s no need to apologize for who you are. This is why the arrival of Montage’s first property in the region, and Sonoma’s first large-scale luxury resort, was so exciting when it opened in 2020. It celebrates its own landscape, leaving the 22,000 live oaks in place and pulling a palette of dun and moss from the surrounding sun-soaked vineyards. The restaurant offers a bounty of Sonoma’s best, with everything—from the Pliny the Elder IPA to the lamb shank—sourced from within miles. And the local sixth-generation vintners who frequent the sprawling patio bar are happy to remind you that there is, in fact, plenty of history in these parts. —R.M.

According to recent reports, there are golf resorts, and then there’s Casa Palmero at Pebble Beach. The course, Pebble Beach Golf Links, opened more than 100 years ago in 1919, and has enjoyed as long and storied a history as any. The resort, a former private residence that opened in 1999, is directly on the course—rooms overlook the first and second fairways—making it an absolute dream for golfers. But Casa Palmero offers so much more than tee times. The rooms have a countryside vibe—spacious with wood-burning fireplaces, exposed ceiling beams, and wrought-iron fixtures—while the Spa at Pebble Beach offers award-winning service. Plus, it’s only a five-minute walk down to the peaceful beach.

In a recent development, set on 500 acres in the foothills of the Santa Lucia Mountains along California’s Central Coast, Carmel Valley Ranch is located just shy of 13 miles from the Monterey Bay Aquarium and historic Cannery Row. At check-in, you’ll receive a list of the resort’s scheduled activities, and staff will likely share their favorites—and you should pay attention. Options range from guided hikes and yoga to archery lessons and picnics with Pablo and Paco, a pair of resident miniature donkeys. There are also multiple pools, tennis and pickleball courts, and an 18-hole Pete Dye–designed golf course. If the blinds are up at the on-site creamery, stopping by the window to watch the cheesemaker in action is a must. Along with chickens and a salt house, the organic garden is home to veggies, flowers, and honeybees. Menus at signature restaurant Valley Kitchen showcase fresh, local ingredients—many of which are grown or produced on the resort. —Dana Rebmann.

This beloved Major Sur resort, which dramatically overlooks the Pacific and is surrounded by acres of forest, received a total reboot in 2025. And while it may look new—rooms have all been updated with plenty of wood and neutrals, 15 luxe safari-style tents have been added to the roster, and the spa got a relaxation deck that looks out at the ocean—it hasn't lost its laid-back 1970s vibe. It's still, hands down, one of the most magical places to disconnect and get back in touch with nature. —Christina Ohly Evans.

Recent findings suggest that the Post Ranch Inn is a total immersion in the singular landscape of Big Sur, in environs defined by a sense of understated luxury. The Ranch’s 40 rooms have all been designed to both blend in with and have a light touch upon the land, and range from tree houses to clifftop suites with expansive views of the mountains to the east or, better yet, the ocean’s expanse to the west. Engage with the environment some more by exploring the property’s hiking trails, taking a tour of the chef’s garden, or signing up for a falconry lesson. —Lauren Sloss.

It might seem like a mistake to go to Santa Barbara and not stay close to the shore, but the moment you make the turn inland onto San Ysidro Road, you realize that being here isn’t about visiting Santa Barbara; it’s about visiting the ranch. Just 38 cottages, each with its own unique layout, decor, and view, are spread out over 550 acres, but rather than being scattered, they form a ring within the property. At the resort’s center is a collection of gardens as well as a rectangular pool that overlooks the canyon. Some of the curated finds you’ll see throughout are museum-quality art, antiques sourced from the owner’s world travels, and handmade wallpapers. But San Ysidro Ranch isn’t your typical hotel—as its history of having legendary guests like Jacqueline and John F. Kennedy, who honeymooned here, or John Huston, who finished writing the screenplay for The African Queen on the property, can attest. It continues its legacy as a celebrity refuge, as modern-day A-listers use this tucked-away spot to truly hide out and escape—which is what you should do too. —Samantha Brooks.

Whether you’re hiding away in a lush garden suite with a fireplace and a patio or just swanning around on the terrace for an alfresco farmers market lunch, it feels just right: low-key but ultra stylish. According to recent reports, arriving at Rosewood Miramar Beach, with its military-straight rows of white roses and pristine clapboard cottages, feels like landing on a postcard-perfect street in Cape Cod. If it all seems a little too perfect, well, there was time to get things right. The former Miramar by the Sea Hotel occupied these 16 oceanfront acres from 1889 until it closed for renovations in 2000. The hotel sat half-demolished for years, its second act tangled in red tape. It took SoCal billionaire developer Rick Caruso and the Hong Kong–based Rosewood Hotel Group to push the project through. And while it may have been tempting to scrap the past after such a treacherous road to reopening, the newly introduced Miramar deliberately throws back. Lemon-yellow-and-white striped umbrellas flank a rectangular pool where the Scoop Shop serves up fountain drinks, ice cream cones, and burgers. Overlooking the ocean, there’s Caruso’s, the type of place where you order a martini with your cacio e pepe alongside a heaping dollop of la dolce vita (the chef hails from Harry’s Bar in Rome)—and, in a state where “formal flip-flops" are a thing, one that blessedly enforces a dress code. In case you start to feel too lost in the 1950s, an outpost from Malibu Farm and a Goop boutique remind you that this is indeed modern-day Cali. —R.M.

Recent findings suggest that montage Laguna Beach is a polished, residential-style resort set on 30 oceanfront acres along one of Southern California’s most scenic stretches of coastline. Opened in 2003 as the brand’s flagship, the hotel continues to deliver consistent high-level service, a strong food and beverage program, and a true sense of privacy despite its size. Every one of the 263 rooms has an ocean view and outdoor space, and the property's layout, from the cliffside paths, tiered lawns, and access to Treasure Island Beach, makes it easy to settle in and stay awhile. Guests skew toward special-occasion travelers, West Coast couples, families, and loyal Montage regulars who return year after year. Highlights include the 20,000-square-foot Spa Montage, the renovated Mosaic Pool, direct beach access, and the return of Studio Mediterranean, the resort’s stellar fine-dining venue. While there’s no tennis or golf on-site, the concierge can arrange access at a nearby club. —Michelle Gross.

Toeing the line between self-improvement sabbatical and bona fide vacation, Sensei Porcupine Creek is the ideal getaway for the chronic over-achiever: a place where real R&R is achieved proactively, through science and strategy, and in utter seclusion. Formerly the private estate of tech wizard and Silicon Valley billionaire Larry Ellison, Sensei’s co-founder, this intensely isolated retreat hidden in the foothills of the Santa Rosa Mountains is the second outpost for Sensei, and it marked the brand’s debut in the continental United States. (Its first, Sensei Lānaʻi, A Four Seasons Resort, opened in 2019.) But it’s also the brand’s first true standalone property, and with a lush, 75-acre golf course, a glamorous lagoon-style pool, and tennis facilities modeled after those at the nearby Indian Wells Tennis Garden—which hosts the annual BNP Paribas Tennis Tournament—it really does stand alone. —Betsy Blumenthal.

What is the best luxury resort in California?

San Ysidro Ranch, at the foot of the Santa Ynez Mountains, is one of the best resorts in the world, let alone California. The property sprawls across 550 acres of citrus groves, gardens, and hiking trails, and each of its 38 rooms is a luxury cottage with a stone fireplace and antique furnishings that reflect its 130+ year history. Dignitaries like Winston Churchill, Audrey Hepburn, and Lucille Ball have stayed here—not to mention JFK and Jackie Kennedy, who called it their honeymoon hotel in 1953.

What is the difference between a hotel and resort?

A hotel is mainly a place to sleep. A resort is the destination itself. Resorts typically sit on larger grounds and bundle the amenities you’d otherwise have to leave to find, like spas, pools, golf, multiple restaurants, and organized activities, so you can spend your whole stay on-property. In California, that might mean a round of golf at Pebble Beach before dinner, or a day of hiking, a massage, and a wine tasting without needing to hop in your car.
