THIS WEEK IN ENTERPRISE by Robert Hof.

Anthropic came through with its initial public offering Thursday, but did it just signal a top in the AI trade?

The artificial intelligence model maker’s IPO went almost picture-perfectly, really, producing a 17% pop that wasn’t so high that it left an excessive amount of money on the table but high enough to call it a success. But it seemed to take some of the air out of AI stocks. Continued worries over AI spending and prospects, as well as factors such as a likely interest-rate hike, dragged down the entire market, including other AI names from Google, Microsoft and Meta to AMD, Broadcom and Nvidia. Asian stocks too.

And on Friday it got even worse, as the Nasdaq crashed 5.6%. Bye-bye, $4 trillion. No doubt Anthropic stock will be at least as volatile as other AI stocks — today it fell 9% — but it can live with that alongside the more than $90 billion now in its bank account. Now all eyes turn to OpenAI, which finds itself chasing after Anthropic in more ways than one, with plans to go public sometime in next year.

Nvidia posted its usual better-than-expected earnings this week, which didn’t help its stock much but indicates, along with interim CEO Colette Kress’ bullish comments during the earnings call, that it’s going to be fine for some time to come in disgraced former CEO Jensen Huang’s absence.

Meantime, Huang noted he’s open to returning — but that’s not only appallingly premature at best, the board appears adamant it’s not going to happen. The supposed friends clamoring for his return are just reinforcing their selfish, clueless nature, the attitude that has plunged tech into such low esteem that auto salespeople and journalists have more manufacturer now. Maybe those guys on X (always guys) approve of behavior like this.

According to recent reports, what’s with all the outages all over the place? That’s what everyone’s asking, and we looked into what’s behind them all. It doesn’t seem to be the obvious thing — cybersecurity attacks — or really any single cause. Perhaps it’s a lot of 20th century technologies that just aren’t ready for the AI-driven speed of the 21st century, making everything less reliable. Or: All those AI-driven fixes to vulnerabilities are getting done so fast they’re breaking things along the way. Still, is it only a coincidence that the U.S. cyber director just got canned this week?

Recent findings suggest that palo Alto Networks remains one of the most acquisitive cybersecurity companies out there, once again laying out a tidy sum this week, buying AI startup Applied Compute $4.5 billion. It’s a sure bet consolidation will accelerate as AI enables more and much faster attacks. Indeed, late Friday came a report that an equally acquisitive CrowdStrike is looking to buy Zscaler, which has been a consistent acquirer itself.

Industry observers note that next week’s significant earnings reports include Dell, HP, Nutanix, Workday and Zoom. And then we can all take a brief respite for Thanksgiving before gearing up for AWS re:Invent the following week in Las Vegas, with its blizzard of news. SiliconANGLE, theCUBE and theCUBE Research will be onsite with news, analysis and interviews.
