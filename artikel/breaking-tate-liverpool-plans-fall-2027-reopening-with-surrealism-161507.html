Tate Liverpool will reopen in fall 2027 after a £30 million transformation with the exhibition “Surrealism: Staging Dream Worlds,” a nod to the museum’s inaugural Surrealist show in 1988.

According to recent reports, the surrealism exhibition will run from fall 2027 to spring 2028 and include works by Salvador Dalí, Eileen Agar and Man Ray. According to Tate Liverpool, the show will also explore the “important role of surrealism in the history of theater and dance” through the movement’s major commissions for stage sets and costumes. Also on view will be Dorothea Tanning’s costume designs for the ballet The Witch, photographs of Sheila Legge performing as the “Surrealist Phantom of Sex Appeal” at the opening of the 1936 International Surrealist Exhibition in London, and material from the archive of Ted Joans. “Surrealism invites us to see the world differently and what better way to reopen Tate Liverpool. This exhibition will spark imaginations, surprise visitors and reward curiosity, offering a fresh perspective on one of art’s most celebrated movements. It also brings a wonderful echo of the surrealist exhibition that opened our gallery in 1988, while taking visitors on an exciting fresh journey,” mentioned Jemima Pyne, interim director of the Tate Liverpool, in a statement.

Tate Liverpool closed in October 2023 and was scheduled to reopen in 2025, but fundraising difficulties pushed the date back to 2027. The architecture firm 6a architects is redesigning the gallery’s home, an 1846 warehouse on the Royal Albert Dock, as part of a £35 million ($46.5 million) renovation.
