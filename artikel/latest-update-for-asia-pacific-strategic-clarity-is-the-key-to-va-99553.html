📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, SINGAPORE, Nov. 23, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Only 20% of employees in Asia Pacific have strong strategic clarity about AI, according to a newly introduced report from Boston Consulting Group (BCG). Among regular AI users in APAC, strong strategic clarity lifts the number of employees reporting measurable business impact by 22 percentage points, versus 4 percentage points for access to AI tools.

In a recent development, employees with strong strategic clarity know that AI is a priority, understand the strategy and their role in it, and have clear guidance on what to do with the time AI saves. Communication also shapes trust. Where employees say leadership communicates very clearly about AI, 61% fully trust leadership, compared with 3% where communication is not clear.

These are among the findings of For Asia-Pacific, Strategic Clarity Is the Key to Value, BCG's second annual APAC AI at Work report, based on a survey of 6,349 employees in Australia, China, India, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, Singapore, South Korea, and Thailand. Respondents in Australia, India, and Japan were surveyed for the global AI at Work 2026 study of 11,749 employees, published in June, and the rest in September and October.

Sources indicate that AI use is now near-universal across Asia Pacific. Eighty-five percent of employees use it regularly, up from 78% in 2025 and in line with 84% globally. Japan saw the sharpest increase over the past year, from 51% to 79%, while India leads the region at 96%.

Far fewer employees use AI deeply. Only 26% use it regularly and for at least half of their daily tasks. Those employees save an average of 14.3 hours a week, compared with 6.2 hours for people who use AI less than a few times a week.

Many companies are going beyond deploying AI tools. Sixty-nine percent of APAC employees say their organization uses AI to reshape end-to-end work, compared with 57% globally, while 45% say it is used to invent newly introduced business models. At companies that reshape or invent with AI, 60% of employees report measurable business impact, compared with 43% at companies that only deploy AI tools.

In a recent development, "Getting AI into employees' hands was the easy part, and Asia Pacific has largely done it," remarked Jeff Walters, managing director and senior partner at BCG and a coauthor of the report. "The measurable impact comes from what happens next, when companies rebuild how work gets done around AI.".

Industry observers note that the report also finds a "joy paradox," with work becoming both more demanding and more enjoyable. Among employees with strong strategic clarity, 76% enjoy their work more and 47% report a higher cognitive load, versus 30% and 21% with no clarity. Only 7% of them are anxious about AI, against 19% without clarity.

"Each market in Asia Pacific needs its own playbook, but three priorities hold everywhere," mentioned Romain de Laubier, Asia-Pacific regional chair at BCG X and a coauthor of the report. "Make strategic clarity the number one priority, measure the value AI creates rather than how much it is used, and put the guardrails in place to use it responsibly.".
