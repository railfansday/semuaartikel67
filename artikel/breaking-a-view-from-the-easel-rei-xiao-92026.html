In a recent development, welcome to the 364th installment of A View From the Easel, a series in which artists reflect on their workspace. This week, Rei Xiao paints a world of surreal, feline figuration into being while navigating the artist visa system and managing their studio assistant (a dignified cat named Astrid).

How long have you been working in this space?

Describe an average day in your studio.

According to recent reports, I work from my apartment. The most predictable parts of my studio day start with putting on my sunscreen and painting clothes. It’s a very small space that I paint in, so I usually have to push stuff aside when I start, like my painting cart, stool, and trash bin. I, then, turn on my air purifier, open the lids of my palettes, scrape paints from the previous session, and play either TV shows I’m familiar with or podcasts hosted by comedians in the background. The day usually ends with my cleaning the palette, emptying and wiping my brush cleaning tank, and diligently washing my brushes. Then I wash my face and go to sleep.
