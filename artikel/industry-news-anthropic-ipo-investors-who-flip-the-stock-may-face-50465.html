Industry News: Anthropic IPO: Investors who flip the stock may face brokerage penalty

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Retail investors with access to Anthropic's initial public offering who sell the stock quickly after buying could get penalized by their brokerage firm.

Anthropic said in an IPO prospectus that it expected to make shares available to retail investors through various brokerages, including Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi, E*Trade and some of their respective affiliates.

Follow CNBC's live coverage of Anthropic's IPO and Nasdaq debut.

It has been revealed that these broker-dealers have different "anti-flipping" policies meant to discourage investors who purchase IPO shares — including those of Anthropic — from dumping them quickly. Charles Schwab is the only one that doesn't have such a policy in place.

"One purpose is to discourage 'flippers' who are just buying with the hope of making a quick profit," Jay Ritter, director of The IPO Initiative at the University of Florida, remarked in an email. "Their selling could put downward pressure on the price once trading starts.".

In a recent development, investors who run afoul of anti-flipping rules may be prohibited from accessing future IPOs, perhaps for several months or even more than a year for repeat offenders. In the worst case, the brokerage may bar them from all future IPOs.

These policies apply only to retail investors who place an order with their brokerage firm to buy the Anthropic IPO at its offering price — something many investors don't have access to.

Industry observers note that for example, Fidelity customers are eligible to participate in the Anthropic IPO if they have at least $25,000 in certain assets, the firm said. Even then, customers are "evaluated and ranked" based on factors like account assets to determine who can access limited shares that may be available, it said.

Importantly, anti-flipping policies don't apply to retail investors who access shares later, once Anthropic stock starts trading on a public exchange, Ritter noted.

Of the retail brokerage firms listed in Anthropic's prospectus, Charles Schwab appears to be the only one without an explicit anti-flipping policy.

Industry observers note that "At this time, Schwab does not apply a flipping restriction to any IPOs unless required by an issuer," Margaret Farrell, a organization spokesperson, wrote in an email. "This is subject to change at Schwab's discretion.".

She confirmed that an anti-flipping policy doesn't apply to the Anthropic IPO.

Other firms aren't as lenient, however.

Here's how they could penalize IPO flippers.

E*TRADE said it "prefers investors hold shares for at least 30 calendar days.".

However, its policy doesn't lay out an explicit penalty for failing to do so.

Selling or transferring out of an IPO within 30 days "may affect eligibility to participate in other new issues in the future," according to its policy. "E*TRADE reserves the right to exclude customers from future participation," it added.

Investors who are allocated Anthropic shares and sell within the first 15 calendar days from the start of trading in the secondary market won't be able to participate in future stock IPOs through Fidelity for a defined period of time, it said:.

"The first day clients can sell without being labeled a 'flipper' is the 16th calendar day after the IPO trades," Fidelity remarked.

Robinhood's policy stipulates that selling IPO shares within 30 days of the IPO is considered flipping.

Such investors "may be prevented from participating in IPO access for 60 days," it mentioned.

SoFi said that selling an IPO position within the first 30 calendar days after trading starts "may affect your ability to participate in future IPOs on SoFi.".
