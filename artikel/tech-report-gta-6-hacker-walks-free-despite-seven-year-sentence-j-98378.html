Tech Report: GTA 6 Hacker Walks Free Despite Seven-Year Sentence, Just Days After Launch

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The hacker behind GTA 6's massive 2022 leak was sentenced to seven years in prison on November 23. But instead of returning to prison, Arion Kurtaj walked out of Woolwich Crown Court that day, just four days after Rockstar's long-awaited game released.

As Dexerto reports, the 21-year-old was released on licence after the court took account of the time he'd already spent in custody. He's also subject to a five-year order limiting what he can do online. So while the sentence sounds surprising, his release doesn't mean the charges were dropped.

Industry observers note that kurtaj was part of the Lapsus$ hacking group and had been detained for years while his case worked its way through the courts. In December 2023, he was placed in a secure hospital after being deemed unfit to stand trial. A later assessment found him fit to plead, and he was moved to prison in January 2026.

On November 16, he pleaded guilty to a series of offences connected to cyberattacks on Rockstar Games and other companies. DualShockers covered his guilty plea at the time, noting how close it was to GTA 6's November 19 release. His sentencing followed exactly one week later.

Kurtaj had already spent enough time in custody to be released on licence rather than sent back to prison. Walking out of court doesn't mean he can go straight back to his old online life.

He's now subject to a five-year Serious Crime Prevention Order restricting his online activity, including a ban on using Discord and Telegram. It's a significant condition for someone who committed cyberattacks while already on bail.

Sources indicate that justice Turner also warned Kurtaj about wasting his abilities on further offences. The judge described him as talented and urged him not to spend his life repeatedly returning to prison.

The case also involved attacks on Uber, Revolut, and BT/EE. According to the UK Crown Prosecution Service, Kurtaj's activities exposed sensitive information and disrupted business systems. In the BT/EE case, prosecutors linked his intrusion to unauthorized SIM swaps, which could let criminals take over other people's calls and messages. He also admitted to possessing a mobile phone in prison, an offence dating to March 2026.

Back in September 2022, Kurtaj obtained 90 GTA 6 gameplay videos from Rockstar's systems and posted the footage online under the name TeaPotUberHacker. It gave everyone an early, unfinished look at the next Grand Theft Auto, long before Rockstar was ready to show it off. Rockstar sources reportedly confirmed the footage was genuine shortly afterwards.

One of the stranger details is how he did it. Kurtaj was already on bail for other cybercrimes and had been moved to a hotel for his own protection. From there, he used a phone, the hotel's television Wi-Fi, and an Amazon Fire Stick to carry out further attacks, including the one on Rockstar.

The leaked footage indicated parts of Vice City and early gameplay involving the game's two leads. Kurtaj also tried to blackmail Rockstar by threatening to release the game's source code unless the company contacted him. The 90 videos were released; the source code was what he threatened to put out next.

The damage wasn't just about fans seeing the game early. Rockstar reportedly spent around $5 million and thousands of staff hours dealing with the breach, according to Dexerto. That was on top of the disruption to other companies targeted in the same hacking spree.

Those clips spread across the internet more than four years before GTA 6's November 19 release. Now that the game is finally out, Kurtaj's case has reached sentencing too. He's left prison, but the online restrictions attached to his release will continue for another five years.
