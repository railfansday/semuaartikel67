Mario Kart World Officially Adds Fresh Courses, Fan Requested Features

🌐 Source: Polygon • 📅 25 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (484 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways

  • Now, more than a year after its release, Nintendo is bringing upcoming courses and fan-requested features to the Switch launch game.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that to no one’s surprise, Mario Kart World remains the best-selling game on the Nintendo Switch 2, with an impressive 16.48 million copies sold. When you consider that there are only around 26.35 million Switch 2 owners total, that means almost 63 percent of Switch 2 players have a copy of Mario’s latest kart racer. It's a somewhat staggering figure, given that the game costs $79.99.

Now, more than a year after its release, Nintendo is bringing upcoming courses and fan-requested features to the Switch launch game.

Nintendo released the latest update for Mario Kart World on Tuesday, and players have responded to it well. Ver 1.9.0 is a free update that adds eight remixed tracks, among other changes. The updated courses use a somewhat hilarious naming convention of slapping a 2 onto the end of each stage name. As a Redditor explained, there are basically three types of course remixes included: reverse variants, entirely upcoming track layouts, and an existing path made into its own track. Specifically, Salty Salty Speedway 2, Mario Bros Circuit 2, and Airship Fortress 2 are new tracks with reused assets; DK Pass 2, Wario Stadium 2, Desert Hills 2, and Koopa Troopa Beach 2 are reverse variants; and Shy Guy Bazaar 2 turns an existing path into its own track.

It has been revealed that beyond the remixed courses, the patch also adds two newly introduced routes for Knockout Tour mode, in which racers compete until only one is left standing. There’s Feather Rally, which connects Mario Bros Circuit to Boo Cinema, and Lucky Cat Rally, which links Question Block Ruins to Sky-High Sunday.

Lastly, 1.9.0 brings an enhanced Replay feature that lets you view past races and battles, so you can relive that time you were in first until you got hit by a blue shell, Bullet Bill, and a lightning bolt.

Sources indicate that unlike some of Mario Kart World’s updates, fans have responded well to the game’s latest patch. “This was a great update and adds more variety to the game’s track selection while being free DLC. I actually can’t wait to see more for it,” one Redditor wrote. Another user commented, “This is a great format for an update- 8 upcoming tracks (with a combination of reverse and new layouts) and 2 new tour rallies. They should have been doing this every 3 or 4 months after release, in my opinion.”.

In a recent development, despite Mario Kart World’s impressive sales figures and generally positive reviews, the game has divided its player base due to certain design decisions. Specifically, many players disliked that the game’s online mode featured interconnecting highways (called intermissions) as race destinations instead of traditional tracks, something Nintendo still hasn’t fixed. Hopefully, this latest revision indicates the game is trending back in the right direction and that a more fundamental rework is on the way.
