Dressing Like Your Boyfriend Is Actually Fine

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that is it so wrong to dress like your boyfriend? Clearly, the internet thinks so. Most recently, Bella Hadid has found herself at the center of this conversation, with people highlighting her various boyfriend eras like some sort of sartorial identity crisis. From the emo-adjacent vibes when she dated the singer, The Weeknd, to the cowboy-rodeo-chic aesthetic with Adan Banuelos, and now her hockey-game-attending, matching outfit moment with new man Nathan Lacy, Bella has seemingly been accused of basing her clothes after her romantic partners.

I personally think it’s a silly argument given that Bella Hadid is one of the most notable supermodels with an eccentric, well-documented high-fashion sense of style. She was awarded Lyst’s 2022 Most Powerful Dresser, and she famously styles much of her own day-to-day looks. So who cares if, during a particular season of her life, she wants to tap into the aesthetic of the man she’s dating?

The same argument has been made about Ariana Grande, Kim Kardashian, and, more recently (even by her own sisters, who do the same thing), Kourtney Kardashian. Ariana’s personal style has never particularly screamed fashion girl to me, and I’m a huge proponent of the idea that not everyone needs to be one anyway. Grande works with stylists to build her looks, so if on her off days she wants to dress in tandem with her beau, there’s truly nothing wrong with that.

Industry observers note that kim Kardashian, meanwhile, notoriously skyrocketed into the high-fashion space during her relationship with Kanye West. To this day, Kim gives style credits to Kanye’s eye for fashion and his introducing her to the world she so desperately wanted to be part of. Did she take what he taught her and expand upon it, eventually establishing herself as a fashion mogul in her own right? Yes. So technically in the long term, borrowing an aesthetic from her boyfriend then husband clearly worked out for her.

And while I personally wouldn’t wear Kourtney’s ultra-rock-girlfriend aesthetic, I actually think it's fun. After spending a decade being terrorized by Scott Disick (terrible boyfriend, excellent family friend to her sisters), I think it’s perfectly fine for my girl to enjoy someone so much she clearly feels safe enough to wrap herself in his sartorial aesthetic.

It has been revealed that this idea that women are in the wrong for “adopting” their man’s fashion seems uninteresting to me. If anything, women should be stealing men’s aesthetics and making them their own more often. There's something cheeky about the whole thing, a small flip of patriarchy on its head. How often do we see the internet go viral over the invisible labor women put into the men they date? Making their style better, recommending the haircut that best frames their face, teaching them how to groom themselves. It’s always give, give, give. So why not take something too? Because the next video down my timeline after those labor of love, “girlfriend effect” ones is women complaining about men taking everything they gave in a relationship (from hair and clothing reccs) and bringing it to the next woman.

Industry observers note that there's something rather liberating about having your own style to return to while allowing yourself to experiment with whatever your boyfriend of the moment is into. It’s like playing dress-up. You’re enjoying a man, his world, and even his wardrobe, without having to make any of it your permanent identity—simply trying it out like free samples of ice cream. And when he’s no longer serving you, you move on. Thank you, next, as Miss Grande states.

Because women are so often expected to do the emotional and personal-care work in relationships, it’s honestly fine to me seeing these celebrities lean into their boyfriends’ fashion sense. (Side note: Incredible that these men have a style at all—it can be sartorially bleak out there!) Maybe his hockey jerseys look good on you. Maybe his leather jackets are better than yours. Maybe you just like the way you look in a cowboy hat right now.

It isn’t hurting anyone. And if anything, the most radical part of all is letting the men, for once, shape the fashion in their relationship. High time we all start letting them handle the burden of lessening the swag gap.
