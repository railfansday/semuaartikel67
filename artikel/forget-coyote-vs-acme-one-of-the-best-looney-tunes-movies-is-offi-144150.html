Forget ‘Coyote vs. Acme,’ One of the Best Looney Tunes Movies Is Officially Leaving HBO Max

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that 2026 was the year the Looney Tunes finally got their day in court. Or, well, on-screen. Nearly three years after Warner Bros. made the much-maligned decision to pull Coyote vs. Acme from its release schedule and shelve it indefinitely, Ketchup Entertainment brought the live-action animation hybrid to theaters with plenty of fanfare. Dave Green's legal comedy that David Zaslav and tech giant didn't want you to see became a success, raking in over $126 million at the box office and earning rave reviews to the tune of a Certified Fresh 96% score from critics and a Verified Hot 94% score from audiences on Rotten Tomatoes. However, the Will Forte and John Cena feature isn't Ketchup's first foray coming to the rescue of the beloved cartoon characters.

Last year, the distributor released Looney Tunes' first fully animated theatrical film, The Day the Earth Blew Up, after Warner Bros. opted to shop the project. While not as high-profile as the very public near-killing of Wile E., it also proved to be a worthwhile move for Ketchup, with a solid $15.5 million box office and similarly strong 86% and 88% scores from critics and audiences. The story centered on Daffy Duck and Porky Pig, both voiced by the late Eric Bauza, as they become Earth's only unlikely hope after they uncover evidence of a sinister alien plot at their local bubblegum factory. To save everyone, they must put their antics aside and work together, unless they drive each other crazy first. The film also marked the directorial debut of animation veteran Peter Browngardt, who envisioned the sci-fi premise while working on the HBO Max animated series Looney Tunes Cartoons.

Industry observers note that in a fitting bit of irony, after its theatrical run was up, The Day the Earth Blew Up went on to become a streaming hit on HBO Max, even though it was Warner Bros.' decision not to release it on the platform following its merger with Discovery that ultimately saw Ketchup land the movie. It served as a reminder that Looney Tunes was still a force to be reckoned with and paved the way for Coyote vs. Acme to find its freedom beyond its production home. Now, though, the animated feature is about to look for fresh residence yet again. In one month, on December 26, Daffy and Porky will say, "That's all, folks!" and leave HBO Max behind right after Christmas.

There's an undeniably bittersweet air to The Day the Earth Blew Up in the wake of Bauza's sudden passing in October at the age of 46 from hypertensive cardiovascular disease. Though the Emmy winner lent his voice to many projects beyond Looney Tunes, he carried the torch from Mel Blanc for many of the franchise's animated characters, from Daffy to Bugs Bunny and Tweety. Browngardt's film is some of his most extensive work as Looney Tunes' first animated feature, and it represents one of a handful of his final performances as the Warner Bros. icons. He gave his final bow as Daffy earlier this month in the short "Daffy Season," which debuted in theaters attached to the Bill Hader-led The Cat in the Hat, ending his time with the franchise, though not representing his final voice performance altogether.

The Day the Earth Blew Up: A Looney Tunes Movie leaves HBO Max on December 26. Stay tuned here at Collider for more on all the biggest titles coming to and leaving streaming throughout the year.
