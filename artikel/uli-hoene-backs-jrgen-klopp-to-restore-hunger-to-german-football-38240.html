Uli Hoeneß backs Jürgen Klopp to restore ‘hunger’ to German football

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bayern Munich legend and honorary president Uli Hoeneß believes Jürgen Klopp can restore the discipline and hunger he feels have been missing from the German national team. Speaking at the Sport BILD Awards, Hoeneß backed the Germany head coach to impose his personality on the team while stressing that the rebuilding process will require patience.

In a recent development, “I think he will bring discipline and hunger back in,” Hoeneß said. “He is hungry himself. But I didn’t expect him to lay hands on everything to move quickly. We need to give him time. I also expect that many things will improve under Per Mertesacker, who is now overseeing the youth setup.”.

Klopp’s Germany delivered their clearest statement yet on Monday night, thrashing the Netherlands 5-0 at Berlin’s Olympiastadion. The emphatic victory secured top spot in Nations League Group A2 and a place in the quarter-finals, giving Germany a significant result as Klopp continues to reshape the national team.

Hoeneß’s comments on hunger were not restricted to Klopp or the German national team. The 74-year-old argued that German football has been affected by broader social changes, recalling the environment in which he developed as a young player.

“I used to play football on the street after school, from two in the afternoon until nightfall,” Hoeneß noted. “That no longer exists today. We live in a country where most people are doing very well. I see this as the greatest danger for our football as well. If someone comes from Brazil, Portugal, or Argentina, they want to climb the social ladder. That is what we lack.”.

Recent findings suggest that “We must learn again to work hard if we want to be successful,” Hoeneß continued. “That applies not only to footballers, but to the whole country.”.

Industry observers note that for Hoeneß, Klopp’s task therefore extends beyond tactical adjustments or short-term results. The Bayern honorary president believes restoring the mentality he associates with successful German teams will be just as important as the structural work being undertaken in Germany’s youth setup.
