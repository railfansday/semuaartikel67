Amazon is buying Decart’s AI chip software for a reported $7.5B and bringing its founders aboard

Amazon confirmed Monday morning that it will acquire Decart’s AI optimization technology, known as the Decart Optimization Stack, and bring on the 3-year-old startup’s founders and team of AI researchers and engineers.

According to recent reports, amazon didn’t disclose financial terms. Amazon agreed to pay about $7.5 billion for the assets, Bloomberg reported, citing a person familiar with the matter. The deal is tied to performance targets over five years, Globes reported.

The deal could boost Amazon’s push to compete with Nvidia, which sells most of the chips used for training and running AI models today. Amazon makes its own AI chips, called Trainium, and has signaled intends to start selling them directly to other companies, beyond its own AWS data centers.

“We believe we’re going to have a very significant business selling chips externally,” CEO Andy Jassy said on the company’s earnings call last month.

Amazon confirmed the deal the same day Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, ending a leadership shakeup at the chipmaker that put CFO Colette Kress in the interim role.

Decart’s software is designed to get more performance out of each chip, one of the main selling points Amazon needs to win those external customers. Microsoft is building its own Maia chips, mainly for its own AI services, and Google has its TPUs.

Decart’s software has also run on chips from Nvidia and Google. Amazon didn’t say whether that will continue.

“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, Amazon’s senior vice president of foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing, in the blog post announcing the acquisition.

Decart’s three co-founders are joining Amazon: CEO Dean Leitersdorf, his brother Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev. Amazon isn’t buying Decart’s AI models, Bloomberg reported. Those include Lucy, which edits live video in real time, and Oasis, which generates interactive virtual worlds.

According to recent reports, the deal runs through Annapurna Labs, Amazon’s chip unit, and Decart’s technology will be used to improve performance on Amazon’s Trainium and Inferentia AI chips, according to Globes. The Israeli business publication reported that Annapurna co-founder Nafea Bshara led the negotiations for Amazon.

The founders, who own about 64% of Decart, have committed to stay at Amazon for five years and could receive about $4.8 billion collectively, Globes reported.

Amazon bought Annapurna, also founded in Israel, in 2015 for a reported $350 million. Its engineers went on to design Trainium and Amazon’s other custom chips.

Amazon and Decart have worked together since 2025. Decart was among the first customers on Trainium3, and Amazon remarked last December that Decart was running real-time video four times faster on Trainium at half the cost of GPUs.

The deal follows Amazon’s release last month of TorchNeuron, a tool that runs common AI code on Trainium with minimal changes. Both moves target the software advantage that has kept developers on Nvidia hardware, as GeekWire explained when the talks surfaced in October.

Decart drew interest from other big buyers before Amazon. Anthropic was in talks to acquire it for about $6 billion, Bloomberg reported in August, before it walked away in September. Nvidia held earlier acquisition talks, according to CTech, and invested in Decart’s $300 million funding round in May.

Industry observers note that “From day one, our mission has been to redefine the boundaries of what AI can do and to make it dramatically more efficient along the way,” Dean Leitersdorf remarked in Amazon’s announcement. “Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own.”.

The deal is subject to regulatory clearance. Amazon didn’t say what happens to Decart’s AI models or to its existing customers, such as eBay, which is also an investor in the startup.
