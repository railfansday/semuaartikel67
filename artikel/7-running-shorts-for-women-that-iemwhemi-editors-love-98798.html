There’s nothing more humiliating than having a huge sweat stain on your butt after strenuous mile repeats—been there, done that. A good run is supposed to lift your mood, not bring you down, so finding the best running shorts to keep the endorphins flowing (and your butt dry) is essential.

In a recent development, not all workout shorts are made the same. Some offer more compression, while others are less restrictive and have convenient features like pockets—the holy grail! “You need a place to carry all your resources—pockets are a non-negotiable for running gels, electrolytes, phone, wallet, and keys,” claims Upcoming York-based running coach and personal trainer Amanda Katz, NASM-CPT.

While many athletic shorts can be used for different purposes, running shorts usually have a built-in interior liner, sweat-wicking material, and a nonconstrictive design to keep you cool and comfortable as you rack up mileage in your sleek running shoes.

Sources indicate that I researched and gathered the best running shorts from a running coach and editors who’ve personally tested them. Whether you’re training for a marathon or are a hobby jogger, all the shorts I picked are from trusted activewear and athleisure brands that will benefit you in the long run (literally). Running shorts with sweat-wicking fabric are the way to go, Katz claims. Most shorts use a blend of polyester, spandex, and/or nylon, which lifts moisture from the skin while staying flexible during movement. Based on testing, it’s best to avoid cotton since it doesn’t dry nearly as fast and clings to the skin when wet.

In a recent development, it’s notable to determine what kind of rise and inseam you desire. High-waisted running shorts offer more coverage and tummy control, while a mid-rise waist sits right at the hips. Ultimately, what you find most flattering for your body type is the best option.

The inseam is the length from the crotch to the bottom. A longer length will show less skin but may disrupt the range of motion, while some running shorts favorites, like the Lululemon Hotty Hot High-Rise Lined Short, offer a 2.5-inch option if you like a mini fit.

As for an interior liner or compression, this will make running shorts fit more snugly. “Compression is a matter of preference, but the goal in this material is to boost recovery,” states Katz. If you want a less constrictive fit, it’s probably best to avoid that feature.
