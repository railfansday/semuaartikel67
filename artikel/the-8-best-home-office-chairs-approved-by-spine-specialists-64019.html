Here are some key features to consider while you search for the best office chair for your body and needs.

Office chairs are not one-size-fits-all, which is why adjustability is crucial. “Adjustability lets the chair match your body so your spine stays in a neutral, supported position,” notes Dr. Gbolohan Okubadejo, a spine surgeon and the head of The Institute of Comprehensive Spine Care. The lumbar support should match the curve of your spine, and your feet should be able to sit flat on the floor, according to Okubadejo.

“An unsupportive chair, or one that does not fit properly, can lead to chronic musculoskeletal injuries over time,” Lees states. “This may lead to pain that persists throughout the day, affecting sleep and stress levels. Long-term injuries may also include nerve damage that can lead to weakness or numbness.”.

It has been revealed that most home office chairs are height adjustable and have armrests that can be raised. If a chair has a headrest, which isn’t essential, that may be adjustable as well. “Being able to tweak depth, tilt, and lumbar support prevents hot spots and improves blood flow during long sits.” Okubadejo says. “Small adjustments through the day break up static strain, so muscles don’t fatigue and posture doesn’t collapse.”.

Sources indicate that lumbar refers to the lower back, and supporting this curve in your spine can relieve disc pressure and keep your pelvis in a neutral position, according to Okubadejo. “Good lumbar support lowers the risk of stiffness and pain during long hours at a desk,” he states.

Like chair height and seat cushioning, lumbar support will vary by person. “Postural muscles start to relax after a few minutes of sitting,” Lees indicates. “This leads to added stress on the spinal joints and extremity joints.” Luckily, many office chairs allow you to adjust the height and curve of the lumbar support. Okubadejo recommends options that can move up and down as well as in and out instead of stationary lumbar support.

Recent findings suggest that the main components of a comfortable office chair are a sturdy frame, a thick seat cushion, and a supportive, breathable back. Most office chairs these days have a metal or plastic frame with a foam cushion and mesh back, although you can find upholstered and leather versions.

Sources indicate that if you have the opportunity to test chairs in person, make sure that the seat is firm enough for you. “If the cushion is too soft, it won’t offer the support needed over time,” Lees notes. “A cushion that is too firm could create pressure points and possibly affect blood flow in the legs.”.

The materials of a chair can contribute to its overall weight and weight limit—both of which are key to keep in mind during your search.
