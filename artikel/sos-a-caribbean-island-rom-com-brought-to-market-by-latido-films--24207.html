’S.O.S’ has a photographer marooned on a deserted Caribbean island with his actress models, held to be among the sexiest women in his country. Paradise? Think again.

Chief International Correspondent.

Latido Films, a sales agent on Rodrigo Sorogoyen’s “The Beasts,” Netflix mega-hit “The Platform” and Ventana Sur 2025 breakout “The Whisper,” is bringing to market “S.O.S,” a blue sky Caribbean island rom-com which renews Latido’s relationship with top Colombian producer Diego F. Ramírez at 64A Films.

A producer on Sundance hit “Dog Eat Dog,” a founding stone in Colombia’s modern film industry, Ramírez has gone on to produce one of Colombia’s most ambitious non-streamer shows, “Escobar, el Patron del Mal,” as well as Toronto premiere “The Dog Washer” and “Killing Jesus,” the feature film debut of Laura Mora, director of “One Hundred Years of Solitude.”.

In a lighter turn for tougher times, ’S.O.S’ weighs in in two ways as a crowdpleaser, a blue sky – literally – comedy, set on an uninhabited Caribbean islet, which also offers the lures of romance and second-chance drama.

In it, Jaime, a once hot-shot photographer, is shipwrecked on the white-sand beach island with Cata and Valeria, the stars of an upcoming telenovela, rated as his country’s most beautiful women.

Paradise? The sojourn sets off as hell. Cata, a post peak-fame actress has been reduced to playing the mother of Valeria, an influencer who broke out aping Cata. The two hate each other’s guts. Battling to survive, however, all three have to smooth their differences and work together in what morphs into a survival adventure and second chance story where they re-connect with the far more sincere people they once were.

Manuela González (Netflix’s ‘Fake Profile’) plays Cata, Juan Fernando Sánchez (“La Huésped”) Jaime and María Camilo Giraldo (“The Ritual”) Valeria.

Fast-paced, but allowing a glimpse of key plot beats, the teaser begins with the shipwreck, flashes back to the photo shoot – “my aunts were your fans,” Valeria tells Cata cattily during makeup – then an establishing shot of the isle in the middle of a vast nowhere.

In a recent development, the trio have food at hand, such as a coconut, but they don’t know how to open them. time exiled from a community hammock. They build a hammock, but Jaime is banished. They seek refuge in a cave, which is infested by bats.

There is, however, a hint in the teaser’s parting shot, however, that love could be in the air between Cata and Jaime.

“S.O.S.” is produced by Colombia’s 64 Films, headed by Ramírez and Nancy Fernández for 64A Films, with Hans García and Ingrid Cruz for the Dominican Republic’s Hannah Films.

Recent findings suggest that “The tone that I’m thinking of is ‘The Blue Lagoon,” with the paradisiacal exuberance of ‘Ticket to Paradise,’” said director Nicolas Reyes (“Decisions: Some Win and Others Lose”), also a first AD on the Colombian unit of “Paddington in. Peru.”.

“For a sales agent, the most interesting and stimulating to be in on a project from its birth, as was the case with ’S.O.S.’ said Latido Films Head of International Sales Juan Torres. “The understanding with 64A is remarkable and we are confident this will have a knock-on in the film’s commercial results.”.
