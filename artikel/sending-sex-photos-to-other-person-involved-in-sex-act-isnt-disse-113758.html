White, defendant was convicted of many crimes, the most serious of which was rape; but one of the crimes was "breach of privacy" under the Kansas revenge porn statute. An excerpt (focused solely on the breach of privacy claim) from Friday's opinion by Kansas Court of Appeals Justice Thomas Malone, joined by Justices Angela Coble and Lori Bolton Fleming:.

White and Sarah (a pseudonym) met as children and, after reconnecting in their 20s, dated for more than 10 years. During that time, they lived together and were, for a time, engaged. But, in 2017, their relationship ended and White moved out of Sarah's home. White and Sarah continued to communicate and see each other occasionally, maintaining an on-and-off sexual relationship. Over the next two years, White would persistently call, text, and email Sarah, and if she did not respond to his messages, he would make threats about committing suicide, which Sarah took seriously. Sarah knew that the relationship was "toxic" and recognized the "abuse that went on throughout the years" but believed she was "trauma bonded" to White and still had feelings for him….

Sarah eventually got a protective order against White, but he "did not stop sending Sarah texts, emails, and voicemails":.

Sources indicate that on one occasion in June 2021, White emailed Sarah with an attached video of the two of them engaging in sexual activity. Near that same time, White texted photographs to Sarah of the two of them having sex…. [T]he State charged White with two counts of breach of privacy for White unlawfully disseminating the illicit video and photographs to Sarah ….

The breach of privacy statute provides:.

Sources indicate that "(a) Breach of privacy is knowingly and without lawful authority: …. (8) disseminating any videotape, photograph, film or image of another identifiable person 18 years of age or older who is nude or engaged in sexual activity and under circumstances in which such identifiable person had a reasonable expectation of privacy, with the intent to harass, threaten or intimidate such identifiable person, and such identifiable person did not consent to such dissemination." ….

The court held White didn't violate the statute:.

Here Sarah consented to the photos being taken and the video being made. White later emailed and texted the sexually explicit material only to Sarah. How did that conduct breach Sarah's privacy? ….

Sarah had an expectation of privacy as to the photos and the video to the extent that she could reasonably expect that no one other than she and White would ever see the sexually explicit images. White did not violate that expectation of privacy.

In a recent development, the evidence showed that White transmitted the sexually explicit material to Sarah with the intent to harass her. But for there to be a breach of privacy under [the statute], the term "disseminating" requires that the illicit material be sent to someone other than the person depicted in the material and who consented to the illicit material being made. White's conduct here more than likely constituted harassment by telecommunication device in violation of K.S.A. 21-6206, but we find it did not constitute breach of privacy….
