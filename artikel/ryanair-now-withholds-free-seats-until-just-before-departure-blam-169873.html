Ryanair Now Withholds Free Seats Until Just Before Departure, Blames Customer Feedback

Recent findings suggest that ryanair has quietly updated its policy for how it assigns seats to those people who don’t pay the seat assignment fee, as reported by The Irish Times. While I wouldn’t consider this to be anything unreasonable, what I’m amused by is the carrier’s justification. The airline claims that it’s doing this due to customer feedback, rather than to generate more revenue.

Recent findings suggest that ryanair is known for being an unapologetic ultra low cost carrier. The airline has low base fares, and then charges fees for just about everything. Among other things, the airline charges to assign seats in advance, and those who don’t pay for specific seat assignments are just randomly assigned seats (and often in a rather punitive way).

However, the way the airline is randomly allocating seats has quietly changed as of late October 2026, coinciding with the start of the winter schedule. Historically, the airline has automatically assigned seats to passengers without seat assignments around 24 hours before departure.

Recent findings suggest that some passengers seemed to “game” this, and would essentially “gamble.” What they’d do is wait until 24 hours out to see what seat they were assigned for free. Then if they weren’t happy with their automatic seat assignment, they could still pay to assign a better seat.

Industry observers note that under the upcoming policy, the airline still issues a boarding pass up to 24 hours out, but without a seat assignment. Then the boarding pass automatically shows the seat assignment within four hours of departure.

The logic here seems pretty clear — some people do care somewhat about where they sit, but want to avoid fees if they can. By withholding seat assignments until hours before departure, passengers are presumably more likely to pay for seat assignments in advance, to avoid that uncertainty.

Ryanair has confirmed this seat assignment policy change in a statement, sharing that “the timing of the allocation of free random seats is determined at all times by the airline.” Right, nobody disputes that the airline has the right to do this.

So, what’s the logic for this policy change? According to the airline, it’s “to allow those passengers who wish to select their preferred seat more time to do so. We had a number of complaints this summer that passengers could not book their preferred seat on the day of travel as it had been already randomly allocated as a free seat up to 24 hours prior to departure.”.

The Ryanair representative went on to say that “passengers who choose a free of charge random seat clearly don’t mind where they sit, and their digital boarding pass is automatically updated between three to four hours prior to departure, which is well before they arrive at either their departure airport or their boarding gate.”.

Ryanair is of course within its rights to have this policy, but I think the logic is pretty obvious, and it’s to generate more revenue. After all, 24 hours out people would see what they were randomly assigned at online check-in, and would still have a lot of choices if they wanted to pay for seats, since most people don’t check-in online exactly at the 24-hour mark.

But within a few hours of departure, virtually everyone has checked in online, and not many seats will be left. So I imagine the goal is to condition more customers to pay for seat assignments in advance. I love the claim that customers complained that their preferred seats on the day of travel were randomly allocated to others for free:.

According to recent reports, ryanair has a new policy for automatic seat allocations. In the past, the airline would automatically assign seats to passengers 24 hours out, giving them quite the window to “gamble” on the free seat assignment, and then pay for a different seat assignment if they weren’t happy with the free one.

The airline has now started only assigning free seats at most four hours out. The airline claims that this is due to customer feedback, so that passengers have more time to assign their choice of paid seats. However, I imagine that’s not quite accurate, and this is instead about conditioning people to pay for seat assignments, rather than “gambling” on the free allocations.
