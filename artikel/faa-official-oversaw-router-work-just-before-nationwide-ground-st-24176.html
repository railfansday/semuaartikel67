FAA official oversaw router work just before nationwide ground stop, report claims

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that an official with the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) was on the ground in Arkansas when new telecommunications wires were replaced, an action that preceded Friday's national ground stop by eight minutes, according to the Washington Post.

The FAA official oversaw the work, tested it, and signed off on it, according to the Post's report. The work involved replacing a router and swapping old copper wires for new fiber-optic cables, part of a national effort by the FAA to upgrade the airline industry's communication infrastructure.

In a recent development, the fact that an FAA official greenlit the work complicates Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's efforts to point fingers at L3Harris Technologies, a tech services provider and contractor tasked with updating the wires in Arkansas and leading the FAA's fiber-optic upgrades. Duffy said on Saturday that his agency is looking into whether "incompetence" by L3Harris led to the 4.5-hour ground stop.

In a recent development, responding to the Post's request for comment, the FAA pointed to statements Duffy made on Tuesday in a news conference at the Capitol. During the conference, Duffy noted that he wanted L3Harris to "hire better minds" for the wire replacement work and was considering bringing in latest contractors to help.

Industry observers note that an exact cause for the communications outage has not yet been confirmed, an anonymous source told the Post. Duffy told reporters on Saturday that the replaced router in Arkansas was faulty and flooded the network with over 1,000 connection attempts every few seconds, overwhelming it and making it difficult for planes in the air to communicate with air traffic controllers.

The FAA and L3Harris officials are at the moment studying the Arkansas router, according to the Post. That device was known as a Juniper router, the Post reported, and L3Harris has stopped installing them, though dozens have already been put in service.
