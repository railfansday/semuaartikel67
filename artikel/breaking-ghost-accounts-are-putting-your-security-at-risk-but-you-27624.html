Breaking: 'Ghost Accounts' Are Putting Your Security at Risk, but You Can Protect Yourself

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, these so-called "ghost accounts"—all the old, forgotten logins that are still active but not in use—could expose your data in the event of a breach, increasing the risk of your information being used in malicious ways. That's why it's important to go through the effort to find and delete dormant accounts. Here's how to get started.

It has been revealed that as McAfee describes, ghost accounts may retain lots of sensitive data, such as your contact details (phone numbers, emails, and addresses), purchase histories, payment details, and passwords. While this doesn't mean your information is sure to be compromised, the more places it lives, the more opportunities there are for bad actors to get ahold of it.

You're less likely to be keeping tabs on activity in older accounts, which may also have weak or reused passwords that haven't been updated in years. If that company or service experiences a data breach, not only is your stored data there exposed, but that information (including your password) can be used to access other accounts using tactics like credential stuffing and phishing. This also increases the risk of identity theft, as threat actors can use your name, birth date, and other information to open fraudulent accounts.

Unfortunately, finding and removing forgotten accounts can be tedious. If you have a password manager, the easiest way to start is to audit older items as well as look at security reports that identify weak or reused passwords, as well as data breaches that have impacted your accounts. If you've saved credentials in your browser keychain, go through the same process to find dormant accounts.

Next, audit accounts you've created using single sign-on, such as Google, Apple, or Facebook. For Google, this is under Account Settings > Linked apps. For Apple, go to System Settings > [Your Name] > Sign in with Apple on your Mac (or the same path in the Settings app on iOS). On Facebook, this is under Settings & privacy > Apps and websites. Disconnecting these accounts won't automatically delete them, but you can add them to the list to take action. Finally, you can search your email(s) on HaveIBeenPwned and username(s) on IDCrawl or WhatsMyName to find dormant accounts.

Once you've found accounts you no longer use, the next step is to deactivate or delete them, which you may be able to do through the settings or by reaching out to customer support. At the very least, remove all identifying information and change the password, and if possible, patch the address to a burner using your browser's or password manager's hide-my-email feature. If you no longer have access to an old account, that doesn't mean it's been deactivated and your information removed, so you may need to reach out to customer support to find out how to access and delete it.
