Breaking: MacBook Neo vs MacBook Air: Which should you buy right now?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that apple’s Mac lineup is stronger than ever this year, largely thanks to the new MacBook Neo. If you’re deciding between the MacBook Neo and the MacBook Air this holiday season, here’s everything you need to know.

Recent findings suggest that the MacBook Neo is Apple’s most affordable laptop ever, starting at $699 (presently on sale for $599). It features a solid aluminum design that comes in a range of fun colors, including Silver, Blush, Citrus, and Indigo.

It has been revealed that unlike Apple’s other Macs, the MacBook Neo is powered by an A-series chip rather than an M-series chip. It uses the A18 Pro chip originally introduced with the iPhone 16 Pro, featuring a 6-core CPU and 5-core GPU.

In a recent development, the MacBook Neo comes with 8GB of unified memory, with no additional RAM configuration options available. It starts with 256GB of storage, with a 512GB configuration available for an additional $100. That $100 upgrade also gets you Touch ID, which is not included in the base model.

There are some other trade-offs compared to the MacBook Air. The MacBook Neo lacks a backlit keyboard, MagSafe charging, and a Force Touch trackpad. It also has more limited USB-C connectivity and supports just one external display.

Recent findings suggest that still, the MacBook Neo offers an impressive feature set. It has a 13-inch display, up to 16 hours of battery life, a 1080p FaceTime camera, and the full macOS experience.

Industry observers note that the MacBook Air, meanwhile, was updated earlier this year with Apple’s M5 chip. It starts at $1,299, making it $600 more expensive than the entry-level MacBook Air. The MacBook Air is currently on sale with a starting price of $999.

For that $600 upgrade, the MacBook Air offers several key advantages over the MacBook Air. The M5 chip has a 10-core CPU and up to a 10-core GPU, enabling better sustained performance than the MacBook Neo.

Industry observers note that the MacBook Air also starts with 16GB of unified memory and 512GB of storage, both double the entry-level MacBook Neo. You can configure it with up to 32GB of memory and 4TB of storage, while the MacBook Neo is limited to 8GB of memory and 512GB of storage.

Apple rates the MacBook Air for up to 18 hours of video streaming and 15 hours of wireless web browsing, compared to 16 hours and 11 hours, respectively, for the MacBook Neo.

As I wrote in my review earlier this year, I think the MacBook Neo is a great Mac, full stop … not just because of the great price. It can handle the vast majority of things most people will throw at it. This includes web browsing, basic photo and video editing, research, and much more.

Industry observers note that the M5 MacBook Air, meanwhile, offers more power and more configuration options. This makes it a great option for people who need more storage, RAM, and power than the MacBook Neo can offer.

One of my favorite things about the MacBook Air is that it’s available in two screen sizes. The 15-inch MacBook Air is one of my favorite Apple laptops with a combination of the ultra-thin and light design and the larger screen. I think a lot of people forget it exists, but it’s an excellent choice.

Industry observers note that to recap, I think the MacBook Neo is a great choice for everyday tasks like web browsing, email, writing, and streaming. The MacBook Air is a better fit for more demanding workflows, including photo and video editing, coding, working with large spreadsheets, and heavy multitasking.

Industry observers note that beyond performance, the MacBook Air offers a handful of niceties that you won’t find on the MacBook Neo. These include a backlit keyboard, a larger display with True Tone, MagSafe charging, a better webcam and speakers, and a Force Touch trackpad. It also includes Touch ID on every configuration, longer battery life, and support for two external displays.

It has been revealed that if it’s in the budget, I think the MacBook Air is an excellent choice for all Mac shoppers. It’s plenty powerful, thin and light, and is highly configurable compared to the MacBook Neo.
