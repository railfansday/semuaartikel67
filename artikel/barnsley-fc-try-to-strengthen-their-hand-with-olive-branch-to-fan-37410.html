The Reds have tried to strengthen their hand by agreeing to meet with unhappy supporters but the biggest thing in their favour at Oakwell could be doubts in the minds of the visitors.

For a first League One away game as Owls manager, Dave Challinor would surely like to face a team as down in the dumps as Barnsley.

Only kept off the bottom by Bromley's woeful goal difference, Daniel Stendel's side have not won in the league since September 2, nine matches ago.

Consecutive cup wins in which they put 14 goals past lower-division opposition feel like a mirage after they were put back in their box by Wigan Athletic. Captain Cameron McGeehan was booked, and is suspended for Friday's home game.

With a pre-match protest planned, chairman Neerav Parekh will meet independent supporters groups and the media to try to address the disquiet, but not until Friday December 4, with a wider fans’ forum to follow.

So it all points one way, until you consider the geography.

Because derbies can be great levellers, and more importantly the Owls have a hang-up against opponents from Yorkshire.

In their last 14 such matches, their only victory was a 2-1 win over Middlesbrough on Easter Monday 2025, when Anthony Musaba scored an 89th-minute winner.

Much of the squad played no part in many of those defeats, and last season's matches against Boro, Hull City and Sheffield United can be written off, so handicapped were the Owls.

But the pattern has continued this season in a division they are expected to be strong in.

Bradford City inflicted their first defeat back in League One with an August 1-0 win which flattered the beaten hosts.

And points dropped against Huddersfield Town and Doncaster Rovers were nails in manager Henrik Pedersen's coffin.

It took a late fightback and a Terriers implosion for Wednesday to escape with a 2-2 draw from a game Martin Drury's side had much the better of for all but the last few minutes.

If that amplified doubts about Pedersen's management, Doncaster's third and at present last league win of the campaign, 2-0 at home to the Owls, confirmed them in the eyes of the board.

The angry away fans who chanted "You're getting sacked in the morning" were right.

It shows how psychological hurdles like this can outlive personnel changes on the field and in the dugout.

It should not have much bearing on Friday, but most probably will. And with four White Rose teams for company in League One, it is a bad division for any Yorkshire team to have a derby hang-up in.

On the more positive side, win in front of a packed away end which can be heard loud and clear in the tunnel and it will go a long way towards forging the bonds Challinor needs to make the most of Wednesday's biggest asset – their fanbase.

But if Barnsley can play on their derby insecurity, a home win would do wonders at the best time of the season to find some momentum.

Even for a derby, the stakes are high.
