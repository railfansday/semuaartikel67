Airports can be a bit intimidating. You’ve got the TSA line to worry about, but more importantly, you have your outfit to figure out. Unfortunately, some people judge what you’re wearing while traveling (it’s me, I’m people). But I also don’t think looking presentable has to be that hard. Some guys show up in full sweats, looking like they're about to spend the next six hours in bed. Others dress like they're heading straight from baggage claim to a dinner reservation. Personally, I think there's a happy medium somewhere in there. You should be comfortable enough to sleep through your flight, but you also shouldn't have to change your entire outfit the second you land. From comfortable pants and breathable T-shirts to stylish sneakers, these are the essentials worth packing for your next flight.

And while I love a good matching sweatsuit as much as the next guy, there are plenty of other ways to dress comfortably for the airport. We’ve got stretchy trousers that look pretty darn good, a solid pair of sneakers, and layers that work alone or together. It's about finding clothes that do a little bit of everything, especially if you like to travel light.

If you’re looking to spruce up your fit for your next flight (and to become someone’s airport crush), here’s what to know.

I'm not saying you can't wear jeans on a plane, but why would you when you have so many more comfortable alternatives? You'll be sitting for hours, probably in a seat that doesn't give you much room to move, so the last thing you want is a waistband digging into your stomach. Look for pants with a little stretch, breathable fabrics, and a relaxed fit that won't feel restrictive when you're sitting down. Brands like Vuori and DUER have gotten particularly good at making pants that feel like loungewear without necessarily looking like it. And if you're committed to sweatpants, that's perfectly fine, too. Just go for a pair with a cleaner, more structured fit.

You really don't need to overthink your airport T-shirt. A good cotton tee will do just fine, especially if it's something you already wear regularly. Personally, I'd avoid anything too tight or made from a heavy fabric, since airports and airplanes can get surprisingly stuffy. A slightly relaxed fit gives you more room to move and makes layering easier, too. I tend to gravitate toward simple, solid-colored tees because they go with practically anything. Uniqlo's AIRism Cotton Oversized T-Shirt is a good example of something that's affordable, comfortable, and easy to dress up or down depending on what else you're wearing.

I don't care if you're flying to Miami in the middle of July; bring a sweatshirt. Airplane cabins have a habit of feeling like refrigerators, and I'd much rather have an extra layer than spend an entire flight shivering. A good crewneck or hoodie is probably all you need, preferably something soft that isn't too bulky to stuff into your bag when you're not wearing it. I'm partial to oversized sweatshirts for traveling, though you don't need to go so substantial that you're drowning in fabric. Something like Alo's Accolade Crewneck works well because you can wear it with sweatpants or a nicer pair of trousers. And honestly, if you're desperate, a sweatshirt can always double as a makeshift pillow.

Depending on where you're headed, a lightweight jacket might be one of the more useful things you bring. I'm not talking about a huge puffer or anything that'll take up half your carry-on. Think windbreakers, chore jackets, and lightweight layers that you can easily throw over a T-shirt or sweatshirt. If you're traveling somewhere with unpredictable weather, a technical jacket from Patagonia or Arc'teryx makes sense. Otherwise, a simple chore jacket from somewhere like Quince or Gap can make an otherwise basic airport outfit look a little more put-together. Plus, you'll probably wear it during the trip anyway, so it's one less thing to pack.

Quince 100% Organic Herringbone Twill Shirt Jacket.

In a recent development, between getting to the airport, navigating security, and walking what feels like three miles to your gate, you'll probably spend more time on your feet than you realize. That's why I'd prioritize comfortable sneakers over anything else when putting together an airport outfit. You want a pair with good cushioning, enough support, and ideally something easy to slip on and off. New Balance, Hoka, and On all make sneakers that fit the bill, though I'd stick with a pair you've already broken in rather than trying something new on travel day. And since you're probably only bringing one or two pairs of shoes on your trip, bonus points if they match the rest of your wardrobe.

On The Roger Centre Tennis Court Shoe.

It has been revealed that you really don't need much here. In fact, I'd argue the best airport outfits are pretty simple. But a few accessories can make even a basic T-shirt-and-sweatpants combination feel more intentional. A classic Yankees cap from '47 is great for early flights when you don't feel like doing your hair, and a good pair of sunglasses is always useful when you're heading somewhere sunny. I also like wearing a watch when I travel, mostly because I don't want to keep pulling out my phone to check the time. None of these things are essential, obviously, but they're easy ways to make the outfit feel a little more like your own.
