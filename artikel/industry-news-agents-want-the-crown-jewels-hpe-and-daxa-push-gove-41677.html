Industry News: Agents want the crown jewels: HPE and Daxa push governance down to the data layer

UPDATED 11:56 EST / NOVEMBER 18 2026.

Enterprises are handing AI agents their most sensitive data, and governance is struggling to keep pace. Techniques such as semantic caching are emerging as a way to enforce controls inline without slowing agents down.

Moving agents from pilot to production means letting them read, change and even delete the records that matter most. That shift makes data access the central security question for agentic AI, according to Huseni Saboowala (pictured, right), co-founder and chief executive officer at Daxa Inc.

Recent findings suggest that “For agents to really get to value, they need to be able to interact with your real enterprise data, and that includes your crown jewels, your intellectual property, your customer data in your [customer relationship management] and your proprietary trading algorithms,” he said. “That creates an unprecedented level of risk for enterprises because the agents are not just reading data; they’re also able to now go manipulate data at scale.”.

Industry observers note that saboowala and Robin Braun (left), vice president of AI business development at Hewlett Packard Enterprise Co., spoke with theCUBE’s Enterprise Editor, Paul Gillin, during coverage of SC26 on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. Daxa is a partner in HPE’s Unleash AI program, which offers pre-validated software on HPE Private Cloud AI. (* Disclosure below.).

In a recent development, firewalls, data loss prevention and identity management still have a job to do. But they were built for consumers and applications, not software that reasons over data, Saboowala explained.

According to recent reports, “Those agents are operating, though, at layer 8, what we call the cognitive layer,” he remarked. “This is where you have the prompts, the responses, the retrievals and the tool calls happening. Traditional tools really are not operating at this layer.”.

According to recent reports, saboowala’s answer is reasoning-based access controls, backed by agents that flag abnormal behavior. HPE builds governance into its Private Cloud AI stack from the start, according to Braun.

“Being able to build in that governance from day one allows you to understand what is it going to look like so that … you’re not bolting it on later,” she said. “Instead, it’s a part of the workflow.”.

Industry observers note that timing matters as much as policy. Offline governance arrives too late for agents working at machine speed, so enforcement must happen inline through semantic caching, specialized small language models and a distributed data plane, Saboowala explained. A regional bank that deployed Daxa with HPE has since connected its agents to source code and regulated customer data.

“I believe that the winners in this agent era are not just the ones who have the most [graphics processing units],” Saboowala said. “But they will be enterprises who have agents they can trust with their data.”.
