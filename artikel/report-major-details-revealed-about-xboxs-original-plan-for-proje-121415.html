We've been hearing about Xbox's Project Helix console for some time now, with Xbox always talking very vaguely about what we can expect from it, and perhaps that's because the vision has changed for the system over the years.

Today, the folks over at Windows Central are revealing details about the "original strategy" for Xbox Project Helix, which include that it was going to be a "full gaming PC" akin to the ROG Xbox Ally with the ability to run console games on top. Steam support would have been included, as would have Windows 11 in general.

They were seemingly even going to drop the paywall for multiplayer games in the process.

Recent findings suggest that "The original blueprint for Helix, which ran under a different codename until last summer, was for it to essentially be a full gaming PC with the capability to run all of your existing Xbox console games on top. It would be functionally similar to the Xbox Ally PC gaming handheld, with a gamepad-first Xbox-style full-screen interface, complete with the capability to exit out to the Windows desktop and install whatever you want. That, indeed, included Steam.

According to recent reports, as part of this strategy, Xbox would have had no choice but to drop the paywall for multiplayer from Xbox Game Pass (formerly Xbox Live Gold). There's no universe where a true PC could function with a multiplayer paywall. I understand that, indeed, removing the paywall was costed into this strategy.".

All of this was apparently conceived prior to the component crisis that's caused hardware prices to rise significantly in recent times, and ultimately there's a feeling that the plan might have changed somewhat as a result.

Recent findings suggest that xbox CEO Asha Sharma has also become the head of the brand since Helix was in the conception phase, and she may have her own ideas about dropping multiplayer paywalls and integrating Steam into the next-gen console. After all, a lot of people have wondered what Xbox stands to gain by partnering with Steam instead of focusing on the Xbox Store.

It has been revealed that it's crucial to highlight here that nobody knows what the current plan is at Xbox HQ, and Windows Central theorises that "maybe Microsoft doesn't even know itself". There's every chance we still may be looking at a console that can run Steam games and not require a multiplayer paywall, but there's probably an equal chance that Xbox has gone back the drawing board and decided to reel in its scope when it comes to PC support.

Sources indicate that let's wait and see, eh? We know we're definitely getting the ability to play PC titles on Xbox Project Helix no matter what, but the safe bet is that it'll be via the Xbox Store - and who knows what'll happen with the multiplayer side of things.

For what it's worth, Xbox has this month committed to Helix being a console and not a PC, which seemingly goes against the original initiative for a so-called "full gaming PC". In that regard, it appears that things have changed.

"We're very clear on the design goal: Helix is designed to be a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC. It is not a PC that can also play console games. The goal is making it easy, intuitive, and instant to access my games. But if you're a hardcore gamer or a power user who wants to have that deeper level of customizability across settings and performance options, you can absolutely go do it.".
