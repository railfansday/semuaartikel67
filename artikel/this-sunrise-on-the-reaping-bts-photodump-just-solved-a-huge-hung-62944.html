Happy Hunger Games! Sunrise on the Reaping is the latest book-to-screen adaptation to hit theaters, which means we finally get to see Haymitch compete in the games. We already knew there would be a lot of references to the original movies and throughout the press tour, but there's much more talk of the events in The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes than we expected. One star just revealed another one that's barely in the movie, and it's kind of a significant deal. SPOILERS AHEAD!

As you may recall, the end of The Songbirds and Snakes had Lucy Gray Baird disappearing into the forest, leaving President Snow betrayed and alone. We never knew what happened to her next. Enter a photo dump posted by Whitney Peak, who plays Lenore Dove in Sunrise on the Reaping. Check out the third photo below:.

A post shared by WHITNEY (@whitneypeak).

The photo has Sunrise on the Reaping's Joseph Zada pointing to a gravestone. Here's what it indicates:.

That you may see sweet Lucy Gray upon the lonesome wild.

The stone was featured in the movie when Haymitch visits the grave of Lenore Dove, but we couldn't make out the words in the inscription until this photo. How poetic is it that Lucy Gray Baird and Lenore Dove's final resting places are right next to each other? The picture seems to solve the mystery of Lucy's body being found at some point. It would make sense given there's 40 years between the two prequels.

In a recent development, the gravestone heavily suggests Lucy Gray Baird was buried in the Covey graveyard, but since we only have a photo to go off of, there's still not a simple answer around what happened to Lucy. I looked around on a Reddit thread discussing the photo, and there's a lot interesting ideas floating around. Here's my favorite:.

In a recent development, I like to think that she was able to survive. With all that the covey knew of the land from their travels, it's conceivable that she was able to survive and either come home when she was old enough that no one would know her except the covey. They'd hide her and keep her name quiet, hidden on the outside of the fence. She'd fish, dig up katniss. I think in order for her song to be passed along, because she wrote the Hanging Tree, she would have had to finish it and teach the others.

It has been revealed that I think it absolutely makes sense that Lucy might have escaped, but ultimately wanted to come home at some point. There's something really sweet about imagining her going home to her Covey and living life among the others in peace after Snow was consumed by being the president of the Capitol. I really like how even with the headstone in sunrise on the reaping, Lucy Gray's fate is still a mystery. I think Suzanne wrote it really really well.
