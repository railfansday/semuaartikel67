In a recent development, leading cryptocurrency Bitcoin (BTC -2.09%) is down 30% since the start of the year. Last week, it also briefly fell below $60,000 as market sentiment soured amid concerning developments at Nvidia, which dragged the overall stock market down.

Sources indicate that bitcoin, which has often followed the movements of the broader market, has shown that it's no safer an option than other high-risk investments. With there being plenty of uncertainty still ahead and a potential for more rate hikes, could Bitcoin fall to $50,000 or lower before the end of the year?

According to recent reports, government regulation can play a key role in the acceptance and growth of cryptocurrency. It's worth noting, then, that since the midterm elections took place earlier this month, Bitcoin's value has dropped immensely. From around $77,000 before the midterm elections took place to around $61,000 now, the cryptocurrency's value has already fallen by more than 20% since then.

Investors may be concerned that, even with a seemingly pro-crypto government, significant reform couldn't take place. The Clarity Act, which would establish clear rules for crypto trading, failed to pass and may now be even less likely under a Democratic-controlled Congress.

To make matters worse, there's the potential for more rate hikes ahead. Next month, it appears all but certain that there will be at least a 25-basis-point hike, and an outside chance that rates could go up by 50 basis points. Interest rate hikes are bad news for speculative investments such as crypto. A massive rate increase could indeed give Bitcoin a further push down in value.

CRYPTO · As of 10:32 PM ET 11/23/26.

According to recent reports, bitcoin is a highly speculative investment, and a lot depends on investor sentiment. Unfortunately, the appetite for risk lessens as interest rates rise. And without a catalyst on the horizon to increase crypto adoption, I don't see why things might turn around anytime soon.

It has been revealed that instead, it appears more likely that the leading cryptocurrency will continue to fall in value as investors grow more concerned about the broader markets. Bitcoin could fall to $50,000 or lower this year, depending on the extent of interest rate increases. However, even if they don't increase significantly, with plenty of concerns about the economy these days, there may be more investors looking to cash out of crypto than those looking to start a position in it.

I expect Bitcoin to fall below $50,000; it's just a matter of how rapidly it happens.
