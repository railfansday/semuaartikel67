SEC Charges Eight Colorado Firms in $37 Million Fake Crypto Platform Scam

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The SEC charged eight Colorado-based entities on Thursday with running a fake investment adviser and crypto trading platform scheme that took more than $37 million from U.S. investors. The agency stated the defendants are likely operated by people overseas.

In a recent development, according to the SEC’s complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado, the defendants defrauded more than 1,500 investors from at least August 2024 through May 2026. The advisers and the trading platforms they pointed victims to were fronts, the agency alleged, and none of the crypto assets investors thought they were buying existed.

Sources indicate that social media ads sent prospective victims to WhatsApp group and private chats run by the scammers. People in those chats posing as investment advisers first gave advice on trading U.S. stocks, then pushed victims to send as much money as possible to a supposed crypto trading platform.

Sources indicate that the complaint says the scheme ran in four adviser and platform pairs, each active for roughly six months. Advisor WeChat Capital and Tech Brokerage Asset Alliance operated from about August to December 2024. HB Wealth Advisors and IdeaCrypto Exchange followed from January to July 2025.

Convergent Wealth Advisors and RealSimple Crypto Exchange ran from about July to December 2025. Associated Wealth Advisors and Essential Crypto Exchange closed out the scheme from January to May 2026. Each pair vanished after collecting millions, the SEC stated, and a newly introduced one took its place.

Victims were told to buy ether, tether or USDC on a U.S. exchange and move it to a self-custody wallet. They then sent it to an address supplied by the adviser, and a realistic app showed balances and gains that were invented.

When investors tried to withdraw their supposed profits, they were told they first had to pay a fee, calculated as a percentage of those profits, in crypto to a separate address. The complaint notes investors who balked were threatened and pressured. Eventually the websites went down and the chats went silent.

According to recent reports, to look legitimate, the defendants made fake SEC certificates and pointed skeptical investors to real filings with the SEC, FINRA and FinCEN. The complaint says those filings carry no regulatory approval, since registering for access to its EDGAR system is ministerial and Forms ADV are not reviewed before publication.

The complaint indicates Convergent Wealth and Essential Crypto also filed false Forms ADV listing regulatory contacts who do not appear to be real and offices in Colorado that did not exist. The SEC mentioned a Form D and the two Forms ADV have been removed from its website.

According to recent reports, three unnamed investors illustrate the damage. One lost nearly $500,000, including money borrowed from a 401(k), a son and a neighbor. Another lost about $850,000, partly by liquidating a 401(k), and a third lost close to $1.6 million, including the investor’s house and IRA.

In a recent development, all eight entities face charges under Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act and Rule 10b-5. The four advisers are also charged under Sections 206(1) and 206(2) of the Investment Advisers Act, and Convergent Wealth and Essential Crypto face an additional charge under Section 207.

Sources indicate that the SEC is seeking permanent injunctions, disgorgement of ill-gotten gains with prejudgment interest, and civil penalties. It also wants the advisers barred from acting as or being associated with any investment adviser, and Convergent Wealth and Essential Crypto barred from filing as exempt reporting advisers.

The agency thanked regulators in Hong Kong, Singapore, mainland China and Switzerland for their help. It also pointed to its investor alerts on group chats and on false claims of SEC registration, and urged investors to check the background of anyone selling them an investment at Investor.gov.

“The SEC continues to seek out and expose fraudulent investment scams that prey on U.S. investors,” mentioned Laura D’Allaird, chief of the SEC Enforcement Division’s Cyber and Emerging Technologies Unit.
