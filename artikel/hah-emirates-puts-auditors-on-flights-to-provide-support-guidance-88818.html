Recent findings suggest that emirates is known for its incredibly high service standards, and that’s largely thanks to the hierarchical system onboard. You have different ranks of flight attendants, then you have supervisors overseeing business class and economy, and then those supervisors report to the purser.

This system ensures that there’s accountability onboard, and I think that lack of hierarchy is one of the reasons we have service challenges at airlines in the US, as no one is really in charge. Anyway, along those lines, Emirates has just introduced a latest initiative, which will no doubt be controversial among flight attendants….

According to recent reports, emirates has a rank above pursers, which are the Standard Pursers (which is a weird name, but the idea is that they’re there to audit and enforce “standards”). These are specially trained former pursers who no longer work flights in a standard capacity (at least until now), but instead, they audit the crews onboard flights. They have completely different uniforms.

Emirates’ Chief Operations Officer, Adel Alredha, has just taken to Instagram to highlight a latest initiative involving this small group of employees:.

On 3 November, Emirates introduced a new initiative on select flights, which will have Standard Pursers work alongside cabin crew teams to provide support and guidance to ensure consistently high standards of service.

This step reflects the airline’s ongoing commitment to maintaining service quality and elevating the customer experience.

So rather than just auditing the cabins, these Emirates employees will actually be working in the cabin, and that’s sure to ruffle some feathers….

A post shared by Adel Alredha (@adelalredha).

Emirates is of course an incredibly global airline with staff from around the world, and I know a thing or two about the internal politics that crews deal with, the different working environments based on who is purser, etc.

For example, you have some pursers who are incredibly strict to the point that they make their colleagues feel uncomfortable and nervous, while you have other pursers who try to encourage good service by creating a fun environment, etc.

According to recent reports, it goes without saying that historically when a flight had an auditor onboard, everyone would be on their absolute best behavior. In a way that’s no doubt a bit chaotic and stressful, since they’re worried about repercussions if something goes wrong.

Sources indicate that when it comes to this initiative, on the one hand, it’s probably good for these auditors to get some renewed firsthand experience with service, guest expectations, etc. On the other hand, I have to imagine that from the perspective of other crew members, this won’t necessarily be perceived as an effort to “provide support and guidance.”.

As one commenter on the Instagram post notes, “this is like sending a child to school along with mama with a stick to correct the kid every step.” Another commenter notes “as if there wasn’t already enough pressure on the crew.”.

I am curious if these flights are still staffed with a regular purser in addition to this special rank of purser, because that could also cause a bit of a butting-of-heads. If an auditor is onboard in their official capacity, it’s clear what everyone’s role onboard is (everyone is there to make sure they perform well!). But if the auditor is there to simply “provide support,” that blurs the lines a little bit, no?

Anyway, there aren’t actually that many of these auditor pursers at the airline, so presumably only a small percentage of flights will have them. But if you do notice a crew member with a completely different uniform (more silver in color), now you know what’s going on. That would also be a great person to provide any feedback to, because they actually have the ability to get those details to the right person.

Emirates has Standard Pursers, who are essentially auditors who are assigned select flights, in order to evaluate service from the crew. As of this month, these auditors are also starting to work on flights alongside the crew, to “provide support and guidance.”.

There’s no denying that this is an initiative that will make some crew members nervous, since this definitely blurs the line of auditing vs. having an extra set of helping hands. However, given the relatively small number of auditors, they shouldn’t be on too many flights.
