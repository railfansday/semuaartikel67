Sam Woods is TheGamer’s Senior Reporter. He’s been in games journalism since 2020 and has undertaken a variety of roles in that time. These have included roles such as SEO Editor, Managing Editor at DualShockers, and Global Head of Gaming, and he's also been seen on the BBC. He was promoted to the Senior Reporter position in 2025 following a series of high-quality reports and exclusives for TheGamer.

Recent findings suggest that since 2020, Sam has written over 3,000 articles and specializes in telling the stories of the people behind the games. He’s interviewed the biggest game directors, some of the industry’s top talents, told the stories of how indie games came to be, and spoken to players about what really makes them tick. He’s also interviewed some of the world’s biggest musical artists to find out how the medium has inspired their work, and TV and Film actors about their roles in video game adaptations. He even hosted a PAX panel in 2021 and made a documentary in 2024 surrounding the creation of a Disney game.

Recent findings suggest that alongside this, Sam focuses on breaking news. He got the scoop on Perfect Dark’s cancellation, provided context on some AI drama, and revealed that Lady Dimitrescu’s actress would love to step back into the character’s giant shoes. These articles have been picked up by the biggest gaming sites, including IGN, GameSpot, and PC Gamer. He also loves to dive into the numbers behind the games.

Sam’s journey in games journalism has taken him all over the world. He’s covered Gamescom in Germany, Summer Game Fest in Los Angeles, and Tokyo Game Show in Japan, each on three occasions.

He’s been learning Japanese since 2024 with the intention of identifying and translating Japanese-language news and bringing it to English-language audiences first, something he’s done countless times.

Sam’s first review was 2020’s Battletoads reboot, and he’s since reviewed games in The Legend of Zelda, Super Mario, and Pokemon franchises.

Industry observers note that gamers are a predictable bunch. Over the last couple of years, we've seen a rise in "gooner games," and players, male, female, and non-binary, have been thirsting on main more and more. More power to you. Grand Theft Auto 6 looks set to take this all to the next level.

We've seen people are genuinely worried about falling in love with the game's protagonist, Lucia, but they just can't keep their hands to themselves; in the first 24 hours since GTA 6 debuted, Google searches for terms like "Lucia Naked" and "GTA 6 Sex" have increased by a factor of 10.

In a recent development, grand Theft Auto 6 is going to be the biggest media event of our lifetimes. It broke records for pre-orders, records for review scores, and records for Twitch viewership numbers. It also looks like it's going to break records for player horniness.

Since its launch, some of our older Grand Theft Auto 6 articles have seen a huge increase in traffic. Namely, one in which the infamous Cyberleek revealed off a naked Lucia parachuting in Vice City and another in which the PlayStation Store revealed that the game would feature "sex scenes". That got me wondering: what are these GTA 6 fans really searching for?

Using Google Trends data, I had a look at a handful of Grand Theft Auto 6 search terms related to these articles, and unsurprisingly, they're being searched a lot.

Searches for "Lucia Naked" are up 350 percent over the previous week, searches for "Jason Naked" are up over 160 percent, and searches for "GTA 6 Sex" are up over 1,200 percent versus this time last week. Searches for "GTA 6 Prostitutes" have seen the biggest spike, with a more than 5000 percent increase week-over-week. Players are clearly keen for a little extracurricular GTA 6 activity.

It's a mixture of good news and bad news for these thirsty players. GTA 6 does officially feature full-frontal nudity, including a full nudist resort, which coincidentally has also seen a more than 5000 percent increase in search over the last week; however, it is dropping the series staple, sex workers. Rockstar remarked it implemented everything it needed to successfully tell Jason and Lucia's "lovers on the run" story, and evidently, prostitutes weren't one of those things.
