According to recent reports, vistra (VST -7.17%) stock was swept up in the artificial intelligence (AI) sell-off in November, but its decline began well before this month's midterm elections. Power producers have faced regulatory scrutiny over rising consumer electricity prices and who foots the bill for data centers' growing energy demands.

Industry observers note that with the market weighing the AI data center build-out narrative, Vistra's beaten-down stock is worth buying the dip on before November ends. Here's why.

After a strong performance in 2025, Vistra stock has fallen 20% this year. Regulators across major data center hubs have pushed back on electricity deals to ensure ratepayers don't end up paying for higher costs and grid expansions.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Industry observers note that midterm election results, notably in Texas, cast a shadow over the ongoing data center build-out. That stated, outgoing Governor Greg Abbott had already paused permits and ERCOT approvals while the grid operator audited grid impacts. Not only that, but evolving rules requiring data centers to bring their own energy favor Vistra, which is contracting directly with hyperscalers while adding capacity.

Vistra has reached agreements with Meta Platforms and Amazon Web Services, and under these power purchase agreements, the hyperscalers are funding capacity uprates, addressing one of the biggest concerns while Vistra locks in long-term cash flow.

It has been revealed that management forecasts adjusted EBITDA of $7.7 billion to $8.5 billion, and most of its 2027 capacity is already hedged, protecting near-term cash flows. The stock is down, and Vistra's management announced it would add another $1.5 billion to its share repurchase program, capitalizing on the beaten-down stock.

For investors bullish on long-term power demand from data centers, Vistra stock is quite appealing at today's price, now trading at just 13 times its forecasted 2027 earnings.
