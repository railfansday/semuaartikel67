Industry observers note that you can see friends often on a tight budget by making the cheap strategy the usual one. Rotate potlucks and game nights between your homes, and look up free museum days, library events, and park concerts. When the group goes out, pick happy hour, a matinee, or a group rate.

The usual blueprint with friends, a restaurant or a bar, comes with a bill every time. In a group where incomes differ, someone ends up turning down the plan or overspending to keep up.

Yet what makes time together good is the conversation, the food, and something to do, and none of it needs a restaurant or a bar.

It has been revealed that dinner out is the easiest blueprint to swap. In a rotating potluck, a different friend hosts each month and everyone brings a dish. Say dinner out runs $55 a person with tax and tip, and a potluck dish costs $15 to make. A group of six that swaps its monthly restaurant night for a rotating potluck cuts each person’s cost from $55 to $15, a $40 saving every month, and each friend hosts just twice a year.

Sources indicate that game nights, watch parties for a show or a significant game, and cooking or baking together work the same way. Each friend brings a snack, a drink, or an ingredient.

Industry observers note that for a paid night out, pick the cheaper time. Happy hour cuts the price of drinks and appetizers, and matinees and discount days cut ticket prices. For bowling or an escape room, ask about a group rate before everyone pays separately. Ask, too, whether a friend’s museum, zoo, or gym membership includes guest passes. Before you book, check for a drink minimum, common at comedy clubs, and for fees that raise the ticket price.

In your group chat this week, pick a date and a host for the first potluck, and have each friend claim a dish so no two people bring the same thing.
