Alright, here’s what’s on tap for the day: Iran and the United States engage in a latest tit-for-tat, Pete Hegseth makes an unexpected visit to Venezuela, and Donald Trump meets with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in China.

Industry observers note that the Iran war appears to be heating up (again), as diplomatic talks have hit a wall (again). The United States and Iran resumed strikes over the past week, ending a roughly two-month lull in the conflict.

The renewed fighting has come as Iran continues to attack shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and amid an ongoing U.S. blockade on Iranian oil exports.

Tit-for-tat. Over the past six days, Iran launched a series of strikes on U.S. assets in Bahrain, which hosts the U.S. Navy’s 5th Fleet, and an air base in Kuwait that hosts U.S. forces.

In a recent development, iran targeted a U.S. early warning radar facility in Bahrain on Friday and took aim at a radar system at a helicopter base in the tiny island nation on Sunday. No damage was reported. Tehran noted the attacks were in retaliation for U.S. strikes on Iranian communications towers as well as ongoing drone surveillance operations.

It has been revealed that on Tuesday, Iran targeted another radar facility in Bahrain with drones, which were reportedly intercepted; the United States retaliated with a strike on an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island. Iran responded on Wednesday with an attack on Ali Al Salem air base in Kuwait. There were no reports of damage. Bahrain also said it intercepted Iranian aerial attacks on Wednesday.

Industry observers note that under pressure. U.S. President Donald Trump has been vague about his prepares in the war, and some reports suggest he is mulling over ordering expansive strikes. But Trump on Thursday indicated that he’s not in a hurry to escalate. “I am not rushing into any decision. We will see what happens after the beginning of the year,” Trump told Axios.

Trump’s comments came as Israel is reportedly pressing the White House to escalate with a fresh bombing campaign. Roman Gofman, the director of the Israeli intelligence agency Mossad, visited Washington this week, where he is stated to have expressed concerns to U.S. officials over alleged Iranian chemical weapons development.

The United States also has intelligence that Iran has drawn up plans to potentially use a dirty bomb or chemical weapon in Washington, according to a CNN report. Trump on Tuesday said the United States was looking into the dirty bomb report and takes it “very seriously.” But he later downplayed the prospect of Iran actually using such a weapon in Washington, and he warned that “if it does happen, we’ll wipe them out.” Trump this week also told Fox News there’s “indication that Iran is looking at gasses, looking at bio, because we’ve shut them out with the nuclear.”.

According to recent reports, “And they’re looking at gas and biological weapons. Not nice. And we’ll see,” he noted.

If Trump does decide to ramp up the war, experts have warned that it would be a huge gamble and would undoubtedly lead to further retaliatory attacks on U.S. forces and assets in the region. The U.S. intelligence community reportedly assesses that Iran has a stockpile of up to 2,500 missiles in its arsenal, despite prior claims from the Trump administration of such capabilities being destroyed. Iran also remains defiant in the face of continued U.S. economic pressure, and Tehran appears emboldened by major Republican losses in the midterm elections.

The United States still has significant military assets in the region, including two aircraft carriers (after yet another reshuffling that saw the USS George Washington leave the Middle East this week, following the arrival of the USS Theodore Roosevelt). But the United States is also facing significant constraints, and the war has depleted stockpiles of key munitions. Due to such shortages, acting U.S. Navy Secretary Hung Cao this week told senators that U.S. forces resorted to firing expired munitions in the war.

U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made a surprise trip to Venezuela, where he signed a defense cooperation agreement with the country that the United States invaded in January. The Defense Department did not share details of the trip with the U.S. press, with the main images and details coming from Venezuelan state media.

Meanwhile, Politico reported that Hegseth’s personal lawyer, Tim Parlatore, also works for the son of a Libyan warlord who has been accused of war crimes.

According to recent reports, the Pentagon nominated Gen. Christopher LaNeve to be permanent chief of staff of the U.S. Army, a role that he has been carrying out in an acting capacity since the ouster of Gen. Randy George in April.

Democrats on Wednesday officially nominated Hakeem Jeffries to be speaker of the House, making him the first black person to serve in that role should he be confirmed.

Rohingya genocide verdict. The International Court of Justice ruled on Thursday that Myanmar committed a genocide against its Rohingya ethnic minority population, the first time that the court has found a state responsible for genocide. The judgment is expected to set a number of international legal precedents, including those concerning standards of evidence and reparations, as journalist Andrew Nachemson wrote in FP.

Trump’s big trilateral. Trump capped off his Asia visit this week by meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, China. In a joint “peace declaration” following their meeting, the three leaders committed to “reform” the United Nations, vowed to increase the supply of petroleum products “for the benefit of all nations,” and warned that the International Criminal Court and other similar entities “must not abuse their jurisdiction.” They also agreed to “conduct a dialogue” on nuclear risk and nonproliferation, saying that “a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.”.

It’s been one week since Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned amid a scandal over multiple extramarital affairs, but some of the United States’ most powerful individuals—including Trump—are calling for his return to the helm of the $4 trillion semiconductor company.

It has been revealed that A petition to “Bring Jensen Back” has received more than 200,000 signatures, including from the world’s richest man, Elon Musk. Trump stated he “would not mind” if Huang came back, and Huang himself fueled the fire with a lengthy post on X in which he wrote: “If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”.

The board, however, appears uninterested. Nvidia chairman Harvey Jones remarked during the firm’s quarterly earnings call on Tuesday that the board “unanimously accepted his [Huang’s] resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”.

It has been revealed that the scrutiny of Huang and Nvidia will continue in the meantime. Huang is scheduled to testify before Congress on Dec. 1, and the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission is reportedly investigating the tech giant.

In a recent development, would you commit to your current job for two to six more years if it meant getting a four-day weekend to play the hottest latest video game on the market? That’s exactly what 43 soldiers in the U.S. military’s 9th Brigade Engineer Battalion did, signing up for an incentive program that gave them a couple of extra days off to play the long-awaited Grand Theft Auto VI video game that was released on Thursday. In exchange, the soldiers will reenlist in the military for a few more years.

It’s not just about the video game—many of them had other reasons to reenlist anyway, and some also got hefty cash bonuses—but one of the soldiers told Task and Purpose that he now had “a good 24 hours to waste on it.”.
