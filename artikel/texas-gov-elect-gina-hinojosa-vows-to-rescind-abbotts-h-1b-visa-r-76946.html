In a recent development, texas Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa (D) said she looks to rescind Gov. Greg Abbott’s hiring freeze on H-1B visa workers at state agencies and public universities on her first day in office, even as she acknowledged that the federal visa program has been abused.

In a recent interview with the Texas Tribune, Hinojosa, a Democrat, dismissed Abbott’s January directive restricting new H-1B visa petitions as a distraction.

"Yeah, that does not make sense to me. I will tell you, I’m not doing culture wars. That’s not what I was elected to do," Hinojosa said.

Abbott issued the order in January to bar state agencies and public universities from filing latest H-1B petitions without written approval from the Texas Workforce Commission, citing reports that employers had displaced U. S. workers with lower-paid foreign labor.

Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa speaks at a press conference Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026, at the Lyndon B. Johnson School of Public Affairs at The University of Texas at Austin. (Jay Janner/The Austin American-Statesman via Getty Images).

"In the most egregious schemes, employers have even fired American workers and replaced them with H-1B employees, often at lower wages," Abbott wrote in his January directive.

While Hinojosa vowed to scrap the restrictions, she conceded that companies have exploited the visa system.

Recent findings suggest that "There are abuses when it comes to H-1Bs, absolutely," Hinojosa told the Texas Tribune. "Companies should not be getting a bunch of tax breaks for jobs to only then use that money to hire H-1B employees. That’s wrong. That’s happening right now. I don’t support that.".

Hinojosa, a Democratic state representative from Austin, defeated Abbott in the Nov. 3 election to deny the three-term Republican incumbent a fourth term, becoming the first Democrat elected governor of Texas since Ann Richards in 1990. With Republicans still holding a majority in the Texas Senate, rescinding Abbott’s executive orders offers Hinojosa an immediate way to reshape state policy without legislative approval.

Abbott’s directive, however, applies only to state agencies and public institutions of higher education, not local K-12 school districts.
